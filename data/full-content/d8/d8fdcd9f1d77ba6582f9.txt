The Court considered the State’s submission that the petitioner had allegedly misused the interim protection previously granted to him and had subsequently been arrested in another NDPS case. Finding no merit in the petition in view of these circumstances, Justice Suvir Sehgal dismissed the application. The petitioner had approached the High Court seeking protection against arrest in the criminal proceedings arising from FIR No. 124. On 2 December 2022, the High Court issued notice of motion and granted him interim protection pending consideration of the anticipatory bail petition. During the subsequent hearing, the State counsel, acting on instructions from ASI Bachhitar Singh, informed the Court that the petitioner had joined the investigation. However, the State opposed the grant of anticipatory bail, alleging that the petitioner had misused the concession extended by the Court and had engaged in unlawful activity. The State further submitted that the petitioner had been named as an accused in FIR No. 36 dated 28 March 2023, registered under the NDPS Act at the same police station, Shimlapuri, Ludhiana. According to the State’s instructions, the petitioner had also been arrested in connection with that subsequent case. Taking these submissions into account, the High Court concluded that the petition lacked merit and dismissed it. The order is brief and does not contain a detailed examination of the evidence, the quantity of contraband or the statutory restrictions under Section 37 of the NDPS Act. The allegations concerning misuse of interim protection are recorded as the State’s submissions rather than findings after a trial. He has specific instructions to state that the petitioner has been named as an accused in FIR No.36 dated 28.03.2023 lodged for offence under the NDPS Act at Police Station Shimlapuri Ludhiana and he has been arrested therein. 4. In view of the above, there is no merit in the petition, which is hereby dismissed.
