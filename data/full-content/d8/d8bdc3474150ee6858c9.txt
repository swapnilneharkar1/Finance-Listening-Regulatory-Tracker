Customers will not be charged directly for sharing financial information.The framework will be rolled out through a common settlement agreement from December 1, with each participant's effective date linked to its signing of the agreement.Financial institutions accessing customer data through India's Account Aggregator (AA) network will pay between Rs 5.88 and Rs 9.60 for each successfully fetched bank account statement, depending on the period of data sought, under a new standardised pricing framework. The charges will be shared among banks providing the data, account aggregators facilitating its transfer and Sahamati, the network's self-regulatory organisation. The framework, scheduled for rollout from December 1, is expected to introduce a commercial model for an ecosystem that has so far largely operated without a standardised data-fetch fee. Advt Under the framework, financial information users (FIUs), including banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and fintech lenders, will pay a standard charge whenever they successfully access a customer's financial information through the network. The fee ranges from Rs 5.88 to Rs 6.55 for a bank statement covering up to seven months and Rs 8.64 to Rs 9.60 for a 13-month statement, per account, excluding goods and services tax (GST). The exact charge within the applicable range depends on the period of data requested.The fee has three components: a charge for the financial information provider (FIP), which holds the customer's data; a digital public infrastructure utility fee for the account aggregator (AA), which facilitates its transfer; and an ecosystem management fee for Sahamati. For the seven-month benchmark, the bank providing the data receives Rs 2.80, while Sahamati gets 40 paise. The remaining Rs 2.68-3.35 goes to the account aggregator.The lender or other FIU will receive a single bill from the account aggregator, which will retain its share and distribute the remaining amounts to the bank and Sahamati. The framework is intended to replace fragmented commercial arrangements with standardised pricing and a common contractual structure. Banks, which have so far borne the cost of maintaining systems to provide customer information through the network without receiving a standard data-fetch fee, will now earn a share of the charges.For customers, the framework does not introduce a direct fee for sharing financial information. The institution requesting the data will bear the cost, and participants cannot directly charge customers for sharing information through the AA network. Customer consent will continue to be required before information is accessed and transferred. Advt The lender impactThe account aggregator framework allows customers to share financial information held by one regulated institution with another through a consent-based digital mechanism. For instance, when a borrower applies for a loan, the lender can request bank statements through the network. The customer approves the request, specifying the information to be shared, its purpose and the recipient. The bank then transmits the information through the account aggregator to the lender. The aggregator facilitates the transfer but cannot read or store the customer's financial information.The standardised data-fetch fee does not replace separate payments that financial institutions make to technology service providers (TSPs) for integration and technology services. These providers help banks, lenders and account aggregators connect their systems to the network. Account aggregators can also charge institutions separately for additional value-added services, such as customised software tools. Advt The additional cost could have a greater bearing on small-ticket digital lenders, which typically screen several applicants before disbursing a loan. Accessing two or three bank accounts for one applicant could cost around Rs 23-34, including GST, for seven-month statements. The expense can accumulate when lenders assess multiple applicants for every loan disbursed, particularly in the Rs 10,000-50,000 segment, where underwriting margins are relatively thin.Sahamati had initially scheduled the pricing framework for November 1 but deferred its rollout to December 1 following requests from industry participants and the need for additional operational preparation. In a November 23 circular, it said the unified settlement and commercial agreement would be made available to participating institutions on December 1. Each participant's effective date under the new framework will be the date on which it signs the agreement. The accompanying standard operating procedure is expected to set out billing, reconciliation and settlement processes. Advt The pricing framework follows nearly three years of deliberations on developing a sustainable commercial model for the AA ecosystem. The rates are expected to be reviewed after six months of implementation, taking into account industry feedback and operational experience.
