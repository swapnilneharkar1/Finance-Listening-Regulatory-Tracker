Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Balaji Agro Industries Vs State of Punjab and Another (Punjab and Haryana High Court) Summary: The Punjab and Haryana High Court considered a writ petition filed by M/s Balaji Agro Industries challenging a show cause notice dated 21 November 2023 suspending its GST registration. The petitioner contended that the proceedings had not been concluded within the time prescribed under Rule 22 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017 and the Punjab Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017. The State Tax Department, in its affidavit dated 13 August 2024, contended that the petitioner had approached the High Court without exhausting the statutory remedies available under the GST legislation. It submitted that the show cause notice in FORM GST REG-17 had been issued because the petitioner was allegedly not found at its registered business address. The Department further asserted that the petitioner had not submitted its reply electronically in FORM GST REG-18 on the common portal, as required under the applicable rules. According to the Department, the prescribed period for passing an order would commence only upon filing of the reply. The Department also argued that the time limit prescribed under Rule 22(3), read with Rule 21A(4), was directory rather than mandatory and that mere expiry of the prescribed period would not automatically revoke the suspension of registration. It alleged that physical verification revealed that the petitioner was non-existent at its registered premises and that verification of inward supplies disclosed transactions with allegedly non-existent suppliers or suppliers whose GST registrations had been suspended or cancelled. The affidavit furnished financial-year-wise details of alleged irregular input tax credit for FY 2018-19 to FY 2022-23 and referred to the mandatory conditions for availing input tax credit under Section 16(2) of the CGST Act, 2017. The Division Bench relied upon the Delhi High Court judgment in Fayiz Nangaparambil Vs Union of India, 2024 DHC 2050 (DB), which held that the expression “shall be passed within 30 days” appearing in Rule 22(3) is directory and not mandatory. Consequently, the proper officer does not lose the authority to pass an order for cancellation of registration merely because 30 days have elapsed from the filing of the reply to the show cause notice. The High Court further observed that the Department’s specific assertion regarding non-filing of the reply remained uncontroverted, despite its written statement having been filed more than two years earlier. No rejoinder had been filed, and there was no representation on behalf of the petitioner at the hearing. Moreover, the challenge was confined to the show cause notice, and no final cancellation order had been passed. Accordingly, the High Court disposed of the writ petition with liberty to the petitioner to pursue remedies before the statutory authorities. The Court did not independently adjudicate the merits of the Department’s allegations concerning fraudulent input tax credit. Cases Discussed Fayiz Nangaparambil Vs Union of India (Delhi High Court); 2024 DHC 2050 (DB): The High Court relied upon this Division Bench judgment holding that the expression “shall be passed within 30 days” under Rule 22(3) of the CGST Rules, 2017 is directory and not mandatory. The proper officer does not lose jurisdiction to pass a cancellation order merely upon expiry of 30 days from the filing of the reply to the show cause notice. The present writ petition challenges the show cause notice dated 21.11.2023 suspending the registration of the petitioner firm on the ground of the same having not been decided within the specified time-frame under Rule 22 of the Central Goods & Services Tax Rules, 2017/Punjab Goods & Services Tax Rules, 2017. 2. That it is pertinent to mention here that as per Rule 22 r.w. Rule 26 of the PGST/CGST Rules 2017 prescribes that reply in Form REG-18 has to be submitted electronically on the common portal i.e. www.gst.gov.in as notified under Section 146 of the Act 2017 consequently the reply submitted by any other mode except the mode or reply discussed above cannot be said to valid submission of reply. That it is settled principal of law that if the law requires something to be done in a particular manner, then it must be done in that manner and if it is not done in that manner, then it would have no existence in the eye of the law. Now in the present case the Petitioner has never submitted any reply to the above show cause notice on the common portal despite of the fact that the Petitioner counsel was advised by the respondents to file the reply on the common portal consequently the petitioner stands gets obliterated that respondents have not decided the SCN despite of ling reply to the SCN therefore cannot take the shelter of Rule 22(3) r.w. Rule 21A(4) of the PGST Rules 2017. 5. That non prejudice to the above submissions it is submitted that the time limit prescribed under Rule 22(3) read with Rule 21A(4) of the PGST Rules 2017 are directory in nature not mandatory. That it is settled law proposition of law that the use of work “Shall” to be construed by taking into consideration the nature, design and consequences, which would flow from construing it one or the other way hence the question whether statute is mandatory or directory, would depend upon the intention of Legislature and not necessarily merely by looking at the language in which it is clothed. That in the present case suo-moto cancellation are curative and precautionary measure being adopted by the revenue department in order to stop the wrong full availment of bogus ITC and further passing of bogus ITC by the tax evader. That GST (being based on the theory of one nation one tax) is a very dynamic tax regime here, where the ITC flows more swiftly than the water as it whole process of passing and availment of ITC is by electronically and has a PAN India replication hence the movement it comes to the knowledge of the authorities that the tax evader is availing/passing of bogus ITC then same have to be stopped immediately. Now the suspension of registration followed by the cancellation of registration is the immediate step whereby revenue can stop the further outflow of bogus ITC the movement the revenue gets the prima facia information of allegation mentioned is sub-section 2 of section 29 of the PGST Act 2017. Additionally, it can never be the intention of the legislature that the tax evader be allowed to evade tax despite the knowledge of evasion of tax by the tax evader consequently because the order was not passed either ways within the stipulated time period mentioned is sub rule 2 of Rule 22 of the CGST Rules 2017 it would not be deemed to be automatic revocation of suspension order of registration. 6. That it is pertinent to mention here that the Goods and Service tax (Value Added Tax) is a value added tax. Accordingly at each stage of the value chain, the tax is payable only on the value addition at that stage. Though the tax is payable on full consideration of supply at each stage, the supplier is allowed to offset tax already paid on the input supply procurement and thus cash is required to be paid only on the value added at each stage. It is only logical that the offset is allowed only for the amount already received by the Government. Section 16(2) of the CGTS Act, therefore lays down mandatory requirement for availing ITC. Possession of a tax invoice or debit note issued by a registered supplier, receipt of inward supplies and payment of tax charged on such supplies to the Government are mandatory conditions prescribed for availing ITC, Payment of tax charged on supplies, of which credit is sought to be availed, to the Government is thus a very important conditions for allowing credit. If it is so found that taxes have not even accrued or have not been paid to the Government or not received by Government, credit cannot be granted to taxpayer. 8. That from the above facts it is clear that the petitioner alongwith some other person are running a web of bogus firms in order to evade tax by issuing good less bogus invoices in order to avail and forward ingenuine ITC through such bogus transactions without paying any tax to the government Ex-chequer at any stage of the inward supply chain. Further, same modus operandi of all the firms reflects the connivance among all above firms to defraud exchequer, without paying any tax resultantly the above action of suspension of registration of the respondent is well with the four corners of law in order to safeguard the interest of the revenue.” 4. Notwithstanding the specific stand taken by the respondent(s), it is evident that the provision as enshrined under Rule 22 of the Central Goods & Services Tax Rules, 2017 read with Rule 26 of the Punjab Goods & Services Tax Rules, 2017 have been held to be directory in nature. The Delhi High Court, in the matter of ‘Fayiz Nangaparambil Vs. Union of India’ reported as 2024 DHC 2050 (DB), has held that the expression “shall be passed within 30 days” used in Rule 22(3) of the CGST Rules, providing a timeline for passing of an order on a SCN issued for cancellation of registration, is only directory, not mandatory. The authorities would not have lost the right to pass an order, after the lapse of the period of 30 days from the filing of the reply. Thus, the star argument of the appellant stands countered through judicial pronouncement. 5. Even otherwise, the specific case of the respondents in the written statement filed by them was to the effect that the period commences from the date of filing of the reply by the person aggrieved and since the reply has not been filed, hence, in the said circumstances, the said clause would not become operative. 6. Notwithstanding that the written statement had been filed more than two years ago, no rejoinder has been filed controverting the assertion made therein, hence, the submission remains uncontradicted. The stand of the department thus remains undisputed. 7. Considering it from any angle, the foundation of the case of the petitioner thus does not seem to carry any weight. It seems that it is for the said reason that the counsel for the petitioner is not appearing. It is also evident that the question raised in the petition already stand answered. Besides, the challenge is only to a Show Cause Notice and no final order had been passed. 8. In the said circumstances, the present writ petition is disposed of at this stage with liberty to the petitioner to pursue his remedies before the statutory authorities. 9. Disposed of with the liberty as aforesaid.
