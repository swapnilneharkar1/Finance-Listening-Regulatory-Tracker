Theerthalingam Contractor Vs Assistant Commissioner (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court, comprising Justice Krishnan Ramasamy, granted relief to L. Theerthalingam Contractor by reducing the late fee imposed for delayed filing of the annual GST return in Form GSTR-9 for FY 2017-18 from Rs. 2,60,800 to Rs. 10,000. The petitioner challenged the order dated 15 December 2023 levying late fee under Section 47 of the GST Act, contending that the authorities had failed to extend the benefit of the amnesty scheme introduced through Notification No. 07/2023–Central Tax dated 31 March 2023 and extended through Notification No. 25/2023–Central Tax dated 17 July 2023. Under the extended scheme, the late fee was waived to the extent it exceeded Rs. 10,000 where the annual return was furnished on or before 31 August 2023. The petitioner had filed Form GSTR-9 on 30 August 2023 and was therefore eligible for the benefit of the scheme. The petitioner also alleged violation of the principles of natural justice because the notices and impugned order had merely been uploaded on the GST portal without physical service or communication through registered post. The High Court held that the respondent ought not to have imposed late fee exceeding the prescribed amnesty limit and that failure to consider the scheme amounted to non-application of mind. Accordingly, the Court modified the impugned order and directed the petitioner to remit Rs. 10,000 within four weeks from receipt of a copy of the order. Upon production of proof of payment along with a copy of the order, the concerned bank was directed to de-freeze the petitioner’s bank account forthwith and permit its normal operation. The writ petition was disposed of without any order as to costs. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF MADRAS HIGH COURT The present Writ Petition has been filed challenging the order dated 15.12.2023 passed by the respondent. 2. The learned counsel for the petitioner submits that the impugned order levying a late fee under Section 47 of the Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 was passed for the belated filing of the annual return in Form GSTR-9 for the Financial Year 2017–2018. He further submits that under the Amnesty Scheme introduced vide Notification No. 07/2023–Central Tax dated 31.03.2023, as extended up to 31.08.2023 vide Notification No. 25/2023–Central Tax dated 17.07.2023, the late fee payable for the Financial Year 2017–2018 was waived to the extent it exceeded Rs.10,000/-, provided the return was filed on or before 31.08.2023. Since the petitioner filed its GSTR-9 on 30.08.2023, it fell squarely within the extended amnesty period. He also submits that the notices in Forms DRC-01A and DRC-01, personal hearing notices, and the impugned order were merely uploaded on the GST portal without physical service or communication through RPAD, thereby violating the principles of natural justice. 3. Heard the learned counsel for the petitioner and the learned Government Advocate (Taxes) appearing for the respondent, and perused the materials on record. 4. In the present case, the petitioner filed Form GSTR-9 on 30.08.2023. By virtue of Notification No. 07/2023–Central Tax dated 31.03.2023, read with Notification No. 25/2023–Central Tax dated 17.07.2023, the period for availing the late-fee waiver for the Financial Year 2017–2018 was extended up to 31.08.2023, restricting the maximum late fee payable to Rs. 10,000/-. As the petitioner filed the return within the said amnesty window, the respondent ought not to have levied a late fee beyond the capped limit prescribed under the aforesaid Notifications. Having failed to consider the benefit of the Amnesty Scheme, the impugned order suffers from non-application of mind and is liable to be modified. 5. In view of the above, the late fee imposed in the impugned order is reduced from Rs.2,60,800/- to Rs.10,000/-. Accordingly, this Writ Petition stands disposed of with a direction to the petitioner to remit a sum of Rs.10,000/- (Rupees Ten Thousand Only) to the respondent within a period of four (4) weeks from the date of receipt of a copy of this order. Upon production of proof of such payment, along with a copy of this order, the Bank concerned is directed to de-freeze the bank account of the petitioner forthwith and permit the petitioner to operate the same normally. There shall be no order as to costs. W.M.P. No. 47065 of 2026, filed to dispense with the production of the impugned order, is allowed. Consequently, the connected Miscellaneous Petition is closed.
