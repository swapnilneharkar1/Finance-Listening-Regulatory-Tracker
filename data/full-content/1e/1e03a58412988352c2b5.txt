Summary: The Income-tax Rules, 2026, notified through Notification No. 22/2026 dated 20 March 2026, introduce significant changes to India’s transfer pricing safe harbour framework under the Income-tax Act, 2025. The revised provisions are particularly relevant to Indian subsidiaries, captive service centres and associated enterprises of multinational groups. Software development, IT-enabled services, knowledge process outsourcing and contract software research and development are brought within a consolidated IT services category carrying a prescribed operating margin on cost of at least 15.5%. The eligibility threshold is substantially increased to ₹2,000 crore, while the special procedure for IT services permits a five-consecutive-tax-year safe harbour option through electronic Form 49. The framework also covers related-party data centre services at a 15% cost mark-up, corporate guarantees, eligible pharmaceutical research and development services and intra-group financing arrangements. Taxpayers must evaluate whether the certainty of safe harbour compensates for any margin exceeding their independently benchmarked arm’s length result. Other important compliance provisions concern transfer pricing ranges, master file reporting through Form 56, country-by-country reporting through Form 59, advance pricing agreements using Forms 51 and 52, APA rollback, associated-enterprise modified returns and statutory timelines for Transfer Pricing Officer orders. The article also considers the separate tax incentive for qualifying foreign cloud service providers procuring services from specified Indian data centres. A practical checklist is provided to help multinational groups assess eligibility, pricing exposure, documentation and cross-border double-taxation risks before electing safe harbour. What safe harbour does Safe harbour allows an eligible assessee to have its international transaction accepted at a prescribed margin or rate without the transfer pricing officer questioning it. The trade-off is that the prescribed price is typically higher than a benchmarked arm’s length margin, and the choice is binding for the period of the option. 2. IT services: one category, 15.5% The earlier categories of software development, IT-enabled services, knowledge process outsourcing and contract software R&D are merged into a single IT services category with an operating margin on cost of at least 15.5%. The earlier safe harbour margins ranged from 17% to 24% across those categories. The eligibility threshold is raised from Rs 300 crore to Rs 2,000 crore of transaction value. An option exercised is valid for five consecutive tax years and the option is made by electronic filing of Form 49 with automated processing. 3. Data centre services For related-party data centre services provided from India to a foreign entity, a mark-up of 15% on operating cost is prescribed as the arm’s length price. The Budget also exempts, until the year ending 31 March 2047, a foreign company’s income from procuring data centre services from a specified data centre, subject to conditions. 4. Other safe harbour items Commentary on the notified rules lists corporate guarantees at a commission of at least 1% a year on the guaranteed amount and R&D services relating to generic pharmaceutical drugs at an operating margin of at least 24%. Intra-group loans are also covered, but the rate details should be read from the Rules. 5. Choosing safe harbour or benchmarking If the benchmark median is below 15.5%, safe harbour costs the group a higher margin in India in exchange for certainty. If the benchmark is above 15.5%, safe harbour is a discount from an adjustment risk. A five-year option reduces compliance, but a poor year inside the period cannot be adjusted. Safe harbour does not bind the other country, so a double tax risk remains if the foreign tax authority takes a different view. 6. Other transfer pricing items in the Rules Range: arm’s length range of the 35th to 65th percentile where there are six or more comparables; otherwise the median approach applies if the price falls outside. Documentation: master file (Form 56) where group revenue exceeds Rs 500 crore and international transactions exceed Rs 50 crore (Rs 10 crore for intangibles); country-by-country report (Form 59) for groups with consolidated revenue of Rs 6,400 crore or more; general records retained for nine years. APAs: application on Form 51 with a fee of Rs 20 lakh, rollback up to four preceding years with an additional fee of Rs 5 lakh, annual compliance report on Form 52. Section 169(1) of the 2025 Act allows associated enterprises affected by an APA to file a return or modified return within the scope of the APA, for agreements entered on or after 1 April 2026. TPO timelines: section 166(7) of the 2025 Act requires the TPO order by 31 January where the limitation expires on 31 March. 7. A planning checklist Question Action Is the transaction within the category and the Rs 2,000 crore limit? Confirm eligibility, including related conditions in the Rules What is the benchmark median? Compare with 15.5% Is a five-year commitment acceptable? Consider restructuring risk Will the foreign tax authority accept the result? Consider an APA Can the documentation threshold be crossed? Plan Form 56 and Form 59 Practical takeaway: the revised regime makes safe harbour attractive for captives and IT services subsidiaries, but the decision should be taken on a numbers-based comparison and with the home country in view.
