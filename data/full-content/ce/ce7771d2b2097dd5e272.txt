(Patna High Court) Summary: The Patna High Court considered a writ petition filed by M/s CICO Patel JV, a joint venture constituted by Patel Engineering Limited and Chongqing International Construction Corporation, seeking reimbursement of the differential tax liability arising from the introduction of GST in respect of construction activities undertaken for the Indira Gandhi Institute of Medical Sciences (IGIMS), Sheikhpura, Patna. The construction contract covered a medical college, boys’ hostel and Type D and E residential accommodation. The petitioner initially sought reimbursement of Rs. 16,03,17,776.87 towards differential tax and additional GST liability incurred following the introduction of GST. Through Interlocutory Application No. 1 of 2025, the petitioner sought amendment of its prayer and revised the reimbursement claim to Rs. 10,33,32,357.91. The petitioner submitted that the differential liability arose because GST had been paid at 18% on the contract value, whereas the applicable rate, according to the petitioner, was 12%. It further contended that the issue concerning reimbursement of differential tax liability arising from the implementation of GST had already been settled by the State and considered by a coordinate Bench of the Patna High Court in M/s Jai Bhawani Construction Vs Union of India & Ors., following which various construction agencies and companies had received reimbursement. The petitioner further submitted that, according to the counter affidavit filed by respondent Nos. 4 to 6, GST returns reflected payment of Rs. 9,16,97,980.80 as GST by the petitioner. However, even this amount had allegedly not been reimbursed on the ground that certain documents were required. IGIMS opposed the grievance by submitting that the petitioner had repeatedly been requested to furnish the necessary documents specified in paragraph 14 of its counter affidavit and that their non-submission had created difficulties in processing the reimbursement claim. The High Court observed that the petitioner’s entitlement to reimbursement of GST arising from the introduction of the GST legislation was an admitted legal position, particularly because the tenders had been issued before GST came into force and the petitioner had participated in the tender process when the Bihar VAT Act, 2005 was applicable. The Court also recorded that the petitioner had deposited GST at 12% on the contract value. Relying upon the earlier coordinate Bench decision in M/s Jai Bhawani Construction Vs Union of India & Ors., the Court held that the issue concerning differential GST reimbursement was no longer in controversy and that the failure of IGIMS to reimburse the differential amount could not be countenanced. Accordingly, the High Court directed the Director of IGIMS to consider the petitioner’s reimbursement claim and take an appropriate decision, preferably within eight weeks from the date of receipt or production of a copy of the order. The Court further clarified that the authorities were entitled to seek only those documents that were relevant and necessary for settlement of the claim. Any demand for irrelevant documents would be contrary to the directions issued in identical matters. The writ petition was disposed of without independently quantifying or directing immediate payment of the entire claimed amount of Rs. 10.33 crore. Cases Discussed M/s Jai Bhawani Construction Vs Union of India & Ors. (Patna High Court); CWJC No. 1452 of 2019 and connected writ petitions; 2019 SCC OnLine Pat 1261; 05/07/2019: Relied upon by the petitioner and followed by the High Court regarding settlement of differential tax reimbursement claims arising from the implementation of GST in respect of construction contracts. The Court applied the earlier decision while recognising the petitioner’s entitlement to reimbursement and directing consideration of its claim by IGIMS. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF PATNA HIGH COURT Mr. Anurag Saurabh, learned Advocate for the petitioner, and Mr. Sunil Kumar Singh, learned Advocate representing the Indira Gandhi Institute of Medical Sciences. 2. The petitioner, a company incorporated under the Indian Companies Act, 1956, has approached this Court seeking a direction upon the respondent authorities of the Indira Gandhi Institute of Medical Sciences (hereinafter referred to as “IGIMS”) to make payment of Rs. 16,03,17,776.87/- by way of refund of the differential amount of tax arising due to the implementation of GST against the invoices raised by the petitioner upon the respondent authorities for the construction of a medical college along with boys’ hostel and Type D & E residences at IGIMS, Sheikhpura, Patna, as well as the additional liability towards payment of GST incurred by the petitioner after the introduction of the Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. 3. Before pressing the prayer referred to hereinabove, learned Advocate for the petitioner submitted that, upon realising that certain corrections were required in the aforesaid paragraph, an interlocutory application bearing I.A. No. 1 of 2025 has been filed seeking amendment of the prayer. Accordingly, the petitioner has modified its prayer and sought payment of Rs. 10,33,32,357.91/- by way of refund of the differential amount of tax, as the difference in the tax amount arose on account of the petitioner having made payment of GST @ 18% on the contract value, whereas the petitioner was required to make payment of GST @ 12% on the contract value. In view of the aforesaid facts, necessary correction has been made in the prayer portion. 4. It is further contended that the issue with regard to the refund of the differential amount of tax arising due to the implementation of GST has already been settled by the State, and this fact has also been taken note of by a learned co-ordinate Bench of this Court in M/s Jai Bhawani Construction vs. The Union of India & Ors. and other analogous cases in C.W.J.C. No. 1452 of 2019 along with other writ petitions. In the light of the aforesaid decision, the differential amount of tax arising due to the implementation of GST has been reimbursed to various construction agencies/companies. 5. It is further argued that even as per the counter affidavit filed on behalf of respondent nos. 4 to 6, it has been admitted that, as per the data reflected in the GST returns, a sum of Rs. 9,16,97,980.80/- has been paid as GST by the petitioner. However, even the admitted amount has not been refunded till date on the pretext of submission of certain irrelevant documents. 6. Learned Advocate for the IGIMS, however, submits that the petitioner has been directed time and again to furnish the necessary documents, as duly mentioned in paragraph no. 14 of the counter affidavit, and that, in absence thereof, the respondent IGIMS is facing difficulty in making reimbursement of the GST amount. 7. Considering the nature of the grievance, as also the admitted position of law that the petitioner is entitled to reimbursement of the GST amount on account of the coming into force of the Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, in view of the fact that the tenders, in question, were issued by the respondent authorities prior to the coming into force of the said Act, and the petitioner had participated in the tender process while the Bihar VAT Act, 2005 was in force, and the petitioner has deposited GST at the rate of 12% on the contract value. Further the issue has no more in controversy and also clarified by a co-ordinate Bench of this Court in M/s Jai Bhawani Construction (supra). The action of the respondent IGIMS, cannot be countenanced in not making refund of such differential amount. 8. Accordingly, this Court directs the Director, IGIMS, to consider the claim of the petitioner and take an appropriate decision, preferably within a period of eight weeks from the date of receipt/production of a copy of this order. 9. Suffice it to observe that the authorities concerned are only required to seek such relevant documents as are necessary for the settlement of the claim of the petitioner. The demand for any irrelevant document would be contrary to the terms of the order passed by this Court in identical matters. 10. The writ petition stands disposed of.
