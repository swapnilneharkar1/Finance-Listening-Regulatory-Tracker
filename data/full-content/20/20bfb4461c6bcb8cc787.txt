Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Amit Humane Vs State of Chhattisgarh (Chhattisgarh High Court) Summary: The Chhattisgarh High Court dismissed a batch of petitions challenging the order dated 11 September 2026 cancelling the Limited Departmental Competitive Examinations conducted in 2021 and 2022 for appointment as State Tax Inspectors, together with the consequential appointment orders. The petitioners, originally serving as Assistant Grade-III employees in the Commercial Tax Department, had qualified in those examinations and subsequently passed the prescribed departmental examinations conducted by the Department of Home. Some candidates selected through the 2021 examination had been confirmed as State Tax Inspectors on 14 March 2024, while candidates selected through the 2022 examination claimed confirmation remained pending. The impugned order required them to return to their substantive posts. The petitioners argued that cancellation after several years of service, without individual show-cause notices, disclosure of the enquiry reports or an opportunity of hearing, violated the principles of natural justice. They relied on their confirmation, completed probation, departmental qualifications and accrued service rights, and invoked Articles 14, 16 and 311 of the Constitution and the applicable service rules. They also submitted that earlier challenges by unsuccessful candidates had failed, that the State had previously defended the selection process as fair, and that no individual fraud or misconduct had been established against them. The State maintained that cancellation followed an extensive investigation by a five-member Enquiry Committee, whose reports dated 3 July 2026 disclosed serious irregularities affecting the integrity of the examinations. For the 2021 examination, the Court recorded defects in the custody and coding of answer booklets: booklets arrived sealed but uncoded, seals had to be opened for coding, and candidates’ names and roll numbers were not effectively concealed. The booklets were ordinary materials available in the open market. The enquiry also disclosed unauthorised re-evaluation of 22 answer sheets by Selection Committee members themselves, manual replacement of original marks with higher marks in red ink, and failure to allocate 30 vacancies according to the prescribed reservation quotas for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and Other Backward Classes. For the 2022 examination, the Court noted missing invigilator names and examination-centre seals, inconsistent invigilator signatures, absence of reliable coding and sealing records, and evaluation by officers who had not been nominated by the Commissioner as required. Although the examination scheme contained no re-evaluation provision, 22 answer sheets in the first paper and 15 in the second paper had been re-evaluated without recorded justification or approval. Loose, unnumbered and unbound answer-sheet pages could be detached, and the total of used and remaining answer sheets was 1,497 against only 1,300 purchased. Certain candidates’ answers corresponded word for word with model answers compiled from different officers’ inputs; according to the Enquiry Committee, this established advance access to those model answers. The Court distinguished the transparent internal selection considered in Pawan Kumar Tiwary and found the observations concerning tainted recruitment in Baishakhi Bhattacharyya applicable. It considered the Government’s options of retaining the process, cancelling it entirely, or separating unaffected candidates from wrongdoers. With more than 500 candidates having appeared and the irregularities affecting the process as a whole, the Court considered segregation impracticable and found wholesale cancellation proportionate. Relying on Supreme Court decisions concerning fundamentally tainted selections and mass examination malpractice, it rejected the contention that individual innocence or the possible rectifiability of irregularities required interference. The Court consequently upheld cancellation of the examinations and appointments, dismissed all the petitions and directed the parties to bear their own costs. Recognising the adverse effect on eligible candidates’ service prospects, it directed the authorities to initiate a fresh recruitment process under the applicable rules and conclude it expeditiously. The fresh process must be fair, transparent and impartial, with strict compliance with the governing rules and procedures. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CHHATTISGARH HIGH COURT 1. Since common facts and grounds involved in all these petitions, they are being considered and decided by this common order. 2. In some cases, the petitioners, who were initially appointed in the respondent department on the post of Assistant Grade III, were appeared in the departmental examination in the year 2021 and they appointed on the post of State Tax Inspector. Subsequently, after clearing the requisite examinations held by the Department of Home, their services were confirmed on the said post on 14/03/2024. In some cases, the petitioners were appeared in the departmental examination in the year 2022 and upon duly qualified in the said examination, they were appointed on the post of State Tax Inspector. Even, according to the petitioners, they cleared the examination conducted by the Department of Home, however, necessary order regarding their confirmation on the said post has not been passed. 3. Challenge in all these petitions is to the order dated 11/09/2026, passed by the respondent authorities whereby the appointment of the petitioners on the post of State Tax Inspector pursuant to the advertisement of the year 2021 and 2022 has been cancelled and the petitioners were directed to join on their initial post i.e. Assistant Grade III at their respective place of posting. 4. For the sake of convenience, the pleadings and documents of the WPS No.7033/2026 are being referred. 5. By these petitions, the petitioners have prayed for following reliefs:- In respect of examination year : 2022 (WPS No.7033/2026) “10.1 Issue an appropriate writ, order or direction, particularly a writ in the nature of Certiorari, quashing the impugned Order dated 11.09.2026 (Annexure P-1) passed by the respondent authorities, whereby the Limited Competitive Examination, 2022 has been declared void and the selection process as well as the appointment order dated 01.07.2022 have been cancelled, insofar as it affects the petitioner. 10.2 Issue an appropriate writ, order or direction declaring that the cancellation of the petitioner’s appointment as State Tax Inspector without issuance of notice/show-cause notice, without supplying the enquiry report and without affording an opportunity of hearing is illegal, arbitrary and violative of the principles of natural justice and Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India. 10.3 Issue an appropriate writ, order or direction directing the respondents to restore the petitioner’s appointment on the post of State Tax Inspector pursuant to the appointment order dated 01.07.2022, with all consequential service benefits, including continuity of service, seniority, pay and other benefits admissible in accordance with law. 10.4 Grant any other relief, order or direction which this Hon’ble Court may deem just and proper in the facts and circumstances of the case.” In respect of examination year : 2021 (WPS No.7147/2026) 10.1 That this Hon’ble Court may be pleased to issue an appropriate writ, order or direction in the nature of certiorari quashing and setting aside the impugned order dated 11.09.2026 (ANNEXURE P-1), so far as it operates against the petitioner, the same being illegal, arbitrary, violative of the principles of natural justice, of Article 311 of the Constitution of India and of the Chhattisgarh Civil Services (Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules, 1966; 10.2 That this Hon’ble Court may be pleased to issue an appropriate writ, order or direction in the nature of mandamus directing the respondents to restore the petitioner to the post of State Tax Inspector with continuity of service, seniority, pay and all consequential service benefits of continuity in service. 10.3 That this Hon’ble Court may be pleased to direct the respondents to release to the petitioner all consequential monetary benefits including arrears of pay and allowances of the post of State Tax Inspector; 10.4) That this Hon’ble Court may be pleased to pass any other order or orders, direction or directions or writ or writs as this Hon’ble Court may deem fit and proper in the facts and circumstances of the case, in the interest of justice.” Facts as projected in the writ petition : 6. (a) Facts of these cases, in brief, are that the petitioners were initially appointed as Assistant Grade-III in the respondents department and were regularized/confirmed upon completion of the prescribed probation. Subsequently, pursuant to the advertisement, the petitioners appeared in the Limited Departmental Competitive Examination, 2021 & 2022 for the post of State Tax Inspector. After declaration of the result of the said departmental examination they were appointed on the post of State Tax Inspector. The petitioners also cleared the departmental examination conducted by the Department of Home. In respect of the candidates appeared in 2021 orders were passed confirming their services on the post of State Tax Inspector whereas despite passing the departmental examination no orders issued regarding confirmation of services of the candidates who appeared in 2022. In the meanwhile, certain unsuccessful candidates challenged the selection process before this Court by filing writ petitions, which were dismissed and the said order has been affirmed by the Division Bench in writ appeal. (b) According to the petitioners they continued to serve as State Tax Inspector and while pursuing their services, the respondent authorities initiated an enquiry into alleged irregularities in the Limited Departmental Competitive Examination. Consequently, by the impugned order dated 11.09.2026, the respondents declared the examination void, cancelled the selection process and appointment orders and directed reversion of the selected candidates, including the petitioners, to their respective substantive posts with immediate effect. Thus, these petitions. Contentions : 7. (i) Mr. Manoj Paranjape, learned Senior counsel appearing for the petitioners would submit that the impugned order dated 11.09.2026, whereby the Limited Departmental Competitive Examination has been declared void and the petitioner’s selection and appointment as State Tax Inspector have been cancelled, is arbitrary, illegal, and violative of the principles of natural justice, as the same has been passed without issuance of any show-cause notice or affording the petitioners an opportunity of hearing. It is contended that the enquiry was conducted behind the back of the petitioners, without supplying him a copy of the enquiry report or the foundational materials relied upon by the respondent authorities. Learned counsel further submits that the impugned order suffers from non-application of mind, as the entire examination and selection process has been declared void and the appointments of all selected candidates have been cancelled without recording any specific finding regarding the petitioner’s involvement in or responsibility for the alleged irregularities. The respondent authorities could not have passed the impugned order, having serious civil consequences, without following due process of law and the principles of natural justice. Accordingly, it is prayed that the impugned order, insofar as it affects the petitioners, be quashed and set aside with all consequential service benefits admissible in law. (ii) Learned senior counsel would submit that the Limited Competitive Examination conducted by the respondents, was governed by the statutory rules. The applicability of the said rules was considered by this Court in WPS No. 513/2023, instituted by an unsuccessful candidate challenging the said selection process, wherein this Court upheld the applicability of the Rules. The said decision was subsequently affirmed by the Division Bench of this Court in WA No. 558/2026. Learned counsel further submits that since neither the enquiry report nor the material forming the basis thereof has ever been furnished to them, nor have the allegations or adverse findings been put to them for their explanation or response. Such non-disclosure has deprived the petitioners of a meaningful and effective opportunity to defend himself, thereby raising a serious question of violation of the principles of natural justice. The impugned action, having been taken without affording the petitioners a meaningful and effective opportunity of hearing, is unsustainable in law and is liable to be interfered with by this Court. 8. I have heard learned counsel for the parties, perused the pleadings and the enquiry reports dated 03/07/2026. Analysis: 13. From the submissions advanced by learned counsel for the parties and the material available on record, it emerges that the petitioners, who were initially appointed to the post of Assistant Grade-III (clerical cadre) in the Commercial Tax Department, participated in the Limited Departmental Competitive Examination conducted for appointment to the post of State Tax Inspector and were declared successful. Consequent thereto, appointment orders were issued in their favour, and they joined their respective posts. It is the specific case of the petitioners that, after successfully completing the prescribed departmental examinations and probationary period, some petitioners were confirmed in service and some petitioners are still not confirmed; and continued to discharge their duties as State Tax Inspectors. According to them, in view of their selection, appointment, confirmation and continued service, the respondent authorities could not have cancelled their appointments and directed their reversion to their substantive posts without affording them an opportunity of hearing. 14. On the other hand, the respondent authorities have justified the impugned action on the ground that, after declaration of the results and issuance of appointment orders, several complaints alleging serious irregularities in the selection process were received. In view of the nature of the allegations, a five-member Enquiry Committee was constituted, which conducted an enquiry, examined the relevant records and recorded the statements of the complainants. The Committee thereafter submitted its report dated 03.07.2026 before the Commissioner, State Tax, Commercial Tax Department, Chhattisgarh, identifying serious irregularities in the recruitment process. The impugned order dated 11.09.2026 was thereafter passed on consideration of the findings recorded in the said report. 15. Upon perusal of enquiry report dated 03/07/2026 of the Limited Departmental Examination of the year 2021, it was found that the examination process exhibited serious procedural irregularities. The answer booklets were brought from the examination centre sealed but uncoded, and the seals were opened for coding before evaluation, contrary to the prescribed procedure. The coding failed to conceal candidates’ names and roll numbers. The coding should have been carried out at the examination centre immediately after the conclusion of the examination, following which the answer booklets should have been sealed. However, if the answer booklets had been brought from the examination centre in a sealed condition, the seals must have been opened to carry out the coding. Such a procedure is not in conformity with the established standards of any examination system. Thus, the procedure for the custody and handling of the answer booklets was defective, making it possible for the answer booklets to be replaced easily. Furthermore, the answer booklets were of an extremely ordinary nature, were readily available in the open market, and were not suitable for use in an examination. 16. The findings of the Committee exposed an unauthorized re-evaluation process involving the fraudulent manual alteration of marks in the answer sheets. Immediately before the declaration of the final results, an illegal re-evaluation of 22 answer sheets was conducted by members of the Selection Committee themselves, without the involvement of independent examiners. During this process, physical evidence of tampering with marks was discovered, wherein the original marks had been struck out and replaced with higher marks manually using a red pen. the Committee ultimately found that the appointments had been made in flagrant violation of the statutory reservation roster and constitutionally mandated reservation quotas. The Selection Committee failed to allocate 30 available vacancies in accordance with the prescribed reservation quotas for Scheduled Castes (SC), Scheduled Tribes (ST), and Other Backward Classes (OBC). These violations constitute serious departures from the applicable statutory framework governing the selection process and undermine the legality and fairness of the appointments made pursuant thereto. 17. Moreover, upon perusal of enquiry report dated 03/07/2026 of the Limited Departmental Examination of the year 2022, it was found in the enquiry report that only the signatures of the invigilators were affixed thereon. The names of the invigilators had not been mentioned, nor had the seal of the examination centre been affixed to the answer sheets. Consequently, there was no evidence to establish that the answers in the said answer sheets had actually been written at Pandit J.N. Pandey Government Multipurpose Higher Secondary School, Raipur, which was stated to be the examination centre. There was also no evidence to establish the identity of the invigilators. In certain answer sheets, it was found that the signatures of different invigilators appeared on the first and last pages. This was an unusual circumstance. After the examination had been conducted at the examination centre, the answer sheets ought to have been coded and sealed; however, neither the note sheets nor the official files contained any evidence to establish that the answer sheets had been coded and sealed at the examination centre. 18. In the said report, as per the statement of the Centre Superintendent, the sealed envelopes containing the question papers received from the Department were opened in the control room, and the question papers were distributed by the officers deputed by the Department in accordance with the seating arrangement. The Department maintained the relevant record thereof. Only the prescribed number of question papers were supplied to the examination centre. With regard to the maintenance of records concerning the answer sheets, it was stated that the examination centre had not maintained any such records. No information was made available as to whether the Department had maintained any records relating thereto. The officers who evaluated the answer sheets informed the Enquiry Committee that they had been summoned over the telephone for the purpose of evaluating the answer sheets and that no written orders had been issued to them in this regard. Under the rules governing the Limited Departmental Competitive Examination, the evaluators were required to be nominated by the Commissioner. However, no evaluator had been nominated by the Commissioner. This was contrary to the provisions of the applicable recruitment rules. 19. It is also apparent from the report that neither the note sheets nor the official records contained any mention of the number of candidates who appeared in the First Paper and the Second Paper. According to the official records produced before the Enquiry Committee, 22 answer sheets in the First Paper and 15 answer sheets in the Second Paper had been re-evaluated. The scheme of examination prescribed under Schedule IV contained no provision for re-evaluation. Further, the note sheets prepared by the Selection Committee did not contain any reference to the circumstances necessitating re-evaluation, the criteria or grounds for re-evaluation, the orders appointing evaluators for re-evaluation, or the approval granted for undertaking such re-evaluation. The answer sheets were re-evaluated before the publication of the selection list, which was not in accordance with law. The answer sheets consisted of 24 pages of ordinary paper. The candidates had not signed the answer sheets. Further, since the pages were neither numbered nor bound together and consisted of separate sheets, they could easily be detached from the sheets containing the candidates’ particulars and marks obtained by them. The total number of answer sheets used and remaining were 1,497, whereas the number of answer sheets purchased were only 1,300. Thus, there was a difference of 197 answer sheets. The use of answer sheets in excess of the number purchased, without maintaining proper accounts and without obtaining the approval of the competent authority, vitiated the examination conducted. The accounts relating to the answer sheets had not been maintained at the examination centre, nor was any record found to indicate that such accounts had been maintained elsewhere. 20. It is further evident from the said enquiry report that for the purpose of preparing the question papers for the Limited Departmental Competitive Examination, question papers and model answers had been obtained separately from different officers. The final question papers and model answers were thereafter prepared on the basis of the question papers and model answers received from those officers. Consequently, the final question papers could not be matched verbatim with any single consolidated source book. However, the answers written by certain candidates in their answer sheets were found to correspond exactly, word to word, with the model answers. According to the Enquiry Committee, this established that the said candidates had received the model answers before the examination. 21. With regard to contention raised by Mr. N. Naha Roy, learned counsel for the petitioners that the case of present petitioners is similar to the decision rendered by the Supreme Court in the matters of Pawan Kumar Tiwari (supra) and the decision rendered by the Supreme Court in Baishakhi Bhattacharyya (supra), is concerned, the same will not be applicable in these petitions. A bare perusal of both the decisions rendered by the Supreme Court in the case of Pawan Kumar Tiwari (supra) and Baishakhi Bhattacharyya (supra), it appears that in the case of Pawan Kumar Tiwari (supra), the candidate therein fulfilled the eligibility condition and were appointed through a transparent internal selection process and were within the sanctioned cadre strength and also were not found guilty of any misconduct or fraud, whereas, in the cases at hand, the internal recruitment process itself is in question and there is a categorical finding with regard to the malpractices in the examination and hence, the observation made by the Supreme Court in the Baishakhi Bhattacharyya (supra) will be squarely applicable in the cases at hand. 22. It is worthwhile to mention here that the Government was well within its right to cancel the process, for, serious irregularities had crept in, tainting the process and in such circumstances, the cancellation of the process cannot be faulted merely on the ground that the irregularities were curable. 23. In the cases at hand, the Government had taken a decision to cancel the appointment on the post of State Tax Inspector pursuance to the advertisements issued in the year 2021 and 2022. In this regard, it is noteworthy to mention here that in the decision making, a possible three options were available to the Government, being – (i) allow the process to be taken to its logical conclusion, without being unduly bothered by the illegalities/irregularities detected and referred to by the Enquiry Committee; (ii) cancel the entire process and start it a new; and (iii) separate the grain from the chaff and to proceed with the former and complete the process. No doubt, the Government could have set right the process by preferring the third option. However, once the Government arrived at the decision considering the illegalities/irregularities detected by the Enquiry Committee that the process ought to be started afresh and preferred the second option to the first and third options, thereby cancelling the previous process, this Court is required to see that the proportionality test to adjudge whether the perfect balance was struck by preferring that option out of the other available options. In the cases at hand, more than 500 candidates appeared in the examination conducted in 2021 and 2022 and hence, at this juncture, it cannot be possible to segregate the wrongdoers and even in that process, the innocent candidates may suffer adversely. 24. A bare perusal of the material available on record, it is manifest that no person of reasonable prudence would be left in doubt that the process had a coat of discernible taint suggesting impropriety and bias, if not corruption; and applying the test of proportionality, the decision taken by the Government of cancelling the process initiated by the earlier cannot be said to be so disproportionate and incommensurate with the illegalities/irregularities detected that interference could have been said to be legitimately warranted. 25. Having considered the rival submissions advanced on behalf of the parties and upon perusal of the enquiry reports, this Court finds that the decision of the respondent authorities to cancel the recruitment examination was preceded by a detailed enquiry conducted by a duly constituted five-member Enquiry Committee. The said Committee, after examining the relevant records and recording the statements of the complainants, submitted its reports, identifying ‘n’ number of serious irregularities in the recruitment process. 26. The task of the petitioners to succeed in these petitions under Article 226 of the Constitution of India gets more difficult and complicated when considered keeping in mind the nature of allegations and the findings of the enquiry report wherein the five members enquiry committee has found large scale illegalities and irregularities in conduct of examination, award of marks and preparation of result. The magnitude and the illegalities are all pervasive and have spuriated the sanctity of the selection process as a whole. 27. The Supreme court in Inderpreet Singh Kahlon and Others Vs. State of Punjab and Others reported in (2006) 11 SCC 356 held thus: “40. We at the outset would furthermore notice that having regard to the submissions made before us by Mr Dwivedi and Mr Rao that the services of the appellants before us were terminated not in terms of the rules but in view of the commission of illegality in the selection process involved, we need not consider the applicability of the relevant provisions of the statutes as also the effect of the provisions of Article 311 of the Constitution of India. An appointment made in violation of Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution of India would be void. It would be a nullity. [See Secy., State of Karnataka v. Umadevi (3).] But before such a finding can be arrived at, the appointing authority must take into consideration the foundational facts. Only when such foundational facts are established, the legal principles can be applied. 41. If the services of the appointees who had put in few years of service were terminated, compliance with three principles at the hands of the State was imperative viz. (1) to establish satisfaction in regard to the sufficiency of the materials collected so as to enable the State to arrive at its satisfaction that the selection process was tainted; (2) to determine the question that the illegalities committed go to the root of the matter which vitiate the entire selection process. Such satisfaction as also the sufficiency of materials were required to be gathered by reason of a thorough investigation in a fair and transparent manner; (3) whether the sufficient material present enabled the State to arrive at a satisfaction that the officers in majority have been found to be part of the fraudulent purpose or the system itself was corrupt. 28. In Krishan Yadav and Another v. State of Haryana and Others reported in (1994) 4 SCC 165, the Supreme Court held thus :, “20. In the above circumstances, what are we to do? The only proper course open to us is to set aside the entire selection. The plea was made that innocent candidates should not be penalised for the misdeeds of others. We are unable to accept this argument. When the entire selection is stinking, conceived in fraud and delivered in deceit, individual innocence has no place as “fraud unravels everything”. To put it in other words, the entire selection is arbitrary. It is that which is faulted and not the individual candidates. Accordingly we hereby set aside the selection of Taxation Inspectors.” 29. The Supreme Court in The Bihar School Examination Board v. Subhas Chandra Sinha and Others reported in 1970 (1) SCC 648, held thus: “15. We are satisfied that no principle of natural justice was violated in this case. The Board through its Chairman and later itself reached the right conclusion that the examinations at this Centre had been vitiated by practising unfair means on a mass scale and the Board had every right to cancel the examination and order that a fresh examination be held. There was no need to give the examinees an opportunity of contesting this conclusion because the evidence in the case was perfectly plain and transparent. We therefore set aside the order of the High Court and ordered dismissal of the writ petition but made no order as to costs.” 30. In Union of India and Others v. O. Chakradhar reported in (2002) 3 SCC 146, the Supreme Court held thus: “8. In our view the nature and the extent of illegalities and irregularities committed in conducting a selection will have to be scrutinized in each case so as to come to a conclusion about future course of action to be adopted in the matter. If the mischief played is so widespread and all-pervasive, affecting the result, so as to make it difficult to pick out the persons who have been unlawfully benefited or wrongfully deprived of their selection, in such cases it will neither be possible nor necessary to issue individual show-cause notices to each selectee. The only way out would be to cancel the whole selection. Motive behind the irregularities committed also has its relevance.” (Emphasis supplied) 31. In the facts and circumstances of the present cases, this Court finds no sufficient ground to interfere with the impugned order dated 11.09.2026, whereby the recruitment examination conducted for promotion of employees from the Clerical Cadre to the post of State Tax Inspector, along with the consequential appointment orders, was cancelled. The decision, having been taken upon consideration of the findings recorded in the enquiry report, cannot be set aside merely on the ground that the irregularities might have been capable of rectification, particularly when the competent authorities considered the irregularities serious enough to warrant cancellation of the entire process. Consequently, this Court finds no merit in all these writ petitions. 32. As an upshot, all the writ petitions, sans substratum, are liable to be and are hereby dismissed, leaving the parties to bear their own cost(s). 33. It is however made clear since cancellation of the recruitment process may adversely affect the service prospects of the eligible candidates, the respondent authorities are directed to initiate a fresh recruitment process, in accordance with law and as per the applicable rules, and conclude the same expeditiously, without any unnecessary delay. 34. It is further made clear that the fresh recruitment process shall be conducted in a fair, transparent, and impartial manner, ensuring strict compliance with the applicable rules and procedures.
