Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); ITO Vs Vishal Jaggi (ITAT Raipur) Delay in Filing Form 67 Alone Cannot Bar Foreign Tax Credit Where the Form Is Available Before the Rectification Order: ITAT Raipur Raipur ITAT upholds relief subject to verification Can the Centralised Processing Centre (CPC) deny foreign tax credit of ₹3,27,072 because Form 67 was filed late, even though the credit was claimed in the original return and the form was on record before the rectification order? In ITO-3(1), Raipur v. Vishal Jaggi, ITA No. 779/RPR/2026, decided on 20 November 2026 for AY 2021–22, the Raipur ITAT upheld the direction to allow the credit after verifying Form 67 and the supporting documents. Following the Madras High Court, the Tribunal treated the filing requirement under Rule 128 as directory in the circumstances of the case. The credit was claimed in the original return The assessee filed his return on 9 August 2021, declaring total income of ₹18,94,890 and claiming foreign tax credit under Section 90. He did not furnish Form 67 or the prescribed supporting material at that stage. The first appellate authority recorded 31 December 2021 as the applicable due date under Section 139(1). The CPC processed the return and issued an intimation under Section 143(1) on 13 October 2022 without granting the credit. The Tribunal’s account records that the intimation gave no reason for withholding the relief. An initial rectification request also failed. The assessee then uploaded Form 67 on 30 May 2024 and filed a fresh application under Section 154 on 8 June 2024. The CPC again refused the credit by its rectification order dated 23 September 2024. The assessee appealed against that order. Why the first appellate authority directed verification The Additional/Joint Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals) considered the form and supporting documentation prescribed under Rule 128(8), along with the filing deadline applicable to the year. The appellate authority noted that Form 67 was already available to the CPC when it passed the September 2024 order. The credit had been claimed from the outset, and the later filing supplied the prescribed form through which the claim could be checked. The appellate authority found the assessee prima facie entitled to the credit despite the delayed filing. It directed the Assessing Officer to allow the relief under Section 90 or Section 91, as applicable, after verifying Form 67 and the other relevant documents. The direction thus left the officer to determine the admissible credit on the evidence. The Revenue challenged that direction before the Tribunal. Madras High Court precedent applied to the rectification stage The Tribunal relied on Duraiswamy Kumaraswamy v. PCIT, W.P. No. 5834 of 2022, decided by the Madras High Court on 6 October 2023. The High Court treated the filing requirement under Rule 128 as directory and held that the taxpayer was entitled to due credit where the material had been filed before the Section 143(1) intimation. In reaching that conclusion, the High Court drew on the Supreme Court’s reasoning in CIT v. G.M. Knitting Industries (P) Ltd., a case involving the later furnishing of a prescribed form in support of a tax claim. The chronology in Vishal Jaggi presented a different procedural stage. Form 67 was filed after the original Section 143(1) intimation but before the rectification order under appeal. The Tribunal focused on the record available to the CPC when it decided the rectification application. Since the original return already contained the claim and Form 67 was available by then, the Tribunal held that the claim was verifiable. It upheld the appellate direction and dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. Author’s comments The sequence of filings gives this decision its practical significance. A return claiming foreign tax credit identifies the relief sought. Form 67 and the supporting records give the tax authority the particulars needed to check the foreign income, the tax paid or deducted abroad and the credit admissible in India. Here, the assessee supplied the missing form before filing the second rectification application, so the CPC had it throughout the proceedings that led to the order under appeal. The ruling is particularly relevant where the original return already claims the credit and a later filing completes the documentation. In an appeal against a rectification order, the original return acknowledgement, the Form 67 upload acknowledgement and the rectification order establish the chronology on which the Tribunal relied. The foreign tax statement or certificate and the reconciliation with the income disclosed in India address the separate question of how much relief is due. The result in this case was a direction to grant the credit after verification. The Tribunal upheld the assessee’s right to have the existing claim examined on the material available when the CPC passed the rectification order. The ₹3,27,072 claimed remained subject to that verification. Cases Discussed Duraiswamy Kumaraswamy Vs PCIT and Others, W.P. No. 5834 of 2022 and W.M.P. Nos. 5925 and 5927 of 2022, dated 06.10.2023 (Madras High Court): The filing requirement under Rule 128 is directory in nature. Foreign tax credit cannot be denied merely because of delayed filing where the relevant material was available before issuance of the intimation under Section 143(1). Relied upon by the Tribunal. Commissioner of Income-Tax, Maharashtra Vs G.M. Knitting Industries (P) Limited, Civil Appeal Nos. 10782 of 2013 and 4048 of 2014, dated 24.06.2015 (Supreme Court): Subsequent furnishing of a prescribed form before completion of assessment could satisfy the procedural requirement for claiming a tax benefit. Referred to through the Madras High Court judgment. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT RAIPUR The present appeal preferred by the Revenue emanates from the order of the Ld.ADDL/JCIT(A)-4, Hyderabad, dated 11.08.2026 for the assessment year 2021-22 as per the grounds of appeal on record. 2. Brief facts in this case are as follows: “The assessee has filed the return of income on 09.08.2021 declaring total income of Rs.18,94,890/-. In the return of income, the assessee has claimed tax relief u/s 90 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 {hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’ in respect of Foreign Tax Credit of Rs.3,27,072/-. The assessee has not filed the Form No. 67. The return was processed by the AO, CPC, Bangalore and an intimation u/s.143(1) of the Act was issued on 13.10.2022 without allowing the claim of the relief claimed and also the reason for not allowing the relief of FTC was also not mentioned. Aggrieved by the intimation the assessee filed for the Rectification of the order and CPC, Bangalore passed the Rectification order disallowing the relief u/s.90 of the Act. Further the assessee filed Form No.67 belatedly online on 30.05.2024. Thereafter the assessee filed Rectification application u/s.154 of the Act on 08.06.2024 but CPC processed the Rectification Order dated 23.09.2024 disallowing the relief u/s.90 of Act. Being aggrieved with the rectification Order dated 23.09.2024, assessee preferred appeal before the ld. CIT(A).” 3. In this regard, the Ld.ADDL/JCIT(A) has held and observed as follows: “7.4 The appellant has made claim of FTC in the original return of income but has not filed Form No. 67 and the certificate/statement as prescribed by Rule 128 (8) of the Income Tax Rules, 1962. The appellant has filed the Form No. 67 only on 30.05.2024. Therefore, as Form No. 67 was not filed by the appellant before the due date specified u/s.139(1) of Income Tax Act, 1961 i.e. 31.12.2021, the credit of FTC was not allowed in the intimation issued on 13.10.2022 and further in latest Rectification order issued on 23.09.2024 based on non-fulfilling of conditions as specified under Rule 128 (8) of the Income Tax Rules, 1962. The appellant has relied on various judicial pronouncements to claim that even if Form No. 67 is filed late then also credit of FTC should be allowed. 7.5 On the issue of granting credit of FTC vis-a-vis filing of Form No. 67 after the specified date the Hon’ble Madras High Court in a recent judgment dated 06.10.2023 in W.P.No.5834 of 2022 and W.M.P.Nos.5925 and 5927 of 2022 in, the case of Duraiswamy Kumaraswamy Vs PCIT and others has observed as under: 11. The law laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court in CoMmissioner of Income-Tax, Maharashtra v. G.M.Knitting Industries (P) Limited in Civil Appeal Nos.10782 of 2013 and 4048-of 2014 dated 24.06.2015, which was referred above, would be squarely applicable to the present case. In the present case, the returns were filed without FTC, however the same was filed before passing of the final assessment order. The filing of FTC in terms of the Rule 128 is only directory in nature. The rule is only for the implementation of the provisions of the Act and it will always be directory in nature. This is what the Hon’ble Supreme Court had held in the above cases when the returns were filed without furnishing Form 3AA and the same can be filed the subsequent to the passing of assessment order. W.P.No.5834 of 2022 12. Further, in the present case, the intimation under Section 143(1) was issued on 26.03.2021, but the FTC was filed on 02.02.2021. Thus, the respondent is supposed to have provided the due credit to the FTC of the petitioner. However, the FTC was rejected by the respondent, which is not proper and the same is not in accordance with law. Therefore, the impugned order is liable to be set aside. 7.6 In this case the appellant has filed Form No. 67 on 30.05.2024 and the and the Rectification order was issued on 23.09.2024 Therefore, in the case of the appellant on the date of issue of rectification order Form No. 67 was available with AO, CPC and the claim made by the appellant for FTC credit was verifiable. Considering the facts and circumstances of the case and the judicial pronouncements on the subject which has been relied upon by the appellant and as stated above the appellant is seen to be prima facie entitled to credit of FTC even though there is delay in submission of Form No. 67. 7.7 In view of the above, The Assessing Officer is therefore directed to .allow the claim of tax relief u/s 90/91 of the Act to the appellant after verification of Form No. 67 and other relevant documents.” 4. The Hon’ble High Court of Madras in the case of W.P.No.5834 of 2022 and W.M.P.Nos.5925 and 5927 of 2022 in, the case of Duraiswamy Kumaraswamy Vs PCIT and others, dated 06.10.2023 on the issue of granting credit of FTC vis-à-vis filing of Form No.67 after the specified date has held that the filing of FTC in terms of the Rule 128 is only directory in nature. In the case of the assessee, Form 67 was filed on 30.05.2024 while rectification order was passed on 23.09.2024. Therefore, on the date of issuing of rectification order, the said Form 67 was already available with the A.O/CPC and therefore, the claim made by the assessee for FTC credit was verifiable. 5. That on examination of facts and circumstances and upon careful consideration of the findings of the Ld.ADDL/JCIT(A), we do not find any infirmity with the findings of the Ld. ADDL/JCIT(A) and relief provided to the assessee is hereby sustained. 6. In the result, appeal of the Revenue is dismissed. Order pronounced in the open court on 20th November, 2026.
