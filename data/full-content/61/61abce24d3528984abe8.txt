Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ford India Private Limited Vs ITO (Madras High Court) The Madras High Court, in Ford India Private Limited Vs ITO, set aside the assessment order dated 15 May 2021 for Assessment Year 2017–18 because the Assessing Officer had completed the assessment while the assessee’s objections were pending before the Dispute Resolution Panel (DRP). The Court held that the assessment could not be finalised without awaiting the directions of the DRP under Section 144C(5) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, read with Section 144C(13). The writ petition was decided on this narrow procedural issue without examining the merits of the underlying assessment. The petitioner had approached the DRP against the proposed assessment. However, before the DRP issued its directions, the Assessing Officer passed the impugned assessment order under Section 143. During the hearing, the Revenue contended that if the petitioner had informed the Assessing Officer within thirty days of its intention to file objections before the DRP, the Assessing Officer would have awaited the DRP’s directions. The Revenue nevertheless acknowledged that the limitation period had been extended owing to the COVID-19 situation and that the documents on record established that the petitioner had approached the DRP. The High Court examined the statutory framework under Section 144C. It noted that Section 144C(5) empowers the DRP to issue directions where objections have been received under Section 144C(2). Section 144C(13) requires the Assessing Officer, upon receiving those directions, to complete the assessment in conformity with them within the prescribed period. Since the petitioner’s objections remained pending, the Assessing Officer was required to await the DRP’s decision before proceeding further. Accordingly, the Madras High Court quashed the assessment order dated 15 May 2021, bearing DIN ITBA/AST/S/143(3)/2021-22/1032928501(1), solely because the DRP’s directions had not been awaited. The Court directed the Assessing Officer to proceed with the assessment de novo, on its own merits and in accordance with law, after receiving the directions under Section 144C(5). It expressly clarified that no opinion had been expressed on the merits of the dispute and that the fresh assessment proceedings must remain uninfluenced by any observations in the judgment. The writ petition and connected miscellaneous petitions were disposed of without any order as to costs.
