Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Devendrakumar Fuljibai Parmar Vs EAK Traders (GSTAT Thane) Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), State Bench, Thane, comprising Judicial Member Ramesh Nair and Technical Member Prallhad S. Paranjape, held that sanctioning a refund of Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) paid on exports was premature in circumstances where the eligibility of the underlying input tax credit (ITC) was the subject matter of pending adjudication proceedings under Section 74 of the CGST Act, 2017. The Revenue challenged the common appellate order dated 30 September 2025 passed by the Additional Commissioner, CGST and Central Excise (Appeals), Raigad Commissionerate, which had set aside two orders rejecting the export refund claims of M/s EAK Traders. EAK Traders was engaged in exporting mobile phones to the United Arab Emirates (UAE) as zero-rated supplies. The taxpayer exported the goods on payment of IGST and subsequently claimed a refund through the Customs authorities. Based on data analytics and an alert issued by the National Customs Targeting Centre (NCTC) and the Directorate General of Analytics and Risk Management (DGARM), the taxpayer was identified as a risky exporter. Consequently, the refund claims were not processed through ICEGATE and were electronically transmitted to the jurisdictional CGST authorities for verification of the credentials of the exporter and its suppliers under CBIC Instruction No. 04/2022-GST dated 28 November 2022. During backward supply chain verification, the Department alleged that several direct suppliers of the taxpayer, including Shree Shyam Enterprises, Bhuvi Enterprises, Mayank Enterprises, Shri Hari Enterprises, R.D. Enterprise and Trisha Venture, were either non-existent at their declared business premises or had their GST registrations retrospectively cancelled for allegedly issuing invoices without actual supply of goods or payment of taxes. Based on these findings, the adjudicating authority rejected refund claims of Rs. 62,10,143 and Rs. 19,89,832, aggregating to Rs. 81,99,975, on the ground that the underlying ITC was ineligible under Sections 16(2)(a) and 16(2)(c) of the CGST Act. Separately, a show cause notice dated 9 December 2024 was issued under Section 74 proposing denial and recovery of the same amount of allegedly wrongful ITC. The first appellate authority allowed the taxpayer’s appeals and set aside both refund rejection orders. It relied substantially upon an Anti-Evasion Branch verification letter dated 16 October 2023, observing that the suppliers were registered at the time of supply, had filed GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns, and had received payments through banking channels. The appellate authority also considered that retrospective cancellation of suppliers’ registrations could not, by itself, justify denial of ITC under Section 16(2)(c), particularly when actual exports through Customs established the genuineness of the export transactions. The Department challenged these findings, contending that the verification letter relied upon by the first appellate authority related only to Shipping Bill No. 1847589 dated 19 June 2023 and could not be extended to nine separate shipping bills covering different periods. It further relied upon paragraph 7 of CBIC Instruction No. 02/2022-GST and alleged that backward supply chain investigations had established the existence of fictitious suppliers issuing invoices without actual movement of goods or payment of tax. Relying upon the Supreme Court judgment in State of Karnataka Vs Ecom Gill Coffee Trading Private Limited, the Department argued that the burden of proving ITC eligibility under Section 155 of the CGST Act could not be discharged merely by producing invoices and proof of payments through banking channels. The taxpayer contended that the departmental appeal had become infructuous because a consequential refund sanction order dated 9 January 2026 had already been issued for Rs. 81,99,975. The Department responded that actual disbursement had been withheld under Section 54(11) of the CGST Act pending litigation and anti-evasion proceedings. The taxpayer further submitted that the risky exporter alert had been removed by the Customs authorities on 31 January 2024 and that the physical movement and export of goods stood established through Customs examination and issuance of Let Export Orders. It also invoked the doctrine of impotentia excusat legem, contending that a bona fide purchaser could not be expected to ensure subsequent tax compliance by suppliers or anticipate retrospective cancellation of their registrations. During the hearing, the Tribunal specifically enquired whether independent statutory proceedings had been initiated for denial and recovery of the allegedly fraudulent ITC underlying the refund claims. The Department produced the show cause notice dated 9 December 2024 issued in FORM GST DRC-01 under Section 74 of the CGST Act, proposing recovery of Rs. 81,99,975. The Tribunal considered this development crucial because the refund claims and the pending demand proceedings concerned the same underlying ITC. The Tribunal observed that eligibility for refund of IGST paid on zero-rated supplies was linked to the lawful availment of ITC used for payment of such IGST, and that the validity of the credit was required to be examined with reference to Section 16(2) of the CGST Act. The Tribunal held that processing or sanctioning the refund independently of the pending Section 74 proceedings would be premature when the foundational ITC itself was disputed on allegations of fraud. It found that the first appellate authority had erred in allowing the refund based on preliminary verification reports without awaiting the outcome of the substantive anti-evasion proceedings. At the same time, GSTAT held that the original adjudication orders rejecting the refunds outright without awaiting the issuance and outcome of the Section 74 show cause notice were also legally and procedurally flawed. Thus, neither outright rejection nor unconditional sanction of the refund was considered appropriate in the circumstances of the case. Accordingly, GSTAT set aside both the first appellate order dated 30 September 2025 and the corresponding orders-in-original and remanded the entire matter to the adjudicating authority for fresh adjudication, keeping all issues open. The authority was directed either to keep the refund applications in abeyance or consolidate their consideration with the ongoing proceedings arising from the Section 74 show cause notice dated 9 December 2024. The final decision regarding sanction or rejection of the refund claims must be taken afresh, following due process of law and strictly based on the outcome of the proceedings concerning the underlying ITC. The taxpayer was directed to cooperate fully with the adjudication proceedings. The departmental appeal was thus allowed by way of remand, without finally determining whether the disputed ITC was fraudulent or otherwise ineligible. Cases Discussed State of Karnataka Vs Ecom Gill Coffee Trading Private Limited (Supreme Court of India); Civil Appeal No. 230 of 2023 and connected appeals; 2023 INSC 212; 13/03/2023: Relied upon by the Department for the proposition that the purchasing dealer claiming input tax credit must establish the genuineness of the underlying purchase transactions and actual movement of goods, and that mere production of invoices and proof of payment through banking channels may not discharge the statutory burden of proof. The Supreme Court rendered this judgment in the context of Section 70 of the Karnataka Value Added Tax Act, 2003. The Department invoked its reasoning in relation to Section 155 of the CGST Act, 2017. GSTAT did not finally adjudicate the disputed ITC eligibility and instead remanded the refund proceedings to await the outcome of the pending Section 74 adjudication. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF GST APPELLATE TRIBUNAL, THANE 1. Introduction 1.1 The present Appeal has been preferred by the Revenue (Department) assailing the Order-in-Appeal passed by the Additional Commissioner, CGST & CX (Appeals), Raigad Commissionerate. The First Appellate Authority, vide the impugned appellate order, had set aside the Orders-in-Original that rejected the refund of Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) paid on the export of goods. 1.2 The present appeal arises out of the impugned Order-in-Appeal No.AKN/ADC/GST/353&354/RGD APP/25-26 dated 30.09.2025, which set aside two underlying Orders-in-Original Nos-: F.No. V-18((Dn-V)Refund/73/Eak Traders/2023-24 dated 09.01.2024 and F.No. V-18((Dn-V)Refund/73/Eak Traders/2023-24 dated 01.04.2024 passed against the Respondent, M/s EAK Traders. The core issue in this appeal pertains to the grounds of refund rejection and the legal challenge regarding the eligibility of Input Tax Credit (ITC) availed from suppliers whose registrations were retrospectively cancelled. 2. Brief Facts of the Case 2.1 The Respondent (Assessee), M/s EAK Traders, is engaged in the business of exporting mobile phones to the United Arab Emirates (UAE) under the category of zero-rated supplies. The Respondent exported the goods on payment of IGST and subsequently sought a refund of the IGST paid on such exports through the Customs Authority. 2.2 Based on data analytics and an alert issued by the National Customs Targeting Centre (NCTC) / Directorate General of Analytics and Risk Management (DGARM), the Respondent was identified as a “Risky Exporter”. Consequently, the IGST refund claims were not processed by ICEGATE (Customs) and were electronically transmitted to the jurisdictional CGST authorities for detailed verification of the credentials of the exporter and their suppliers, in terms of CBIC Instruction No. 04/2022-GST dated 28.11.2022. 2.3 During the ensuing backward supply chain verification, the Department observed that several direct tier-1 suppliers of the Respondent (including M/s Shree Shyam Enterprises, M/s Bhuvi Enterprises, M/s Mayank Enterprises, M/s Shri Hari Enterprises, M/s R.D. Enterprise, and M/s Trisha Venture) were either non-existent at their declared places of business or had their GST registrations suo-moto cancelled by the jurisdictional authorities for operating as fake invoicing syndicates without any underlying supply of goods or payment of taxes. 2.4 Based on these adverse findings, the Adjudicating Authority (Assistant Commissioner, CGST & C.Ex, Division-V, Raigad) rejected the refund claims of the Respondent to the extent of Rs. 62,10,143/- (vide Order-in-Original dated 09.01.2024) and Rs. 19,89,832/- (vide Order-in-Original dated 05.04.2024/01.04.2024), totaling Rs. 81,99,975/-. The rejection was grounded on Section 16(2)(a) and (c) of the CGST Act, 2017, concluding that the ITC was availed on invoices issued by suppliers whose registrations were cancelled, making the credit fraudulent and ineligible. 2.5 Concurrently, based on the same investigation, the Deputy Commissioner (Preventive), CGST & C. Ex Raigad also issued a separate Show Cause Notice F.No. V/PI/RGD/Gr-VIII/EAK/30-217/23-24/4685 dated 09.12.2024 under Section 74 of the CGST Act to the Respondent for the denial and recovery of the alleged wrongfully availed ITC amounting to Rs. 81,99,975/- covering the period FY 2022-23 to 2023-24. 2.6 Aggrieved by the refund rejection orders, the Respondent filed appeals before the First Appellate Authority. The Additional Commissioner (Appeals), Raigad, vide the impugned Order-in-Appeal dated 30.09.2025, set aside both rejection orders and allowed the refunds. The Appellate Authority relied heavily on a localized verification letter from the Anti-Evasion Branch dated 16.10.2023, observing that the suppliers were active at the time of supply, filed GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, and payments were made through banking channels. The Authority held that denying ITC based on the retrospective cancellation of a supplier’s registration goes beyond Section 16(2)(c) and that the actual export of goods through Customs proved the transaction’s genuineness. 2.7 Aggrieved by the impugned Order-in-Appeal, the Revenue (Department) has preferred the present appeals before this Tribunal. 3. Grounds of Appeal (Submissions by the Appellant – Department) 3.1 Shri Chetan More, learned Authorized Representative appearing for the Department vehemently argued that the First Appellate Authority committed a gross error in law and fact by setting aside the well-reasoned Orders-in-Original. 3.2 It was submitted that the Appellate Authority’s reliance on the Anti-Evasion communication dated 16.10.2023 is misplaced and legally flawed. The said communication arose from an NCTC alert received in June 2023 and was strictly confined to the verification of a single shipping bill (No. 1847589 dated 19.06.2023). The Appellate Authority committed a patent perversity by improperly stretching a localized verification report for one shipping bill across nine distinct shipping bills spanning multiple periods to give a blanket clean chit to the Respondent. 3.3 The Department argued that the Appellate Authority completely ignored the specific mandate of CBIC Instruction No. 02/2022-GST (Para 7), which categorically states that in case of retrospective cancellation of the registration of a supplier, the recipient is not entitled to claim ITC in respect of invoices issued after the effective date of cancellation. Backward supply chain investigations conclusively unmasked the Respondent’s direct tier-1 suppliers as dummy, non-existent paper entities that generated fake invoices without any actual movement of goods or tax deposition. 3.4 The Department strongly relied on the Hon’ble Supreme Court’s judgment in State of Karnataka v. Ecom Gill Coffee Trading Pvt. Ltd., asserting that the statutory burden of proof under Section 155 of the CGST Act is not discharged merely by producing paper invoices or bank payment proofs. The claimant must establish the actual physical movement of goods and the real existence of the supplying entities, which the Respondent utterly failed to do, given the fictitious nature of its suppliers. 4. Submissions by the Respondent (Assessee) 4.1 The Respondent (Assessee) staunchly defended the impugned Order-in-Appeal. The Respondent submitted that the Department’s appeal is essentially moot because the Adjudicating Authority has already followed the Order-in-Appeal and passed a consequential Refund Sanction Order dated 09.01.2026 for Rs. 81,99,975/-. In rebuttal, the Department submitted that this was merely an administrative sanction on paper, while the actual disbursement was lawfully withheld under Section 54(11) of the CGST Act to protect public revenue pending this litigation and anti-evasion proceedings. 4.2 The Respondent submitted that the “Risky Exporter” alert originally placed by DGARM was officially removed by the Customs Authorities (SIIB) on 31.01.2024, which independently validates the integrity of their exports. It was contended that the physical movement of goods is unquestionable, as the goods were physically examined and cleared by Customs via Let Export Orders (LEO). 4.3 The Respondent maintained that they had fulfilled all conditions under Section 16(2)(c) by making payments to registered suppliers through formal banking channels. The Respondent invoked the legal doctrine of impotentia excusat legem, arguing that a bona fide purchaser cannot be penalized or compelled to perform the impossible task of ensuring a supplier’s post-transaction tax compliance or predicting a retrospective cancellation of the supplier’s registration over which the buyer has no control. 4.4 The Respondent submitted that various judgments consistently hold that an innocent recipient cannot be denied ITC due to the default or subsequent cancellation of the selling dealer in the absence of proven collusion. 5. Questions of Law Involved 5.1 The present Appeals gives rise to the following substantial questions of law: (a) Whether a refund of Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) paid on exported goods under Section 54 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 96 can be conclusively sanctioned when a substantive Show Cause Notice under Section 74 of the Act, seeking the denial and recovery of the very same underlying Input Tax Credit (ITC), has been issued and is currently pending adjudication? (b) Whether the First Appellate Authority’s order allowing the refund was legally premature and detrimental to the public exchequer in light of the ongoing anti-evasion investigations and the specific statutory framework linking refunds to valid ITC? (c) Whether the entire matter of the refund claims ought to be remanded to the Adjudicating Authority to be decided afresh, strictly contingent upon the final outcome of the pending demand proceedings for the underlying ITC? 6. Discussion and Findings 6.1 We have carefully considered the submissions made by the Appellant (Department) and the Respondent (Assessee), and have meticulously perused the records of the case. We find that a crucial factual and legal development was brought to the notice of this Tribunal during the course of the hearing, which fundamentally alters the trajectory of these proceedings. 6.2 During the hearing, the Tribunal explicitly queried whether any independent statutory proceedings had been initiated for the denial or recovery of the allegedly fraudulent ITC involved in these refund matters. In response, the Appellant (Department) placed on record a substantive Show Cause Notice dated 09.12.2024 supra issued in FORM GST DRC-01 under Section 74 of the CGST Act, 2017. 6.3 This Show Cause Notice explicitly proposes the denial and recovery of wrongfully availed ITC amounting to Rs. 81,99,975/- spanning the financial years 2021-22 to 2023-24. The demand is based on a comprehensive backward supply chain investigation which concluded that the Respondent’s direct suppliers were allegedly non-existent entities whose registrations were cancelled ab-initio for operating fake invoicing rings. 6.4 The statutory framework governing refunds under Section 54(3) of the CGST Act, read with Rule 96 of the CGST Rules, 2017, dictates that a refund of IGST paid on zero-rated supplies is fundamentally predicated on the lawful and valid availment of the ITC used to pay such IGST. The eligibility of this ITC is strictly governed by the mandatory conditions set out in Section 16(2) of the Act. 6.5 It is a settled principle of law that a consequential relief such as a refund cannot be granted if the foundational basis of that relief (the ITC) is legally disputed and is the subject matter of an active statutory demand proceeding for fraud or collusion. A refund of IGST is inextricably linked to the lawful availment of the underlying ITC. 6.6 In the present factual matrix, since the foundational ITC itself is under a severe cloud of alleged fraud and is now the subject of a pending statutory demand notice under Section 74 of the CGST Act, evaluating or sanctioning the refund in isolation is legally incongruous. Sanctioning the refund while the Section 74 proceedings are underway is entirely premature and severely jeopardizes the Revenue’s interest. The First Appellate Authority erred in rushing to allow the refund based on preliminary clearance reports, thereby usurping the outcome of the substantive anti-evasion proceedings. 6.7 Similarly, the initial Orders-in-Original, which rejected the refunds outright without waiting for the issuance and outcome of the Section 74 SCN, were legally and procedurally flawed. 6.8 Therefore, to ensure consistency, prevent multiplicity of proceedings, and safeguard the interests of both the Revenue and the Assessee, the entire matter of the refund claims must be reconsidered afresh. The refund claims cannot be adjudicated in a vacuum; their admissibility is entirely dependent on, and must be decided strictly on the basis of, the final outcome of the pending Show Cause Notice issued under Section 74 of the CGST Act for the denial and recovery of the ITC. 7. ORDER 7.1 For the facts, reasons, and legal provisions discussed hereinabove, the impugned Order-in-Appeal passed by the Additional Commissioner, CGST & CX (Appeals), Raigad Commissionerate (including Order-in-Appeal dated 30.09.2025), and the corresponding Orders-in-Original passed by the Assistant Commissioner, CGST & C.Ex, Division-V, Raigad are hereby set aside. 7.2 The matters are remanded in their entirety, keeping all the issues open, to the Adjudicating Authority for a fresh, de novo adjudication. 7.3 The Adjudicating Authority is directed to keep the processing of these refund applications in abeyance or consolidate them with the ongoing adjudication proceedings initiated under Section 74 of the CGST Act, 2017 arising from SCN dated 09.12.2024 7.4 The final decision regarding the sanction or rejection of the refund claims shall be taken afresh, following due process of law, strictly based on the final outcome of the said Section 74 demand proceedings concerning the underlying Input Tax Credit. 7.5 The Respondent (Assessee) is directed to cooperate fully with the adjudication proceedings. 7.6 Appeal filed by the Department are allowed by way of remand to the adjudicating/refund sanctioning authority.
