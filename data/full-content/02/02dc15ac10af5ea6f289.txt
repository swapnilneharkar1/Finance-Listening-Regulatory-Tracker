Ishwar Chandra Barik Vs Commissioner (Appeal), GST, Central Excise & Customs and Others, set aside an appellate order dismissing a service tax appeal for alleged non-payment of mandatory pre-deposit, where the assessee asserted that the requisite amount had already been deposited. The petitioner challenged Order-in-Appeal No.89/ST/RKL-GST/2025 dated 17 September 2025, passed by the Commissioner (Appeal), GST, Central Excise & Customs, Bhubaneswar. The principal grievance was that the statutory appeal had been rejected without properly accounting for the pre-deposit stated to have been made on 30 August 2024. The Court directed the appellate authority to verify the payment and, if found correct, hear and dispose of the appeal in accordance with law, subject to compliance with other statutory requirements. The petitioner was engaged in providing works contract services involving earthwork, civil work, steel work and miscellaneous activities for M/s. TATA Steel Ltd., Joda, Odisha. For compliance with the erstwhile service tax regime under Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994, the petitioner had obtained Service Tax Registration No. AGMPB8026HSD001. According to the petitioner’s submissions, a show cause notice dated 19 October 2020 was issued by the Assistant Commissioner, CGST & Central Excise, Keonjhar Division, on the basis of information attributed to the Central Board of Direct Taxes. Subsequently, an Order-in-Original dated 1 July 2024 was passed invoking Section 73(1) of the Finance Act, 1994, confirming a service tax demand of Rs.16,54,964, along with interest stated to have been levied under Section 75 and penalties under Sections 77 and 78. Aggrieved by the adjudication order, the petitioner preferred an appeal before the Commissioner (Appeal) on 27 August 2024 under Section 85 of the Finance Act, 1994. The petitioner submitted that a mandatory pre-deposit of 7.5% of the disputed tax, amounting to Rs.1,24,125, had been paid on 30 August 2024. The statutory pre-deposit framework applicable to service tax appeals is discussed in the TaxGuru article on appeals before the Commissioner (Appeals) under service tax. The petitioner’s contention was that the appellate authority had nevertheless dismissed the appeal on the erroneous premise that the required deposit had not been made. The petitioner accordingly sought quashing of the appellate order and restoration of the appeal for consideration. The petitioner also raised a substantive dispute concerning the valuation of works contract services. It was submitted that the services provided to TATA Steel Ltd. were eligible for 60% abatement under the applicable Valuation Rules, but that benefit had not been extended while determining the service tax liability. This contention concerned the merits of the underlying assessment, independently of the procedural question regarding pre-deposit. The writ petition, however, principally sought relief against dismissal of the statutory appeal without consideration of the payment allegedly already made. The High Court did not determine the correctness of the claimed abatement, the resulting taxable value, or the ultimate service tax liability. Opposing the writ petition, the learned Senior Standing Counsel for the Revenue submitted that there was no illegality in the appellate order dated 17 September 2025. Nevertheless, the Revenue also submitted that, if the petitioner had deposited the pre-deposit amount, the concerned authority could be directed to consider the same and pass an appropriate order. After hearing both sides and examining the record, the Division Bench considered it appropriate to interfere with the dismissal order. The Court set aside the Order-in-Appeal and remitted the matter to the appellate authority for verification of the amount deposited towards the statutory pre-deposit. The Court did not itself record a final determination that the payment had been correctly made or that every condition for maintainability of the appeal stood fulfilled. The High Court directed that if the pre-deposit was verified as correct, the appeal should be heard and disposed of in accordance with law, subject to satisfaction of the other statutory requirements. The writ petition and pending interlocutory applications, if any, were accordingly disposed of. The decision is significant for its procedural consequence: an appellate rejection based on alleged non-payment of pre-deposit was set aside so that the claimed payment could be verified and the statutory appeal considered where the requirements were met. It does not dispense with mandatory pre-deposit or grant unconditional relief on the merits. For the broader procedural framework, reference may also be made to clarifications on mandatory pre-deposit for service tax appeals and the CBIC instruction concerning pre-deposit payment methods. The judgment ultimately leaves verification of payment and adjudication of the original tax dispute to the statutory appellate authority. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ORISSA HIGH COURT 1. This matter is taken up through hybrid arrangement. 2. In this writ petition, the petitioner challenges Order-in-Appeal No.89/ST/RKL-GST/2025 dated 17.09.2025 passed by the Commissioner (Appeal), GST, Central Excise & Customs, Bhubaneswar (opposite party no.1) at Annexure-4, whereby the appeal was dismissed on the ground of non-payment of pre-deposit, although the pre-deposit had in fact been made on 30.08.2024. Therefore, this writ petition is filed to set aside the impugned order. 3. Mr. Chitta Ranjan Das, learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that the petitioner is engaged in execution of ‘works contract service’ in relation to earth work, civil work, steel work and other miscellaneous works for M/s. TATA Steel Ltd., Joda, Odisha. In order to comply with the obligations under the erstwhile Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 (for short, “the Act”), the petitioner was registered before the jurisdictional authority and was assigned Service Tax Registration No. AGMPB8026HSD001. He further submitted that on the basis of Central Board of Direct Tax (CBDT), a show cause notice dated 19.10.2020 was issued by Assistant Commissioner, CGST & Central Excise, Keonjhar Division (Opposite Party No.3) and by invoking power conferred under Section 73(1) of the Act vide Order-in-Original dated 01.07.2024 besides demand of tax to the tune of Rs.16,54,964/-, interest under Section 75 has been levied and penalty under Sections 77 and 78 was imposed. 3.1. Against the Order-in-Original dated 01.07.2024, the Petitioner preferred an appeal under Section 85 of the Act before Opposite Party No.1 on 27.08.2024 and deposited pre-deposit of 7.5% of the disputed tax amounting to Rs.1,24,125/- on 30.08.2024. It is also submitted that the works contract service provided to M/s. TATA Steel Ltd. is entitled to 60% abatement under the Valuation Rules, which has not been extended to the Petitioner. Therefore, the impugned Order-in-Appeal dated 17.09.2025 was passed rejecting the appeal on the misconceived ground of non-payment of pre-deposit, though the Petitioner had deposited the pre-deposit on 30.08.2024. Hence, the impugned order deserves to be set aside and the appeal be restored to its original number. 4. Mr. Sujan Kumar Roy Choudhury, learned Senior Standing Counsel vehemently opposing the contentions of the learned counsel for the petitioner submitted that there is no illegality in the order dated 17.09.2025 passed by the authority concerned. He further submitted that if the petitioner deposited the pre-deposit amount, the authority concerned may be directed to consider the same and pass appropriate order. 5. Heard learned counsel for the parties. 6. Considered the submissions made by the learned counsel for the parties and after going through the record, this Court feels it to apposite to set aside the order dated 17.09.2025 passed by the Commissioner (Appeal), GST, Central Excise & Customs, Bhubaneswar (opposite party no.1) at Annexure-4. As sequel to such order setting aside the impugned order, the matter is remitted to the appellate authority concerned to consider and verify the amount deposited towards pre-deposit. If the same is found to be correct, the appeal subject to satisfaction of other statutory requirements, be heard and dispose of in accordance with law. 7. With the aforesaid observations, the writ petition stands disposed of. As a result of disposal of the writ petition, all pending Interlocutory Application(s), if any, shall stand disposed of.
