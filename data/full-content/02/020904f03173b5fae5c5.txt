Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Alok Kumar Yadav Vs Union Agro Rajnandgaon (GSTAT Agra) Summary: The GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), State Bench, Agra, dismissed the second appeal filed by the Uttar Pradesh State Tax Department against the order of the first appellate authority setting aside a penalty of Rs. 11,87,525 imposed under Section 129(3) of the CGST Act, 2017. The dispute arose when the Uttar Pradesh mobile squad intercepted vehicle No. RJ-11 GB 4032 on 19 November 2024 at Saiyan, Agra, while goods were being transported from Chhattisgarh to Himachal Pradesh. The supplier, M/s Shri Mahaveer Oil Carrier, Chhattisgarh, had dispatched industrial monocarboxylic fatty acids and acid oils from refining industrial fatty alcohols, classified under HSN 3823, to M/s RRD Oil and First Pvt. Ltd., Himachal Pradesh, under invoice No. UAR 2425-675 dated 14 November 2024. The intercepting officer alleged that the goods attracted GST at 18%, whereas the supplier had declared them as second-grade rice bran oil under HSN 15159040, attracting GST at 5%. Treating the classification and tax-rate difference as deliberate misdeclaration, the officer detained and seized the goods and vehicle under Section 129(1) and imposed penalty under Section 129(3). The Tribunal noted that the supplier was registered in Chhattisgarh and the recipient in Himachal Pradesh, while Uttar Pradesh was merely a transit State. It framed two questions: whether the authorities of a transit State could initiate proceedings under Section 129(3) and whether a classification dispute involving competing GST rates of 5% and 18%, requiring chemical laboratory analysis, could be determined through summary detention and penalty proceedings. Relying on the Allahabad High Court decision in M/s Maruti Enterprises Vs State of U.P. and Others (2026), the Tribunal held that transit-State authorities may intercept vehicles and verify accompanying documents under Section 68 read with Rule 138B, but the mere physical presence of goods in transit does not confer jurisdiction to levy tax or impose penalty where no taxable event or revenue accrual arises in that State. Where discrepancies are detected in an interstate consignment merely passing through a transit State, the officer may record the discrepancy and forward the information to the jurisdictional authorities of the originating or destination State for appropriate action. The Tribunal further held that Section 129 is a summary mechanism intended to ensure that goods in transit are accompanied by prescribed documents, such as tax invoices and e-way bills, and to prevent outright tax evasion. Where the goods are accompanied by valid documents and the dispute concerns tariff classification or the applicable tax rate requiring chemical analysis, the detaining officer cannot assume the role of the jurisdictional assessing authority and determine the classification through summary proceedings under Section 129(3). Such disputes fall within the domain of regular assessment or adjudication under Sections 73, 74 or 74A by the proper officer having jurisdiction over the taxpayer. Answering both questions in favour of the taxpayer and against the Department, GSTAT dismissed the second appeal filed by the Uttar Pradesh State Tax Department and upheld the first appellate order setting aside the penalty. The Tribunal thus distinguished the limited power of transit-State authorities to verify documents from the substantive jurisdiction to determine classification and impose tax-related penalties. The order was pronounced in open court on 19 November 2026 by Judicial Member Ajeet Singh and Technical Member Vivek Kumar. Cases Discussed M/s Maruti Enterprises Vs State of U.P. and Another (Allahabad High Court); Writ Tax No. 1423 of 2026; Neutral Citation: 2026:AHC:112742-DB; 14/05/2026: Relied upon as a binding precedent for the proposition that GST authorities of a transit State may intercept and verify interstate goods but cannot impose tax or penalty merely because goods pass through that State when the supply originates and terminates outside its territory and no tax incidence arises there. The transit-State authorities may record discrepancies and communicate them to the jurisdictional authorities of the originating or destination State. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF GST APPELLATE TRIBUNAL, AGRA 1. This second appeal u/s 112(1) CGST/UPGST, Act, 2017, has been preferred by state tax department Uttar Pradesh against order dated- 01.05.2025, passed by first appellate authority in appeal no. 1582/2025, whereby by allowing the appeal, order passed by proper officer and consequential demand of penalty have been set aside in favour of assesse. For convenience appellant and respondent hereinafter in the order will be addressed as department and taxpayer, respectively. 2. In brief, facts of the case may be stated thus, on 19.11.2024, Assistant commissioner, state tax, Agra, working in mobile squad 10th unit, had intercepted vehicle number RJ-11 GB 4032 at Saiyan, District-Agra. On verifying the tax invoice and e-way bill etc. it was came to fore that supplier firm M/s Shri Mahaveer Oil carrier, Chhattisgarh, dispatched industrial monocarboxylic fatty acids, acid oils from refining industrial fatty alcohols, HSN code 3823 to recipient firms M/s RRD oil and first pvt. Ltd Himachal Pradesh under tax invoice no. UAR 2425-675 dated 14.11.2024. Intercepting officer was of the opinion that goods transported should have been taxed with 18% GST rate while supplier deliberately declared goods as second grade rice brown oil HSN code 15159040 taxed with GST rate of 5%. Thus, by assuming that taxpayer intentionally declared commodity concerned in lower tax rate slab of 5% instead of higher tax slab of 18%. 3. On the above, learned intercepting/seizing officer detained and seized the goods along with vehicle u/s 129(1) of CGST, Act and after serving SCN imposed penalty of Rs. 11,87,525/- on taxpayer by invoking power conferred on him by section 129(3) of CGST Act. 4. Felling aggrieved by the order in original passed by proper officer as aforesaid, taxpayer approached to first appellate authority which allowed the appeal and set aside the order in original and consequential demand of penalty, hence present second appeal by department. 5. Learned departmental representative submits that as order under appeal is not sustainable in law appeal be allowed and impugned order be set aside. 6. Per contra, learned counsel for taxpayer submits for dismissal of appeal. 7. It is not in dispute that in this matter supplier firm runs its business as registered entity in the state of Chhattisgarh while recipient firm registered in the state of Himachal Pradesh. In this way, state of Uttar Pradesh is only a transit state for movement of goods from Chhattisgarh to Himachal Pradesh. State tax authorities of Uttar Pradesh has launched the proceedings for penalty u/s 129(3) of CGST/UPGST, Act by alleging that goods which were being transported should have been taxed at the rate of 18% while the taxpayer declared it in lower tax slab of 5%. 8. In view of above factual situation the questions before this tribunal for answer are as under- i) Whether being working in a transit state tax authorities of Uttar Pradesh are empowered to launch proceedings u/s 129(3) of CGST/UPGST, Act? ii) Whether a dispute regarding classification and rate of GST (5% vs 18%) requiring chemical laboratory analysis falls within the summary purview of section 129 detention and penalty proceedings? FINDINGS WITH REASON 9. First of all, we would like to see the jurisdictional issue covered by the binding precedent of Allahabad High court in M/s Maruti enterprises vs state of U.P and others (2026). In which goods were moving from West Bengal to Delhi via Uttar Pradesh and UP GST authorities intercepted the vehicle and imposed penalty u/s 129(3). Setting aside the penalty the Hon’ble High court laid down that although a transit state authorities have the statutory power u/s 68 read with rule 138 b b to intercept and verify documents accompanied with goods in transit but mere physical presence of goods with in such transit state does not coffer jurisdiction to levy tax or impose penalties when no taxable event or revenue accrual arises within that state. Hon’ble High court has further held that where an irregularity or discrepancy is noticed in an interstate consignment merely passing through a transit state, the transit state officer can at the best record the discrepancy on verification report/documents and forward the information to jurisdictional assessing authorities of the originating states or destination state for appropriate action under law. In this way, learned proper officer was not authorized under law to impose penalty on taxpayer in the matter in hand. 10. Apart from above, it is also a well settled preposition of law that section 129 of CGST Act is a summary mechanism meant to ensure goods in transit are accompanied by valid prescribed documents (tax invoice and e-way bill etc.) to prevent outright evasion of tax. Where like in the present matter goods are accompanied by valid documents and the dispute pertains to the rate of tax or tariff classification based on chemical analysis, the detaining officer cannot step into the shoes of the jurisdictional assessing authorities to determine classification and impose summary penalty u/s 129(3). Matter in hand clearly falls within exclusive domain of regular assessment/adjudication u/s 73/74 or section 74A by the proper officer having jurisdiction over the taxpayer. 11. Thus, in view of the above discussion of facts and legal position we answer both the questions formulated in negative and against the department. Resultantly, second appeal preferred by state tax department of Uttar Pradesh on the direction of its commissioner fails and accordingly dismissed. Order in appeal is upheld. 12. There is no pending interim application in the appeal. 13. Order is pronounced in open court on this day of 19th of November, 2026. 14. Copy of order be communicated to all concerned, as per rules.
