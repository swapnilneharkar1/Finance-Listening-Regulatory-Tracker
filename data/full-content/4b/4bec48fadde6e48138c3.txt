Trading in the top five government securities (G-Secs) accounted for 72 per cent of volumes over the past 12 months, with select maturities seeing the most trading, particularly the 10-year benchmark bond, Jain noted, emphasising that this poses challenges for the Centre and States’ borrowing programmes. “The borrowing requirements of the Centre and states have steadily increased, making even a small rise in yields significant for the government’s interest burden,” Jain noted. Flagging the implications of recent geopolitical developments on the cost and stability of government borrowing, Jain also stressed that the institutional capacity to absorb longer-tenor issuances remains “relatively limited” and there is a structural reduction in the demand for government securities. Commercial banks accounted for 35 per cent of total government securities holdings at the end of March 2026, making them the largest holders, followed by insurance companies and provident funds. While insurers and pension funds have historically been the most consistent buyers of longer-dated government securities, demand from them as well as banks for long-term sovereign debt has marginally declined, with the former shifting towards greater equity exposure. “Asset managers and other investors are also becoming more selective about duration, liquidity and mark-to-market risks. Jain said underwriting auction prices had, on a few occasions, been volatile and not in line with market-relevant levels, even when market conditions improved and were orderly. He urged primary dealers (PDs) to bid responsibly and make greater efforts to ensure the success of both underwriting and main auctions. “PDs are urged to place bids in underwriting auctions responsibly, having regard to prevailing market conditions,” he said. For context, PDs account for around 60 per cent of turnover in dated central government securities, 45 per cent in treasury bills and about 50 per cent in state government securities – in the secondary market. They have exclusive access to underwriting auctions for dated central government securities. Such auctions involve them bidding for the right to underwrite a portion of the government bond issue before the main auction – so that borrowing plans go through even when investor demand is weak. A failed auction can disrupt the market or elevate borrowing costs, and the underwriters act as a backstop for such scenarios. In return, PDs quote a commission or underwriting fees — a charge for taking on this risk — and those quotes are generally viewed by the market as an indication of prevailing conditions that may influence the government’s borrowing costs. Jain noted that there had been no devolvement of such auctions, in the recent past. PDs’ subscription in primary auctions of central government securities, as a share of notified amounts, declined from 65 per cent in FY25 to 54 per cent in FY26, before rising to 58 per cent in the first half of FY27. Their average participation in state government securities auctions also fell to 41 per cent from 45 per cent over the previous two years. Participation in treasury bill auctions remained between 73 per cent and 76 per cent. There are 21 primary dealers in the country, comprising seven standalone PDs and 14 bank-owned PDs. On secondary market liquidity, the Deputy Governor said securities with maturities of up to five years and those above 15 years accounted for only around 7 per cent each of trading in the first half of 2026-27 (FY27). State government securities accounted for just 5 per cent of secondary market turnover during the period, despite an increase in state borrowing and the introduction of the Benchmark Issuance Strategy. While reducing the frequency of benchmark issuances and increasing the volume of each auction have helped improve liquidity, trading activity remains low compared with other countries. Jain said PDs could help deepen the market by stepping up market-making activities, particularly by providing two-way quotes in off-the-run securities. Jain also called for greater retail participation in the government securities market. Since the launch of the Retail Direct Market Making Scheme in November 2021, PDs have executed trades worth Rs 8,438 crore with Retail Direct gilt account holders up to October 2026, a figure he described as modest. He urged PDs to conduct awareness campaigns to educate retail investors about government securities and develop products suited to their needs, including shorter-tenor STRIPs, which are relatively less complex. On the foreign exchange market, Jain said standalone primary dealers (SPDs) were permitted to offer all foreign exchange market-making facilities available to Category-I authorised dealers, subject to prudential guidelines. However, initial uptake had been slow, and SPDs needed to accelerate the development of the required infrastructure, risk management systems and skills to participate actively in the market.
