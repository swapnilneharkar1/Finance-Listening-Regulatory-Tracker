(GSTAT Lucknow) Summary: The GST Appellate Tribunal, State Bench Lucknow, in Manoj Traders Vs Sanjay Kumar Pathak & Ors., allowed the assessee’s second appeal against the confirmation of GST demand and penalty arising from loose handwritten order slips recovered during a survey. The appellant, a sole proprietorship dealing in kirana items, including edible oil, sugar, flour and other food grains, was surveyed by the Special Investigation Branch on 24 April 2018. The survey authorities recorded physical stock valued at Rs.24,50,345 and seized certain handwritten slips. Proceedings under Section 74 of the UPGST/CGST Act resulted in an original demand of Rs.7,90,164, comprising CGST of Rs.1,44,375, SGST of Rs.1,44,375, interest of Rs.2,12,664 and penalty of Rs.2,88,750. In the first appeal, the appellate authority rejected the demand relating to physical stock discrepancies as speculative but sustained tax of Rs.22,316.15 and an equal penalty, together with interest, by treating the entire amount of Rs.4,46,323 appearing on three loose order slips as taxable sales attracting GST at 5%. The appellant contended that the handwritten slips were temporary customer order notes prepared before delivery and not independent records of unaccounted sales. The slips included exempt commodities such as loose flour (Atta) and Maida, while the taxable transactions had already been invoiced and entered in the regular sales register. The appellant produced sales records, trading accounts, invoices and the GSTR-3B return for April 2018 to establish that the relevant transactions were either exempt or already accounted for and subjected to applicable GST. It was specifically submitted that entries aggregating Rs.1,74,560 appearing in one of the disputed slips matched the invoices issued on 23 April 2018. The appellant argued that imposing GST again on these transactions would amount to double taxation and that no suppression of turnover or wilful misstatement had occurred. The department, however, maintained that the slips were not part of the official accounting books when the survey was conducted and represented unverified, out-of-books sales justifying tax and equal penalty under Section 74. After examining the disputed slips, sales register, invoices, trading accounts and GST returns, the Tribunal found that the first slip, valued at Rs.4,525, concerned five bags of Atta, which the order treated as exempt. The second slip, valued at Rs.41,760, included Atta worth Rs.2,760 and palm oil worth Rs.39,000, requiring separate treatment of the exempt and taxable components. The entries in the third slip were reconciled with regular invoices dated 23 April 2018, including invoices for exempt goods and taxable supplies attracting GST at 5%. The Tribunal also recorded that the appellant had reported taxable turnover of Rs.93,49,520 and paid CGST and SGST of Rs.2,33,738.12 each in the April 2018 GSTR-3B return. Although the departmental representative was directed to verify the sales register uploaded on the GSTAT portal, the department expressed its inability to verify the records because the relevant back data was unavailable. No further departmental reconciliation or verification was undertaken. The Tribunal concluded that the loose slips were temporary order notes and that the corresponding sales were either exempt or properly recorded in the books and GST returns. Consequently, the surviving tax demand of Rs.22,316.15 and equal penalty of Rs.22,316.15, together with interest, were held unsustainable. The second appeal was allowed, and the first appellate order dated 29 December 2025 was set aside to the extent that it confirmed these liabilities. The appellant was exonerated from tax, interest and penalty arising from the disputed survey proceedings for April 2018, and the Tribunal directed that any amount deposited during the proceedings be refunded in accordance with law. The decision emphasises the evidentiary importance of reconciling loose documents with regular accounting records and GST returns before treating such documents as proof of suppressed taxable turnover. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF GST APPELLATE TRIBUNAL, STATE BENCH LUCKNOW 1. BRIEF FACTS OF THE CASE 1.1 The present appeal has been filed by M/s Manoj Traders (the Appellant), a registered sole proprietorship firm dealing in Kirana items, including edible oil, sugar, and exempt food grains like flour (Atta) and Maida. On 24.04.2018, the Special Investigation Branch (SIB), Division-B, Ayodhya, surveyed the Appellant’s business premises, recording physical stock worth Rs.24,50,345.00 and seizing certain loose handwritten order slips. Proceedings under Section 74 of the UPGST/CGST Act culminated in Order-in-Original No. ZD090622085655Z dated 28.06.2022, creating a total demand of Rs.7,90,164.00 (CGST Rs.1,44,375.00, SGST Rs.1,44,375.00, interest Rs.2,12,664.00, and penalty Rs.2,88,750.00). 1.2 On first appeal, the Additional Commissioner, Grade-2 (Appeal) II, Ayodhya, vide Order-in-Appeal No. AD0910220177221/23 dated 29.12.2025, dropped the demand arising from physical stock discrepancies as speculative. However, the Appellate Authority sustained a 5% tax and equal penalty on Rs.4,46,323.00 based on three loose order slips, modifying the demand to CGST Rs.11,158.07, SGST Rs.11,158.07, penalty Rs.22,316.15, plus interest. Aggrieved by the sustained demand of tax (Rs.22,316.15) and penalty (Rs.22,316.15), the Appellant has filed this Second Appeal before this bench for complete set-aside of the order. 1.3 Shri Sunil Kumar Jaiswal, Advocate appeared on behalf of the appellant (“hereinafter to be referred as the appellant”.) and Shri Sanjeev Kumar, Deputy Commissioner, appeared on behalf of the department. (“hereinafter to be referred as the Ld Respondent.”) 2. GROUNDS OF APPEAL AND CONTENTIONS OF THE APPELLANT The learned counsel for the Appellant presented the following submissions and grounds: 2.1 The First Appellate Authority erred in treating the entire amount of Rs.4,46,323.00 listed in the loose slips as taxable sales @ 5%. The loose slips explicitly contain orders for non-taxable/exempted food grain commodities, such as Flour (Atta) and Maida. Levying tax @ 5% on tax-free/exempted goods is contrary to the statutory provisions of the GST Act. 2.2 The handwritten loose slips recovered during the survey were temporary notes used to write down customer orders prior to delivery. Upon confirmation, goods were dispatched and regular tax invoices were raised. 2.3 A major portion of the orders noted on the loose slips had already been billed and recorded in the regular Sales Register for the period 01.04.2018 to 23.04.2018. Specifically, entries in Loose Slip No. 3 totalling Rs.1,74,560.00 (consisting of Invoice Nos. 12, 13, 14, 16, 18, GST-196, GST-197, GST-198, and GST-200) strictly tally with the validly issued invoices and regular accounting ledgers of the Appellant for 23.04.2018. Tax on these transactions had already been fully declared and paid in Form GSTR-3B for April 2018. 2.4 Subjecting transactions to tax again based on order slips when the corresponding tax has already been reported and paid through statutory monthly returns constitutes impermissible double taxation. 2.5 The Appellant maintains regular accounts and filed GSTR-3B for April 2018 declaring total taxable turnover and paying total tax due. There was no suppression of turnover or wilful misstatement. 3. RESPONDENT’S SUBMISSIONS 3.1 The learned Departmental Representative appearing on behalf of the Revenue submitted the present is of based on the report submitted by SIB survey, which was conducted on 24.04.2018. During survey some loose handwritten slips on the business premises and the same were not produced as part of the official accounting books at the time of inspection. 3.2 The First Appellate Authority acted within legal bounds by sustaining tax and penalty @ 5% on the unverified transaction values amounting to Rs.4,46,323.00 found recorded on those loose slips. Ld respondent further submitted and reiterated that the Proper Officer and First Appellate Authority correctly inferred that the loose slips represented out-of-books sales made to evade output tax liability under Section 74 of the UPGST Act 2017 and the sustained demand of tax and equal penalty deserves to be upheld. 4. ISSUES FOR DETERMINATION Whether the First Appellate Authority was legally justified in sustaining tax of Rs.22,316.15 and penalty of Rs.22,316.15 @ 5% on the aggregate value of Rs.4,46,323.00 appearing in the loose slips, or whether the transactions in the loose slips stand fully reconciled with exempted supplies and accounted taxable sales already reported by the Appellant? 5. Heard and perused the record upload onto the GSTAT portal. 6. FINDINGS 6.1 This bench has carefully examined the appellate records, the Order-in-Original, the First Appellate Order, the Sales Register, GSTR-3B returns, Trading Accounts, and the specific loose slips under dispute. 6.1.1 Verification of Loose Slip Items & Account Reconciliation- Slip No. 1 (Rs.4,525.00): Consists of 5 bags of Atta @ Rs.905/bag. Loose/unbranded Atta (flour) is an exempted commodity under the GST tariff schedule. The First Appellate Authority itself noted that no tax is applicable on this slip. Slip No. 2 (Rs.41,760.00): Comprises 3 bags of Atta @ Rs.920 (Rs.2,760.00) and 30 tins of Palm Oil @ Rs.1,300 (Rs.39,000.00). The Atta component (Rs.2,760.00) is exempt from GST. Slip No. 3 & Regular Invoices: Examination of Loose Slip No. 3 reveals that the individual entries written on the slip match the corresponding sales invoices generated by the Appellant on 23.04.2018. For quick reference and reconciliation, the highlighted mapping table between the loose slip entries and regular tax invoices is detailed below: Sr. No. Invoice No. / Reference Invoice Date Total Amount (Rs.) Nature / Classification 1 Invoice No. 12 23.04.2018 2,190.00 Exempted Goods 2 Invoice No. 13 23.04.2018 37,000.00 Exempted Goods 3 Invoice No. 14 23.04.2018 20,000.00 Exempted Goods 4 Invoice No. 16 23.04.2018 8,000.00 Exempted Goods 5 Invoice No. 18 23.04.2018 31,970.00 Exempted Goods 6 Invoice No. 19 23.04.2018 3,760.00 Exempted Goods 7 GST-196 23.04.2018 33,480.00 Taxable Goods @ 5% 8 GST-197 23.04.2018 15,000.00 Taxable Goods @ 5% 9 GST-198 23.04.2018 16,950.00 Taxable Goods @ 5% 10 GST-200 23.04.2018 41,760.00 Taxable Goods @ 5% 6.2 Deductions for Exempted Goods and Accounted Turnover: 6.2.1 The Ld respondent was directed to verify the data of sales register submitted by the appellant onto the portal. The Ld respondent submitted inability to verify the same because it a B2C data which has no further trail for reconciliation or verification by the department. 6.2.2 The primary records confirm that Atta, Maida, and Choker sales are wholly exempt from GST. Imposing a 5% tax on exempt goods is bad in eye of law. 6.2.3 Furthermore, the taxable sales recorded on the order slips were duly accounted for in the Appellant’s Sales Register for April 2018, and the total taxable turnover of Rs.93,49,520.00 along with tax paid (CGST Rs.2,33,738.12 + SGST Rs.2,33,738.12) was duly reported in the GSTR-3B return for April 2018. This Bench holds that on perusing the facts with the details submitted by the appellant establishes that the loose slips were temporary order notes, and all corresponding sales were either exempt from tax or properly recorded in the books of account and tax returns of dt. 23.04.2018. Therefore, the remaining demand of tax (Rs.22,316.15) and penalty (Rs.22,316.15) sustained by the First Appellate Authority is devoid of legal merit and cannot be sustained. ORDER (i) In the light of the findings above, the Second Appeal filed by the Appellant is ALLOWED. (ii) The impugned Order-in-Appeal No. AD0910220177221/23 dated 29.12.2025 passed by the First Appellate Authority is hereby SET ASIDE to the extent it confirmed tax of Rs.22,316.15, penalty of Rs.22,316.15, and interest against the Appellant. (iii) The Appellant is fully exonerated from all tax, interest, and penalty liabilities arising out of the survey dated 24.04.2018 for the disputed period April 2018. Any amount paid by the Appellant while the proceedings shall be refunded to the Appellant in accordance with the law. The order is pronounced in open court.
