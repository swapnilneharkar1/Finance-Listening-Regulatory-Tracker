Vs Assistant Commissioner of GST & Central Excise (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court allowed the writ petition filed by N.C. John & Sons Pvt. Ltd., challenging the Order-in-Original dated 8 December 2025 and consequential Form GST DRC-07 dated 9 December 2025 issued under Rule 96(10) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017, for the financial years 2020-21 and 2021-22. The Court observed that Rule 96(10) was omitted with effect from 8 October 2024 through Notification No. 20/2024-Central Tax. The question of whether such omission operated prospectively or retrospectively had already been considered by the Supreme Court in Goodluck India Limited & Anr. Vs Union of India & Ors., decided on 6 August 2026. The Supreme Court held that the omission of the provision without a saving clause applied to all pending proceedings, thereby giving retrospective effect to the omission in respect of such matters. Applying the Supreme Court’s ruling, the Madras High Court concluded that the impugned proceedings concerning the earlier financial years could not be sustained under the omitted provision. Accordingly, the Court quashed both the Order-in-Original and consequential Form GST DRC-07. The writ petition was allowed without any order as to costs. The miscellaneous petition seeking dispensation from production of the original impugned proceedings was also allowed, and the connected miscellaneous petition was closed. Cases Discussed Goodluck India Limited & Anr. Vs Union of India & Ors. (Supreme Court); 2026 INSC 821; SLP (C) No. 24550 of 2025; 06.08.2026. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF MADRAS HIGH COURT The petitioner challenges the impugned order-in-original dated 08.12.2025 along with the consequential Form GST DRC-07 dated 09.12.2025, issued by the respondent under Rule 96(10) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017. 2. The said sub-rule (10) of Rule 96 was subsequently omitted vide Notification No. 20/2024-Central Tax, effective from 08.10.2024. The question concerning the nature of this omission—whether it operates prospectively or retrospectively—came up for consideration before the Hon’ble Supreme Court in M/s. Goodluck India Limited & Anr. v. Union of India & Ors. (SLP (C) No. 24550 of 2025). By an order dated 06.08.2026, the Hon’ble Supreme Court concluded that since the sub-rule was omitted without a saving clause, the omission applies squarely to all pending proceedings to give quietus to the litigation. Consequently, the effect of the omission is retrospective in respect of all pending matters. 3. In the present case, the impugned order-in-original dated 08.12.2025 was issued by invoking powers under Rule 96(10) for the Financial Years 2020 to 2021 and 2021–2022. In view of the law laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court, the omission of Rule 96(10) applies retrospectively to these pending proceedings. In such a view of the matter, the impugned order-in-original dated 08.12.2025 along with the consequential Form GST DRC-07, dated 09.12.2025, cannot be sustained and are hereby quashed in terms of the landmark ruling passed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court. 4. Accordingly, the Writ Petition is allowed. There shall be no order as to costs. W.M.P.No.5475 of 2026 filed to dispense with the production of original impugned proceedings, is allowed. Consequently, the connected Miscellaneous Petition is closed.
