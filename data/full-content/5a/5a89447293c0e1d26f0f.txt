Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); PCIT-1 Vs Mangalam Drugs & Organics Ltd (Bombay High Court) Statement recorded during survey under Section 133A cannot, by itself, sustain additions for bogus purchases or excess depreciation in the absence of independent corroborative evidence. Additions founded solely on the director’s survey statement are unsustainable. Case Summary: The Revenue challenged the ITAT order dated 16 May 2019 in ITA No. 5276/Mum/2018 for AY 2009-10, concerning deletion of additions towards alleged bogus purchases of Rs.8.40 crore and excess depreciation claimed on alleged bogus capital expenditure. Facts: The assessee filed its return declaring loss of Rs.1,27,64,552 under the regular provisions and income of Rs.16,97,026 under Section 115JB. Reassessment was initiated under Section 148 based on information from the Maharashtra Sales Tax Department regarding alleged bogus purchases from two parties aggregating Rs.8.40 crore. Survey under Section 133A was conducted on 27 August 2013. Statements of the company’s director and VP Finance were allegedly recorded admitting bogus bills. The AO treated purchases of Rs.8.40 crore as unexplained expenditure under Section 69. The AO also made an addition towards excess depreciation based on alleged accommodation bills for capital expenditure. AO/CIT(A) Findings: The AO treated the purchases as non-genuine and added Rs.8.40 crore under Section 69 based on survey findings and statements. The CIT(A) upheld reopening but deleted the addition on merits, holding that the principal evidence relied upon was the statement of the director recorded during survey under Section 133A. ITAT Findings: The ITAT upheld the deletion of the bogus purchase addition, relying on the decision in S. Khader Khan Son, which held that statements recorded during survey proceedings do not have the evidentiary value of sworn statements and cannot independently justify additions. The ITAT also deleted the excess depreciation addition. High Court Findings: The Bombay High Court held that the bogus purchase issue was fact-driven and covered by Commissioner of Income-tax v. S. Khader Khan Son, affirmed by the Supreme Court. The Court observed that no reliance could be placed on the director’s statement recorded under Section 133A. Further, the Income Tax Department had not recorded statements on oath from any other relevant party to corroborate the allegations. Accordingly, Questions I and II raised no substantial question of law. Questions III and IV regarding retraction and Section 114 of the Evidence Act also did not give rise to substantial questions of law, in the absence of reliable independent evidence. The addition relating to excess depreciation was likewise based on the same director’s statement and was correctly deleted by the ITAT. Outcome: The Revenue’s appeal was dismissed. Deletion of the addition of Rs.8.40 crore towards alleged bogus purchases and the addition relating to excess depreciation was upheld. None of the five proposed questions gave rise to a substantial question of law. No order as to costs. Key Legal Principle: A statement recorded during survey under Section 133A, without independent corroborative material or relevant sworn statements, cannot by itself be the foundation for additions towards bogus purchases or excess depreciation. The burden of sustaining an addition must rest on reliable supporting evidence and not merely on an admission recorded during survey. Cases Discussed 1. Commissioner of Income-tax Vs S. Khader Khan Son (Madras High Court); (2008) 300 ITR 157 (Madras). Relevance: The Madras High Court decision concerning the evidentiary value of statements recorded under Section 133A formed the principal basis for deleting the addition. The Bombay High Court held that the present controversy was squarely covered by this precedent. 2. Commissioner of Income-tax, Salem Vs S. Khader Khan Son (Supreme Court); (2012) 25 taxmann.com 413 (SC). Relevance: The Supreme Court approved the Madras High Court decision. The Bombay High Court relied on that approval while rejecting the Revenue’s proposed substantial questions of law. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF BOMBAY HIGH COURT 1. The above Appeal has been filed by the Revenue challenging the order dated 16th May 2019 passed by the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) in Appeal No.5276/Mum/2018. The Appeal before the ITAT was filed by the Revenue challenging the order passed by the CIT (Appeals). The Assessment Year in question is A.Y.2009-10. According to the Revenue, the impugned order of the ITAT gives rise to the following 5 questions of law :- (I) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Hon’ble ITAT erred in dismissing the appeal of the Department on the basis of the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in case of [(2012) 25 taxmann.com 413 (SC)] alone whereas the same was not applicable at all in the facts of the case? (II) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Hon’ble ITAT erred in dismissing the appeal of the Department by ignoring the statements of other officers/staff of the assessee-company as well as the statement of two hawala operators by the Sales Tax authorities? (III) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, was there a valid retraction of the statement of the Director of the assessee-company and whether the Hon’ble ITAT erred in dismissing the appeal of the Department when the onus for valid retraction was not discharged by the assessee? (IV) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Hon’ble ITAT erred in dismissing the appeal of the Department violating the principle laid down in section 114 of the India Evidence Act, 1872? (V) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, the Hon’ble ITAT was right in failing to adjudicate on the deletion of addition in respect of excess claim of depreciation made by the assessee? 2. The brief facts in the present case are that the Assessee filed his Return of Income for A.Y.2009-10 on 25th September 2009 declaring an income of Rs. – 1,27,64,552/- under the regular provisions of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (the Act) and Rs.16,97,026/- under the special provisions, namely, under Section 115JB of the Act. Thereafter, the case of the Assessee was reopened by issuing a Notice under Section 148 of the Act on 15th March 2014 after recording reasons that income of the Assessee had escaped assessment due to the fact that the Assessee had made bogus purchases from two parties to the tune of Rs.8.40 Crores. The said information was received by the Assessing Officer from the Sales Tax Department, Government of Maharashtra. Accordingly, a survey under Section 133A of the Act was conducted on the Assessee on 27th August 2013. This survey was carried out on the Assessee in order to verify whether the Assessee is engaged in procuring bogus purchase bills. The Investigation Wing of the Mumbai unit observed that the Assessee had obtained bogus bills from hawala parties to inflate its turnover with a primary motive of enhancing its bank credit limits. It also found that the Assessee had taken accommodation entries of bogus capital expenditure from hawala dealers in order to claim higher depreciation. According to the Revenue, during the course of the survey, a statement was recorded of Mr. Govardhan M. Dhoot, a Director of the Assessee Company and also that of Mr. Ajay Samant, the V. P. Finance who admitted that the Assessee had obtained bogus bills to the tune of Rs.8.40 Crores. During the course of the assessment proceedings, the Assessee was called upon to prove genuineness of its purchases by filing bills, vouchers and various other evidences, which according to the Assessee, were duly filed. However, the Assessing Officer treated the purchases as non-genuine on the basis of evidence gathered during the course of the survey proceedings and the statements recorded during the survey. He, therefore, added the entire purchases of Rs.8.40 Crores as unexplained expenditure under Section 69 of the Act. 3. Being aggrieved by the order of the Assessing Officer, the Assessee preferred an Appeal to the CIT (A). Though the CIT (A) upheld the action of reopening the assessment, the addition made was deleted on merits. The main ground of deletion of Rs.8.40 Crores was on the basis that the only evidence was the so-called statement of Mr.Govardhan M. Dhoot, Director of the Company recorded under Section 133A of the Act. Relying upon a decision of the Madras High Court in the case of Commissioner of Income-tax V/S S. Khader Khan Son [(2008) 300 ITR 157 (Madras)] and which was affirmed by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Commissioner of Income-tax, Salem V/S S. Khader Khan Son [(2012) 25 taxmann.com 413 (SC)], the CIT (A) deleted the addition. Being aggrieved, the Revenue preferred an Appeal before the ITAT which passed the impugned order confirming the order passed by the CIT (A) and dismissing the Appeal of the Revenue. 4. Having gone through the order passed by the CIT (A) as well as the order passed by the ITAT, we find that the entire issue is not only fact driven, but in the facts of the present case, is squarely covered by the decision of the Hon’ble Madras High Court in the case of Commissioner of Income-tax V/S S. Khader Khan Son [(2008) 300 ITR 157 (Madras)] and which was approved by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in [(2012) 25 taxmann.com 413 (SC)]. Once these are the facts before us, we are clearly of the view that Questions (I) and (II) do not arise for our consideration and hence are not entertained. 5. As far as Question (III) and (IV) are concerned, considering that no reliance could be placed on the statement made by the Director of the Assessee Company and coupled with the facts that there were no statements recorded on oath by the Income Tax Department of any other party having a bearing to the case at hand, we find that Questions (III) and (IV) also do not give rise to any substantial question of law and hence not entertained. 6. As far as Question (V) is concerned, here too, the addition of depreciation was done by the Assessing Officer on the basis of the statement recorded of the Director of the Assessee Company. Since that statement could never be relied upon, the ITAT, in our view, correctly deleted the aforesaid addition. Hence, even Question (V) does not give rise to any substantial question of law and hence not entertained. 7. The Appeal is accordingly dismissed. However, there shall be no order as to costs. 8.
