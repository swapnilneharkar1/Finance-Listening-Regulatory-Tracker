Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited Vs DCIT (ITAT Mumbai) Summary: The Mumbai Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal adjudicated cross-appeals filed by Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited and the Revenue for Assessment Year 2017-18 involving seven issues relating to business expenditure, capital gains exemption, transfer pricing, disallowance under Section 14A, foreign exchange losses and Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT). The Tribunal upheld the disallowance of ₹3,64,272 representing interest paid under Section 201(1A) for delayed remittance of TDS. Relying on the jurisdictional Bombay High Court judgment in Ferro Alloys Corporation Ltd. v. CIT, it held that the compensatory character of interest does not automatically establish its deductibility as business expenditure under Section 37(1). Regarding capital gains of ₹8,91,74,349 arising from land acquired for the Bangalore Metro Rail Project, the Tribunal held that failure to claim exemption in the original return cannot independently determine the taxability of compensation. It restored the matter to the CIT(A) for examination under Section 96 of the RFCTLARR Act, 2013 and CBDT Circular No. 36/2016 dated 25 October 2016. The Tribunal also restored the transfer pricing adjustment of ₹99,45,52,070 concerning electricity supplied by a captive Gas Turbine Unit. It directed fresh examination of the applicability of transfer pricing provisions despite the non-claim of deduction under Section 80-IA and determination of the correct arm’s length price, wherever applicable. The disallowance under Section 14A read with Rule 8D was remanded for reconsideration of the expenditure actually incurred, the Assessing Officer’s recorded dissatisfaction and the relevance of available interest-free funds. The Tribunal observed that the presumption concerning investment from interest-free funds would not necessarily resolve a disallowance involving other expenditure. The Tribunal upheld the deduction of establishment and supervision expenses under Section 37(1), notwithstanding their capitalisation in the books of account, following its earlier decisions in the assessee’s own case. The issue concerning foreign exchange losses was restored to the CIT(A) for factual verification. The Tribunal further upheld deletion of the MAT addition of ₹4,42,45,505, following the Special Bench decision in ACIT v. Vireet Investment (P.) Ltd., holding that Section 14A read with Rule 8D cannot be directly imported into the computation under clause (f) of Explanation 1 to Section 115JB(2). Consequently, both appeals were partly allowed for statistical purposes. The Tribunal further clarified that the non-claim of deduction under Section 80-IA does not, by itself, settle the applicability of transfer pricing provisions to inter-unit transactions. In the present case, the assessee contended that its captive Gas Turbine Unit had incurred substantial accumulated losses and that no deduction under Section 80-IA had been claimed. It also relied upon an internal Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP) of ₹7.99 per unit against the external CUP of ₹3.79 per unit adopted by the TPO. The Tribunal observed that the applicability of transfer pricing provisions and the determination of the correct arm’s length price were two distinct questions requiring independent examination. Accordingly, the CIT(A) was directed to reconsider both aspects and determine the consequential tax impact on expenditure claimed by the power-consuming units. The assessee has relied upon various decisions to contend that interest under section 201(1A) is compensatory and not penal in nature, not attracting Explanation to section 37 of the Act. The question for consideration is whether such interest is allowable as deduction under the main provision of section 37(1) of the Act. The learned CIT(A) has followed the decision of the Bangalore Bench of the Tribunal in Expat Engineering India Ltd.(supra) The relevant part of the decision of the Tribunal is reproduced as under: “9. We have heard rival submissions and perused the material on record. The Bangalore Bench of the Tribunal in assessee’s own case had categorically held after relying various judicial pronouncements that interest u/s 201(1A) of the I.T.Act cannot be claimed as a deduction. The relevant finding of the Bangalore Bench of the Tribunal in assessee’s own case, reads as follows:- “11. We have considered the rival submissions and perused the material on record. The coordinate Bench of this Tribunal in Velankani Information Systems Ltd. (supra) dealt with this issue and held that interest on delayed payment of TDS cannot be allowed as deduction. The relevant observations of the Tribunal are as follows:- “21. As far as delay in remittance of tax deducted at source u/s. 201(14) of the Act is concerned, we find that the Hon’ble Madras High Court has taken a view that interest paid u/s. 201(1A) is also in the nature of tax and notwithstanding the fact that it is not the tax liability of the assessee, the same cannot be allowed as a deduction. The following were the relevant observations of the Hon’ble Madras High Court:- “14. As already noticed the payment of interest takes colour from the nature of the levy with reference to which such interest is paid and the tax required to be but not paid in time, which rendered the assessee liable for payment of interest was in the nature of a direct tax and similar to the income-tax payable under the Income-tax Act. The interest paid under Section 201(1A) of the Act, therefore, would not assume the character of business expenditure and cannot be regarded as a compensatory payment as contended by learned counsel for the assessee. 15. Counsel for the assessee in support of his submission that the interest paid by the assessee was merely compensatory in character besides relying on the case of Makalakshmi Sugar MillsCo. also relied on the decision of the apex court in the cases of Prakash Cotton Mills Pvt Ltd. v. CIT [1993] 201 ITR 684; Malwa Vanaspati and Chemical Co. v. CIT [1997] 225 ITR 383 and CIT v. Ahmedabad Cotton Manufacturing Co. Ltd. [1994] 205 ITR 163. In all these cases, the court was concerned with an indirect tax payable by the assessee in the course of its business and admissible as business expenditure. Further liability for interest which had been incurred by the assessee therein was regarded as compensatory in nature and allowable as business expenditure. 16. The ratio of those cases is not applicable here. Income-tax is not allowable as business expenditure. The amount deducted as tax is not an item of expenditure. The amount not deducted and remitted has the character of tax and has to be remitted to the State and cannot be utilised by the assessee for its own business. The Supreme Court in the case of Bharat Commerce and Industries [1998] 230 ITR 733, rejected the argument advanced by the assessee that retention of money payable to the State as tax or income-tax would augment the capital of the assessee and the expenditure incurred, namely, interest paid for the period of such retention would assume character of business expenditure. The court held that an assessee could not possibly claim that it was borrowing from the State, the amounts payable by it as income-tax, and utilising the same as capital in its business, to contend that the interest paid for the period of delay in payment of tax amounted to a business expenditure”. (emphasis supplied) 22. The decision cited by the ld. counsel for the assessee of Kolkata Bench of the Tribunal on the issue is contrary to the decision of the Hon’ble Madras High Court. Though the decision of the Tribunal is later in point of time, judicial discipline demands that the decision of the Hon’ble Madras High Court is to be followed. It is also worthwhile to mention that the Kolkata Bench of Tribunal in the case of assessee, did not consider or did not have an occasion to consider the decision of the Hon’ble Madras High Court in the case of Chennai Properties and Investment Ltd. (supra). In these circumstances, we follow the decision of the Hon’ble Madras High Court & uphold the order of the CIT(A) insofar as it relates to disallowance of interest on delayed remittance of tax deducted at source u/s. 201(1A) of the Act.” 12 Following the above decision of the Tribunal, we hold that interest on delayed payment of TDS is not an allowable deduction and dismiss the ground raised by the assessee. The Id. AR submitted a detailed written submission on various case laws which we will discuss in the ensuing paras 13. At the outset it has to be mentioned that the question whether interest paid u/s.201(14) of the Act, is an allowable business expenditure has to be examined within the parameters of Sec. 37(1) of the Act and not on the basis of the prohibition contained in Sec.40a(ii) of the Act. Sec.37(1) of the Act provides that expenditure which is not capital or personal in nature laid out or expended wholly and exclusively for the purpose of business shall be allowed in computing the income chargeable under the head “profits and gains of business or profession”. Sec.40(ii) provides that any sum paid on account of any rate or tax levied on the profits and gains of any business or profession or assessed at a proportion of, or otherwise on the basis of any such profits or gains, shall not be deducted in computing the income chargeable under the head “profits and gains of business or profession”. Both these sections operate in different fields, the former is an enabling provision while the latter is a disabling provision. 14. The question directly arose for consideration before the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Bharat Commerce and Industries Ltd. v. CIT [1998] 230 ITR 733 and the question that was considered by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in that case was whether payments required to be made by way of income-tax ‘under the Income- tax Act are not deductible as expenditure and the further amounts which a person may be required to pay by a reason of failure to comply with the provisions requiring the payments of the tax are also amounts which cannot be regarded as deductible expenditure under Section 37 of the Act. The exact question considered by the Hon’ble Court was:- “Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case the claim for deduction of interest levied under Section 139 to the extent of Rs. 11,470/- and interest levied under Section 215 to the extent of Rs. 1,04,339/- was rightly rejected as not allowable under Section 37 of the Income-Tax Act, 1961 for the assessment year 1972-73?” 15. The Hon’ble Court held as follows: “It cannot be said, in the present case, that the payment of interest is in any way an expense incurred wholly or exclusively for the purpose of assessee’s business. Nor is it a payment made for the purpose of preserving and protecting the Apart from section 37, the assessee has also present into service Section 36(1) (iii) which permits deduction in respect of the amount of interest paid in respect of capital borrowed for the purposes of the assessee’s business or profession. For the reasons set out earlier, the claim for deduction under section 36(1)(iii) is also misconceived just as the assessee’s claim under section 37 is misconceived. In the premises, the question raised has to be answered in favour of the revenue and against the assessee. The appeals are, therefore, dismissed with costs.” 16. Therefore it is clear that the basis why tax or interest is not allowed as deduction u/s.37(1) of the Act is on the reasoning that it cannot be regarded as an expense incurred wholly or exclusively for the purpose of Assessee’s business. Therefore the allowability of interest on taxes paid should not be looked out from the definition of tax as given in Sec.2(43) of the Act. The submissions made by the learned counsel for the Assessee are based on a misconception that the definition of interest u/s.2(43) of the Act is relevant and that the disallowance in question has to be judged in the parameters of Sec. 40(a)(iib) of the Act. We shall now deal with the various submissions made by the learned counsel for the Assessee. 17. In the case of Harshad Shantilal Mehta vs Custodian, (1998) 231 ITR 871 (SC) the Supreme Court was concerned with the interpretation of Sec. 11 of The Special Court (Trail of Offenders Relating Transactions in Securities) Act, 1992, (hereinafter referred to as the Special Act) which reads thus:- “11. Discharge of liabilities – (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Code and any other law for the time being in force, the Special Court may make such order as it may deem fit directing the Custodian for the disposal of the property under attachment. (2) The following liabilities shall be paid or discharged in full, as far as may be, in the order as under:- (a) all revenues, taxes, cesses and rates due from the persons notified by the Custodian under sub-section (2) of Sec.3 to the Central Government or any State Government or any local authority (b) all amounts due from the person so notified by the Custodian to any bank of financial institution or mutual fund; and (c) any other liability as may be specified by the Special Court from time to time.” 18. The Hon’ble Supreme Court framed the following questions for consideration:- (1) What is meant by revenues, taxes, cesses and rates due? Does the word “due” refer merely to the liability to pay such taxes etc., or does it refer to a liability which has crystalised into a legally ascertained sum immediately payable? (2) Do the taxes (in clause (a) of Section 11(2) refer only to taxes (2) relating to a specific period or to all taxes due from the notified person? (3) At what point or time should the taxes have become due? (4) Does the Special Court have any discretion relating to the extent of payments to be made under Section 11(2)(a) from out of the attached funds/property? (5) Whether taxes include penalty or interest? 19. While answering Question No.5, the Court held:- “One other connected question remains: whether “taxes” under Section 11(2)(a) would include interest or penalty as well? We are concerned in the present case with penalty and interest under the Income Tax Act. Tax, penalty and interest are different concepts under the Income Tax Act. The definition of “tax” under Section 2(43) does not include penalty or interest. Similarly, under Section 157, it is provided that when any tax, interest, penalty, fine or any other sum is payable in consequence of any order passed under this Act, the Assessing Officer shall serve upon the assessee a notice of demand as prescribed. Provisions for imposition of penalty and interest are distinct from the provisions for imposition of tax. Learned Special Court judge, after examining various authorities in paragraphs 61 to 70 of his judgment, has come to the conclusion that neither penalty nor interest can be considered as tax under Section 11(2)(a). We agree with the reasoning and conclusion drawn by the Special Court in this connection.” 20. As can be seen from the issue involved in the aforesaid case, it was a case where the Hon’ble Court had to decide whether interest liability is also liability within the meaning of Sec.11(2)(a) of the Special Act. The aforesaid decision rendered in a different context cannot be extended to the provisions of Sec.37(1) of the Act and hence the aforesaid decision is not of any relevance to the issue in this appeal. In assessee’s case the issue under consideration is the allowability of interest u/s.201(1A) as deduction u/s.37(1) of the Act and therefore the decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court can be applied. 21. It also referred to the orders passed in its own case for earlier years and prayed that the disallowance be restricted to ₹ 1,90,09,495/- as voluntarily offered by it. 11.5The learned CIT(A) considered the assessment order and the considered in the assessee’s own case for earlier assessment years, where the jurisdictional High Court’s decision in HDFC Bank Ltd (supra) had also been taken into consideration. Following those decisions, and taking note of the assessee’s computation based on apportionment of expenditure between exempt and non-exempt income, the learned CIT(A) held that the disallowance made by the AO was not justified beyond the amount of ₹1,90,09,495/- offered by the assessee. The ground was accordingly allowed to that extent. 11.6 We have considered the rival submissions and carefully perused the material available on record. The Revenue has challenged the relief granted by the learned CIT(A) in respect of the disallowance made under section 14A read with Rule 8D. The assessee had computed an amount of ₹1,90,09,495/- as indirect expenditure attributable to exempt income on the basis of apportionment between exempt and non-exempt income. The assessee had accordingly requested the AO to restrict the disallowance to that amount. The AO, however, was not satisfied with the assessee’s computation. He invoked Rule 8D and made the impugned disallowance under Rule 8D(2)(ii). Thus, the disallowance made by the AO was towards the expenditure computed under Rule 8D(2)(ii). No separate disallowance was made by the AO in respect of direct expenditure, under Rule 8D(2)(i). 11.7 The learned CIT(A), while allowing relief, proceeded mainly on the basis that the assessee had sufficient interest-free funds to cover the investments and relied upon the decision of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in HDFC Bank Ltd. lays down the principle that where sufficient interest-free funds are available, a presumption arises that the investments have been made out of such funds. 11.8 In the present case, however, the actual disallowance made by the AO was under Rule 8D(2)(ii), and the record does not show any separate disallowance of direct interest expenditure under Rule 8D(2)(i). Therefore, the question whether the assessee had sufficient interest-free funds to cover the investments, was not relevant to a disallowance of expenditure under Rule 8D(2)(ii), if interest is not part of expenditure considered for disallowance. The learned CIT(A)’s reliance on HDFC Bank Ltd. (supra) is, therefore, academic in the facts of the present case to the extent the impugned addition is considered with reference to the assessee’s voluntary computation of ₹ 1,90,09,495/- and the disallowance actually made by the AO. The material question is whether the AO was justified in rejecting the assessee’s computation and invoking Rule 8D, and, if so, whether the disallowance made under the particular limb of Rule 8D invoked by the AO is sustainable. Since the AO has recorded dissatisfaction with the assessee’s computation and thereafter proceeded to determine the disallowance under Rule 8D, the issue is required to be examined with reference to the precise computation made by the AO and the nature of expenditure to which Rule 8D(2)(ii) has been applied. 11.9 We, therefore, restore this issue to the file of the ld CIT(A) for fresh examination. The ld CIT(A) shall examine the assessee’s computation of ₹ was recorded by the AO, the expenditure actually considered while applying Rule 8D(2)(ii) and whether the voluntary disallowance made by the assessee include proportionate interest expenditure. The ld CIT(A) shall also examine the availability of interest-free funds and the applicability of the ratio laid down by the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in HDFC Bank Ltd.(supra) to the extent relevant to the disallowance of interest expenditure, if so covered under disallowance made under Rule 8D(2)(ii). The disallowance shall thereafter be recomputed strictly in accordance with section 14A and Rule 8D, after providing adequate opportunity of being heard to the assessee. The ground raised by the Revenue is accordingly allowed for statistical purposes 12. The ground number 3 of the appeal of the Revenue relates to disallowance of establishment expenses charged to capital work- in-progress in books of accounts, but claimed as revenue expenditure in return of the income. 12.1 During the course of assessment proceedings, the Assessing Officer, by notice under section 142(1) dated 01.03.2021, called upon the assessee to furnish details of the Establishment expenses debited to the Profit & Loss Account for A.Y. 2017-18 and to clarify whether the said amount had been capitalized to Capital Work-in-Progress (“CWIP”). In response, by letter dated 19.03.2021, the assessee stated that a sum of Rs. 79,72,61,476/- representing salary/establishment charges incurred on monitoring and supervision of the execution of various projects, claimed as a revenue deduction in the computation of total income on the ground that the expenditure was routine in nature, incurred in the day-to-day functioning and expansion of the assessee’s existing business rather than in setting up a new line of business, and was accordingly allowable under section 37(1) of the Act. In support, the assessee relied on CIT v. Alembic Glass Industries Ltd. (103 ITR 715), Shivkami Mills v. CIT (120 ITR 211), and CIT v. Shah Theatres (169 ITR 499). 12.2 The Assessing Officer did not accept this submission. Referring to Note 4, Page 137 of the Annual Report for A.Y. 2017-18, the AO observed that the Establishment charges of Rs. 79.73 crores had been added as expenditure during the year to the opening balance of CWIP under the sub-head “Construction period expenses pending apportionment (net of recovery),” and that the assessee’s own books therefore characterized the expenditure as capital in nature. Relying on the principle that the treatment given by an assessee to an item in its own books, while not conclusive, is indicative of its true character, and on the Supreme Court’s decision in CIT v. Sri Mangayarkarasi Mills (P.) Ltd. (315 ITR 114), the AO held that the claim under section 37(1) could not be sustained once the expenditure was admittedly capital in the assessee’s own audited accounts, since section 37(1) expressly excludes expenditure of a capital nature. The AO further noted that the identical issue had been decided against the assessee for A.Y. 2003-04 to A.Y. 2013-14, with the disallowances for A.Y. 2003-04 to A.Y. 2009-10 having already been confirmed by the CIT(A), and that the assessee’s made in the earlier years. On this basis, a show-cause notice dated 26.04.2021 was issued proposing to treat the sum of Rs. 79,72,61,476/- as capital expenditure and disallow the corresponding deduction. 12.3 In reply dated 04.05.2021, the assessee contended, without conceding the capital/revenue characterization, that if the amount was treated as capitalized, the corresponding sum ought to be reduced from the block of assets so as to make it eligible for depreciation. By further submission dated 12.10.2021 made against the draft assessment order, the assessee pointed out that the Tribunal, for A.Y. 2003-04 to A.Y. 2007-08, had allowed the identical expenditure as revenue in nature, and that the CIT(A) had consistently allowed the same for A.Y. 2012-13 to A.Y. 2016-17. Without prejudice to its primary contention that the expenditure was revenue in nature, the assessee submitted that Rs. 61,56,41,319/- (being the amount capitalized in the books during the year) had been backed out of the block of assets, and claimed depreciation thereon — computed at the normal rate of 15% plus additional depreciation of 20% on the component used for more than 180 days (Rs. 14,12,28,832/-, yielding Rs. 2,11,84,325/- and Rs. 2,82,45,766/- respectively), and at half the normal rate (7.5% and 10%, in terms of the second proviso to section 32(1)(ii)) on the component used for less than 180 days (Rs. 47,44,12,487/-, yielding Rs. 3,55,80,937/- and Rs. 4,74,41,249/- respectively), aggregating total depreciation of Rs. 13,24,52,276/-. On this computation, the assessee submitted restricted to Rs. 79,72,61,476/- minus Rs. 13,24,52,276/-, i.e., approximately Rs. 66.48 crore. 12.4 The Assessing Officer, having considered this submission, declined to grant the depreciation claim and confirmed the disallowance in full. As regards the favourable orders of the Tribunal and the CIT(A) for the earlier years, the AO held that those orders had not attained finality, the Department having preferred appeals before higher forums on the issue, and that no relief could be granted to the assessee on that basis. As regards the alternative claim for depreciation, the AO held that since the capitalized amount formed part of CWIP which was admittedly incomplete and not put to use during the year, the asset did not qualify for depreciation, and that the mere making of book entries did not by itself entitle the assessee to the claim. Accordingly, the entire sum of Rs. 79,72,61,476/- was treated as capital expenditure and disallowed while computing the assessee’s income from business. 12.5 The relevant finding of the learned CIT(A) is reproduced as under: 8.3 Decision: I have considered the A.O.’s order as well as the appellant’s submissions. That the Revenue’s appeals against the earlier orders remain pending does not, by itself, deprive those orders of their binding character for the present purpose; judicial discipline requires us to follow a decision of a Co-ordinate Bench in the assessee’s own case unless and until it is set aside. The ground number 4 of the appeal of the Revenue relates to deletion of the addition on account of foreign exchange (forex) losses in closing stock. 13.1 During the course of assessment, the Assessing Officer proposed to include a proportionate share of Exchange Rate Variation (“ERV”) cost in the valuation of the assessee’s closing stock, on the ground that an identical addition had been sustained in the assessee’s own case for Assessment Years 2015- 16 and 2016-17. Reliance was placed on the order of this Tribunal in Reliance Industrial Infrastructure Ltd. v. Addl. CIT, ITA Nos. 69 & 70/Mum/2009, dated 05.04.2013, holding that Section 14A cannot be imported into clause (f) of Explanation 1 to Section 115JB, and on the consistent view taken by the learned CIT(A) in the assessee’s own case for Assessment Years 2008-09 to 2016-17. Vireet Investment (P.) Ltd. [2017] 165 ITD 27/82 taxmann.com 415, which holds that the computation under clause (f) of Explanation 1 to Section 115JB(2) is to be made without recourse to Section 14A read with Rule 8D. Following this binding precedent, and consistently with the view taken in the assessee’s own case for Assessment Year 2016-17, the learned CIT(A) deleted the addition of Rs. 4,42,45,505/- and allowed the ground. 14.4 We have heard rival submissions and perused the record. The learned CIT(A) has deleted the addition by following the binding decision of the Special Bench in Vireet Investment (P.) Ltd. (supra), which holds that Section 14A cannot be read into clause (f) of Explanation 1 to Section 115JB. We find no infirmity in this view. Ground No. 5 of the Revenue’s appeal is accordingly dismissed. 15. In the result, the appeals of the assessee and Revenue both are partly allowed for statistical purposes.
