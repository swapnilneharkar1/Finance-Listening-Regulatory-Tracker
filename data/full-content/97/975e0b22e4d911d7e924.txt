Articles 4 min read Mizuho extends global banking push to India with Avendus deal Gaurav Deepak's Avendus Capital has completed a deal with Mizuho Financial Group to ensure operational independence. The acquisition allows Avendus to leverage Mizuho's extensive network and international expertise in investment banking. Deepak engaged in lengthy discussions before finalizing details of the agreement, which included preserving Avendus's brand identity. As the firm continues to grow, it plans to pursue an eventual stock-market listing. Advt Also Read:The next banking war isn't bank vs bank as many Indians' new money manager is a robotAvendus, which calls itself India’s largest investment bank by revenue, had previously avoided pitching for some big overseas acquisitions because it lacked those global relationships. India is also a notoriously low-fee market, where league-table rankings don’t necessarily translate into investment-banking revenue.The takeover talks began in February 2024, with some conversations between Deepak and senior Mizuho executives lasting as long as four hours, he said in an interview at his Mumbai office. About 200 people from the two companies were involved in the transaction, a process Deepak likened to an Indian wedding.The central question was what Mizuho’s control would mean for Avendus. “Who are we to each other?” Deepak recalled asking repeatedly. The answer, he said, was eventually enshrined in the agreement: Avendus would retain operational autonomy even as Mizuho became its controlling shareholder.Other OptionsAvendus could have pursued a stock-market listing or brought in another financial investor, Deepak said, while Mizuho had other options for expanding in India. Regulatory reviews pushed the deal closing beyond the original July 2026 target.Japanese companies are increasingly looking to diversify investments and supply chains, particularly in advanced technology, critical materials and manufacturing. That’s creating more scope for India-related transactions, Yoshiro Hamamoto, president of Mizuho Securities Co., the group’s investment-banking arm, said in an interview earlier this week. Mizuho plans to preserve Avendus’s brand and operating style while integrating the businesses gradually over time, he said. Advt Many Japanese companies still have far less exposure to India than to China or the US, Hamamoto said. Since the Avendus deal was announced, India has come up in “every single talk with clients,” as companies seek investments, partners and ways to expand in the country.“We don’t have any strong franchise in India,” Hamamoto said, adding the firm has relationships with about 75% of Japanese companies.Global PushThe Tokyo-based lender has been building an international advisory network through acquisitions of US investment bank Greenhill & Co., middle-market adviser Capstone Partners and London-based energy specialist Augusta & Co. The firm’s goal is to become one of the world’s top investment banks by 2030, Mizuho has said. Advt Integrating those businesses now takes priority over another round of acquisitions, Hamamoto said. Avendus is intended to plug India into that network, giving Indian companies access to Mizuho bankers overseas while helping global clients navigate a market where Avendus has spent decades building relationships.Competition will be fierce, with Wall Street titans including Goldman Sachs Group Inc., JPMorgan Chase & Co. and Morgan Stanley already pursuing many of India’s corporate mandates. Mizuho fended off bids from Japanese securities firm Nomura Holdings Inc. and interest from US private equity firm Carlyle Group Inc. before signing the agreement for Avendus. Advt Three founders each held a 5.5% stake in the company when the deal was announced, according to filings reviewed by the Bloomberg Billionaires Index. The new ownership structure is designed to preserve Avendus.Deepak, 52, and fellow co-founder Kaushal Aggarwal aren’t selling their shares and have committed to remain for another 15 years, with their equity subject to long-term restrictions. A third co-founder, Ranu Vohra, is exiting.The partners plan to establish common performance targets across businesses, sectors and products, Hamamoto said, though he expects the firms to preserve distinct operating styles initially, with deeper integration taking place gradually over five to 10 years.Deepak said Avendus still plans an eventual listing in India, although he gave no timetable. The firm began in 1999 above an auto garage in Mumbai and survived the dot-com bust.Avendus has expanded beyond mergers and capital raising into wealth management, institutional equities, credit and equity capital markets in recent years. The firm ranked 13th in Bloomberg’s India equity capital markets league table in 2026, up from 20th in 2025, overtaking Bank of America Corp. and HSBC Holdings Plc. Its deals this year include the initial public offering of National Stock Exchange Ltd. and share sales by SoftBank Group Corp.-backed Lenskart Solutions Ltd.The partnership is also expected to reach beyond dealmaking. Hamamoto said Mizuho could give its wealthy Japanese clients access to Indian private-market products, while Avendus could help newly wealthy Indians invest in Japan and other overseas markets.Deepak said he also sees opportunities in asset management and institutional equities, including distributing Avendus’s India research to Mizuho’s global clients. Private credit is another area he expects to expand.“With Mizuho as a partner, I would actually increase my aspirations and ambition,” Deepak said. “If we were to take 10 years to achieve the same thing, I would like to do it in five.” By Anto Antony , Bloomberg Published On Nov 26, 2026 at 07:41 AM IST Telegram Facebook Copy Link Comments What are your thoughts?
