Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re Globe Enterprises (India) Limited (NCLT Ahmedabad) Summary: The Ahmedabad Bench of the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), comprising Judicial Member Chitra Hankare and Technical Member Dr. Velamur G. Venkata Chalapathy, allowed a joint company application filed by Globe Enterprises (India) Limited (Demerged Company) and Morabia Creations Limited (Resulting Company) under Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Compromises, Arrangements and Amalgamations) Rules, 2016. The application concerned a proposed Scheme of Arrangement for separating the trading business of Globe Enterprises from its manufacturing operations. The respective Boards of Directors had approved the Scheme through resolutions dated 07.02.2026, and the appointed date was fixed as 01.04.2026. Both companies had their registered offices within the territorial jurisdiction of the Registrar of Companies, Ahmedabad. Globe Enterprises was engaged in the textile, clothing and apparel business, encompassing manufacturing and trading activities, including garment and apparel trading under the established brands INDIGENX and ORIJEAN. The proposed restructuring contemplated the transfer of the trading business to Morabia Creations, enabling independent management, specialised resource allocation, operational efficiency, improved decision-making and focused business development. According to the applicants, the demerger would facilitate independent access to equity and debt capital markets, attract investors aligned with the trading business, improve market responsiveness and unlock shareholder value through independent market-driven valuation. The resulting company would acquire the INDIGENX and ORIJEAN brands and operate independently without being controlled by the existing promoters of Globe Enterprises, thereby reducing potential conflicts of interest between the two entities. The equity shares of Globe Enterprises were listed on the National Stock Exchange of India Limited (NSE), whereas Morabia Creations was an unlisted company. The Scheme contemplated listing the equity shares of Morabia Creations on NSE after the Scheme became effective, subject to compliance with the applicable SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, and other relevant SEBI regulations and circulars. The authorised equity share capital of Globe Enterprises stood at Rs.91 crore as on 30.06.2026, while Morabia Creations had authorised and paid-up equity share capital of Rs.2 crore. Neither company had outstanding preference shares or debentures. Morabia Creations had ten equity shareholders, and the requisite number of shareholders had furnished written consent approving the Scheme. It had no secured or unsecured creditors. The applicant companies furnished Chartered Accountants’ certificates confirming their respective lists of equity shareholders, secured creditors and unsecured creditors. Their statutory auditors had examined the proposed Scheme under Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act, 2013, and certified compliance with the accounting standards under Section 133 of the Act. The proposed accounting treatment was specified in Clause 15 of the Scheme. A valuation report dated 30.09.2025 prepared by Procurve Valux Private Limited was also submitted. The applicants represented that no proceedings under Sections 210 to 227 of the Companies Act were pending, that the Scheme did not contemplate debt restructuring and that the Competition Act, 2002, and FEMA provisions were not applicable. A letter dated 06.07.2026 from NSE conveying its no objection to the proposed Scheme was placed on record. After examining the application and supporting documents, the Tribunal directed Globe Enterprises to convene separate meetings of its equity shareholders, secured creditors and unsecured creditors within 45 days from the date of the order, since the requisite consent affidavits had not been obtained. In the case of Morabia Creations, the Tribunal dispensed with the equity shareholders’ meeting in view of the written consents. As the resulting company had no secured or unsecured creditors, creditor meetings were not required. The Tribunal directed publication of advertisements in Financial Express in English and Sandesh in Gujarati at least one month before the meetings. Individual notices, together with the Scheme and explanatory statements required under Section 102 read with Sections 230 to 232 of the Companies Act and Rule 6 of the applicable Rules, were also required to be dispatched at least one month before the meetings. The Tribunal appointed Advocate Chirag Gujar as Chairman and Advocate Megha Chitre as Scrutinizer for the meetings. The quorum was to be determined under Rule 5 of the Companies (Compromises, Arrangements and Amalgamations) Rules, 2016. Meetings could be conducted physically or through video conferencing, with voting through polling papers or electronic voting facilities. The Chairman was directed to submit the meeting results to the Tribunal in Form CAA-4 within seven days of conclusion of the meetings, in accordance with Rule 14. The applicants were directed to pay Rs.75,000 per meeting to the Chairman and not more than Rs.35,000 to the Scrutinizer as their respective fees. The NCLT further directed the applicant companies to issue notices in Form CAA-3 under Section 230(5) of the Companies Act and Rule 8 of the applicable Rules to the Central Government through the Regional Director, Registrar of Companies, Official Liquidator, Income Tax Department, SEBI, BSE, NSE and other applicable sectoral regulators. The authorities were required to submit their representations, if any, within 30 days of receipt of the notices, failing which they would be presumed to have no objection to the proposed Scheme. The applicants were also directed to file a compliance affidavit with the Registry within seven days of the order. Accordingly, the joint company application was allowed and disposed of. The order constitutes first-stage procedural directions concerning stakeholder meetings, dispensation of meetings and regulatory notices, rather than final sanction of the proposed demerger Scheme. The Demerged Company is currently engaged in the business of textile, clothing, and apparel, encompassing both manufacturing and trading activities. It carries out trading operations, particularly in garments and apparel, under the well-recognized brand names INDIGENX and ORIJEAN. By separating the trading operations into an independent entry, the management intends to allocate resources more effectively, streamline business processes, promote specialisation, and unlock greater shareholder value. This strategic restructuring aims to foster independent growth opportunities and better market positioning for each business segment, benefiting stakeholders and enabling stronger business sustainability. b. The Trading Business of Globe Enterprise (India) Limited, specifically encompassing its well-established brands INDIGENX and ORIJEAN, has significantly matured and evolved over the years, establishing itself firmly in the textile trading sector. To effectively leverage its strong market presence, extensive distribution network, and established brand reputation, the Company intends to segregate its Trading Business into a separate listed entity through a strategic demerger scheme. This restructuring will enhance dedicated management focus, improve operational efficiency, and facilitate agile decision-making specifically tailored to trading activities, distinguishing clearly from the Company’s manufacturing operations. c. The proposed demerger is strategically beneficial for Globe Enterprise (India) Limited and its shareholders, employees, creditors, and other stakeholders for the following key reasons: India’s textile trading market offers substantial growth potential driven by rising consumer demand, rapidly evolving market trends, increased digitalization, and improved logistics infrastructure. Creating a dedicated trading entity allows Globe to efficiently capitalize on these specific opportunities without the constraints of manufacturing operations. A standalone trading entity will possess greater flexibility to implement tailored market strategies, swiftly adapt to changing consumer preferences, optimize procurement and distribution processes, and enhance responsiveness in an increasingly competitive marketplace. The new, focused trading entity will have enhanced access to capital markets, both equity and debt, nabling targeted investment in market expansion, technology upgrades, strategic partnerships, and brand-building initiatives specific to the trading segment. As a specialized trading-focused entity, it will attract strategic investors, stakeholders, and business partners whose risk profiles and investment interests are closely aligned with the dynamics of the textile trading sector, thereby optimizing valuation and fostering sustained value creation. This Scheme will unlock intrinsic value for shareholders through independent market driven valuation of the new entity’s shares, providing enhanced investment flexibility and the option to remain invested specifically in a robust, trading-focused listed company. The restructuring will support long-term strategic synergies and stability between Globe’s manufacturing and the newly independent trading business, ensuring mutually beneficial operational alignment and competitive advantages in their respective domains. The resulting company will acquire the INDIGENX and ORIJEAN brands, and it will operate independently without being controlled by the existing Globe promoters. This will allow the resulting company to independently strategize, innovate, and attract fresh investment, thereby providing a clear distinction and reducing potential conflicts of interest between the resulting and the demerged entities. 8.
