Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re NM Automation & Control Private Limited (NCLT Delhi) Summary : The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), New Delhi Bench, Court-VI, in the matter of the proposed amalgamation of NM Automation & Control Private Limited with Sonepar India Private Limited, held that the requirement of convening a meeting of unsecured creditors could not be dispensed with merely because the transferor company was a wholly owned subsidiary of the transferee company and the proposed merger was stated to have no adverse effect on creditors. By its order dated 5 November 2026, the Tribunal dispensed with the meeting of equity shareholders because all shareholders had furnished consent affidavits, observed that no meeting of secured creditors was necessary as there were none, but directed that a meeting of 896 unsecured creditors involving aggregate dues of ₹303,44,70,214.67 (approximately ₹303.45 crore) be convened because their consent affidavits had not been obtained. The application was considered under Sections 230–232 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with the Companies (Compromises, Arrangements and Amalgamations) Rules, 2016. The proposed scheme contemplated the amalgamation of NM Automation & Control Private Limited, incorporated on 25 June 2007 and having its registered office in Mumbai, with its holding company, Sonepar India Private Limited, incorporated on 1 October 2009 and having its registered office in New Delhi. The appointed date was 1 April 2026. The transferee company’s Board of Directors approved the scheme on 11 May 2026. The applicant explained that the merger would consolidate business operations, reduce administrative and regulatory compliance costs, improve resource utilisation, strengthen financial efficiency and eliminate duplication of corporate functions. Since the transferor company was wholly owned by the transferee company, no fresh shares were proposed to be issued as merger consideration, and the existing equity shares held by the transferee company in the transferor company would stand cancelled. The scheme also provided for continuity of employment, preservation of service benefits and transfer of employee welfare obligations. The applicant submitted that its two equity shareholders had unanimously consented to the scheme through affidavits, thereby justifying dispensation of their meeting under Section 230(9). It further stated that it had no secured creditors. Regarding unsecured creditors, however, the applicant acknowledged that no consent affidavits had been obtained. It nevertheless requested dispensation of their meeting on the ground that the merger involved a wholly owned subsidiary and would not adversely affect their rights or interests. The applicant undertook to convene the meeting if directed by the Tribunal. The application was supported by incorporation documents, financial statements, board resolutions, shareholder consent affidavits, Chartered Accountant certificates and the statutory auditor’s certificate concerning the proposed accounting treatment. After examining the documents and submissions, the Tribunal accepted the request for dispensation of the equity shareholders’ meeting, noting the unanimous consent of both shareholders. It also recorded that a secured creditors’ meeting was unnecessary because the transferee company had no secured creditors. However, the Tribunal declined to dispense with the unsecured creditors’ meeting in the absence of their consent affidavits. It directed that the meeting be conducted through video conferencing with remote electronic voting, in compliance with the applicable Ministry of Corporate Affairs circulars. Ms. Anu Jagmohan Singh, former NCLT Member, was appointed Chairperson, and Advocate Himanshu Kaushik was appointed Scrutinizer. The Tribunal further directed the applicant to issue notices to unsecured creditors at least 30 days before the meeting, publish advertisements in the English and Hindi Delhi editions of Business Standard, circulate the scheme and prescribed explanatory statements, and provide provisional financial statements meeting the statutory recency requirement. Notices in Form CAA-3 were also required to be issued to the Regional Director, Registrar of Companies, Official Liquidator, Income Tax authorities and applicable sectoral regulators. The Chairperson was directed to report the meeting results in Form CAA-4 within seven days of its conclusion. The Tribunal disposed of the application with these procedural directions, leaving the applicant entitled to move an appropriate subsequent application after compliance. The order was a first-stage procedural direction concerning meetings and notices, not a final sanction of the amalgamation scheme.
