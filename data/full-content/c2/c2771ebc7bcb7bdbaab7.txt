Articles 2 min read IRDAI flags conflict in banks, auto dealers selling insurance Ajay Seth, the chairman of IRDAI, advocates for enhanced corporate governance within the insurance industry to mitigate conflicts of interest. He pointed out potential dangers when lenders offer insurance and when car dealers provide combined insurance and repair services. Seth stressed the need for boards to implement protective measures for customer interests and to refine governance. Mayur Shetty TNN Published On Nov 19, 2026 at 03:05 PM IST var news_msid = 135985069; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook MUMBAI: IRDAI chairman has called for stronger corporate governance in the insurance sector, citing potential conflicts of interest when lenders sell insurance and automobile dealers combine insurance distribution with vehicle repair services. He also called for boards to act as the first gatekeepers of governance, drawing on RBI's engagement with bank and NBFC boards. Separately, he questioned whether financial regulators should erect barriers between sectors or allow savings to move towards more efficient managers, while flagging risks from the widespread use of AI in insurance underwriting and claims settlement. Advt Speaking at the Gatekeepers of Governance 2026 event in Mumbai helmed by former Sebi chief M Damodaran, Ajay Seth, chairman of IRDAI, said business models that had served the industry for decades could also create conflicts of interest. He said boards needed to establish safeguards to ensure these conflicts did not compromise customer interests."Some business models have served very well over decades, but that brings in the associated risks in terms of conflict of interest," Seth said. Citing lenders selling insurance and automobile dealers combining insurance distribution with damage repair services, he said, "These are the two different signs of services. They have to be kept separately rather than mixing with each other."Seth said boards had a responsibility to establish safeguards against such conflicts. "The role of the board becomes more important, that what kind of a guardrail it sets to take care that those conflicts of interest do not come in the way of providing the best advice, best service, best product to the customers," he said.He also called for the insurance sector to strengthen corporate governance by adopting practices followed by RBI in its engagement with banks and NBFCs. He cited RBI's structured interactions with company boards and independent directors as an approach that had helped improve governance across the financial sector. Advt On the movement of household savings between financial products, Seth questioned whether regulators should create barriers between sectors or facilitate the flow of savings towards more efficient managers. He said changing preferences were reshaping the way savings were managed and called for greater coordination among financial regulators."So, as the shift in the economy is taking place in terms of the savings management and the preference of the people are changing, do we build higher and higher walls so that the water doesn't come in? Or we build boats so that we can sail wherever we want to be," he said. Advt Seth also flagged the risks arising from the growing use of artificial intelligence in insurance. "Are those models trained enough for our economy, our behaviour of the policyholders and the associated risks? Are humans in charge of taking the final decision?" he said.
