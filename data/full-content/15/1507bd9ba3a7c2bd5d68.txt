Vs DCIT (ITAT Delhi) Transfer Pricing: ITAT Remands JLL’s ₹138.09 Crore Adjustment for Reasoned Comparability Analysis The Delhi Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has restored the transfer pricing dispute concerning Jones Lang LaSalle Property Consultants (India) Pvt. Ltd. to the Assessing Officer/Transfer Pricing Officer for fresh examination. The Tribunal held that the inclusion or exclusion of comparable companies must rest on relevant financial information, functional analysis and cogent reasons. However, deficiencies in the TPO’s analysis did not automatically justify deleting the entire adjustment. The Tribunal also found that the assessee had not furnished the underlying material necessary for a conclusive examination of the disputed comparables. The order was pronounced on 9 November 2026 by Judicial Member Kavitha Rajagopal and Accountant Member Sanjay Awasthi in ITA No.520/Del/2023, concerning Assessment Year 2019-20. Background of the Dispute The assessee provided real estate consultancy, property management, advisory, brokerage and allied services. It filed its return declaring income of approximately ₹299.78 crore under the normal provisions. Following a reference under Section 92CA of the Income-tax Act, 1961, the TPO proposed aggregate transfer pricing adjustments of approximately ₹196.45 crore. These comprised ₹57.80 crore towards business service charges paid to associated enterprises and ₹138.65 crore towards property management, advisory and brokerage services. After the Dispute Resolution Panel’s directions, the latter adjustment was reduced to ₹138,08,78,127. The business service charges adjustment remained unchanged, resulting in a final aggregate adjustment of approximately ₹195.88 crore. The assessee challenged the final assessment order dated 23 January 2023 before the Tribunal. Dispute Over Comparable Selection The assessee had adopted the Transactional Net Margin Method, selecting 19 comparable companies for benchmarking. The TPO rejected these companies and undertook a fresh search using filters relating to export revenue, employee expenditure and related party transactions. The final set comprised Lancor Maintenance and Services Ltd., BVG India Ltd., Interactive Manpower Solution Pvt. Ltd. and Info Edge (India) Ltd. After giving effect to the DRP’s directions, the average operating profit-to-operating cost margin of these companies was marginally revised from 20.85% to 20.80%. The assessee alleged inconsistent application of the 70% export-income filter, inadequate disclosure of the search strategy and inclusion of functionally dissimilar companies. It also challenged the treatment of deemed international transactions and computation of operating margins. Comparability Requires Financial and Functional Evidence The Tribunal explained that determining the arm’s length price under TNMM requires examination of the functions performed, assets employed and risks assumed by the tested party and the proposed comparables. This exercise must comply with Rules 10B(2) and 10B(3) of the Income-tax Rules, 1962. Selection or rejection of companies cannot be supported by general observations alone. The assessee had raised specific objections concerning functional differences, filters and comparable selection. These objections were material to the determination of the arm’s length price and required proper adjudication through reasoned findings. Missing Comparable Data Prevented Final Adjudication The Tribunal also identified a significant limitation in the assessee’s presentation. While challenging the disputed companies, the assessee primarily relied on observations in the TPO’s order. It did not place independent annual reports, financial statements, segmental information or other relevant details before the Tribunal to enable an effective comparability examination. Without this material, the Tribunal could not meaningfully compare the companies’ functions, assets and risks or conclusively decide whether their inclusion or exclusion was correct. It therefore declined to substitute its own findings on comparability without the necessary supporting record. Remand Distinguished From Filling Evidentiary Gaps The assessee argued that the Revenue should not receive repeated opportunities merely to improve its case. It sought deletion of the adjustment instead of another round of proceedings. The Tribunal accepted that remand should not be ordered routinely to enable a party to fill evidentiary gaps. Nevertheless, it distinguished that situation from restoring issues where material objections had not received reasoned adjudication. In this case, the missing comparable data and unresolved objections made fresh examination necessary. The alleged deficiencies in the TPO’s approach did not independently establish that the entire adjustment deserved deletion. Fresh Examination Subject to Express Limits The Tribunal restored the issues arising from grounds 13 to 20 to the AO/TPO, subject to the binding DRP directions and applicable statutory provisions. The authorities must consider the material already available, examine the assessee’s objections and pass a reasoned and speaking order after providing an adequate hearing. The examination must remain confined to the disputed issues. Matters that had otherwise attained finality cannot be reopened. The Tribunal expressed no opinion on the ultimate inclusion or exclusion of any disputed comparable and left legally permissible contentions open. Other Grounds and Outcome The assessee sought withdrawal of the business service charges grounds based on a BAPA agreement. The additional ground challenging the transfer pricing reference and the TDS credit ground were not pressed. Interest and penalty-related grounds were treated as consequential. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes, with the disputed comparability issues remanded rather than finally decided in the assessee’s favour. Cases Discussed Rajesh Babulbhai Damania v. Brief facts are that the assessee is engaged in the business of providing real-estate consultancy, property management, advisory and brokerage services and other allied services. For the year under consideration, the assessee filed its return of income dated 29.11.2019 declaring total income of Rs.299,77,83,130/- under the normal provisions and Rs.308,90,47,905/- u/s 115JB of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (‘the Act’, for short). The assessee’s case was selected for scrutiny and notices u/s 143(2) and 142(1) of the Act were duly issued and served upon the assessee. The ld. AO observed that the assessee had entered into international transaction with its Associated Enterprises (AEs), and, hence, a reference was made to the Ld. TPO u/s 92CA of the Act for determination of the Arm’s Length Price (ALP) of the said transactions. The Ld. TPO, vide order dated 28.01.2022, passed u/s 92CA(3) of the Act, proposed an aggregate adjustment of Rs.196,44,87,167/- comprising Rs.57,79,53,359/- towards business service charges paid to AEs and Rs.138,65,33,808/- in respect of property management, advisory and brokerage services rendered to its AEs pursuant to which the Ld. AO passed the draft assessment order dated 31.03.2022 incorporating the aforesaid adjustment. Aggrieved by the proposed adjustment, the assessee filed its objection before the Hon’ble DRP challenging the determination of ALP at ‘nil’ in respect of business service charges and contending that the service had actually been received from its AEs for the purpose of its business. The assessee also challenged the rejection of comparable companies selected in its TP study pertaining to the property management, advisory and brokerage services and also challenged the inclusion of functionally dissimilar companies, application of various filters, computation of operating margins and inclusions of deemed international transactions in the adjustment base. It is further observed that the assessee had adopted Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM) as the Most Appropriate Method (MAM) by benchmarking the relevant international transactions and had selected 19 comparable companies which were rejected by the Ld. TPO while benchmarking and the Ld. TPO had undertaken a fresh comparability exercise by applying various filters including export revenue, employee expenditure and related party transaction filters. The Ld. TPO had finally selected four comparable companies and determined their average operating profit to operating cost margin at 20.85% resulting in the proposed adjustment of Rs.138.65 crore. After duly considering the objections raised by the assessee, the Hon’ble DRP, vide directions dated 22.12.2022, sustained the adjustments relating to business service charges and further issued directions in respect of several objections concerning the selection and rejection of comparable companies, inclusion of deemed international transactions and computation of operating margins. The Hon’ble DRP directed the Ld. TPO/AO to reconsider the submission of the assessee and pass speaking orders in respect of certain companies. However, in the case of Lancor Maintenance and Services Ltd., the Hon’ble DRP specifically upheld its inclusion as a comparable. The Hon’ble DRP also directed that no fresh inquiry should be conducted and that verification should be confined to the material already available on record. Pursuant to the directions of the Hon’ble DRP, the Ld. TPO passed an order giving effect to the directions and retained the same four comparable companies, namely, Lancor Maintenance and Services Ltd., BVG India Ltd., Interactive Manpower Solution Pvt. Ltd., and Info Edge (India) Ltd. The average operating margin was marginally reduced from 20.85% to 20.80% on account of revision of the margins of Interactive Manpower Solutions Pvt. Ltd. From 31.31% to 31.12%. Further, the adjustment relating to property management, advisory and brokerage services was reduced from Rs.138,65,33,808/- to Rs.138,08,78,127/-, whereas the adjustment relating to business service charges remained unchanged at Rs.57,79,53,359, thereby determining the aggregate transfer pricing adjustment at Rs.195,88,31,486/-. The Ld. AO passed the final assessment order dated 23.01.2023 u/s 143(3) r.w.s. 144C(13) of the Act, thereby incorporating the transfer pricing adjustments. Aggrieved by the final assessment order, the assessee is in appeal before us challenging the impugned order on the aforementioned grounds. 5. The Ld. AR for the assessee, vide application dated 11.04.2023, proposed to withdraw grounds No.5 to 12 on the basis of the BAPA agreement and at the time of hearing stated that the assessee intends not to press the additional ground and ground No.21, and Ground No.1 is general in nature and, hence, these grounds require no adjudication. 6. Ground No.2 to 4 raised by the assessee challenges the action of the Hon’ble DRP in remanding the issues to the file of the Ld. TPO/AO for passing a speaking order which according to the assessee is in contravention to the express provisions of the Act and Grounds No.13 to 20 is on the merits. 7. We have heard the rival submissions and perused the material available on record. The controversy in the present grounds relates to the determination of the arm’s length price of international transactions pertaining to property management, advisory and brokerage services. The assessee had adopted TNMM and selected certain comparable companies, whereas the TPO rejected several comparables selected by the assessee and introduced four additional companies, resulting in a substantial transfer pricing adjustment. 8. The ld. AR submitted that the Ld. TPO had rejected the comparability analysis undertaken by the assessee without properly appreciating the functional profile of the selected companies and had applied various filters, particularly the 70% export-income filter, inconsistently. It was further contended that the companies introduced by the Ld. TPO were functionally dissimilar to the assessee and that the search strategy adopted by the Ld. TPO was not adequately disclosed. The ld. AR also submitted that the Hon’ble DRP, instead of independently adjudicating the detailed objections raised by the assessee, had directed the Ld. TPO to pass a speaking order, and that the consequential order substantially reiterated the original findings. 9. The ld. AR, in paragraph 9 of the written submissions, further submitted that the assessee’s transfer pricing study had been arbitrarily rejected, the comparables selected by the assessee had been excluded by applying irrelevant filters and generalised functional analysis, and the additional comparables introduced by the Ld.TPO were functionally dissimilar. It was contended that the Hon’ble DRP had disposed of the objections through cryptic observations without adequately examining the material placed on record. 10. The ld. AR further submitted that, where the facts and applicable law are clear, repeated opportunities should not be granted to the Department merely to improve its case. In support of this contention, reliance was placed on the decisions in Rajesh Babulbhai Damania v. CIT, 251 ITR 541, and ACIT v. Kumar Ice, 237 ITR (AT), as cited in the written submissions. The ld. AR accordingly urged that the adjustment be deleted rather than restoring the matter for another round of proceedings. 11. On the other hand, the Revenue’s position, as emerging from the Ld.TPO’s order, is that the comparables selected by the assessee did not satisfy the prescribed comparability criteria and that the Ld. TPO was justified in undertaking an independent examination for determining the arm’s length price. 12. Having considered the rival contentions, we find that the determination of the arm’s length price under TNMM requires examination of the functions performed, assets employed and risks assumed by the tested party and the comparable companies in accordance with Rule 10B(2) and Rule 10B(3) of the Income-tax Rules, 1962. The inclusion or exclusion of comparable companies must be supported by cogent reasons based on relevant financial information and functional analysis. We further observe that the assessee had raised specific objections regarding the selection and rejection of comparable companies, application of filters and functional differences, which require proper adjudication. 13. We have also considered the contention of the ld. AR that repeated opportunities should not be granted to the Revenue merely to fill lacunae in its case. There can be no dispute with the proposition that remand should not be ordered routinely to enable a party to improve its case. However, a distinction has to be drawn between granting an opportunity to fill evidentiary lacunae and restoring an issue where material objections have not been adjudicated through reasoned findings. The former cannot be permitted as a matter of course, whereas the latter may be necessary to ensure proper adjudication in accordance with law. 14. In the present case, the controversy involves examination of several comparable companies, application of filters and determination of operating margins. The assessee’s objections are material to the determination of the arm’s length price and cannot be rejected without proper examination. At the same time, the alleged deficiencies in the Ld.TPO’s analysis do not, by themselves, establish that the entire adjustment is liable to be deleted. The judicial precedents relied upon by the ld. AR must also be considered in the context of their respective facts and the nature of the deficiencies involved. 15. We further find that, during the course of hearing before us, the ld. AR, while challenging the inclusion and exclusion of various comparable companies, primarily placed reliance on the observations recorded in the TPO’s order. However, no independent details, annual reports, financial statements, segmental information or other relevant material concerning the disputed comparable companies were placed before us to enable an effective examination of their functional comparability. In the absence of such material, it is not possible for us to undertake a meaningful comparison of the functions performed, assets employed and risks assumed by the respective companies or to conclusively determine the correctness of their inclusion or exclusion. Merely relying upon the observations of the Ld. TPO, without furnishing the underlying financial and functional details of the comparable companies, would not be sufficient to adjudicate the issue conclusively on merits. We are, therefore, of the considered view that it would not be appropriate for us to substitute our own findings on comparability without the requisite material being available on record. 16. We have also considered the contention of the ld. AR that repeated opportunities should not be granted to the Revenue merely to improve its case or fill lacunae in the assessment proceedings. While we agree with the proposition that remand should not be ordered as a matter of routine, we find that, in the present case, the material necessary for examining the functional comparability of the disputed companies has not been placed before us. Further, the objections raised by the assessee regarding the application of filters and selection and rejection of comparables require proper examination through reasoned findings. Therefore, the restoration of these issues would not amount to granting an opportunity to the Revenue merely to improve its case, but would be necessary for their effective adjudication in accordance with law. Accordingly, we deem it appropriate to restore the issues arising from grounds Nos. 13 to 20 to the file of the Ld. AO/TPO for fresh examination, subject to the binding directions of the Hon’ble DRP and the applicable statutory provisions. The Ld.AO/TPO shall consider the material already available on record, examine the objections raised by the assessee and pass a reasoned and speaking order after affording adequate opportunity of being heard to the assessee. The scope of fresh examination shall remain confined to the disputed issues, without reopening matters which have otherwise attained finality. 17. We make it clear that no opinion is expressed on the ultimate inclusion or exclusion of the disputed comparables, and all legally permissible contentions of both parties are left open. 18. Accordingly, grounds Nos. 13 to 20 are allowed for statistical purposes. 19. Ground No.22 and 23 are consequential in nature and does not require further adjudication. 20. In the result, the appeal filed by the assessee is hereby allowed for statistical purpose. Order pronounced in the open court on 09.11.2026.
