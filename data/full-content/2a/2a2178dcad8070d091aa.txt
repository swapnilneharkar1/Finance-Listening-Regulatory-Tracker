Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Ashray Infrastructure Vs Commissioner of Central Excise & ST, Surat-I (CESTAT Ahmedabad) Summary: Ashray Infrastructure, a partnership firm engaged in construction of commercial or industrial buildings and civil structures, challenged a service tax demand arising from freight expenses recorded during financial years 2010-11 to 2012-13. The department treated those expenses as consideration for Goods Transport Agency (GTA) services liable to tax under the reverse charge mechanism. The show cause notice dated 27 July 2015 proposed service tax of ₹11,07,215 with interest and penalty. The adjudicating authority confirmed ₹10,85,973, together with interest and penalties under Sections 77 and 78 of the Finance Act, 1994. The Commissioner (Appeals), Surat-I, upheld that decision on 15 November 2017. The appellant submitted that no transporter had issued a consignment note. It argued that the statutory definition of GTA required both transportation of goods by road and issuance of a consignment note by the service provider. Without that document, payment of freight charges alone could not establish receipt of taxable GTA services. The appellant further explained that some bills concerned the sale of building materials such as kapchi and white sand rather than a standalone transportation service. Another transaction involved supply of tangible goods. It also disputed invocation of the extended limitation period, describing the controversy as one involving interpretation of the statute. The Revenue representative supported the impugned order. The Tribunal examined the record and accepted that no consignment notes had been issued in the transportation transactions. It also noted that certain invoices were for supply of materials and that the transaction involving tangible goods could not be classified as GTA service. The Tribunal referred extensively to Vedanta Limited, AIMS Industries and Chartered Logistics Limited. Those decisions discussed the distinction between a transporter and a GTA, the mandatory nature of consignment notes, and why invoices or payment vouchers do not automatically substitute for consignment notes. The authorities reproduced in the order included Lakshminarayana Mining Co., South Eastern Coalfields Ltd., Nandganj Sihori Sugar Co. Ltd., Birla Ready Mix, Mahanadi Coalfields Ltd., East India Minerals Ltd. and Narendra Road Lines Pvt. Ltd. They supported the proposition that the statutory definition must be satisfied before reverse-charge GTA liability can arise. The Tribunal also noted the Supreme Court order reported at 2024 (4) TMI 8, dismissing the civil appeal against the Tribunal’s decision in Service Tax Appeal No. 10857/2022. The Supreme Court order quoted in the judgment did not supply independent detailed reasoning. Applying the cited authorities to the facts, the Tribunal held that the disputed transportation could not be treated as GTA service in the absence of consignment notes. It accordingly set aside the impugned order and allowed Ashray Infrastructure’s appeal on 14 August 2024. Cases Discussed Vedanta Limited v. CGST — 2023 (9) TMI 1062 (CESTAT) AIMS Industries v. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CESTAT AHMEDABAD The brief facts of the case are that the appellant is a partnership firm and is engaged in providing taxable service under the category of ‘Construction of Commercial or Industrial Building and Civil Structure‘ as defined under Section 65 of the Finance Act, 1994. During the course of audit of the financial records of the assessee for the Financial Year 2010-11 to 2012-13, it is observed that the appellant had made payment towards freight charges for the transportation of goods but no service tax has been paid by them. Accordingly, a show cause notice dated 27.07.2015 was issued to the appellant proposing demand of service tax under the category of GTA Service under reverse charge mechanism, for the amount of Rs. 11,07,215/- along with interest and penalty. The said show cause notice was adjudicated by the Adjudicating Authority vide order-in-original No. 165/ADJ/ADC-KSM/OA/2016-16 dated 29.02.2016 whereby the proposal made in the show cause notice has been confirmed and demand of service tax of Rs. 10,85,973/- along with interest and imposition of penalties under Section 77 and 78 was confirmed. Being aggrieved by the said order-in- original the appellant filed appeal before the Commissioner (Appeals) who vide order-in-appeal No.CCESA-SRT-APPEAL-PS-198-2017-18 dated 15.11.2017 which is impugned herein, upheld the order-in-original and rejected the appeal filed by the appellant. Therefore the present appeal filed by the appellant before this Tribunal. 2. Shri Jigar Shah, learned Counsel with Shri Amber Kumarawat, Advocate appearing for the appellant, at the outset submits that though the appellant have received transportation service but no consignment note was issued by the transporters. It is trite law that to classify transporter service under GTA service, it is necessary that the transporter issues consignment note. However, in the present case no consignment note was issued therefore, as per the statutory provisions, the service of transportation cannot be classified under GTA service. He further submits that some of the transactions of supply of tangible goods as well as supply of material such as kapchi, white sand etc. which is nothing but sale of goods therefore, the Adjudicating Authority has wrongly included those transaction assuming GTA service. Therefore, those transactions which are other than pure transportation cannot be classified under GTA service. He further submits that the issue involved in the present case, the dispute is arising out of interpretation of statute therefore, the demand is also not maintainable on the ground of time-bar. 3. In the present case, admittedly, no consignment note was issued by the goods transporter. In other words, the transport of coal is done by the transport contractor which satisfied the first condition but no consignment note being issued. The slip issued by the appellant as recipient of service is taken with such activity of transport to bring in tax liability. We find that such attempt is beyond the scope of law and without merit. 7.
