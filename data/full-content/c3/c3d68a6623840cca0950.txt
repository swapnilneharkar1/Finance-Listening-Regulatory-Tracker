However, the cost of funds, changes in loan composition and competitive pressure on lending yields remain key variables that could affect its ability to sustain margins.The bank’s net interest income grew around 5.2% sequentially in the second quarter, supported by healthy loan growth and stable margins. Loans increased 6.6% sequentially, while deposits rose 4.5% over the same period and 16% year on year. Elara Capital said the margin trajectory would depend on funding costs, changes in loan composition and the bank’s ability to maintain lending yields amid competition. Advt Jewel loans, which account for around 29% of the loan book, grew 2.5% sequentially, with limited scope to raise yields in the segment. Around 57% of the bank’s loans are linked to the external benchmark lending rate, providing some support following the repo rate increase. Net interest margin nevertheless remained unchanged from the preceding quarter at 4.26%.Deposit mobilisation will remain important as the bank seeks to sustain loan growth. Deposits grew 4.5% sequentially, while current and savings account deposits increased 5.4% and 3.9%, respectively. The CASA ratio remained steady at 27.5%, supported by a 4.6% sequential increase in CASA balances.The bank followed a calibrated lending approach amid geopolitical uncertainty and consciously moderated growth in lower-yielding segments. Retail, agriculture and MSME advances accounted for around 86% of its loan book, a share that remained stable during the quarter. The composition of future loan growth will be important in determining whether the bank can maintain margins while expanding its balance sheet.Asset qualityAsset quality and provisioning are additional factors to monitor. Slippages improved to around Rs 1.2 billion, or 44 basis points, from 53 basis points in the preceding quarter. However, credit costs increased to 42 basis points from 35 basis points after the bank created Rs 250 million in floating provisions. Cumulative floating provisions stood at around Rs 1.25 billion, while total provisions amounted to around 1.7% of loans. Advt Potential stress in the MSME segment and the transition to expected credit loss provisioning could affect future asset quality and credit costs, although the bank expects the impact of the transition to be limited given its existing buffers.The brokerage expects Karur Vysya Bank to sustain a return on assets above 2% and deliver earnings growth of around 20% annually over FY27-FY29. It retained its buy rating with a target price of Rs 415, based on a valuation of two times estimated adjusted book value for September 2028, while cautioning that competitive pressures could limit a significant valuation rerating.
