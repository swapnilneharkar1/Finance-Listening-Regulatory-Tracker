The 268-basis-point gap reflects the challenges banks face in securing enough deposits to sustain loan growth.The mismatch has persisted, pushing banks to look beyond traditional deposits and rely more heavily on market borrowings and other funding sources. While deposits remain larger than loans in absolute terms, their slower growth is changing the funding equation for lenders. Advt Scheduled commercial banks added Rs 40.8 lakh crore in deposits during the year, taking the total, excluding interbank deposits, to Rs 282.5 lakh crore as of Oct 31, from Rs 241.8 lakh crore a year earlier. Credit, excluding interbank lending, increased by Rs 37.9 lakh crore to Rs 231.82 lakh crore from Rs 193.94 lakh crore. As a result deposit accretion exceeded incremental credit by Rs 2.9 lakh crore, even as lending grew faster in percentage terms because it started from a smaller base.Time deposits, which account for the bulk of bank funding, grew 17.6% to Rs 247.7 lakh crore, adding Rs 36.9 lakh crore. Demand deposits rose at a more modest 12.1% to Rs 34.8 lakh crore. On the asset side, loans, cash credits and overdrafts, which account for nearly 98% of bank credit, grew 19.3% to Rs 226.9 lakh crore. Domestic bill financing also expanded sharply, with inland bills purchased and discounted rising 38.8% and 34.2%, respectively.Food credit outstanding with commercial banks surged 75.2% to Rs 1.2 lakh crore from Rs 70,371 crore a year earlier. Including co-operative banks, credit growth across all scheduled banks stood at 19.3%, taking outstanding loans to Rs 237 lakh crore. Deposits across these banks grew 16.8% to Rs 288.2 lakh crore.
