(Bombay High Court) Summary : The Bombay High Court, Kolhapur Bench, comprising Justice Sharmila U. Deshmukh and Justice Shailesh P. Brahme, allowed two connected writ petitions filed by Balaji Enterprises, through its proprietor Suresh D. Bairani, and Tirupati Metal, through its proprietor Ram D. Bairani. The petitioners challenged the continuation of provisional attachment orders issued under Section 83 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 and the Maharashtra Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017, contending that the attachments had automatically ceased to have effect after expiry of the statutory period of one year and that their continued operation was without authority of law. The provisional attachments had been effected through FORM GST DRC-22 dated 24 June 2025, covering agricultural and residential properties as well as bank accounts belonging to the petitioners. The petitioners submitted that Section 83(2) expressly provides that every provisional attachment ceases to have effect after expiry of one year from the date of the attachment order. Consequently, the attachments in the present matters ceased to operate from 23 June 2026. However, despite expiry of the statutory period, the GST authorities had not communicated the cessation of attachment to the concerned authorities, resulting in continued restrictions over the attached properties and bank accounts. The Division Bench examined the statutory provision and observed that the consequence of expiry of the one-year period was expressly provided under Section 83(2). The provisional attachment had therefore outlived its statutory life and ceased to have effect by operation of law. Its continued existence after expiry of the prescribed period was contrary to the statutory provisions. The Court accordingly allowed both writ petitions in terms of prayer clauses (a) and (b), and directed Respondent No. 3 to issue communications to the concerned authorities informing them about expiry of the provisional attachment of the properties expeditiously and, in any event, within one week. The State submitted that adjudication proceedings had already been completed and recovery proceedings would be initiated. The High Court clarified that its order was confined to expiry of the provisional attachment after one year and would not prevent the Department from taking further recovery steps in accordance with law. Thus, the judgment distinguishes the automatic cessation of provisional attachment under Section 83(2) from the Department’s independent statutory power to initiate recovery proceedings after adjudication. The decision reinforces that GST authorities cannot continue provisional attachment beyond its statutory life merely because a formal communication of release has not been issued. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF BOMBAY HIGH COURT 1. As both the Petitions raise common issues, the same were taken up for consideration together. Common submissions were advanced and both the Petitions are being disposed of by the common order. 2. By the present Petition, the Petitioner challenges the provisional attachment orders passed by the Respondent No. 3 on the ground that the orders have ceased to have effect after the expiry of 1 year as per Section 83(2) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (for short “CGST Act”) and Maharashtra Goods and Services Tax, 2017 and its continuity are illegal and without authority of law. 3. It is undisputed that the provisional attachment of property under Section 83 was ordered by issuing form GST DRC-22 by which the agricultural and residential property and bank accounts came to be provisionally attached on 24th June, 2025. Considering the provisions of Section 83 of the CGST Act, the provisional attachment ceases to have effect after the expiry of the period of 1 year from the date of the order. In the present case, the provisional attachment ceased to have effect from 23rd June, 2026. However, no steps were taken by the Respondents to intimate the concerned authorities about the expiry of the provisional attachment by reason of which the Petitioners are constrained to approach this Court. 4. The statutory provision makes it clear that the provisional attachment has outlived its life, and therefore, have ceased to have effect. 5. In view of the statutory provision, the Petitions are allowed in terms of prayer clauses (a) and (b). 6. The Petitions are allowed as the continuity of the provisional attachment after lapse of 1 year is contrary to the statutory provisions. Respondent No. 3 to issue communications to the concerned authorities informing them about the expiry of the provisional attachment of the properties expeditiously, and in any event, within a period of 1 week. 7. Learned AGP submits that the adjudication is completed and recovery process will be initiated. The passing of the present order is in the context of the expiry of the provisional attachment and will not come in the way of the Respondents to take further steps in accordance with law.
