(Chhattisgarh High Court) Summary: The Chhattisgarh High Court partly allowed the appeal of Santosh Kumar Dubey, protecting his appointment as Skilled Attendant at Government Polytechnic, Durg after approximately ten years of service. Another applicant, Mohd. Farid Shams, had been denied marks for relevant experience because his formal experience certificate was not enclosed with the application. The Division Bench found that Shams deserved reconsideration, but that an innocent, long-serving employee should not be displaced because of the selection authority’s error. The recruitment began with an advertisement dated 7 February 2013. Shams disclosed that he had worked as a Laboratory Technician since 20 August 2008, but was placed at serial number 16 with 64.45 marks when his experience was not counted. His objection was rejected, while Dubey was selected on 30 August 2013 for the unreserved Skilled Attendant post. Shams challenged the selection in WPS No. 1551 of 2014. The Single Judge, by order dated 27 January 2023, directed a fresh interview and preparation of a revised merit list after considering his documents, including an appointment letter, confirmation letter and subsequently obtained experience certificate. Dubey argued that he had been duly selected, had the necessary qualifications and had committed no fraud, suppression or misrepresentation. He had served approximately ten years and had been promoted to Laboratory Technician on 26 February 2021. Shams maintained that the denial of experience marks was unjustified. His counsel undertook that, if he were appointed while Dubey was allowed to continue, he would not claim back wages or other consequential service benefits. The State pointed to the existence of only one unreserved Skilled Attendant vacancy. The Division Bench agreed that Shams’s experience should be reconsidered. It nevertheless held that setting aside Dubey’s appointment after ten years of faultless service would be inequitable, particularly after his promotion. The Court considered Rajesh Kumar Daria v. Rajasthan Public Service Commission and Pawan Kumar Agrawal v. Government of Chhattisgarh, where recruitment errors were addressed without unnecessarily displacing persons who had already served for years. The appeal was partly allowed. Dubey’s selection and appointment were protected, while the State was directed to reconsider Shams through an interview under the selection rules. If selected, Shams would be appointed against an available vacancy or a supernumerary Skilled Attendant post, without consequential benefits in accordance with his undertaking. Rajasthan Public Service Commission & Others (Supreme Court); (2007) 8 SCC 785 — Considered regarding correction of horizontal reservation errors without unnecessarily disturbing established appointments. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF CHHATTISGARH HIGH COURT Hon’ble Shri Justice Ramesh Sinha, Chief Justice; Hon’ble Shri Justice Naresh Kumar Chandravanshi Order on Board — Per Naresh Kumar Chandravanshi, Judge — 16.10.2023 Heard Mr. Siddhartha Tiwari, learned counsel appearing for the appellant, Mr. H. S. Ahluwalia, Deputy A.G. appearing for the State/respondents and Mr. Pragalbha Sharma, learned counsel appearing for the respondent No.4. 1. By way of this appeal filed under Section 2 of the Chhattisgarh High Court (Appeal to Division Bench Act, 2006), appellant has sought for the following relief:- “It is therefore, prayed that this Hon’ble Court may kindly be pleased to allow this writ appeal and may kindly quash/ set aside the order dated 27.01.2023 passed by the Hon’ble Single Bench in WP(S) No.1551/2014 and may pass any other relief as this Hon’ble Court may deem fit, in the interest of justice.” 2. Brief facts of the case are that pursuant to the advertisement dated 07.02.2013 issued by the Principal, Government Polytechnic, Durg (C.G.). The writ petitioner/respondent No.4 (herein) had submitted his application form for the post of Skilled Attendant and with regard to his experience, he had mentioned in his application that he is working as a Lab Technician in the Department of Mechanical Engineers at Disha Institute of Management & Technology, Raipur since 20.08.2008 till the date of submission of application form, however, he did not have an experience certificate. When the merit list was released by respondent No. 3, he came to know that he has placed at serial No.16 in the said list with only 64.45 marks. The respondent authorities have not allotted marks for the experience certificate to the writ petitioner/respondent No.4 (herein). Thereafter, the writ petitioner/respondent No.4 (herein) made an objection to the effect that he has not been awarded any marks for his experience, however, the same has been rejected vide memo dated 26.07.2013. It is stated that, when the writ petitioner/respondent No.4 (herein) applied for the same post at Government Polytechnic, Kanker, his experience of 4 ½ years was recognized. On 30.08.2013 the respondent authorities released the select list of candidates in all categories, wherein appellant (respondent No.4 in writ petition, henceforth referred as appellant) was subsequently selected vide impugned selection list dated 30.08.2013 for the post of Skilled Attendant (Refrigeration & Air Condition) under unreserved category, which was challenged by the writ petitioner/respondent No.4 (herein) by filing writ petition before the learned Single Bench bearing WPS No.1551/2014, which has been allowed vide impugned order dated 27.01.2023, against which the appellant has preferred the present appeal. 3. Learned counsel for the appellant submit that the order passed by the learned Single Judge is illegal, erronous and deserves to be set aside, as the appellant was selected on the basis of merit on the post of Skilled Attendant (R&AC) having all the requisite qualifications and even he was allotted maximum marks for his experience of more than 12 years, whereas the respondent No.4 herein, had not submitted the experience certificate at the time of filing application, as such he was rightly not allotted the marks for experience. The appellant has been working in the respondent Department since 30.08.2013 i.e. for about 10 years and he has been promoted also, whereas the respondent No.4’s writ petition has been allowed after about 10 years, which is illegal and accordingly relief granted to him deserves to be set aside. He further submits that if the respondent No.4 herein has not been allotted the marks for his experience, the same is not the fault of the appellant. The appellant has not obtained the aforesaid post by playing any fraud or suppressing any material fact or furnishing any fabricated document and he has been selected based upon his merits. Therefore, the impugned order passed by the learned Single Judge directing the respondent authorities to reconsider the petitioner’s case and reconduct the interview as per rules and again prepare the merit list, is liable to be set aside. 4. Learned counsel for the respondent No.4 (petitioner therein) opposes the submission made by the appellant’s counsel and submits that the respondent No.4 was wrongly denied the marks for having experience over the aforesaid post, whereas he possessed the requisite qualification as well as experience for the aforesaid post, thus impugned order does not suffers from any illegalities or infirmities. Therefore, the appeal may kindly be dismissed. However, he fairly submits that if the appellant is permitted to continue his service, considering his long period of service and no fault on his part and respondent No.4 (petitioner therein) is provided post of skilled attendant then learned counsel appearing for the respondent No.4 herein gave undertaking that his client (respondent No.4 herein) will not claim any back wages or other service benefit. 5. Learned State counsel submits that there is only one post of Skilled Attendant in the respondent Department in the unreserved category, therefore, it would be difficult for the Department to keep the respondent No.4 in service, as the appellant is already working on the aforesaid post. On being inquired, he informed the Court that appellant has been promoted to the post of Lab Technician from the post of Skilled Attendant. 6. We have heard learned counsel for the parties and perused the material carefully available on record. 7. The learned Single Bench vide order dated 27.01.2023 allowed the writ petition and after considering the facts of the case and various judgment of Apex Court has observed in para 10 and 11 as under:- “10. ……………it is clear from the application form of the candidate that he attached his appointment letter dated 19.08.2008 and further his confirmation letter dated 27.08.2009 and he obtained his experience certificate on 25.06.2013, undoubtedly the petitioner possessed the required experience certificate and as per rules of selection process he was entitled to experience certificate marks but the respondent authorities very rigidly rejected his objection on this ground that he has not filed his experience certificate with his application form. Applying the law laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court and the facts of this case the impugned order and selection is deserves to be set aside. 11. Accordingly, the writ petition is allowed. The impugned order dated 30.08.2013 (Annexure-P/10) and order dated 30.08.2013 (Annexure-P/11) are set aside. The respondent authorities are directed to re-conduct the interview as per selection rules and consider the petitioner’s case with his all certificates and again prepare the merit list within the period of three months from the receipt of copy of this order.” 8. In the case in hand, pursuant to the advertisement issued on 07.02.2013, the respondent No.4 herein had applied for the post of Skilled Attendant with requisite documents except the experience certificate, but he mentioned in his application that he has been working as Lab Technician in the Department of Mechanical Engineers at Disha Institute of Management and Technology, Kanker since 20.08.2008 till the date of submission of his application, but on account of non submission of experience certificate, he was not awarded marks for the experience and was not selected for the aforesaid post. The respondent No.4 filed his objection against the same and even filed experience certificate on 19.02.2013 praying that since he had already submitted an appointment letter dated 19.08.2008 issued by the Department of Mechanical Engineers at Disha Institute of Management and Technology, Kanker on the post of Lab Technician and confirmation letter dated 27.08.2009, as such he possessed the experience of more than 4 ½ years despite non submission of experience certificate and subsequently the experience certificate was also submitted, which was obtained on 25.06.2013, but despite the same his case was not considered, against which he filed a writ petition bearing WPS No.1551/2014 before the learned Single Bench and the learned Single Bench vide order dated 27.01.2023 allowed the writ petition and granted relief to the respondent No.4 herein, as has been mentioned in preceding paragraphs. 9. True it is that though, the petitioner had not submitted the experience certificate along with documents while applying for the aforesaid post, but since he was working with some other Department on the post of Lab Technician and subsequently submitted experience certificate, based on which the writ petition filed by the respondent No.4 was allowed, therefore, we do not find any illegality and infirmity in the impugned order warranting any interference of this Court. But the fact remains that the respondent No.4 was selected vide order dated 30.08.2013 on the aforesaid post and since then he has been working i.e., for about 10 years and also got promoted, he was having all the requisite qualifications at the time of submitting application and even possessed the experience certificate, as such he was awarded marks for having experience and was selected, thus the respondent No.4 is nowhere at fault. Therefore, we do not find it just and proper to remove him from his service after such a long period of about 10 years and that to without his fault. 10. In the case of Rajesh Kumar Daria v. Rajasthan Public Service Commission and others and other connected matters (supra) has granted relief to the persons, who have wrongly been selected by applying wrong method of reservation policy. In the aforesaid case, their Lordships of the Supreme Court have held as under :- “11. Similarly, we find that in regard to 24 posts for OBC, 19 candidates were selected by RPSC in accordance with merit from among OBC candidates which included three woman candidates. Thereafter, another five women were selected under the category of ‘OBC – Women’, instead of adding only two which was the shortfall. Thus there were in all 8 women candidates, among the 24 OBC candidates found in the Selection List. The proper course was to list 24 OBC candidates as per the merit and then find out number of woman candidates among them, and only fill the shortfall to make up the quota of five for woman. 12. The appellants’ grievance that the selection process adopted by RPSC was contrary to the reservations policy contained in Rule 9(3) is justified. But the question is whether the entire selection should be set aside and whether all appellants should be granted relief. On completion of the selection process, 97 candidates were appointed in the year 2002 and have been serving as Judicial Officers for more than five years. There has also been a subsequent selection and appointments in the year 2005. Further all the selected candidates are not impleaded as parties. Even from among the original ten writ petitioners, only seven are before us. On the facts and circumstances, we do not propose to disturb the selection list dated 30.12.2001 or interfere with the appointments already made in pursuance of it. We will only consider whether the appellants before us are entitled to relief. We find that even if the selection list had been prepared by applying horizontal reservation properly, only the appellant (Rajesh Kumar Daria) in this appeal, and appellant Nos.3 and 6 in the connected appeal (Mohan Lal Soni and Sunil Kumar Gupta) will get selected. The other appellants were not eligible to be selected.” 13. In view of the above and in view of available vacancies, we deem it just and proper to accommodate those three candidates without disturbing the selections and appointments already made, to do complete justice, in the following manner : 13.1. Sunil Kumar Gupta (general category candidate with 184 marks) and Mohan Lal Soni (OBC candidate with 169 marks), who ought to have been selected in the 2001 selection list, and who were denied appointment in view of excess selection of women candidates, shall be deemed to have been selected by RPSC. As a consequence, necessary letters of appointment shall be issued to them. Their seniority for all purposes will however be counted only from the date of actual appointment. 13.2. Rajesh Kumar Daria (OBC candidate with 171 marks) was also not selected because of the selection of excess women candidates. He ought to have been selected and appointed in the 2001 selection. We are told that Rajesh Kumar Daria got selected in the subsequent 2005 examination and was appointed in the Rajasthan Judicial Service on 12.2.2005. Considering the above fact, we direct that he should be given his position in the 2001 selection list. Interests of justice would be served if he is placed as the last candidate in the 2001 selection list. As he worked from 12.2.2005, we make it clear that such retrospective seniority will not entitle him to any monetary benefits, but will only be counted for promotions and pensionary benefits.” 11. In the matter of Pawan Kumar Agrawal & others v. Govt. of Chhattisgarh & others , Division Bench of this Court has also granted relief to the persons, who have been selected by erroneous interpretation of horizontal and compartment-wise reservation by respondent No. 2. In the aforesaid case, learned Division Bench has held as under :- “14. On the completion of selection process, the candidates were appointed in the year 2004 and have been serving as Judicial Officers for more than seven years. There have also been subsequent selection and appointments in the year 2006, 2008 and 2011. Respondent Nos. 4 and 5 have been serving as Judicial Officers since last more than seven years. In such a state of affairs, we are of the opinion that it would not be proper to set aside the selection and appointment of respondent Nos. 4 and 5 at this juncture for no fault of theirs. At the same time, it cannot be denied that the petitioners, who were entitled to be selected and should have been placed immediately below. Ku. Shraddha Shukla and above respondent Nos. 4 and 5 in the select list, have been wrongly deprived of their right to be appointed on the post of Civil Judge Class II by erroneous interpretation of horizontal and compartment-wise reservation by respondent No. 2. 15. Thus, in the totality of the facts and circumstances of the case, we deem it appropriate to direct respondent No. 1 to appoint the petitioners, subject to fulfillment of necessary formalities like police verification, etc. against the available vacancies for the post of Civil Judge (Junior Division). The seniority of the petitioners will, however, be reckoned from the date of their appointment. With the aforesaid observations, the petition is, accordingly, allowed. No order as to costs.” 12. Reverting into the instant case it is apparent from record that the appointment of appellant was made way back on 30.08.2013 and till disposal of the writ petition the appellant has worked in the department for about 10 years. Further in the instant writ appeal this court vide order dated 20.03.2023 has granted interim relief in favour of the appellant that the effect and operation of the impugned order dated 27.01.2023 passed by the learned single judge shall remain stayed till the next date of hearing. Therefore in light of the interim order passed by this court, the appellant is still working within the department. Further it has been intimated by the state counsel that the appellant has been promoted to the post of Laboratory Technician vide order dated 26.02.2021 and the said order has been placed on record by the state counsel. 13. It is worthy to note here that due to fault on the part of the selection committee of the respondent authorities, the respondent No-4 was deprived to get its fruits on time. That as per present scenario it will not proper to remove the appellant from post who had put its service with the department for about 10 years, therefore, in balance of equities and in the interest of justice, the writ appeal is partly allowed and set aside the finding of learned Single Judge with respect to the appellant, wherein his appointment order and selection was set aside by the learned Single Judge. 14. It will proper to direct the state respondent authorities to conduct the interview as per selection rules to consider the case of the respondent No-4 herein and, if he get selected, provide him appointment by accommodating in any vacant post of Skilled Attendant, if available, otherwise by creating one supernumerary post of Skilled Attendant. If respondent No-4 is selected he will not be entitled for any consequential benefits with respect to his appointment made in the post of Skilled Attendant, as undertaking has also been given by the counsel appearing for the respondent No.4 herein (petitioner). 15. The writ appeal stands allowed to the extent indicated hereinabove.
