(Gauhati High Court) Summary: The Gauhati High Court granted interim relief to Hindustan Construction Company Limited by staying a show cause notice dated 31 December 2025 issued by the Additional Director, Directorate General of GST Intelligence (DGGI), Lucknow Zonal Unit, under Section 74 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. The notice concerned FY 2020-21 and arose from payments received pursuant to an arbitration award and subsequent settlement agreement with the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) in respect of a road construction contract undertaken before the introduction of GST. The petitioner submitted that the impugned show cause notice had been issued on the last permissible date for initiating proceedings under Section 74 for FY 2020-21. It contended that invocation of Section 74 requires satisfaction of the proper officer that non-payment or short payment of tax, erroneous refund or wrongful availment or utilisation of input tax credit resulted from fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts with intent to evade tax. In the absence of these statutory ingredients, proceedings could, at best, be initiated under Section 73 of the CGST Act. The petitioner further submitted that receipt of the settlement amount had already been disclosed in Form GSTR-9, thereby undermining the allegation of non-disclosure and the basis for invoking Section 74. The underlying dispute related to a contract awarded for four-laning specified stretches of National Highway No. 54 between Maibang and Nrimbanglo in Assam. The original completion period was 30 months, and the contract was extended from time to time, with the final extension ending on 20 February 2017. Disputes subsequently arose between the petitioner and NHAI concerning additional expenditure incurred due to extension of the contract period and non-payment for certified excavation work involving hard rock. According to the petitioner, both claims related to the period preceding the implementation of GST. Arbitration proceedings culminated in an award dated 16 December 2017, under which the Arbitral Tribunal awarded Rs. 199,55,40,086 along with interest at 10% from 23 September 2013 and a further amount of Rs. 49,57,96,512 along with interest at 10%. Subsequently, considering its longstanding contractual relationship with NHAI, the petitioner entered into a settlement agreement on 14 August 2020, agreeing to reduce the amount payable. The petitioner contended that the amounts represented compensation or dues arising from contractual works completed before GST came into force and that receipt of the settlement amount had been duly disclosed in its annual GST return. The petitioner also relied upon the Supreme Court decision in M/s Tata Sons Limited Vs Union of India through Secretary, Ministry of Finance and Others, reported in 2026 (8) TMI 1587, to contend that a show cause notice under Section 74 cannot be sustained unless allegations of fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts with intent to evade tax are clearly made and substantiated. The petitioner submitted that the absence of such jurisdictional ingredients warranted interference with the notice at the threshold. The counsel representing the CGST Department sought an opportunity to file a response. After considering the submissions and examining the available materials, including the contents of the impugned show cause notice, Justice Devashis Baruah held that the petitioner had made out a case for interim protection. Accordingly, the High Court stayed the show cause notice dated 31 December 2025 until the next date of hearing, issued notice returnable on 27 November 2026 and directed the respondents to file their affidavits on or before that date. The order grants interim relief and does not finally determine the taxability of the arbitration settlement, the validity of the invocation of Section 74 or the petitioner’s ultimate GST liability. Cases Discussed M/s Tata Sons Limited Vs Union of India through Secretary, Ministry of Finance and Others (Supreme Court of India); 2026 (8) TMI 1587: Relied upon by the petitioner in support of the contention that initiation of proceedings under Section 74 of the CGST Act requires clear and substantiated allegations of fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression of facts to evade tax, and that a show cause notice lacking the necessary jurisdictional ingredients may be challenged at the threshold. The Gauhati High Court granted interim protection after considering the submissions and materials on record. Issue notice, making it returnable on 27.11.2026. 3. Mr. S Chakraborty, the learned counsel appears on behalf of the respondent No.1; Mr. B. Gogoi, the learned Addl.AG, Assam as well as the learned Standing Counsel, Finance & Taxation Department accepts notice on behalf of the respondent No. 2, and Mr. K. Jain, the learned counsel accepts notice on behalf of the respondent Nos.3, 4, and 5. 4. Extra copies of the writ petition be served upon the learned counsels for the respondents by tomorrow. 5. The present writ petition has been filed by the petitioner challenging the Show Cause notice dated 31.12.2025 issued by the respondent No.3 and seeking appropriate directions for setting aside the said show cause notice dated 31.12.2025. 6. Mr. B Raichandani, the learned Senior Counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner submitted that for the financial year 2020-21, the impugned Show Cause Notice was issued on 31.12.2025, which is the last day for issuance of a notice under Section 74 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (for short, ‘the CGST Act of 2017’). 7. The learned Senior Counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner submitted that for invocation of the provisions of Section 74 of the CGST Act of 2017, there has to be a satisfaction of the proper officer that any tax has not been paid, or short paid, or erroneously refunded, or input tax has been wrongly availed or utilized by reason of fraud, or any willful misstatement, or suppression of facts to evade tax. 8. The learned Senior Counsel submitted that if the ingredients of a fraud, willful misstatement, or suppression of facts to evade tax are missing, the proceedings can at best be a proceedings under Section 73 of the CGST Act of 2017. 9. The learned Senior Counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner drawing the attention of this Court to the Form GSTR-9, which is enclosed to the writ petition, submitted that the question of non-disclosure of the receipt of the settlement award does not arise inasmuch as, in the GSTR-9, the same has been disclosed. Under such circumstances, the three ingredients for initiation of action under Section 74 of the CGST Act of 2017 is not met in the present case. 10. The learned counsel appearing on behalf of the petitioner further submitted that it is surprising as to on what basis the proceedings under Section 74 of the CGST Act of 2017 has been initiated. A letter of acceptance was issued to the petitioner in respect to a contract for Construction for 4-Laning Road from km 126/450 to km 140/700 & km 164/080 to km 165/400 (Maibang to Nrimbanglo) Section of NH-54 in the State of Assam. 11. The period of completion was 30 months. The contract in question was extended from time to time. The last extension of the contract was only up to 20.02.2017. 12. A dispute arose between the petitioner and the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) on account of the additional cost which the petitioner incurred on account of the extension of the contract, and further the non-payment of the certification of the work done in respect of excavation in hard rock. 13. The learned counsel further submitted that on account of these two aspects, which were claims prior to the GST regime coming into effect, the arbitration proceedings were initiated. The Arbitral Tribunal, by an award dated 16.12.2017 directed that the petitioner is entitled to an amount of Rs.199,55,40,086/- along with interest @ 10% from 23.09.2013, and further directed payment of an amount of Rs.49,57,96,512/- along with interest @ 10%. 14. The learned Senior Counsel for the petitioner reiterated that these amounts which were awarded were prior to the GST regime coming into force and are at best compensation. Thereupon, taking into account that the petitioner had been a long-standing contractor under the National Highways Authority of India, a settlement agreement was entered into on 14.08.2020, whereby the petitioner agreed to reduce the said amount. Further to that the said disclosure were duly made in the GSTR-9 further. 15. The learned Senior Counsel, therefore, submitted that in the instant case, the question of invoking the provisions of the CGST Act of 2017 does not arise, inasmuch as the payment of the dues was in respect to the contract work which stood completed as on 20.02.2017, prior to the GST regime. 16. The learned Senior Counsel further referring to the recent judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of M/S Tata Sons Limited Vs. Union of India through Secretary, Ministry of Finance and Others, reported in 2026 (8) TMI 1587 submitted that the Supreme Court categorically observed that unless and until there are clear allegations of fraud, willful misstatement, or suppression of facts to evade tax, and such allegations are substantiated, the Show Cause Notice under Section 74 of CGST Act of 2017 cannot be permitted to be issued, and such show cause notice can be set aside at the threshold. 17. Mr. SC Keyal, the learned Senior Counsel appearing on behalf of the CGST Department submitted that he would file his response. 18. This Court, having taken into consideration the respective submissions and having perused the materials on record, including the contents of the Show Cause Notice is of the opinion that the petitioner has been able to make out a case for staying the Show Cause proceedings. 19. Accordingly, this Court, therefore, stays the impugned Show Cause Notice dated 31.12.2025 till the next date. 20. List accordingly. 21. Affidavits of the respondents be filed on or before the next date.
