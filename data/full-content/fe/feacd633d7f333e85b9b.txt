Articles 2 min read World Bank warns of fresh food-price shocks World Bank economists indicate that global food commodity prices may exceed their 2022 levels due to multiple risk factors. These factors include rising production costs, extreme weather, and geopolitical conflicts, which could impact food supply. The edible-oil sector in India, heavily reliant on imports, faces heightened risks from these global price fluctuations. Future projections suggest that grain and oil prices are likely to increase significantly over the next few years. Advt The warning accompanied their analysis, 'Overlapping shocks: Risks to global food markets in 2027'. It builds on the World Bank's October Commodity Markets Outlook rather than announcing a new forecast.That outlook projects the Bank's global food commodity price index will rise by about 10 per cent in 2026 and another 7 per cent in 2027, before declining by about 5 per cent in 2028.Its forecast tables show grain prices rising by approximately 14 per cent this year and 9 per cent next year. The oils and meals index is projected to increase by about 17 per cent and 8 per cent, respectively.These are international commodity-price projections, not forecasts of equivalent increases in Indian grocery bills.For India, edible oils provide a direct connection to the global outlook. A December 2025 government backgrounder put the country's import dependence at 56.25 per cent of edible-oil consumption in 2023-24.Reliance on overseas supplies exposed Indian consumers to international price fluctuations and supply disruptions, it said. It also described government efforts to expand domestic oil-palm and oilseed production.In a separate analysis published on November 12, the World Bank economists said global edible-oil supplies were expected to grow by 2.7 million tonnes in 2026-27, less than half the long-term average annual increase of 5.7 million tonnes. Advt At the same time, demand for soybean and palm oil for biofuels was increasing, supported by high energy prices and policies in the United States and Indonesia, they said.The economists also warned that declining inventories would leave food markets more vulnerable to weather and trade disruptions.Their earlier analysis outlined a scenario in which prolonged conflict and stronger El Nino effects could push the food price index 12 per cent above its baseline forecast for 2027.
