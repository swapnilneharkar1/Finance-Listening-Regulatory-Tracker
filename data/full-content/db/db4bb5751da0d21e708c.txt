Articles 4 min read Go public or stay private: Tata Sons gears up for decision this week Board split on RBI prod for listing Reeba Zachariah TNN Published On Nov 23, 2026 at 07:45 AM IST var news_msid = 136087079; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook MUMBAI: Tata Sons is heading into one of its most consequential weeks in decades. When its board meets on November 26, it will confront a choice that could determine whether the Tata Group's closely held holding company remains private or opens its doors to the public.It has to pick between two competing paths: RBI's Nov 16 letter reiterating that Tata Sons must comply with rules governing upper-layer non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) to list its shares, and Tata Trusts' Sept 28 proposal to merge two companies into Tata Sons, which would take the combined entity outside the NBFC and core investment company (CIC) framework and avoid a listing. Advt The six-member board is already divided, with Trusts chairman Noel Tata, a director of Tata Sons, opposed to an IPO while the other five directors favour compliance with RBI's listing requirement. The divide extends to Trusts, where trustees including Noel's older half-brother Jimmy Tata, Venu Srinivasan and Vijay Singh, are in favour of an IPO.RBI's latest missive and Trusts' merger plan both followed the dramatic Sept 17 Tata Sons board meet, where almost all directors argued in favour of compliance.Noel, however, maintained that compliance need not mean a listing and the company should examine alternatives.The RBI letter makes no reference to the merger proposal, but it refers to the Tata Sons board meeting and says it understood the company's Oct 6 request for guidance on listing. Noel has questioned whether Tata Sons has sought the RBI's guidance on his merger proposal and asked the company to evaluate it and present its pros and cons to the board this week.Senior advocate Harish Salve who advises Tata Sons and chairman N Chandrasekaran has publicly endorsed a listing. "If the RBI says go list yourself, there is no use saying my shareholders don't agree," he said, maintaining that going public would bring greater transparency and prevent the company from being paralysed by disagreements among Tata Trusts trustees. Advt Senior advocate Abhishek Manu Singhvi, representing Trusts, has argued for their primacy as Tata Sons' controlling shareholder. He said the company cannot have a "runaway board" taking major decisions in disagreement with the Trusts. Tata Sons is the only company among the relevant upper-layer NBFCs to remain unlisted.If both items are put to a vote, the outcome is expected to be 5:1 in each case, but with Noel being in a minority: voting against an IPO and in favour of the Trusts' merger proposal. That, however, would reopen the dispute over Article 121 of Tata Sons' Articles of Association.Noel's position is that a resolution under Article 121 must secure the affirmative vote of a majority of Trust-nominated directors. With Noel and Srinivasan as the two Trust nominees, the Trusts contend that both must vote in favour. Tata Sons disputes this interpretation, maintaining that if the two Trust nominees vote in opposite directions, Article 121 allows the chairman of the meeting to exercise a casting vote to break the tie. It relied on precisely that interpretation at the Sept 17 meeting, when Noel opposed Chandrasekaran's reappointment and Srinivasan supported it. Advt Also on Thursday's agenda is the Shapoorji Pallonji (SP) Group's proposal to monetise part of its 18.4% holding in Tata Sons for Rs 25,000 crore, a transaction that would alter the holding company's shareholding structure. Noel has proposed that it be implemented through a selective reduction of capital, with the shares valued under rule 11UA of the Income Tax Rules. Chandrasekaran is expected to present the board with an assessment of how Tata Sons would fund the payout, including through internal cash flows, the sale of listed investments or stake sales in newer businesses.The listing, merger and SP proposals are among seven items on the agenda. Others include dematerialisation of Tata Sons shares, including holdings of Trusts and Ratan Tata; consideration of the company's second-quarter FY27 results; business reviews of Agratas (battery venture), Tata AIG General Insurance, Tejas Networks (telecom equipment) and Air India; and a review of Tata Sons' guarantees and investments. Advt But first the board will take up disputed minutes of Sept 17 meeting. Noel expects his objections, including his dissent over Chandrasekaran's reappointment and his interpretation of Article 121 to be reflected in the final record. Once final minutes are signed by Chandrasekaran, the board will turn to the proposals, with India Inc watching closely.
