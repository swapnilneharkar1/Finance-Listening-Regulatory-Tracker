Articles 3 min read How a digital bank's collapse toppled Paraguay's president Santiago Pena Paraguay's digital bank ueno bank has collapsed, leading to the freezing of nearly three million customer accounts. This event has resulted in an impeachment of President Santiago Pena, who is now facing serious charges. The central bank had previously put ueno into resolution after discovering breaches of solvency requirements. Public protests erupted, reflecting widespread anger over the government's handling of the crisis. Advt Authorities quickly arrested Pena, an economist and close ally of US President Donald Trump, and prosecutors charged him on Saturday with breach of trust and administration for personal gain. A judge ordered him held in pretrial detention at a military facility.Pena has denied wrongdoing and his attorney, Ricardo Preda, told journalists that he was pleading not guilty to the charges.The scandal has triggered public protests and sharp rebukes from the opposition."We are facing the biggest corruption scandal in our democratic history," said opposition Senator Rafael Filizzola on Friday. "This impeachment is a first step toward ensuring justice is served."Manuel Ferreira, a Paraguayan economist and former finance minister, echoed much of the public anger over the case. "This was a modern-day Bonnie and Clyde," he said. "They didn't rob us with a pistol, they robbed us with an app. The head of the gang was the president of the republic."Also read: Paraguay's ousted president faces prosecutors after his removal over bank collapseCENTRAL BANK CHIEF ALSO CHARGEDUntil March 2025, Pena had been a business partner of the group that owns ueno. After his election in 2023, he tapped ueno's former president, Carlos Carvallo, to lead the central bank.Carvallo resigned as central bank chief earlier this week. Prosecutors charged him on Saturday with breach of trust, producing false public documents and administration for personal gain. They sought his pretrial detention, which was approved by a judge. Advt Carvallo did not respond to a request for comment. It was not immediately clear if he had entered a plea in the case.Pena's lawyer, Preda, objected to the impeachment, saying that ousting a president during a bank resolution could itself be destabilizing."What sets a serious country apart is not that its banks don't fail, but how it responds," Preda said on Friday. "If there is one thing that undermines confidence in the financial system, it is attempting to oust the president without evidence."Ueno was launched in 2021 as a sleek, millennial-friendly banking app that quickly won over Paraguayans who had been underserved by brick-and-mortar institutions. Advt It soon became deeply embedded in Paraguay's payments system. It paired its digital offering with physical branches, later sponsoring sports teams, businesses and events. As of June, public institutions - including Paraguay's state pension fund - held roughly $850 million in accounts with the bank.But in recent months, internal regulatory reports warning of excessive lending to certain companies were leaked to local media outlets ABC and Ultima Hora, triggering political scrutiny of the bank's lending practices.Following growing public pressure, Paraguay's central bank on Tuesday put ueno into resolution - its strongest measure ‌short of immediate liquidation - after determining that ueno had breached minimum solvency requirements, locking ​millions of customers temporarily out of their accounts. Advt The central bank said on Saturday that the equivalent of $160 million had since been paid out to nearly 1.2 million savers under the state deposit guarantee scheme.Authorities have promised that depositors will be reimbursed and say the crisis is confined to ueno. But analysts say the damage to Paraguay's reputation as a stable investment destination in South America may linger far longer than the bank resolution itself."This is the moment when Paraguay must demonstrate that its institutions are functioning," Ferreira said.
