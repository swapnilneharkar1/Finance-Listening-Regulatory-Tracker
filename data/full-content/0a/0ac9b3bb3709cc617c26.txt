Articles 2 min read Rupee reversal, debt supply drag India bonds in holiday-shortened week Indian government bonds fell after the rupee lost its positive momentum against the dollar on Monday. A recent debt sale raised concerns about supply when the Indian states auctioned bonds at higher yields. The rupee also ended down 0.3%, reflecting increased dollar demand from large corporations. Benchmark Brent crude oil prices remained close to $92 a barrel, raising inflation concerns in India. Indian debt markets will be closed on Tuesday for a local holiday.Indian states raised around 297 billion rupees ($3.20 billion) through sale of debt at Monday's auction, at yields which were slightly above estimates, indicating weaker demand.Last week, the 10-year bond yield dropped 12 basis points, a decline that continued early in the day on Monday to as low as 7.1678% before it reversed direction. Advt The Indian rupee snapped a five-session winning streak, hitting bond market sentiment, as dollar demand from large companies picked up and support from a rally in Indian stocks faded. The currency ended down 0.3% at 92.91.Benchmark Brent crude oil prices held near $92 a barrel, with focus remaining on risks of escalation between the US and Iran. High oil prices pose risks for major importer India, threatening to widen its current account deficit, weaken the rupee and accelerate inflation.Investors are also focusing on the Reserve Bank of India's policy decision due on December 4. A majority of economists surveyed by Reuters expect the central bank to raise its policy repo rate by 25 basis points."Considering the RBI's stance and prevailing global oil situation, we expect RBI to adopt calibrated tightening of 25 bps each in upcoming policies in December and February," said Prashant Pimple, fixed income CIO at Baroda BNP Paribas Mutual Fund.RATESOvernight index swap rates ended marginally higher in a shallow trading session.The one-year rate ended at 6.47%, while the two-year rate ended at 6.435% and the liquid five-year rate ended at 6.45%.
