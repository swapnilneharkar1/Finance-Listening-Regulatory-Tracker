The delay has left payment companies dependent on government support even as transaction volumes, regulatory compliance requirements and cybersecurity costs rise.The Payments Council of India (PCI), which represents digital payments companies, has raised concerns with the government over the delay in implementing MDR and the unpaid incentives. The proposed rollout, initially scheduled for October 15, was deferred until after Diwali to avoid imposing additional costs on merchants during the festive season. However, no fresh implementation date had been announced. Advt The Centre had raised the outlay for the digital payments incentive scheme to Rs 2,196.21 crore in the revised estimates for FY26, from the initial Budget estimate of Rs 437 crore. It has provided Rs 2,000 crore for the scheme in FY27. However, fintech firms are yet to receive incentives for the past six quarters, according to industry sources. Some payment companies have also recognised the incentives in their accounts before receiving the funds.The proposed MDR framework is intended to provide a revenue stream for participants in the payments ecosystem, including banks, payment service providers and UPI application providers. Under the framework announced in September, eligible person-to-merchant (P2M) transactions above Rs 2,000 would attract a 0.4% MDR, capped at Rs 300 for transactions of Rs 75,000 and above. Specified transactions above Rs 2,000 in sectors such as railways, telecom, insurance, fuel and agricultural inputs would attract a flat fee of Rs 5. Capital-market transactions, including payments relating to mutual funds, securities, stockbrokers and dealers, would attract an MDR of 0.02%, capped at Rs 300.The proposed charges would apply to only about 4% of merchant transactions, with around 96% remaining unaffected. Payments below Rs 2,000 and transactions covered under the zero-MDR framework for small merchants would continue to remain free. Person-to-person transfers would also remain outside the framework. The Finance Ministry has clarified that customers should not bear the MDR, with banks advised to ensure that merchants do not pass on the charge.The challengeThe uncertainty over MDR comes as the UPI network continues to expand rapidly. It processed a record 25.47 billion transactions worth Rs 31.53 lakh crore in October, with transaction volumes rising 23% year on year and transaction value increasing 16%. The growing scale of the network has increased the need for investments in payment infrastructure, merchant onboarding, customer servicing and fraud prevention. Advt NPCI has also introduced the UPI Vyapaar Saksham scheme to expand payment acceptance among small merchants, particularly in Tier 3 to Tier 6 centres, northeastern states, Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. The scheme provides for deployment of physical QR codes and soundboxes, along with transaction-linked incentives to encourage sustained usage. It will run for three years from the implementation of the MDR framework, linking the expansion of merchant acceptance infrastructure to the rollout of the new revenue structure.The delay in implementing MDR has complicated the industry's efforts to reduce dependence on government support. Payment companies had expected the proposed fee to create a more predictable revenue stream, allowing them to recover part of the costs of maintaining the digital payments network. The prolonged uncertainty could affect investment decisions, particularly in smaller towns and rural areas where merchant acceptance infrastructure still needs to expand. Advt The challenge for policymakers is to balance the financial sustainability of the payments ecosystem with the objective of keeping digital transactions affordable and encouraging wider adoption. While the proposed MDR framework seeks to generate revenue from larger merchant transactions without affecting most everyday payments, its delayed implementation and the backlog of government incentives have left payment companies without clarity on when a more sustainable revenue model will take shape.
