The deficit was equivalent to 15.9 per cent of total receipts.The RBI said, "An average surplus of 5.4 per cent of total receipts was recorded during 2022-23 to 2023-24." The report tracks the same 2.25 lakh Gram Panchayats across 25 states over three years. The figures for 2024-25 are provisional. Advt Central grants recorded by the Gram Panchayats in the study fell to Rs 27,226 crore in 2024-25 from Rs 35,398 crore a year earlier, accounting for most of the decline in receipts. State grants fell to Rs 10,507 crore from Rs 10,929 crore."Grants, particularly those from the Centre, remain the primary driver of local development financing," the report said.Grants accounted for 89.1 per cent of total receipts in 2024-25. Revenue raised by the Gram Panchayats themselves rose to Rs 4,738 crore from Rs 4,556 crore, but made up only 10.9 per cent of receipts. Non-tax sources, including interest receipts and user charges, brought in more than twice as much as local taxes."Given the dominance of intergovernmental transfers in Gram Panchayat finances, greater predictability in the flow of funds remains critical," the RBI said.The report said the Sixteenth Finance Commission recommended Rs 4.35 lakh crore in grants for rural local bodies for 2026-31, up 83.8 per cent from the previous commission's recommendation. Part of the funding will be linked to how much revenue Panchayats raise on their own from 2028-29. The RBI pointed to updated property and asset registers and better collection of local taxes and user charges as ways to strengthen those revenues.
