(Madras High Court) Summary — The Madras High Court dismissed the Revenue’s appeal against the Tribunal’s direction to the Assessing Officer to recompute the deduction under Section 10A of the Income-tax Act, 1961 by excluding freight and insurance expenses from both export turnover and total turnover. The Revenue questioned whether the Tribunal could exclude those expenses from total turnover when Explanation 2(iv) specifically defines export turnover. The Tribunal’s direction followed the Chennai ITAT Special Bench decision in ITO v. Sak Soft Ltd. Before the High Court, counsel for both sides agreed that the Supreme Court’s ruling in CIT v. HCL Technologies Ltd. covered the issue. The High Court accordingly answered both substantial questions of law in favour of the assessee and dismissed the appeal without costs. Cases Discussed Commissioner of Income-Tax, Central–III, New Delhi v. HCL Technologies Ltd. — (2018) 404 ITR 719 (SC), Civil Appeal Nos. 8489–8490 of 2013 and connected appeals, decided on 24 April 2018 (Supreme Court). The High Court applied this ruling after both sides accepted that it governed the exclusion of the specified expenses from both export turnover and total turnover under Section 10A. Income-tax Officer, Company Ward-VI(1), Chennai v. Sak Soft Ltd. — (2009) 313 ITR (AT) 353, IT Appeal Nos. 691, 1953 and 2477 (Mad.) of 2007 and 175, 500, 1130, 1138 and 1139 (Mad.) of 2008, decided on 6 March 2009 (ITAT Chennai Special Bench). The Tribunal relied on this Special Bench decision, which addressed corresponding exclusions from export turnover and total turnover under Section 10B. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF MADRAS HIGH COURT The substantial questions arising for consideration on 01.02.2016 in this appeal is as follows:- (i) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case, the Tribunal was right in directing the Assessing Officer to recompute the income in the light of the decision of the Special Bench in the case of Sak Foft by excluding the freight and insurance expenses both from the export turnover and also from the total turnover while computing deduction under Section 10A of the Income Tax Act ? and (ii) Is not the finding of the Tribunal bad especially when Explanation 2(iv). to Section 10A defines the word ‘Export – Turnover’ whereby it had been clearly stated that it would not include freight, telecommunication charges attributable to the delivery of the articles or things or computer software outside India or expenses, if any, incurred in foreign exchange while computing deduction under Section 10A of the Income Tax Act? Both Mrs.V.Pushpa, learned Senior Standing Counsel appearing for appellant and Ms.Samyuktha Banusekar, learned counsel appearing for respondent / assessee accede to the position that the issues has been covered by the judgment of the Supreme Court in Commissioner of Income-Tax, Central – III v HCL Technologies Limited1 . Hence the substantial questions are answered in favour of the assessee and adverse to the revenue. 3. This Tax Case (Appeal) is dismissed. No costs.
