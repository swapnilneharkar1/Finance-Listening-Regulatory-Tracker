Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Nathaneil Junior Education Society Vs PCIT (Central) (ITAT Delhi) Summary: The Delhi Bench of the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), in Nathaneil Junior Education Society Vs PCIT (Central), allowed six connected appeals challenging orders dated 30 December 2024 cancelling registrations granted to educational and charitable institutions under Section 12A read with Sections 12AA and 12AB(4) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The Tribunal followed its coordinate Bench decisions holding that the PCIT (Central) lacked jurisdiction to cancel the registrations in the circumstances considered and that the provisions relating to specified violations introduced by the Finance Act, 2022 could not be applied retrospectively. The appeals were filed by Nathaneil Junior Education Society, Divine Education Charitable Trust, Presidium Education & Charitable Trust, Adarsh Shiv Shakti Shiksha Samiti, Shakuntalam Education Society and Sh. Narendra Charitable Trust. Since identical issues were involved, the Tribunal took ITA No. 214/Del/2025 concerning Nathaneil Junior Education Society as the lead appeal and held that its decision would apply mutatis mutandis to the remaining appeals, subject to variations in figures. The assessee challenged the cancellation order on several legal grounds, including limitation, inadequate opportunity of hearing, denial of cross-examination, absence of jurisdiction and retrospective application of Section 12AB(4). It disputed the conclusion of the PCIT (Central) that its activities were not genuine or were not being carried out in accordance with its stated objects. The assessee also contended that jurisdiction over registration and cancellation continued to vest with the authority responsible for exemptions notwithstanding centralisation of assessment proceedings. Both parties agreed before the Tribunal that the issues were covered by the coordinate Bench decision in Lala Sher Singh Memorial Jeevan Vigyan Trust Society Vs PCIT (Central) and connected appeals involving Jindal Charitable Society and Florence Nightingale Educational Society, decided on 16 June 2025. Consequently, the Tribunal reproduced the operative portion of that decision while adjudicating the present appeals. The earlier common order concerned cancellation proceedings initiated following a search and seizure operation conducted on 24 August 2022 involving the Mother’s Pride, Presidium and Prudence groups. The Department alleged diversion of funds, unexplained investments and other irregularities involving trusts and societies operating educational institutions. In those proceedings, the concerned cases had been centralised under Section 127, and the Assessing Officer had referred the matter to the PCIT (Central) for cancellation of registration under Section 12AB(4). The jurisdictional controversy centred on CBDT Notification No. 52/2014 dated 22 October 2014 and Notification No. 53/2014. These notifications were relied upon to establish the jurisdiction of Commissioners of Income Tax (Exemptions) over specified classes of assessees claiming exemptions. The coordinate Bench had held that transferring assessment jurisdiction for coordinated post-search investigation did not automatically transfer the distinct statutory power to grant or cancel charitable registration. The Tribunal referred to Meenakshi Foundation Vs PCIT, wherein the coordinate Bench held that the power to cancel registration vested with the CIT (Exemptions) and not the PCIT (Central) in the circumstances considered. That decision had followed Lakhmi Chand Charitable Society Vs PCIT, which examined the validity of references made under the second proviso to Section 143(3) and the retrospective invocation of Section 12AB(4). In Lakhmi Chand Charitable Society, the Tribunal had observed that a reference under the second proviso to Section 143(3) was required to be made during the pendency of assessment proceedings so that the Assessing Officer could give effect to the registration authority’s decision while completing the assessment. A reference made after completion of assessment proceedings, particularly where the enabling provision was not in force at the relevant time, could not sustain the cancellation proceedings in that case. The coordinate Bench had also relied upon Aggarwal Vidya Pracharni Sabha Vs PCIT, which examined the distinction between transfer of assessment jurisdiction under Section 127 and the special jurisdiction vested in the CIT (Exemptions). The decision held that an order transferring a case between Assessing Officers for assessment purposes did not, by itself, transfer the powers of the Commissioner of Income Tax (Exemptions) relating to cancellation of registration. The Tribunal further considered the effect of Section 12AA(5), under which the provisions of Section 12AA ceased to apply on or after 1 April 2021, and the amendments to Section 12AB(4) introduced by the Finance Act, 2022 with effect from 1 April 2022. The earlier decisions had held that cancellation proceedings invoking the discontinued statutory provisions or applying the newly introduced specified violations retrospectively to earlier assessment years were legally unsustainable in the cases considered. Reliance was also placed upon Islamic Academy of Education Vs PCIT (Central), wherein the Bangalore Bench examined cancellation of registration under the amended statutory framework and held that violations pertaining to an earlier period could not automatically justify cancellation of registration granted for subsequent assessment years. The decision emphasised consideration of the applicable statutory provisions and relevant violations independently. The earlier common order additionally relied upon Human Welfare Foundation Vs DCIT (Exemptions), reported in 174 taxmann.com 650, which disapproved retrospective cancellation of registration under Section 12AB(4). Reference was made to CBDT Circular No. 11 of 2022 dated 3 June 2022, explaining the amended statutory framework relating to specified violations and cancellation of registration or approval. The coordinate Bench in Lala Sher Singh Memorial had concluded that the expression “specified violation” and the consequential withdrawal mechanism introduced by the Finance Act, 2022 with effect from 1 April 2022 were applicable from Assessment Year 2023-24 onwards and could not be retrospectively applied. It also referred to paragraph 9.3.3 of CBDT Circular No. 23/2022 dated 15 November 2022 in support of its interpretation. Respectfully following the aforesaid common order, the Delhi ITAT allowed all six appeals in the present proceedings. The decision rested on the legal issues concerning jurisdiction and retrospective cancellation of registration as addressed in the applicable coordinate Bench precedents. The Tribunal did not separately adjudicate the factual allegations against each educational institution or independently determine the remaining grounds relating to limitation, cross-examination and natural justice. While dealing with this particular aspect of the matter the Bench has been pleased to observe as follows: “8.1.9 registration Before the amendment by the Finance Act, 2022, Section 12AB(4) provided for cancellation of registration in case of any violation under Section 13. The amended Section 12AB(4) does not consider a violation of Section 13(1)(c) and Section 13(1)(d) as specified violations. Consequently, the registration cannot be cancelled on the ground that the assessee has violated Section 13(1)(c) or Section 13(1)(d). 8.1.10 The Finance Act 2023 has inserted clause (g) in Explanation to Section 12AB(4) to provide that giving incomplete, false, or inaccurate information in a registration application under Section 12A(1)(ac) will be deemed as a “specified violation” that can lead to the cancellation of registration. 8.2 Thus, it means that the following registration could be cancelled: 8.2.1 The PCIT/CIT can cancel the following registrations granted to a trust or institution: (a) Final registration or provisional registration granted under section 12AB(1)(a)/(b)/(c); (b) Final registration granted under section 12AA(1). The erstwhile provision did not cover cases of provisional registration granted under section 12AB(1)(c). Now, the provisional registration granted for the first time can also be cancelled by the authorities. 8.3 As seen from the above, since the assessee has secured the registration u/s 12A of the Act dated 4.6.1992, which was effective till the date of 23.9.2021 and this registration granted u/s 12A cannot be cancelled u/s 12AB(4)(ii) of the Act for the previous year 2020-21 covering the assessment year 2021-22. On the other hand, he could cancel the registration from assessment year 2022-23 onwards u/s 12AB(4)(ii) of the Act. In our opinion, if there is any violation in the previous assessment year 2020-21 relating to the assessment year2021-22, this cannot be reason to cancel the registration granted for the assessment year 2022-23 to 2026-27 as the assumption of jurisdiction u/s 12AB(4)(ii) of the Act is itself wrong on the reasons discussed herein above. The specific violation committed by the assessee in any of these assessment years is to be considered independently and not the violation committed in assessment year 2021-22 for cancelling the registration granted u/s 12AB of the Act for the assessment year 2022-23 to 2026-27. In view of the above discussions and considering the amendment in section 12AB(4) made by Finance Act, 2022, CBDT circular No. 11 of 2022 and the judicial pronouncements of various Hon’ble high courts and co-ordinate benches of tribunal, we are of the view that the registration granted u/s 12A cannot be cancelled with retrospective effect from AY 2018-19 when the CIT was granted power to cancel w.e.f. 1.4.2022, thus the same can be done from AY 2022-23.
