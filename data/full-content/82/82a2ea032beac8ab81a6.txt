CompaniesBombay HC Sets Aside ICICI Bank's Sanction To Prosecute Former CEO Chanda KochharAuthored by: Vikas KumarUpdated: Nov 18, 2026, 13:56 ISTKochhar contended in her plea said that the sanction granted by the bank under Section 19 of the Prevention of Corruption Act is contradictory to its initial stand.Chanda Kochhar (File Photo)Photo : REUTERSfollow usfollow usThe Bombay High Court has set aside the sanction granted by ICICI Bank to the CBI to prosecute its former chief executive Chanda Kochhar in the alleged ICICI Bank-Videocon loan fraud case, according to a LiveLaw report. The development marks a significant legal setback for the probe into the controversial loan dealings and raises fresh questions over the validity of the prosecution sanction against the former bank chief, who has been accused of alleged irregularities in the lending of loans to the Videocon Group.Single-judge Justice Milind Jadhav pronounced the order on a plea filed by Kochhar challenging the sanction granted by the ICICI Bank to prosecute her, the report said. Kochhar contended in her plea through senior advocate Amit Desai that the sanction granted by the bank under Section 19 of the Prevention of Corruption Act is contradictory to its initial stand that the bank did not suffer any loss from the loans granted during the period between June 2009 to October 2011 and that the loans were as per the then existing policies.The ICICI Bank–Videocon loan case revolves around allegations of quid pro quo, conflict of interest and irregularities in loans sanctioned by ICICI Bank to the Videocon Group when Chanda Kochhar was heading the bank.The controversy centred on allegations that loans extended to Videocon Group companies by ICICI Bank were linked to financial benefits allegedly received by companies associated with Kochhar's husband, Deepak Kochhar. The case triggered investigations by the CBI and Enforcement Directorate (ED), and became one of India's most high-profile corporate banking controversies.The CBI said that these loans were later termed as non-performing assets resulting in wrongful loss to ICICI bank and wrongful gain to the borrowers and accused persons.
