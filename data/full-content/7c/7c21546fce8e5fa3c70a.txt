(Supreme Court of India) Summary : The Supreme Court condoned the delay, declined to interfere with the High Court’s judgment in the facts and circumstances of the case, dismissed the special leave petition and disposed of pending applications. The statutory analysis below comes from the High Court judgment. The dispute concerned services supplied by the developer of the GMR Aviation SEZ to GMR Aerospace Engineering, an approved co-developer and unit providing aircraft maintenance, repair and overhaul facilities. Under a sub-lease dated 1 June 2010, the developer leased 24.55 acres to the unit and supplied electricity and water for commercial operations. The Development Commissioner had certified the exemption for services consumed within the SEZ for authorised operations. The developer invoiced the services without charging service tax. The Board of Approval rejected the request for retrospective issue of Forms A1 and A2 on 8 March 2017. After a show-cause notice dated 12 April 2017 and an earlier writ order directing consideration after a personal hearing, the Commissioner’s Order-in-Original dated 20 February 2018 confirmed the service tax demand under Section 73(2) of the Finance Act, 1994 for October 2011 to March 2016 and imposed penalties under Sections 77 and 78. The petitioners argued that Section 26(1)(e) of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 granted exemption for taxable services provided to a developer or unit for authorised operations, subject to the conditions prescribed under Section 26(2) and Rule 22 of the SEZ Rules, 2006. They relied on the overriding effect of Section 51. The Department invoked the statutory appeal remedy and maintained that the SEZ legislation was not a self-contained code, that the Finance Act notifications were consistent with it and that Forms A1 and A2 were mandatory. The High Court treated the alternative remedy as a self-imposed restraint under Article 226 and examined the challenge to the Commissioner’s jurisdiction and the legal authority for applying the notification conditions. The High Court distinguished Section 7, which concerns enactments listed in the First Schedule and does not cover the Finance Act, from Section 26, which expressly provides the service tax exemption, and Section 50, which contemplates state legislation for state tax relief. Reading Section 26(2) with the definition of “prescribed” in Section 2(w), it held that the manner and conditions of exemption were governed by rules made under the SEZ Act. Compliance with Rule 22 was undisputed, and that rule did not require Forms A1 and A2. Section 93 of the Finance Act conferred a general exemption power and its notifications could not add conditions to the specific SEZ exemption. Applying Girnar Traders, the High Court found that Section 26 and the SEZ Rules supplied a complete framework for determining eligibility, covered duties, qualifying operations and exemption conditions. The Department also relied on Circular No. 105/08/2008 dated 16 September 2008 and Rule 47(5), inserted in 2016, to argue that the SEZ regime depended on machinery under other enactments. The High Court held that Rule 47(5) addressed refund, demand, adjudication, review and appeal in matters concerning unauthorised operations, leaving the exemption framework under Rules 22 and 31 intact. It allowed the writ petition, set aside the Order-in-Original and set aside the notifications dated 1 March 2011, 20 June 2012 and 1 July 2013 insofar as they related to SEZs. It made no order as to costs and closed pending miscellaneous petitions. Cases Discussed AIR 1956 SC 604 — Ravula Subba Rao v. Commissioner of Income Tax, Madras (Supreme Court). The Department cited the decision on whether the Income Tax Act’s registration requirements excluded reliance on the Powers of Attorney Act. (2011) 3 SCC 1 — Girnar Traders (3) v. State of Maharashtra (Supreme Court). The High Court applied its test for determining whether legislation provides a self-contained framework. W.A. Nos. 1118 and 1119 of 2015, decided 12 April 2016 — Nokia India Sales Pvt. Ltd. v. Assistant Commissioner (CT), Sriperumbudur Assessment Circle (Madras High Court). The High Court drew on its description of the scheme of the SEZ Act and the scope of Section 53. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER Delay condoned. In the facts and circumstances of the present case, we see no reason to interfere with the impugned judgment and order of the High Court. The Special Leave Petition is accordingly dismissed. Pending applications, if any, are disposed of.
