The campaign, designated Crafty Falcon by the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In), targets non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), digital lenders, fintech platforms, payment aggregators and cryptocurrency exchanges. Its most alarming feature is that attackers are attempting to use financial institutions' own digital payment systems to move money, bypassing checks designed to ensure that transactions are genuine and authorised. Advt What is Crafty Falcon and why has it raised alarm?Crafty Falcon is the name CERT-In has given to an AI-assisted cyber threat actor targeting financial institutions. In its November 4, 2026 advisory, the agency classified the threat as critical and warned that attacks were escalating. Some institutions were being targeted again despite having taken remedial measures following an earlier advisory issued on October 9.The campaign targets NBFCs, digital lending platforms, fintech companies, payment aggregators, payment service providers and cryptocurrency exchanges. Rather than merely stealing customer information or disrupting online services, attackers are trying to compromise the systems through which these organisations process payments, disburse loans and settle merchant transactions.This is particularly serious because financial institutions increasingly rely on interconnected applications and services. A weakness in one system can provide attackers with a route into another system that has the ability to move money.How is AI changing the nature of cyberattacks?A conventional cyberattack can involve several stages: examining a target's systems, finding weaknesses, breaking in, gaining higher access privileges and maintaining access without being detected. Each stage can require technical expertise and considerable manual effort.AI-assisted tools can automate much of this work. They can scan computer systems, identify possible weaknesses, generate computer code to exploit them and modify their approach when security measures block an attempt. CERT-In says the tools associated with Crafty Falcon can execute attack sequences autonomously, generate customised scripts and adapt them in response to a target's systems. Advt In effect, attackers can use automated tools to keep probing a financial institution's defences rather than relying on a person to carry out every step. CERT-In has also found numerous similar but slightly different scripts, suggesting AI-assisted code generation and refinement. The infrastructure used for attacks can change its IP addresses, the numerical addresses identifying devices and servers on the internet, within hours or days, making it harder to block attackers using known addresses.Which AI tools are being used by the attackers?CERT-In has identified four tools or frameworks associated with the campaign: ARTEX, PentAGI, Cairn and CyberStrikeAI. These are not AI models specifically developed to steal money from banks. They are tools designed to automate security testing and offensive cyber operations, capabilities that can be misused for malicious attacks. Advt ARTEX is a large language model-based offensive security tool, while PentAGI is an autonomous, multi-agent penetration-testing system. The latter uses multiple specialised software agents to perform different security-testing tasks. Cairn is an attack search engine and CyberStrikeAI is an offensive security framework.Their significance lies in their ability to automate reconnaissance, vulnerability investigation and the development of customised attack scripts. Security researchers have linked some of these tools to China-associated cyber activity, including attacks on South Korean financial institutions. However, CERT-In has not attributed Crafty Falcon to China or established that it is a state-sponsored operation. The use of these tools alone does not establish who is behind the campaign. Advt How are attackers breaking into financial institutions' systems?CERT-In has identified several entry points, including cloud infrastructure, exposed testing environments, application software and weaknesses in access controls. Attackers are not necessarily breaking directly into a bank's core banking system. They are exploiting the interconnected applications and services through which financial institutions conduct business.As financial institutions automate lending, payments and settlements, APIs have become critical parts of the financial system. Compromising these channels can allow attackers to misuse the very infrastructure designed to make transactions faster and more efficient. The challenge is therefore not simply to prevent unauthorised access to computer networks, but to ensure that every instruction to move money is independently authenticated, authorised and verified, even when it appears to come from an institution's own trusted systems.
