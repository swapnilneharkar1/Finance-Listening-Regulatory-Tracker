Vs Centre For Policy Research (Supreme Court of India) Summary: Supreme Court Refuses to Interfere with Interim Stay on Cancellation of Registration The Supreme Court of India, in PCIT (Central) Delhi & Anr. Vs Centre For Policy Research, dismissed the Special Leave Petition filed by the Income Tax Department against the Delhi High Court’s order granting interim protection to the Centre for Policy Research (CPR) against cancellation of its charitable registration. The Supreme Court observed that the impugned order was interim in nature and, therefore, declined to interfere with it. Consequently, the interim protection granted by the Delhi High Court continued to operate, subject to the conditions prescribed therein. The Supreme Court did not adjudicate the substantive questions concerning retrospective cancellation of registration, jurisdiction or violation of principles of natural justice on their merits. Cancellation of Registration Under Section 12AB(4) The Centre for Policy Research, an organization established in 1973 and engaged in policy research, approached the Delhi High Court challenging the order dated 30 June 2023 passed by the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax under Section 12A read with Section 12AA and Section 12AB(4) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The petitioner raised several objections concerning the jurisdiction of the tax authorities, retrospective cancellation of registration and procedural deficiencies in the proceedings. It contended that the cancellation order relied upon alleged violations pertaining to several earlier financial years, notwithstanding that its last surviving registration had been granted on 28 May 2021. Non-Supply of Survey Report and Denial of Cross-Examination The petitioner submitted that the first show cause notice dated 22 December 2022 referred to a survey report prepared by the Investigation Wing of the Income Tax Department, but a copy of the report had not been supplied to it. It further alleged that the cancellation order relied upon statements of various persons without providing an opportunity for cross-examination. The petitioner also contended that no personal hearing was granted in connection with the third show cause notice dated 22 June 2023, despite the statutory requirement of providing a reasonable opportunity of being heard. During the proceedings before the High Court, the Additional Solicitor General appearing for the Revenue conceded that the survey report had not been furnished and that the petitioner’s request for cross-examination of persons whose statements were relied upon had been rejected. These admissions were relevant to the examination of the alleged violation of principles of natural justice. Retrospective Cancellation of Charitable Registration The Delhi High Court examined the scope of Section 12AB(4)(ii), which empowers the Principal Commissioner or Commissioner to cancel the registration of a trust or institution, after affording a reasonable opportunity of being heard, for the previous year in which specified violations have taken place and all subsequent previous years. The petitioner argued that the provision did not authorize retrospective cancellation of registration for earlier periods in the manner adopted by the Revenue. The Department maintained that the cancellation order operated against the last surviving registration granted on 28 May 2021 and that the statutory consequences concerning earlier previous years would follow in accordance with law. Without examining the substantive merits of the allegations, the High Court prima facie found that the petitioner had established a case against cancellation of registration for years preceding the period commencing on 28 May 2021. Interim Stay Granted Subject to Accounting and Audit Safeguards The Delhi High Court considered the balance of convenience and the possibility of irreparable injury to the petitioner. It observed that cancellation of registration would disable the organization from accepting contributions from domestic contributors and could adversely affect its employees, ongoing research activities and proposed programmes. Considering these circumstances, the High Court stayed the operation of the cancellation order dated 30 June 2023, subject to the petitioner maintaining proper accounts of contributions received, including particulars of contributors. The petitioner was also directed to disclose the manner in which such contributions were utilized through an affidavit accompanied by a certificate from its statutory auditor and ensure that the funds were spent in accordance with its stated objects. The Revenue was granted liberty to seek modification of the interim order in the event of any violation. The Supreme Court subsequently dismissed the Revenue’s Special Leave Petition solely on account of the interim nature of the High Court’s order, leaving the substantive issues open for adjudication before the High Court. FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER In view of the fact that the order impugned is interim in nature, we are not inclined to interfere with the impugned judgment passed by the High Court. Hence, the Special Leave Petition is dismissed. Pending applications, if any, shall stand disposed of.
