Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Rakam Pal Singh Vs Godvin Oil Services And 3 Others (Allahabad High Court) Summary: The Allahabad High Court, in Rakam Pal Singh Vs M/S Godvin Oil Services And 3 Others, partly allowed an appeal seeking enhancement of compensation awarded by the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal. The appellant had suffered serious injuries in a road accident on 09.05.2015 when a tanker struck his motorcycle from behind, resulting in amputation of his left leg below the knee. The Tribunal had awarded compensation of ₹6,16,700 with interest at 7% per annum, assessing his monthly income at ₹4,500 and reducing his permanent disability from 70%, as certified by the Chief Medical Officer, to 50%. The claimant maintained that he worked as a mechanic repairing currency-counting machines and earned ₹2,97,000 annually. He challenged the Tribunal’s rejection of his income-tax returns, its low assessment of monthly income and its reduction of the disability percentage. The respondents argued that the returns related to financial years subsequent to the accident, that the alleged occupation and income were not reliably established, and that the disability certificate had not been formally proved by examining the doctors concerned. The High Court upheld the Tribunal’s rejection of the claimed income, observing that a specific monthly or annual income must be established through reliable evidence and cannot be accepted merely on pleadings or oral testimony. Nevertheless, the Court held that the Tribunal should have considered the applicable minimum wages for an unskilled worker in 2015. Accordingly, the monthly income was reassessed at ₹6,734 instead of ₹4,500. On disability, the Court held that the absence of formal proof of the medical certificate did not justify reducing the disability percentage when the actual amputation was undisputed. The Court emphasised that the loss of a leg below the knee substantially restricts the ability to undertake physical work and consequently affects earning capacity. Considering the claimant’s circumstances, the High Court accepted 70% functional disability instead of the 50% adopted by the Tribunal. Applying the principles laid down by the Supreme Court in National Insurance Company Limited Vs Pranay Sethi And Ors., (2017) 16 SCC 680, the High Court added 10% towards future prospects because the claimant was approximately 54 years old. Applying a multiplier of 11 and 70% functional disability, the loss of future earning capacity was assessed at ₹6,84,444. The Court also awarded ₹3,00,000 towards non-pecuniary damages, including pain, suffering and loss of amenities, in addition to the medical and other expenses recognised by the Tribunal. The total compensation was consequently enhanced to ₹12,74,444, against the Tribunal’s award of ₹6,16,700, resulting in additional compensation of ₹6,57,744. Interest at 7% per annum was directed on the enhanced amount from the date of filing the claim petition until actual payment. The appeal was accordingly allowed in part, and the Tribunal’s award was modified. Cases Discussed 1. National Insurance Company Limited Vs Pranay Sethi And Ors. (Supreme Court of India); (2017) 16 SCC 680. The Allahabad High Court expressly relied upon this Supreme Court decision while allowing an addition of 10% towards future prospects for the injured claimant, who was approximately 54 years old at the time of the accident. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF ALLAHABAD HIGH COURT 1. Heard Sri Vijay Prakash Mishra, learned counsel for appellant and Sri Suresh Srivastava, learned counsel for respondent. 2. The appellant has preferred the present appeal for enhancement of the award passed by the learned Tribunal in MACP No. 531 of 2014, Rakam Pal Singh Vs. M/s Godwin Oil Services, Rohta Road, Meerut and others, whereby compensation of Rs.6,16,700/- along with interest at the rate of 7% per annum has been awarded. 3. Briefly stated, it was asserted by the appellant-claimant that while he was coming to Meerut on his motorcycle on 09.05.2015, he was hit by a tanker bearing registration No. UP-15-BT-1866, which was coming from behind. At that time, the claimant was turning towards petrol pump. The said accident resulted in serious injuries to him and also led to the amputation of his left leg.The learned Tribunal, after assessing the evidence available on record, came to the conclusion that the factum of the accident was duly established by the claimant. Accordingly, compensation was awarded by considering the disability and other relevant factors, including the income of the claimant, on the basis of the evidence available on record.It was asserted by the claimant that, at the relevant time, he was working as a mechanic engaged in repairing currency-counting machines and was earning an annual income of Rs.2,97,000/-. Income tax returns were also filed by the appellant. A disability certificate issued by the Chief Medical Officer, indicating 70% permanent disability, was also filed. 4. The learned Tribunal assessed the monthly income of the injured claimant at Rs.4,500/-. The income claimed by the claimant was discarded on the ground that he had failed to establish his alleged income. It was observed that he used to work from his residence and had neither any office nor any firm registered in his name. The income tax returns filed by the appellant-claimant, indicating his annual income to be approximately Rs.3,00,000/-, were also disbelieved on the ground that the same had been filed two years after the accident.The age of the injured claimant was assessed at 54 years on the relevant date on the basis of the matriculation certificate filed by him. Thereafter, the learned Tribunal, while assessing the monthly income of the claimant at Rs.4,500/-, added 10% towards future prospects and applied a multiplier of 11. However, the permanent disability of 70% was only partially accepted by the Tribunal and the same was reduced to 50%. After applying the multiplier of 11, the loss of future earnings was determined at Rs.6,53,400/-. After applying 50% disability, the loss of earning capacity was assessed at Rs.3,26,700/-. An amount of Rs.2,70,000/- was awarded towards medical expenses, Rs.10,000/- towards transportation expenses and Rs.10,000/- towards a healthy diet. Accordingly, the total compensation was determined at Rs.6,16,700/-. 5. Learned counsel for the appellant submitted that the income of the appellant was wrongly assessed on the lower side. It was established by the claimant that his annual income was Rs.2,97,000/-. It was further submitted that the income tax returns filed by him were wrongly discarded.It was also submitted that there was no justification for reducing the permanent disability from 70% to 50%, as assessed by the panel of doctors and mentioned in the disability certificate. There is no denial of the fact that one of the legs of the appellant was amputated during the course of treatment. It was, therefore, submitted that the findings recorded by the learned Tribunal require reassessment and modification, as the compensation has not been properly computed. It was further submitted that the addition of 10% towards future prospects is also not proper in the facts and circumstances of the present case. 6. Per contra, learned counsel for the respondent submitted that the findings recorded by the learned Tribunal are based upon the evidence available on record and do not call for any interference by this Court. It was submitted that the income tax returns relied upon by the appellant-claimant pertained to the financial years subsequent to the accident and were filed after a period of two years. It was further submitted that the appellant had failed to establish either his occupation or his alleged income. Accordingly, the monthly income of Rs.4,500/- assessed by the learned Tribunal is justified. 7. It was further submitted that the disability certificate relied upon by the appellant was not duly proved, as neither the panel of doctors who had issued the certificate nor the doctor who had treated the claimant was produced before the Tribunal. In the absence of their evidence, it was not possible for the learned Tribunal to assess the functional disability suffered by the claimant on account of the accident. Therefore, the physical disability mentioned in the disability certificate could not, by itself, be made the basis for determining the compensation. The learned Tribunal has, therefore, rightly reduced the disability from 70% to 50%. It was lastly submitted that the other parameters adopted by the learned Tribunal for assessing the income and determining the total compensation do not warrant any interference by this Court. 8. Heard learned counsel for the parties and perused the findings recorded by the learned Tribunal. 9. The findings recorded by the learned Tribunal with respect to the factum of the accident have been carefully perused and considered. The said findings have been recorded after proper appreciation of the facts and evidence available on record and do not call for any interference by this Court.The other findings recorded by the learned Tribunal with respect to the assessment of compensation also do not require interference, except to the extent of reassessment as discussed hereinafter. 10. The finding of the learned Tribunal disbelieving the income of the injured claimant, as asserted by him, is proper, legal and sound. It is incumbent upon every claimant to establish his income by reliable evidence. Where a specific monthly or annual income is claimed, the same cannot be accepted merely on the basis of pleadings or oral testimony, in the absence of any reliable evidence in support thereof. Therefore, this Court also does not find any perversity in the finding recorded by the learned Tribunal regarding the assessment of income of the appellant. 11. However, considering the fact that the accident had taken place in the year 2015, the income of the appellant ought to have been assessed on the basis of the minimum wages prescribed for unskilled workers under the applicable Minimum Wages Act. There was no justification for assessing the income at a lower rate when the minimum wages prescribed for an unskilled worker were available for consideration. The relevant minimum wages indicate that the monthly income of an unskilled worker at the relevant time was Rs.6,734/-. Accordingly, for the purpose of computation of compensation, the monthly income of the appellant is determined at Rs.6,734/-. 12. Similarly, the finding of the learned Tribunal reducing the permanent disability of the appellant from 70% to 50% also requires consideration. It is true that the disability certificate indicating 70% permanent disability was not duly proved by producing the panel of doctors who had issued the same. However, this circumstance alone cannot be a sufficient ground either to discard the disability certificate or to reduce the percentage of disability mentioned therein, particularly when the factum of disability is otherwise established from the evidence available on record. 13. In the present case, there is no denial, even on behalf of the respondents, that the appellant’s left leg below the knee had to be amputated during the course of treatment for the injuries sustained in the accident. Once this fact is established, the effect of such amputation upon the earning capacity of the appellant is required to be considered.Even if it is accepted that the appellant failed to establish his alleged income and is treated as an unskilled worker, the amputation of one of his legs would certainly have an adverse effect upon his earning capacity. The ability of an unskilled worker to earn his livelihood depends substantially upon his physical capacity to undertake manual work. Amputation of a leg below the knee would undoubtedly restrict his ability to perform several categories of physical work and would adversely affect his future earning capacity. In these circumstances, the learned Tribunal was not justified in reducing the disability from 70% to 50% merely because the disability certificate was not formally proved by examining the panel of doctors. The factum of amputation, which is not disputed, and its consequential effect upon the earning capacity of the appellant ought to have been given due consideration. 14. Considering the aforesaid facts and circumstances, this Court finds that the monthly income of the appellant is liable to be assessed at Rs.6,734/-. Since the appellant was aged about 54 years at the time of the accident, an addition of 10% towards future prospects is also liable to be made in view of the principles laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in National Insurance Company Limited Vs. Pranay Sethi and others, (2017) 16 SCC 680. Accordingly, the annual income of the appellant, after addition of future prospects, comes to Rs.88,888.80. Applying the multiplier of 11, the total loss of future earnings comes to Rs.9,77,776.80. 15. So far as the functional disability is concerned, considering the fact that the left leg of the appellant below the knee was amputated during the course of treatment and the nature of work which he was performing before the accident, this Court finds that the loss of earning capacity deserves to be assessed at 70%. Accordingly, the compensation towards loss of future earning capacity comes to Rs.6,84,444/-. 16. Apart from the aforesaid amount, the appellant is also entitled to compensation under the non-pecuniary heads. The amputation of his left leg has not only affected his earning capacity but has also adversely affected his normal activities, enjoyment of life and ability to lead a normal life. Keeping in view the nature of injuries, permanent disability and its consequences, this Court finds it appropriate to award a lump sum amount of Rs.3,00,000/- towards other non-pecuniary damages, including pain and suffering, loss of amenities of life and other consequential sufferings, to the extent not already compensated by the learned Tribunal. 17. Accordingly, the appellant is entitled to compensation as reassessed hereinabove, as follows: REASSESSMENT OF COMPENSATION Particulars Amount (a) Monthly income Rs.6,734/- (b) Annual income (6,734 × 12) Rs.80,808/- (c) Future prospects @ 10% Rs.8,080.80 (d) Annual income after future prospects Rs.88,888.80 (e) Multiplier (age 54 years) 11 (f) Total loss of future earnings Rs.9,77,776.80 (g) Functional disability 70% (h) Loss of future earning capacity Rs.6,84,444/- (i) Medical expenses Rs.2,70,000/- (j) Transportation expenses Rs.10,000/- (k) Healthy diet Rs.10,000/- (l) Other non-pecuniary damages (lump sum) Rs.3,00,000/- Total compensation Rs.12,74,444/- Compensation awarded by Tribunal Rs.6,16,700/- Enhanced Compensation Rs.6,57,744/- Interest: 7% per annum on the enhanced compensation, subject to the final directions of the Court. 18. The total compensation payable to the appellant comes to Rs.12,74,444/- as against Rs.6,16,700/- awarded by the learned Tribunal. The appellant shall also be entitled to interest at the rate of 7% per annum, as awarded by the learned Tribunal, on the enhanced amount from the date of filing of the claim petition until its actual payment. 19. Consequently, the appeal deserves to be allowed in part and the impugned award stands modified to the aforesaid extent.
