Sudha Vs ITO (Madras High Court) Summary: The Madras High Court allowed a writ petition filed by the widow of a deceased assessee for AY 2022–23. The assessee died on 3 November 2022, and the Department was informed of his death on 17 February 2026. Despite that intimation, the Income Tax Officer issued a show-cause notice under Section 148A(1) on 26 March 2026. No one appeared, and the officer passed an ex parte order under Section 148A(3) and issued a notice under Section 148, both dated 29 June 2026. The Department contended that Section 159 permitted proceedings against the legal heirs. The Court found that the impugned order and notice were issued against the deceased assessee himself and were therefore a nullity. It set both aside, granting the Department liberty to issue a fresh notice to the eligible person under Section 159 in accordance with law, if so advised. The Court allowed the application to dispense with production of the certified copy, closed the connected stay application and made no order as to costs. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF MADRAS HIGH COURT 1. The present Writ Petition has been filed challenging the order passed under Section 148A(3) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, and the consequent Notice issued under Section 148 of the Act, both dated 29.06.2026. 2. The learned counsel appearing for the petitioner submits that the issue relates to the Assessment Year 2022-2023. She further submits that the petitioner is the wife of the deceased assessee, who passed away on 03.11.2022. The factum of death of the petitioner’s husband was intimated to the respondent by way of a communication dated 17.02.2026. Despite the same, a Notice under Section 148A(1) dated 26.03.2026 was issued. Thereafter, since no one appeared, an ex parte order was passed under Section 148A(3) on 29.06.2026, followed by the Notice under Section 148. 3. The learned Senior Standing Counsel appearing for the respondent, by referring to Section 159 of the Act, submits that the proceedings can be validly initiated and continued against the legal heirs of the deceased assessee. 4. Upon hearing the learned counsel appearing on either side and perusing the materials available on record, it is evident that there is no dispute regarding the fact that the petitioner’s husband was the original assessee and that he passed away on 03.11.2022. It is also undisputed that the death of the petitioner’s husband was duly intimated to the respondent Department vide communication dated 17.02.2026. In spite of the said intimation, the respondent issued the preliminary notice on 26.03.2026 and subsequently passed the impugned order under Section 148A(3) on 29.06.2026, along with the Notice under Section 148, against a deceased person. It is settled law that any notice issued or order passed against a deceased person is a nullity and is unsustainable in law. Therefore, the same is liable to be quashed. Accordingly, the impugned order and notice, both dated 29.06.2026, are set aside. However, liberty is granted to the Department to invoke the provisions of Section 159 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, and issue notice afresh to the eligible person in accordance with law, if so advised. 5. Accordingly, this Writ Petition stands allowed. No costs. W.M.P. No. 46824 of 2026 filed to dispense with the production of the certified copy of the impugned order/notice, is allowed. Consequently, the connected Miscellaneous Petition for stay is closed.
