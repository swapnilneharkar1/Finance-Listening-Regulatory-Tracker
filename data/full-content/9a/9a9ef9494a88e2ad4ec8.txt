Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Shah Batteries Private Limited Vs Union of India & Others (Telangana High Court) Summary: The Telangana High Court, in Shah Batteries Private Limited Vs Union of India & Others, allowed a writ petition challenging the cancellation of the petitioner’s GST registration through an order dated 31 August 2026. The petitioner contended that the department had issued a show cause notice on 18 June 2026, to which it submitted a detailed reply comprising approximately 50 pages on 23 June 2026. However, the cancellation order incorrectly recorded that no reply had been submitted. The Court observed that correspondence furnished by the respondents themselves established that the department had received the petitioner’s reply. Consequently, the cancellation order had been passed without properly examining the available records, considering the taxpayer’s submissions or applying due mind to the facts. Significantly, the department’s Senior Standing Counsel, upon instructions, also acknowledged that the reasons recorded in the order did not appear to have been based on verification of the facts. The High Court held that the cancellation order was unsustainable on this ground alone and set aside the order dated 31 August 2026 without examining the substantive merits of the allegations. However, the Court expressly clarified that quashing the cancellation order would not prevent the GST authorities from continuing proceedings in accordance with law on the original show cause notice dated 18 June 2026 or issuing a fresh show cause notice, if considered appropriate. The writ petition was accordingly allowed and disposed of without costs. The decision highlights the requirement that GST authorities must consider replies actually submitted by taxpayers and verify departmental records before passing adverse registration cancellation orders. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF TELANGANA HIGH COURT Heard Mr. A.V.A. Siva Kartikeya, learned counsel appearing for the petitioner and Mr. Dominic Fernandes, learned Senior Standing Counsel for Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) appearing for respondent Nos.2 to 5. 2. The instant is the case where the writ petition has been filed assailing the impugned order dated 31.08.2026 whereby the respondents have issued the order of cancellation of registration under GST Law sofar as the petitioner is concerned. 3. At the outset, the learned counsel for the petitioner, assailing the impugned order of cancellation of registration, submits that the show cause notice was issued in this case on 18.06.2026, to which a detailed reply was submitted by the petitioner on 23.06.2026 running into about 50 pages. Yet even the impugned order is passed. 4. It is reflected that no reply to the show cause notice was submitted, whereas the mail that has been furnished by the respondents clearly indicates that the respondents have accepted the fact that reply has in fact been submitted to the Department. In view of the same, the impugned order per se seems to have been passed, firstly not appreciating the reply that has been submitted by the petitioner and secondly the averments having been made in the impugned order seems to be without proper application of mind and also without properly verifying the records. Without dwelling into the merits of the case, the impugned order apparently seems to be an erroneous one on this very ground itself. 5. Learned Senior Standing Counsel for the Department also, upon instructions, submits that the order and the reasons reflected therein does not seem to be upon verification of facts. The impugned order, therefore, is not sustainable on this very ground and the same deserves to be set aside. 6. In view of the submissions made by learned counsel for the parties and considering the facts and circumstances of the case, the impugned order is set aside and the Writ Petition stands allowed. The quashment of the impugned order is only on the ground of the respondents having wrongly mentioned of the petitioner having not submitted the reply, whereas the reply in fact was duly submitted and available with the respondents. Setting aside of the impugned order would not preclude the respondents from proceeding further in accordance with law in sofar as proceeding further with the show cause notice that was issued at the first instance on 18.06.2026. The authorities would also be at liberty, if they so want, to issue a fresh show cause notice and take appropriate steps. 7. With the aforesaid direction, the Writ Petition is disposed of. There shall be no order as to costs. Miscellaneous applications, if any pending, shall stand closed.
