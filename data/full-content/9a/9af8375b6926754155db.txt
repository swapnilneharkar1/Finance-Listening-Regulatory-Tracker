Anushka Sengupta ETBFSI Published On Nov 23, 2026 at 02:16 PM IST var news_msid = 136095332; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Nearly 75% of urban Indian consumers have used at least one financial product within their primary payment application, with credit and savings emerging as the most widely adopted features, according to a latest joint report by Amazon Pay and Pahlé India Foundation.The report found that 27% of respondents regularly use credit, EMI or Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) features within their payment applications, while 25% use savings and auto-save features.However, adoption drops significantly for other financial products. Around 17% of respondents have opened a fixed deposit or recurring deposit through a payment application, while 15% have purchased insurance and 11% have invested in mutual funds or systematic investment plans (SIPs). Advt The findings highlighted the growing role of payment applications as distribution channels for financial products, while also pointing to considerable scope for insurers, banks and investment platforms to deepen customer engagement beyond payments.Insurance and investment adoption remain lowDespite the increasing integration of financial services within payment applications, insurance and investment products have yet to see comparable adoption.The report found that 46% of respondents were aware of insurance products available through payment applications but had not used them. The corresponding share stood at 54% for mutual funds and 55% for digital gold.This indicated that awareness is translating into actual adoption at a slower pace, particularly for products that require greater consumer consideration and financial commitment.The report noted that convenience and recommendations from friends and family were among the leading factors influencing consumers to try their first non-payment financial product, cited by 25% and 18% of respondents, respectively.Around 52% of respondents also said they would prefer a single application for payments, investments, insurance and credit, indicating consumer appetite for consolidated financial services platforms. Advt Digital credit leads financial product adoptionCredit-related features remain among the most widely used financial services within payment applications.The report found that 52% of respondents had used digital credit at least once, while 52% had also used BNPL or Pay Later products, which are increasingly serving as an entry point to formal credit.However, the report highlighted a significant gap between awareness and actual usage. Around 88% of urban consumers are aware of digital credit products, but a substantial section has not yet translated that awareness into borrowing.Consumer confidence also remains a challenge. While respondents rated their trust in payment applications for security at 4.11 out of five and for payments at 3.98, the score fell to 3.10 for borrowing. Advt Around 51.5% said they would consider taking credit through their payment application if fraud protection were guaranteed, compared with 16.7% who said they would still decline.Transaction history emerging as a financial planning toolThe report also highlighted how payment applications are increasingly being used to track and manage personal finances.Around 79% of respondents agreed or strongly agreed that tracking payment history within applications helped them plan and manage their finances.The findings suggested that transaction data and customer engagement could become important tools for financial service providers looking to cross-sell savings, insurance, investment and credit products. Advt However, converting existing payment users into customers of other financial products will depend on building trust, simplifying product journeys and improving awareness.The report, based on a survey of 5,149 urban consumers across 100 cities and 20 states, indicated that the next phase of digital financial services growth will depend not merely on expanding payment adoption but on converting payment platforms into broader financial management ecosystems.
