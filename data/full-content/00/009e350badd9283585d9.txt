Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); PCIT Vs Bank of Baroda (Bombay High Court) Broken period interest paid on securities held to maturity is allowable as revenue expenditure, and provision for wage revision based on a reasonable estimate of an existing business liability is deductible on accrual basis. Where the issues are covered by settled judicial precedents and no substantial question of law arises, the Revenue appeal is dismissed. Case Summary: The Revenue challenged the ITAT order dated 22 May 2019 in ITA No. 1222/Mum/2018 for AY 2014-15, raising two questions regarding allowability of broken period interest on securities held to maturity and deduction of provision for wage revision amounting to Rs.300 crore. Issue 1 – Broken Period Interest: The Revenue contended that broken period interest paid on purchase of securities held to maturity was wrongly allowed as revenue expenditure by treating the securities as trading stock. The Bombay High Court held that the issue was squarely covered by American Express International Banking Corporation v. Commissioner of Income Tax (2002) 125 Taxman 488 (Bom). No substantial question of law arose and the question was not entertained. Issue 2 – Provision for Wage Revision: The Bank had made a provision of Rs.300 crore based on an estimated wage increase of 13.5%. The existing wage agreement had expired in October 2011 and negotiations between the Indian Banks Association and employee unions were ongoing. Considering the negotiation trends and earlier settlements, the Bank reasonably estimated its liability and made provision in its books following the mercantile system of accounting. The Court held that the liability could reasonably be estimated during AY 2014-15 and had accrued during the relevant year. Judicial Finding: Relying on Bharat Earth Movers v. Commissioner of Income Tax (2000) 245 ITR 428 (SC), the Court reiterated that a business liability which has definitely arisen during the accounting year is deductible even if actual quantification or payment is deferred, provided the liability is capable of reasonable estimation. Such liability is present and accrued and is not contingent merely because settlement or payment will occur later. The Court also noted that similar wage revision provisions had been allowed in the Bank’s own earlier assessment years. Outcome: The Bombay High Court dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. Neither question raised a substantial question of law. Broken period interest was allowable as revenue expenditure and the provision for wage revision of Rs.300 crore was deductible as an accrued business liability. No order as to costs. Key Legal Principle: A liability is not contingent merely because its final amount or payment date is uncertain. If it has arisen during the relevant year and can be estimated with reasonable certainty based on available facts, it is deductible under the accrual method of accounting. Cases Discussed 1. Bharat Earth Movers Vs Commissioner of Income-Tax (Supreme Court); (2000) 245 ITR 428. Relevance: The Supreme Court held that a business liability which has definitely arisen during the accounting year is deductible where it can be estimated with reasonable certainty, even if payment or final quantification occurs subsequently. The Bombay High Court applied this principle to the bank’s ₹300 crore wage revision provision. 2. American Express International Banking Corporation Vs Commissioner of Income Tax (Bombay High Court); (2002) 125 Taxmann 488 (Bom). Relevance: The Bombay High Court relied on this precedent to reject the Revenue’s challenge concerning deduction of broken-period interest paid on the purchase of securities held to maturity. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF BOMBAY HIGH COURT 1. The above Appeal has been filed by the Revenue challenging the order dated 22nd May 2019 passed by the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal in Appeal No.1222/Mum/2018. The Assessment Year in question is A. Y. 2014-15. According to the Revenue, the impugned order of the ITAT gives rise to the following two questions of law:- i. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law, the Hon’ble ITAT has erred in relying on the decision of the Bombay High Court in the case of American Express International Banking Corporation and allowing broken period interest paid on purchasing Securities Held to Maturity as Revenue expenditure presuming the entire stock of securities as trading stock? ii. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law, the Hon’ble ITAT has erred in allowing the provision for Wage Revision of Rs.300,00,00,000/- without appreciating the fact that the provision was made for a contingent liability? 2. As far as Question (i) is concerned, we find that the same is squarely covered by a decision of this Court in the case of American Express International Banking Corporation v/s the Commissioner of Income Tax (2002) 125 Taxmann 488 (Bom). Accordingly, we find that Question (i) does not give rise to any substantial question of law and hence not entertained. 3. As far as Question (ii) is concerned, we find that the bank has made a provision of wage revision amounting to Rs.300 Crores. This figure was arrived at based on the indicative increase in the wages payable by the bank. The existing wage agreement had expired in October 2011 and negotiations on the revised wage agreement between the Indian Banks Association representing the management of various banks and the employees union/association were in progress. Taking into consideration the trend of the negotiations, and the settlements reached in the earlier wage negotiations, the Assessee Bank quantified the likely increase @13.5% and accordingly made provisions in its books since the basis of accounting followed was on an accrual basis. According to the Assessee, and in our view correctly so, the claim made should be during the Assessment Year 2014-15 because the liability could be quantified during the Assessment Year based on the ongoing negotiations and the indicative percentage of wage increase that was available at that point of time. Since the Assessee had followed the accrual basis of accounting, the same accrued as a deduction. It was the case of the Assessee that the provisions for wage arrears made on the basis of the reasonable estimate is allowable as a deduction, and in support thereof, inter alia relied upon a decision of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Bharat Earth Movers v/s Commissioner of Income -Tax (2000) 245 ITR 428 , the relevant portion of which reads thus: “The law is settled: if a business liability has definitely arisen in the accounting year, the deduction should be allowed although the liability may have to be quantified and discharged at a future date. What should be certain is the incurring of the liability. It should also be capable of being estimated with reasonable certainty though the actual quantification may not be possible. If these requirements are satisfied the liability is not a contingent one. The liability is in praesenti though it will be discharged at a future date. It does not make any difference if the future date on which the liability shall have to be discharged is not certain.” 4. From the record we further find that in fact this estimation of wage arrears has been allowed in the Assessee’s own case in several previous Assessment Years. Those also have been taken note of, not only by the CIT(A) but also the ITAT. Once these are the facts before us, we find that even Question (ii) as projected by the Revenue does not give rise to any substantial question of law and hence is not entertained. Accordingly, the Appeal is dismissed. However, there shall be no order as to costs. 5. This order will be digitally signed by the Private Secretary/ Personal Assistant of this Court. All concerned will act on production by fax or email of a digitally signed copy of this order.
