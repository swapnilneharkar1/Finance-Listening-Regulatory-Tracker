ETBFSI Research ETBFSI Updated On Nov 20, 2026 at 10:49 AM IST var news_msid = 136009781; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook Five banks could face a net ECL hit exceeding 5 per cent of net worth: Bandhan Bank, IndusInd Bank, City Union Bank, IDFC First Bank and Bank of India.Estimated ECL impact is 70-120 basis points for most PSBs, rising to 250 basis points at Bank of India and 110-150 basis points for mid-sized private banks.Revised risk weights could partly offset the provisioning burden, while SBI plans to amortise its transition impact over four to five years.Higher standard provisioning and potential slippages could push credit costs up by 10-20 basis points year-on-year in FY28 for most banks.The transition to the expected credit loss (ECL) provisioning framework could result in a net hit exceeding 5 per cent of net worth at five banks, creating a significant adjustment for their balance sheets even as asset quality across the banking sector remains broadly stable. Bandhan Bank, IndusInd Bank, City Union Bank, IDFC First Bank and Bank of India are among the lenders facing the highest estimated impact. Most other banks are likely to see the net ECL hit range between 2 per cent and 5 per cent of net worth.The impact is expected to be higher for mid-sized private banks, reflecting their customer profiles and portfolio behaviour. While most public sector banks are estimated to face a relatively lower provisioning impact, Bank of India stands out with a significantly higher estimated hit. The extent of the impact will also vary depending on the assumptions banks have used to assess their portfolios and the manner in which they recognise the transition charge, according to ICICI Securities. Advt The estimated ECL impact for most public sector banks is in the range of 70-120 basis points, with Bank of India an outlier at around 250 basis points. Mid-sized private banks face a higher estimated impact of 110-150 basis points. HDFC Bank, Axis Bank and Karur Vysya Bank have relatively healthy cushions, while Kotak Mahindra Bank and Federal Bank have indicated that their estimated impact could be below 2 per cent.Impact on loan portfoliosMost banks have quantified the likely ECL impact on their loan portfolios and retained their earlier estimates. There have been some revisions, however. Bandhan Bank has raised its estimate compared with the draft guidelines, while Punjab National Bank and City Union Bank have lowered theirs. These differences reflect the varying assessments lenders have made of the expected credit losses in their portfolios.The calculations are not restricted to loan books at every lender. Some banks have also included the impact on their held-to-maturity bond portfolios, following the hardening of yields. This adds to the estimated transition burden for these lenders, beyond the provisioning requirement on loans.The manner in which banks recognise the impact will determine how it is spread across reporting periods. State Bank of India intends to amortise the transition impact over four to five years, while other lenders are expected to recognise it upfront. The difference could therefore influence the immediate effect on reported earnings. Advt There could be some relief from changes in risk weights. Revised risk weights for AA-rated, BBB-rated and unrated exposures, as well as mortgage loans, could release capital and partly offset the ECL impact. IDFC First Bank has indicated that the net effect on its common equity tier-1 capital could be broadly neutral after accounting for these changes.The transition could also increase provisioning requirements even without a significant deterioration in reported asset quality. Fresh Stage 2 loan formation remains comfortable across most banks, but could still lead to an additional 12-15 basis points of standard asset provisioning. The increase is expected to be more pronounced among mid-sized private banks and select public sector lenders. Advt The brokerage expects credit costs to rise by 10-20 basis points year-on-year in FY28 for most banks, factoring in a possible increase in net slippages and higher standard provisions. This could weigh on profitability even as the broader improvement in asset quality is sustained. The projected increase in credit costs, together with the initial ECL transition impact, will be an important factor shaping banks' earnings in the coming years.
