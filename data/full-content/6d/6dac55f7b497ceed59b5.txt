Bank credit grew 19.5% year on year against deposit growth of 16.9% in the fortnight ended October 31, widening the gap to 268 basis points. The incremental credit-deposit ratio has climbed to 90.1% in FY27 so far, leaving banks increasingly dependent on higher-cost term deposits, bulk deposits and short-term market borrowings to finance lending. Advt The pressure is reflected in the banking system's funding position. As of October 31, bank credit stood at Rs 231.8 lakh crore and aggregate deposits at Rs 282.5 lakh crore. Credit increased by Rs 4.40 lakh crore during the fortnight, compared with a Rs 3.75 lakh crore rise in deposits. More significantly, deposits remained Rs 1.45 lakh crore below their September-end level, despite the latest fortnightly increase. The overall credit-deposit ratio rose to 82.1%, while the incremental ratio increased from 84% on October 15 to 90.1%.The immediate sources of additional funding are likely to be retail term deposits, current and savings accounts (CASA), and institutional deposits. However, attracting these funds is becoming more expensive. An analysis of 36 banks found that deposits grew 16% year on year in the July-September quarter, led by term deposits, which rose 19.2%. Current account deposits increased 13.2%. The CASA ratio fell to 34.4% from 35.6% in the preceding quarter, partly because strong foreign currency non-resident bank (FCNR(B)) inflows increased the share of other deposits in the overall funding mix. The shift towards term deposits is helping banks mobilise funds, but it also raises their funding costs.FCNR(B) supportFCNR(B) deposits had provided banks with a significant source of liquidity earlier this year. Banks collectively mobilised nearly $13.3 billion through the special swap window, which closed on August 31. These inflows temporarily eased liquidity pressures and reduced dependence on wholesale deposits. However, the funding relief has not removed the need for banks to compete for fresh institutional and retail money as credit demand remains strong. Smaller and mid-sized lenders that did not benefit significantly from the FCNR(B) inflows are facing greater funding pressure.The competition is also visible in the certificate of deposit (CD) market. Banks issued CDs worth Rs 51,601 crore in the fortnight ended October 31, compared with Rs 45,781 crore in the previous fortnight. The highest rate offered on these issuances rose to 8.04% from 6.92%. The weighted average call rate stood at 5.55% in the week ended November 13, against the policy repo rate of 5.50%. These indicators point to higher marginal costs for banks accessing short-term funds, although they do not represent the average cost of deposits across the banking system. Advt Bulk deposits are another increasingly expensive source of funding. Rates on large-ticket deposits of Rs 3 crore and above have risen by 50-100 basis points as banks compete for institutional funds. The pressure is also being felt in retail term deposits, with lenders raising rates to attract and retain household savings. This competition could increase the cost of funds, particularly for banks that rely more heavily on wholesale deposits than on a stable retail deposit base.The next phase of deposit mobilisation will therefore depend on banks' ability to deepen their retail franchises, attract salary and transaction accounts, and secure a larger share of household savings. Banks with wider branch networks and stronger CASA franchises are relatively better placed to mobilise stable, lower-cost deposits. Those more dependent on bulk deposits and market borrowings could face sharper funding-cost pressures. Advt The challenge is that higher deposit rates can erode net interest margins, particularly if banks cannot reprice loans at the same pace. While short-term borrowings and institutional deposits can bridge temporary funding gaps, sustained credit growth will require a more durable increase in domestic retail deposits. For banks, the question is no longer just how quickly they can grow their loan books, but whether they can attract enough savings to fund that expansion without compromising profitability.
