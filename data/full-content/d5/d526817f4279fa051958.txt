Summary : Canara Bank describes itself in its official export and import policy as an Authorised Dealer Category I bank. That status permits the bank, within FEMA, RBI directions and its internal controls, to handle current-account transactions and permissible capital-account transactions; it does not convert every requested overseas payment into a permitted remittance. Resident individuals may use the Liberalised Remittance Scheme for allowed purposes within the prevailing aggregate limit, while trade, entity and non-resident flows follow different documentation and account rules. Canara Bank offers branch and digital outward-remittance routes, SWIFT-related services and specified inward remittance facilities. Its Western Union and Ria Money Transfer Service Scheme channel is for eligible inward personal remittances, not outward remittance. Charges, the exchange rate, correspondent deductions, GST and possible TCS must be separated and confirmed before execution. This profile is a factual service guide, not an endorsement or a comparison with another bank. Its current official export and import policy expressly states that the bank acts as an Authorised Dealer Category I. RBI’s current categorisation table identifies commercial banks as eligible AD Category I entities and describes the category’s permissible activity as all current-account transactions, unless restricted by government in consultation with RBI, and permissible capital-account transactions. The customer’s transaction must still comply with FEMA, the applicable rules or directions, sanctions controls, KYC and the bank’s risk checks. AD Category I status is therefore an authorisation boundary, not an approval stamp for any product promoted as “forex.” Canara Bank’s remittance service concerns permitted currency conversion and payment. It should not be confused with an overseas leveraged foreign-exchange or contract-for-difference platform. RBI’s forex FAQ says residents should transact only with authorised persons and for permitted purposes. The Liberalised Remittance Scheme does not permit remittance for margins or margin calls to overseas exchanges or counterparties. Who May Use the Services and for Which Purposes Resident individuals under LRS RBI’s current LRS direction permits a resident individual, including a minor through the required guardian process, to remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year for permitted current or capital-account transactions. The limit is aggregate across authorised dealers and purposes; opening a second bank relationship does not create another limit. Permitted current-account uses include private visits, gifts or donations, going abroad for employment, emigration, maintenance of close relatives, business travel, medical treatment and studies abroad, subject to the detailed rules and supporting documents. Form A2 and the customer declaration allow the bank to capture purpose, source and cumulative utilisation. Transactions outside the resident-individual LRS Companies, firms, trusts and other entities do not use the resident-individual LRS merely because an employee operates the account. Import and export payments, service transactions, overseas direct investment and external commercial borrowing follow their own FEMA and RBI frameworks. Non-resident account remittances depend on whether the funds are in an NRE, FCNR(B) or NRO account and on the underlying source. For NRO repatriation, the bank’s forms page flags Form A2 and tax documentation such as Form 15CA and, where applicable, Form 15CB. MTSS inward remittance customers The bank’s Western Union and Ria page states that branches handle inward remittances under the Money Transfer Service Scheme. RBI’s MTSS direction limits the scheme to personal remittances such as family maintenance and certain tourist-related receipts. It is not a channel for outward remittance, trade payments, investment, property purchase or credit to an NRE account. A recipient must provide the control number and acceptable identity evidence and pass the bank’s verification. KYC, Documents and Purpose Coding Documentation varies by transaction, but the bank must identify the customer and beneficial owner, establish the purpose, screen parties and countries, and obtain the declaration required by the relevant route. A resident-individual outward remittance ordinarily requires an operative account, PAN, KYC-compliant identity and address records, Form A2/LRS declaration, beneficiary bank details and purpose evidence. The evidence can include an admission letter and fee demand for education, medical estimate, visa or employment material, gift declaration, invoice or travel documents. A quote or beneficiary instruction does not replace the purpose evidence. The schedule states that prescribed charges exclude GST, out-of-pocket expenses such as correspondent bank, courier, telecommunication and SWIFT charges can be recovered, and the prevailing TT selling rate is used where a rupee equivalent is needed for charge application. A customer should obtain a transaction-specific quote because the exchange rate is time-sensitive and overseas intermediary or beneficiary banks can deduct amounts outside Canara Bank’s control. The remitter should also choose or confirm the available charge instruction—such as who bears correspondent deductions—rather than assuming the beneficiary will receive the exact rupee-converted amount. GST GST can apply to the bank’s taxable service charges and to foreign-currency conversion under the statutory valuation rules. It is not accurate to multiply the entire remitted principal by the standard service rate without considering the specific supply and prescribed valuation. The bank’s invoice or advice should separately show the taxable value and GST collected. Correspondent-bank deductions made abroad may not appear in the same way as the Indian bank’s own fee. TCS under LRS For remittances made under LRS on or after 1 April 2026, the current Income-tax Act framework generally uses a ₹10 lakh financial-year threshold. Education or medical remittances attract two per cent TCS on the covered excess, while other LRS purposes generally attract twenty per cent on the covered excess; the statutory treatment of qualifying education-loan remittances must be checked separately. TCS is a collection and credit mechanism, not an additional remittance purpose or a final income-tax determination. The bank relies on PAN, purpose coding and cumulative data, so incorrect purpose selection can produce an incorrect collection. The remitter should reconcile the bank advice with AIS/Form 26AS and the return. Process, Settlement, Returns and Refunds 1. Confirm that the purpose is permitted and identify whether LRS, trade, NRI-account or another route applies. 2. Obtain the beneficiary’s verified name, account/IBAN, bank, SWIFT/BIC, address and any intermediary-bank details. 3. Submit KYC, Form A2/declaration and purpose documents through the branch or supported digital channel. 4. Review the exchange rate, bank fee, GST, possible TCS, charge instruction and estimated beneficiary amount. 5. Authorise only after checking every field; retain the acknowledgement and UTR/SWIFT reference. 6. Track settlement with the beneficiary and report a mismatch promptly with the transaction reference. Settlement time is not a universal promise. Cut-off time, currency, destination, compliance review, intermediary routing, local holidays and beneficiary-bank controls can affect completion. A returned remittance may be reconverted at the rate applicable on return and may bear foreign-bank or bank charges, so the rupee refund can differ from the original debit. Cancellation after dispatch may depend on correspondent cooperation and is not guaranteed. The customer should ask the bank for the reason code and a component-wise reconciliation of any returned amount. Grievance Route and Escalation Canara Bank provides an online grievance registration and status-tracking channel, regional and circle contacts, a nodal grievance officer and a sub-appellate authority. The initial complaint should include the account, date, amount, currency, beneficiary, UTR/SWIFT reference, purpose, fee advice and the specific remedy requested without sending passwords or one-time codes. If the bank does not resolve an eligible complaint within the prescribed period, or the response is unsatisfactory, the customer may use the RBI Integrated Ombudsman route subject to its maintainability conditions. A dispute with an overseas beneficiary or intermediary may still require evidence and cooperation outside the Indian grievance channel. Service Limitations to Keep Explicit AD Category I status does not authorise a prohibited purpose or waive sanctions and KYC review. LRS is only for resident individuals and is an aggregate annual limit across authorised dealers. LRS cannot be used to fund overseas forex margin, margin calls or leveraged CFD trading. MTSS at Canara Bank is an inward personal-remittance channel, not an outward or trade channel. Published bank fees do not fix a live exchange rate or eliminate correspondent deductions. A digital initiation can still be held for documents, compliance review or beneficiary correction. Frequently Asked Questions Is Canara Bank an Authorised Dealer Category I bank? Yes. Canara Bank’s current official export and import policy expressly describes the bank as an Authorised Dealer Category I. Can every resident remit USD 250,000 automatically? No. USD 250,000 is the aggregate LRS ceiling for a resident individual in a financial year. The purpose must be permitted, documents must be satisfactory and other legal controls still apply. Can LRS fund an overseas forex trading margin account? No. RBI’s LRS direction excludes remittances for margins or margin calls to overseas exchanges or counterparties. Does Western Union or Ria at Canara Bank support outward remittance? The bank’s MTSS service page describes eligible inward personal remittances. It is not an outward-remittance route. Will the beneficiary receive the exact amount shown in the initial conversion? Not necessarily. The charge instruction, correspondent and beneficiary-bank deductions, exchange-rate timing and compliance events can affect the credited amount. Is GST charged on the entire overseas remittance? GST treatment depends on taxable service charges and the prescribed valuation of currency conversion. The bank advice should be used instead of applying a flat rate to the principal. Where should a failed or delayed remittance complaint start? Start with Canara Bank’s complaint channel using the UTR or SWIFT reference and supporting advice, then use the bank escalation ladder and, if eligible, the RBI Ombudsman route.
