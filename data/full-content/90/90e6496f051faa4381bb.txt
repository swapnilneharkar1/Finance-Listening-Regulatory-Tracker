Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Satish Kumar Pandey Vs Century Real Estate Holdings Private Limited (Karnataka RERA) Resale of an Apartment After Failure to Deliver Possession Does Not Defeat the Allottees’ Right to Refund With Interest Under RERA Summary: The Karnataka Real Estate Regulatory Authority (K-RERA) held that a promoter remained liable to refund the original allottees’ payments with interest after missing the contractual possession deadline and selling the apartment to a third party. In Satish Kumar Pandey and Sangeeta Pandey v. Century Real Estate Holdings Private Limited and Another, the Authority applied Sections 18 and 19(4) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016. It quantified the amount payable at ₹5,74,90,331, inclusive of interest calculated through 19 July 2026, and directed payment of further interest until final payment. Booking and Contractual Possession Deadline The complainants paid a booking advance of ₹40 lakh on 10 July 2013, comprising ₹20 lakh each for two units in the Century Ethos project in Bengaluru. They subsequently selected Apartment C-802 in Chrome Tower. When they cancelled the second booking, its advance was adjusted against the allotted apartment. The sale and construction agreements dated 13 March 2014 fixed the total consideration at ₹2,87,98,492. Under Clause 3 of the construction agreement, possession was due within 36 months of receipt of the commencement certificate, with a six-month grace period. The certificate was issued on 20 January 2017, making 20 July 2020 the contractual deadline. The Authority found that the promoter failed to deliver possession by that date despite having received more than 65% of the consideration by 2017. The promoter attributed the delay to the pandemic and lockdowns and relied on the extension of the project’s registration until 30 September 2022. Resale Discussions and Third-Party Transfer In January 2023, the promoter’s customer relations team began discussing a repurchase arrangement with the complainants. The complainants said the proposed transaction remained incomplete despite extensions and follow-up demands. On 26 August 2024, they communicated that they were revoking the resale arrangement and wanted the apartment registered in their names upon issuance of the occupancy certificate. When they visited the project in May 2025, they found third parties occupying the apartment and learned that it had already been conveyed to Karthik Shankarlingegowda under a registered sale deed dated 15 May 2024. The promoter relied on Clause 10 of the construction agreement, which contemplated written notice of termination by the allottees, resale of the apartment and repayment of their advance upon receipt of the resale consideration. It argued that the complainants had agreed to the resale and that ₹92,81,250 had been credited to their account as part payment. The promoter also stated that the complainants returned that amount on 29 August 2024. The Authority’s final calculation recorded zero refund from the promoter. Refund Right Survived the Resale The complainants initially sought cancellation of the third party’s sale deed and execution of a deed in their favour, with refund and interest as alternative relief. They later amended their complaint to seek refund with interest, compensation and litigation costs. The Authority allowed the amendment and proceeded on the amended prayers. The request to cancel the subsequent sale deed and convey the apartment to the complainants consequently fell outside the issues for determination. The Authority found that the complainants had shown interest in the buyback option and consented to a resale. It also found that the promoter had breached the agreements by missing the possession deadline. Once the promoter had conveyed the apartment to the subsequent purchaser, the Authority treated refund with interest as the available remedy. It relied on the contractual repayment obligation following resale and referred to Section 11(4), which makes promoters responsible for their statutory and contractual obligations to allottees. The Authority relied on the Supreme Court decisions in Newtech Promoters and Developers Private Limited v. State of Uttar Pradesh and Imperia Structures Limited v. Anil Patni. Under Section 18(1), an allottee who chooses to withdraw when a promoter fails to deliver possession as agreed can demand return of the amount paid with prescribed interest. An allottee who stays in the project can claim interest for each month of delay until possession. The complainants pursued the refund remedy through their amended complaint. Amount Awarded and Further Interest On 20 November 2026, Chairperson Rakesh Singh partly allowed Complaint No. 00877/2025. The Authority’s calculation comprised ₹2,87,98,492 in principal and ₹2,86,91,839 in interest through 19 July 2026. It calculated interest on the relevant instalments at 9% up to 30 April 2017 and applied the marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR) plus two percentage points for the period beginning 1 May 2017. The order directed payment within 60 days, with further interest from 20 July 2026 until final payment. The Authority directed the complainants to approach the Adjudicating Officer for their compensation claim and permitted them to enforce the order in accordance with law if the respondents failed to comply. It made no order as to costs. Cases Discussed: Newtech Promoters and Developers Pvt. Ltd. Vs State of Uttar Pradesh & Ors. (Supreme Court of India); Civil Appeal Nos. 6745–6749 of 2021 and connected appeals; 11/11/2021. An allottee possesses an unqualified right to seek refund with prescribed interest where the promoter fails to deliver possession within the agreed period. Imperia Structures Ltd. Vs Anil Patni & Anr. (Supreme Court of India); Civil Appeal Nos. 3581–3590 of 2020; 02/11/2020; (2020) 10 SCC 783. Section 18 of RERA confers an unqualified right to refund with prescribed interest where the allottee elects to withdraw from the project following delayed possession. Pioneer Urban Land & Infrastructure Ltd. Vs Geetu Gidwani Verma & Anr. (Supreme Court of India); Civil Appeal No. 1677 of 2019 with Civil Appeal No. 12238 of 2018; 02/04/2019. The Supreme Court upheld the relief granted to homebuyers on account of delayed delivery of possession. Bangalore Development Authority Vs Syndicate Bank (Supreme Court of India); (2007) 6 SCC 711. Failure to deliver possession within the specified period may entitle the allottee to refund with reasonable interest, depending upon the applicable contractual terms and circumstances. Fortune Infrastructure & Anr. Vs Trevor D’Lima & Ors. (Supreme Court of India); Civil Appeal Nos. 3533–3534 of 2017; 12/03/2018; (2018) 5 SCC 442. An allottee cannot be compelled to wait indefinitely for possession and may seek refund of the amount paid along with compensation. Army Welfare Housing Organisation Vs Capt. Nagarajan Vasudeva Rao (Madras High Court). The decision was cited concerning entitlement to compensation for delayed possession under Section 18 of RERA and the prescribed rate of interest under the Tamil Nadu RERA Rules. The Complaint is filed under section 31 of Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 against Project CENTURY ETHOS developed by Respondent/Promoter CENTURY REAL ESTATE HOLDINGS PRIVATE LIMITED situated at Century Ethos, Amruthahalli, Yelahanka Hobli, Bangalore North, Bengaluru North, Bengaluru Urban. 2. The complainants filed amendment application under Order 6 Rule 17 CPC r/w sec 88 of RERA Act for deletion of the above prayer and to add the relief for refund of entire sale consideration with interest for compensation under section 71 r/w Section 12, 18 and 19 of the RERA Act and for litigation cost. 4. The Project has been registered under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA), bearing Registration No. PRM/KA/RERA/1251/309/PR/171014/000283. The registration was valid up to 30.09.2020 including the extension granted up to 30.09.2021 on account of COVID-19 and the further extension granted under Section 6 of the RERA Act up to 30.09.2022. The brief fact of the complaint is as under:- 5. The Complainants have booked Plot no. C-802 on 10.07.2013 Project CENTURY ETHOS developed by Respondent/Promoter CENTURY REAL ESTATE HOLDINGS PRIVATE LIMITED, situated at Century Ethos, Amruthahalli, Yelahanka Hobli, Bangalore North, Bengaluru North, Bengaluru Urban. 6. The Complainants have booked an apartment No.C-802 in the respondent project “Century Ethos” on 10.07.2013. The Complainants paid a booking advance amount of Rs.40,00,000/- (Rs.20,00,000/- each for two units) on 10.07.2013. The Complainants subsequently selected Unit C-802 in the Chrome Block. The Sale Agreement and Construction Agreement was executed on 13.03.2014 for the total sale consideration of Rs.2,87,98,492/- (Rupees Two Crores Eighty Seven Lakhs Ninety Eight Thousand Four Hundred and Ninety Two only). Under Clause 3 of the Construction Agreement, the possession was to be delivered within 36 months from the receipt of the Commencement Certificate, plus 6-month grace period. Since the Commencement Certificate was issued on 20.01.2017, the final contractually mandated delivery date was established as 20.07.2020. However, Respondent No.1 failed to deliver apartment within the mandatory delivery date, despite receiving over 65% of the consideration by 2017. 7. In the month of January 2023, Respondent No.1’s CRM team approached the complainant and initiated discussions for a repurchase mandate, culminating in a draft agreement that materially altered oral timelines. Following unfulfilled extension promises by the Developer extending upto April 2024, the Complainants issued follow-up emails demanding interest and a 15-day notice to complete the transaction. Receiving no response, the Complainants formally revoked the resale arrangement on 26.08.2024, notifying Respondent No.1 of their intent to retain their interest and seek formal registration of the unit in their names upon issuance of the Occupancy Certificate. 8. The dispute escalated on 25.05.2025 when the Complainants visited the site and discovered third parties occupying the property. Subsequent inquiries revealed that Respondent No.1 had executed and registered a Sale Deed dated 15.05.2024 conveying Unit C-802 to Respondent No.2, without any prior cancellation notice, termination process, or refund to the Complainants. Aggrieved by this unilateral sale, suppression of material facts, and direct breach of agreement for sale and construction agreement of 2014, the Complainants have filed this action seeking primary reliefs under Section 18 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016. Hence, this complaint. 9. The Complainants have produced documents such as copies of Sale and Construction Agreement, Emails, Payment Receipts and Photographs. 10. It is pertinent note that the respondent No.1 is not in a position to cancel the sale deed executed in favour of respondent no.2 and consequently not in a position to execute the sale deed and hand over the possession to the complainants. The alternative remedy is available to the complainants for refund of the amount along with interest as per Section 18 and 19(4) of RERA Act. The higher market value of the property at the time of resale is also to be considered and it cannot be undervalued for the purpose of depriving the right of the complainants for refund with interest. 38. The Section 18 RERA Act stipulates that if a person fails to deliver possession, faces business discontinuation, or breach of the agreement they must return the amount received with interest. Section 19(4) specifies that allottee is entitled to claim refund, interest, and compensation if the promoter fails to deliver possession or violates the agreement for sale. Therefore, the section 18 and section 19 (4) of the RERA act can be made applicable in this case. 39. Hon’ble Supreme court in Appeal No.6750-57/2021 in the case of M/s Newtech promoters and developers Private Limited Versus State of UP & others, which is held as under: “Section 18(1) of the Act spells out the consequences possession of an apartments, plot or building either in terms of the agreements for sale or complete the project by the date specified therein or on account of discontinuance of his business as a revocation of the registration under the Act or any other reason, the allottee/home buyer holds an unqualified right to seek refund of the amount with interest at such rate as may be prescribed in this behalf”. 40. In the Judgment reported in Civil Appeals 3581-3590 of 2020 at Para 23 between M/s Imperia Structures Limited Vs Anil Patni & Another by Hon’ble Supreme Court it is held that: “In terms of section 18 of the RERA Act, if a promoter fails to complete or is unable to give possession of an apartment duly completed by the date specified in the agreement, the promoter would be liable, on demand, to return the amount received by him in respect of that apartment if the allottee wishes to withdraw from the project. Such right of an allottee is specifically made “without prejudice to any other remedy available to him”. The right so given to the allottee is unqualified and if availed, the money deposited by the allottee has to be refunded with interest at such rate as may be prescribed. The proviso of section 18(1) contemplates a situation where the allottee does not intend to withdraw from the project. In that case, he is entitled to and must be paid interest for every month of delay till the handing over of the possession. It is upto the allottee to proceed either under section 18(1) or under the provision of section 18(1). The case of Himanshu Giri came under the later category. The RERA Act thus definitely provides a remedy to an allottee who wishes to withdraw from the project or claim return on his investment.” 41.
