EconomyUS Fed Holds Rates: Will RBI Get Room To Rethink PolicyAuthored by: Vikas KumarUpdated: Oct 29, 2026, 10:12 ISTUS Federal Reserve decision has significant implications for India, influencing the rupee, foreign investments, and the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy. Geopolitical factors and energy prices will also be crucial for market performance.Kevin Warshfollow usfollow usThe US Federal Reserve has kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 3.75%-4%, as policymakers await more data on inflation before deciding on their next move. The decision comes amid persistent price pressures and a resilient US economy, with the central bank leaving the door open to further rate hikes if inflation remains elevated. Two policymakers dissented, favouring a 25-basis-point increase.In a 10-2 decision the US Fed said it would maintain its benchmark interest rate within the current 3.75%-4.00% range while acknowledging that inflation "remains elevated," upgrading its assessment of the economy, and hinting that further rate hikes may be coming depending on the performance of inflation."The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2% goal," the rate-setting Federal Open Market Committee said.Dhiraj Relli, MD & CEO, HDFC Securities on the announcement said, "The Fed described inflation as ‘elevated’ and said additional data would guide future policy decisions. The dollar weakened following the announcement, with the dollar index falling 0.2% to 102.15 and the euro rising to $1.1160. Market pricing now implies roughly an 85% probability of a Fed rate hike in December. Investor attention will now turn to the European Central Bank and Bank of Japan policy decisions on Thursday and Friday, both expected to keep rates unchanged. "Beyond central banks, the November 3 U.S. midterm elections will be a major macro event, as voters will determine control of Congress," he added.Prospects for India market: For India, the Fed’s decision has implications for the rupee, foreign investor flows, equity markets and the Reserve Bank of India’s monetary policy outlook. While a pause in US rate changes avoids an immediate increase in the relative attractiveness of dollar-denominated assets, the prospect of further tightening could keep global investors cautious about emerging markets"For domestic markets, geopolitical developments remain a key factor to monitor, as they could directly influence energy prices, risk sentiment, and inflation expectations. The ongoing quarterly earnings season will also remain an important driver of stock-specific and broader market performance," Dhiraj Relli said. The impact on the Indian rupee will depend largely on how investors interpret the Fed’s guidance and how the US dollar responds.Thomas Priju, Portfolio Manager, Karma Capital told Times Now Digital, "For Indian equities, the implications could be significant. A further increase in US interest rates could keep US bond yields and the dollar elevated, putting pressure on emerging-market capital flows and increasing volatility in the Nifty. High-valuation stocks and segments dependent on foreign portfolio flows could remain particularly sensitive to changes in global liquidity conditions.""However, the impact on Indian markets will also depend on domestic earnings growth, valuations and the trajectory of foreign investor flows. Investors should remain selective and focus on companies with strong earnings visibility and balance sheets, while monitoring US inflation data and the Fed’s subsequent communication for clues on the future rate trajectory."Will RBI change its policy stance?In its recently announced policy decision in October, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) had announced to hike the policy rate, a move coming after a series of pause. The Indian central bank will continue to prioritise domestic inflation and growth conditions. A prolonged period of elevated US interest rates could limit the RBI’s flexibility if the rupee comes under pressure, particularly if higher oil prices add to imported inflation.Emkay Global in its research report said, the US Fed move reinforces the RBI's reaction function, making the case for measured hikes ahead. The combination of elevated US rates, high oil prices, and weak natural dollar inflows argues for a higher Indian risk premium, limiting the RBI's ability to look through global tightening even though domestic policy remains primarily driven by inflation. "The October MPC minutes reinforce this: resilient growth gives the RBI room to respond, but members favor measured rather than aggressive tightening, with disagreement principally over how long the cycle ultimately needs to run.
