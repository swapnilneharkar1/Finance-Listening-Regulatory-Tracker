(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India dismissed Civil Appeal No. 9015 of 2026 filed by Shefali Agarwal against Stone Age Pvt. Ltd. and others, finding no good ground to interfere with the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT), Principal Bench, New Delhi, in Company Appeal (AT) No. 225 of 2023. The short Supreme Court order does not independently decide the underlying issues or provide detailed reasons. The NCLAT had dismissed an appeal against the NCLT Jaipur order dated 12 October 2023 in proceedings under Sections 241 and 242 of the Companies Act, 2013. The dispute concerned the appellant’s removal from directorship in 2012, dilution of her shareholding from 31% to approximately 17% following a 2013 allotment, a further 2017 dilution to 9.92%, and valuation of her shares. The NCLT had granted relief against the 2017 allotment and restored her holding to 17%, but declined to disturb the earlier events. Before the NCLAT, the appellant argued that the transactions constituted continuing oppression, that she was not properly notified of meetings, and that valuation should include Orvi Design Studio. The NCLAT held that challenges to the 2012 removal and 2013 allotment were barred by limitation, considering her January 2013 notice, the evidence concerning knowledge of the allotment, and the delay until the October 2018 petition. It also declined to entertain a new argument concerning the Articles of Association and upheld the registered-valuer mechanism, with valuation as of 12 October 2023 excluding the later Orvi transaction. The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal and disposed of pending applications, leaving the NCLAT decision undisturbed. Background of the Company Dispute The appellant became a director in 1994. Her shareholding reached 31% and she held 62,000 shares after the 2009 bonus allotment. Following marital differences with Respondent No. 2, she disputed her removal from directorship and successive allotments to others. She filed a company petition on 15 October 2018 alleging oppression and mismanagement. Issues Considered by the NCLAT Limitation and Knowledge of Earlier Transactions: The NCLAT held that the challenge to the 2012 directorship event and the 2013 allotment was time-barred. It considered the appellant’s legal notice dated 5 January 2013, her brother’s participation in the 2013 board meeting, and the parties’ pleadings concerning knowledge. It rejected the contention that continuing consequences alone revived claims relating to those earlier events. Notice of Meetings and New Appellate Grounds: The appellant alleged that proper notices had not been served and relied on Article 110 of the Articles of Association. The NCLAT declined to determine a new argument about reappointment and retirement by rotation that had not been raised before the NCLT. Valuation of Shares and Orvi Design Studio: The NCLT directed appointment of a registered valuer to value the company’s shares as of 12 October 2023, excluding the Orvi Design Studio acquisition. The NCLAT found no reason to interfere, noting that the appellant had opportunities to make submissions before the valuer and NCLT but had not used them. Supreme Court’s Order The Supreme Court, after hearing counsel and examining the record, found no good ground to interfere with the NCLAT order. It dismissed the appeal and disposed of pending applications. The Supreme Court did not separately endorse each proposition in the NCLAT’s reasoning through a reasoned judgment.
