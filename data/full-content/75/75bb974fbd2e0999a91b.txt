Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Powerlite Generator Systems Private Limited Vs Commissioner State Tax (GSTAT Agra) Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), Agra Bench, held that the mere existence of earlier invoices and e-way bills, subsequently replaced by fresh invoices and e-way bills for the same transactions, could not justify detention, seizure and imposition of penalty under Section 129 of the CGST/UPGST Act without evidence of a statutory contravention. Powerlite Generator Systems Private Limited challenged the order dated 25 September 2025 passed by the Additional Commissioner, Grade-II (Appeals), State Tax, Agra, confirming the seizure and penalty proceedings for the tax period 2025-26. On 25 May 2025, the Assistant Commissioner, Mobile Squad-IV, Agra, intercepted a vehicle transporting three old generators from Sonipat, Haryana, to Hyderabad, Telangana. The driver produced three tax invoices and corresponding e-way bills issued by Balaji Generator Service, Mahalaxmi Generator Services and WAE Limited. Upon verification of the GST e-way bill portal, the Mobile Squad Officer noticed that Mahalaxmi Generator Services and WAE Limited had previously generated separate invoices and e-way bills for the same transactions. Suspecting that multiple invoices and e-way bills had been generated with an intention to evade tax, the officer detained and subsequently seized two of the three generators. The goods were released upon payment of a penalty of Rs. 2,16,000. The first appellate authority dismissed the taxpayer’s appeal. Before GSTAT, the taxpayer submitted that the earlier invoices and e-way bills had not been acted upon because the goods could not be dispatched on the originally scheduled dates. Fresh invoices and corresponding e-way bills were therefore generated for the actual movement of goods. The earlier e-way bills continued to appear on the portal because the concerned suppliers could not cancel them. The taxpayer further contended that the Department had neither established transportation of additional goods nor identified any discrepancy between the physical goods and the accompanying documents. Moreover, the first appellate authority had itself recorded that the earlier invoices were not reflected in GSTR-2B, and there was no evidence of wrongful availment or passing on of input tax credit. The Tribunal observed that Section 129 is attracted when goods are transported in contravention of the provisions of the GST Act or the rules framed thereunder. Consequently, the Department must establish the relevant contravention before initiating proceedings under that provision. In the present case, all three generators were accompanied by valid invoices and corresponding e-way bills. No finding had been recorded that the physical goods differed from those described in the accompanying documents. GSTAT therefore found that the transportation complied with Rules 138 and 138A of the CGST/UPGST Rules. The Tribunal further rejected the Department’s suspicion that multiple invoices could have been used for wrongful availment or passing on of input tax credit. No material had been brought on record to establish that the taxpayer had actually availed ITC twice against the same supply or that the suppliers had passed on duplicate credit. GSTAT clarified that issues concerning wrongful availment or passing on of ITC must be examined through appropriate proceedings by the competent jurisdictional authority. The Mobile Squad Officer had no jurisdiction to adjudicate ITC availment, and an unsupported possibility of duplicate credit could not constitute the sole basis for detention and seizure under Section 129. Accordingly, GSTAT concluded that the Department had failed to establish any contravention of the GST Act or Rules in the transportation of goods. The Tribunal allowed the appeal, set aside both the first appellate order dated 25 September 2025 and the original order passed by the proper officer, and held that the penalty of Rs. 2,16,000 was unsustainable. Since the amount had already been deposited for release of the seized goods, the taxpayer was held entitled to consequential refund in accordance with the applicable statutory provisions, subject to verification and permissible adjustment. The Department was directed to give effect to the order in accordance with law. No order as to costs was passed. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF GST APPELLATE TRIBUNAL, AGRA 1. This second appeal has been preferred by M/s Powerlite Generator Systems Private Limited, bearing GSTIN/Temporary ID 36AAHCP4263A1ZO/092500025921TMP, against the order dated 25.09.2025 passed by the Additional Commissioner, Grade-II (Appeals II), State Tax, Agra, in Appeal No. AD0907250202191/2025 for the tax period 2025-26, whereby the appeal filed by the appellant against the seizure and penalty proceedings initiated under Section 129(3) of the CGST/UPGST Act was dismissed. For the sake of convenience, the appellant shall hereinafter be referred to as the taxpayer and the respondent as the Department. Brief facts of the case- 2. The facts giving rise to the present appeal are that on 25.05.2025, Shri Sachin Kumar, Assistant Commissioner, Mobile Squad-IV, Agra, intercepted vehicle No. HR38 X3514 carrying three old generators form Sonipat, Haryana to the destination Hyderabad, Telangana. The vehicle in which generators were transported was intercepted in transit on 25.05.2025 and at the time of interception, the driver produced the following documents- (i) Invoice No. 2643 dated 21.05.2025 issued by M/s Balaji Generator Service, Sonipat, Haryana, along with E-way Bill No. 382004429406 dated 21.05.2025. (ii) Invoice No. 1517 dated 22.05.2025 issued by M/s Mahalaxmi Generator Services, Kurukshetra, Haryana, along with E-way Bill No. 322004745627 dated 22.05.2025. (iii) Invoice No. W/SR/2526/230541 dated 22.05.2025 issued by M/s WAE Limited, Noida, Uttar Pradesh, along with E-way Bill No. 471570994035 dated 22.05.2025. 3. Upon examination of the documents and verification through the E-Way Bill portal, the Mobile Squad Officer noticed that M/s Mahalaxmi Generator Services had also generated an earlier invoice No. 1515 dated 21.05.2025 and corresponding E-way Bill No. 382004102437 dated 21.05.2025 for the same transaction and the same vehicle. Similarly, M/s WAE Limited had earlier issued Invoice No. W/SR/2526/230503 dated 19.05.2025 and generated E-way Bill No. 491570951573 in respect of the same transaction. 4. On the basis of the existence of these earlier invoices and e-way bills, the Mobile Squad Officer formed the opinion that multiple invoices and e-way bills had been generated for the same transactions with an intention to evade tax. Consequently, two out of the three generators were detained and subsequently seized. The goods were released upon payment of penalty amounting to Rs. 2,16,000/-. 5- Aggrieved by the action of the Mobile Squad Officer, the taxpayer preferred an appeal before the Additional Commissioner, Grade-II (Appeals), Agra. However, the first appellate authority dismissed the appeal vide order dated 25.09.2025. Hence, the present second appeal. Submissions of the appellant 6. Shri Akash Singhal, learned counsel appearing on behalf of the appellant, submitted that the goods in question were being transported under valid tax invoices and e-way bills generated in accordance with Rule 138 of the CGST/UPGST Rules. 7. It was submitted that all the three generators were covered by the corresponding tax invoices and e-way bills, which were duly produced before the Mobile Squad Officer at the time of interception. 8. Learned counsel further stated that the earlier invoice No. 1515 dated 21.05.2025 issued by M/s Mahalaxmi Generator Services could not be acted upon because the goods were not dispatched on that date. Consequently, a fresh invoice No. 1517 dated 22.05.2025 was issued and a fresh e-way bill was generated for the actual movement of goods. 9. Likewise, M/s WAE Limited had initially issued Invoice No. W/SR/2526/230503 dated 19.05.2025, but owing to delay in dispatch, a fresh invoice No. W/SR/2526/230541 dated 22.05.2025 was issued and a corresponding fresh e-way bill was generated. 10. It was further submitted that the earlier e-way bills remained reflected on the portal because the concerned suppliers could not cancel them. However, the goods were actually transported under the subsequently issued invoices and corresponding e-way bills. 11. Learned counsel contended that the Department had not established that any additional goods were being transported, that any goods were found in excess of the quantity mentioned in the documents, or that the taxpayer had attempted to evade payment of tax. 12. It was also submitted that the first appellate authority had itself observed that the earlier bills were not reflected in GSTR-2B. Therefore, the allegation of wrongful availment or passing on of input tax credit was without any supporting material. 13. Accordingly, it was argued that mere generation of multiple e-way bills for the same transaction, particularly when the earlier documents were not acted upon for actual transportation, could not constitute sufficient ground for invoking Section 129 of the Act. Submissions of the Department 14. Shri Mukesh Kumar, Deputy Commissioner/Authorized Departmental Representative, appearing on behalf of the Department, supported the impugned appellate order. 15. It was submitted that multiple invoices and e-way bills had been generated by the suppliers for the same transactions and the same vehicle, which indicated an attempt to evade tax. Accordingly, the seizure and penalty proceedings initiated by the Mobile Squad Officer were justified. 16. The Department prayed that the impugned appellate order be affirmed and the second appeal be dismissed. Discussion and findings 17. We have carefully considered the submissions advanced by the learned counsel for the appellant and the learned Departmental Representative. We have also examined the facts and circumstances of the case and the material available on record. 18. The principal issue requiring determination in the present appeal is whether the mere existence of earlier invoices and e-way bills, subsequently replaced by fresh invoices and e-way bills for the same transactions, is sufficient to establish contravention of the provisions of the Act and justify detention, seizure and imposition of penalty under Section 129 of the CGST/UPGST Act. 19. At the outset, it is relevant to observe that the provisions of Section 129 are attracted when goods are transported in contravention of the provisions of the Act or the Rules made thereunder. Therefore, before initiating proceedings under the said provision, the Department is required to establish the relevant contravention on the basis of the facts and circumstances of the particular case. 20. In the present case, it is an undisputed position that the vehicle was carrying three old generators and that the driver produced three tax invoices along with their corresponding e-way bills at the time of interception. The details of the goods and the transportation documents were available before the Mobile Squad Officer. 21. There is also no finding recorded by the Mobile Squad Officer that the physical goods found in the vehicle were different from the goods covered by the invoices and e-way bills produced by the driver. This clearly indicate that the goods were being transported with valid documents as per the provisions of rule 138 and 138 A and there was no contravention of any provision of act and rules. 22. In the present case, the goods were accompanied by the subsequently issued tax invoices and corresponding e-way bills. The Department has not established any discrepancy between the actual goods transported and the documents accompanying the vehicle. 23. The Department’s suspicion is based upon the possibility that multiple invoices could have been used for passing on or availing input tax credit. However, the first appellate authority itself recorded that the earlier bills were not reflected in GSTR-2B. 24. In any event, no material has been brought on record to establish that the taxpayer actually availed input tax credit twice against the same supply or that the suppliers passed on any duplicate input tax credit. 25. Any issue relating to wrongful availment or passing on of input tax credit is required to be examined in appropriate proceedings by the competent jurisdictional authority in accordance with the provisions of the Act and mobile squad officer has no jurisdiction to adjudicate availment of Input Tax Credit and such a possibility, without any supporting evidence, cannot be made the sole basis for detention and seizure of goods under Section 129. 26. On an overall consideration of the facts and circumstances of the case, we are of the considered opinion that the Department has not been able to establish that the goods were transported without valid documents. 27. On the basis of foregoing discussion, it is clear that in this case goods were being transported in compliance with the legal provisions and there was no contravention of any provision of the act or the rules in the transportation of the goods. 28. We, therefore, find that the seizure proceedings initiated against the appellant, as well as the consequential imposition of penalty amounting to Rs. 2,16,000/-, are not sustainable on the facts and material available on record and the appellant is accordingly entitled to relief. ORDER: 29. In view of the foregoing discussion and findings, this appeal is hereby allowed and the impugned order dated 25.09.2025 passed by the Additional Commissioner, Grade-II (Appeals), State Tax, Agra, in Appeal No. AD0907250202191/2025 as well Order in Original passed by proper officer are hereby set aside. 30. Since the aforesaid amount of penalty has already been deposited by the appellant for release of the seized goods, the appellant shall be entitled to consequential refund of the amount deposited, in accordance with the applicable provisions of the Act and Rules, subject to verification and adjustment, if any, permissible under law. The Department is directed to give effect to this order in accordance with law. 31. No order as to cost. 32. There is no pending application in the appeal. 33. Pronounced in the open court on this 19th, day of November 2026. 34. Order be communicated to all concerned as per rules.
