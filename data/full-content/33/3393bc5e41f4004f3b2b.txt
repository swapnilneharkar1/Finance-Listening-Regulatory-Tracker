Advt This comes at a time when financial institutions are grappling with the risks of AI systems accessing internal applications, sensitive data and enterprise infrastructure. Last week, a software bug at JPMorgan Chase disrupted some customer transactions, with knock-on effects on transactions involving other banks. Days later, the bank disclosed a separate cyberattack on third-party service providers that may have exposed customer names and email addresses held in marketing data. Visa and Mastercard also reported incidents involving third-party providers.The growing use of AI is adding another layer of risk. In October, major US banks including JPM, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Morgan Stanley detected third-party AI agents accessing customer accounts, in some cases appearing to act beyond users' instructions.As a result, cybersecurity work, traditionally centred on security operations centres (SOCs), monitoring, vulnerability management and compliance, is moving up the value chain, and GCCs are playing a key role. Experts and top-level executives in these GCCs said threat intelligence and hunting, security engineering, cloud security and forensics currently have high adoption, while security architecture is emerging as the next frontier.ANSR Research said AI security has become the top-priority skill for GCCs with cybersecurity capabilities. Cyber resilience, cloud security and cyber product automation are currently among the leading next-gen functions delivered from India's BFSI GCCs. Senior India-based executives are increasingly taking ownership of security standards, policies, best practices and governance. While their global headquarters retain responsibility for risk appetite, regulatory accountability and board-level reporting, the Indian operations are assuming a larger share of global cybersecurity ownership. Advt JPM said its India teams are a key part of its global technology and cybersecurity organisation. "Security and resilience are top priorities for JPMorganChase and something we invest in continuously," said Vibhavari Jahagirdar, head of global technology, India, JPMorganChase. "Our teams in India are an important part of our global technology and cybersecurity organisation, working alongside colleagues around the world as we continue to strengthen the resilience of the firm and deploy AI responsibly."
