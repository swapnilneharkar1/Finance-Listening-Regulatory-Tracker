Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); In re West Gujarat Expressway Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Summary : The National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai Bench-II, admitted West Gujarat Expressway Limited’s petition to initiate the corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP) against itself under Section 10 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC). The company filed its petition on 25 February 2026 under Rule 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016 in Form 6. The Bench found territorial jurisdiction under Section 60(1) because the company’s registered office was in Mumbai. IL&FS Transportation Networks Limited (ITNL) and Infrastructure Leasing & Financial Services Limited (IL&FS) incorporated the company as a special purpose vehicle in March 2005. Under a 20-year concession agreement with the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI), it developed, operated and maintained the Jetpur–Gondal–Rajkot section of NH-8B on a Build-Operate-Transfer basis. NHAI issued a termination notice on 21 December 2021. The NCLAT vacated its interim protection on 16 March 2022, and the Delhi High Court subsequently referred the parties to conciliation, followed by arbitration. The company handed over the project to NHAI on 1 April 2022 and ceased significant business operations. The senior lenders, L&T Finance and L&T Infra Credit, later assigned their debt to CFM ARC. The company’s insolvency application followed the IL&FS group resolution process. The NCLT superseded the IL&FS board on 1 October 2018 under Sections 241 and 242 of the Companies Act, 2013. The NCLAT stayed proceedings against the group on 15 October 2018 and approved its resolution framework, including the Revised Distribution Framework, on 12 March 2020 under the supervision of Justice (Retd.) D.K. Jain. The NCLAT classified West Gujarat Expressway as a “Red Entity” on 6 December 2022. The company disclosed that it had ceased providing for interest on its debts from that date. Following an in-principle settlement recorded in July 2024, NHAI and the company executed a settlement agreement under Section 73 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 on 1 July 2025. The settlement amount was ₹71 crore, of which the company received ₹69.53 crore after deductions. NHAI’s lien of ₹75 crore was released in August 2025. By order dated 27 May 2025, the NCLT permitted the company to file under Section 10 after distributing available amounts in accordance with the Revised Distribution Framework. It also permitted reimbursement of ₹1,43,22,334 to ITNL and withdrawal of ₹93,85,900 to maintain the company until 31 March 2026. The company reported completing a final distribution of ₹79.05 crore on 10 December 2025. The Bench found the filing duly authorised under Section 10(3). The company’s board, ITNL’s board, the new IL&FS board and the shareholders approved the proposal following Alvarez & Marsal’s recommendation. Justice (Retd.) D.K. Jain approved it on 3 February 2025. The Bench held that the Serious Fraud Investigation Office investigation did not bar admission and directed the resolution professional to take it into account when discharging duties, including under Sections 43 to 51 and 66 of the IBC. The FY 2024–25 audited accounts, prepared on a non-going-concern basis, showed assets of ₹85,34,10,000, liabilities of ₹3,19,81,00,000 and negative net worth of ₹2,34,46,90,000, with ₹54,40,40,000 in interest unprovided for. Grant Thornton recorded claims of ₹3,85,61,06,573, of which ₹3,77,14,77,804 was admitted. The company separately disclosed pending government dues of ₹2,03,48,870, unadmitted claims of ₹6,42,79,899 and a subsisting charge of ₹1,41,26,00,000 in favour of IDBI Trusteeship Services Limited. The Bench found the debt in default of ₹2,27,20,58,957.71 as of 14 January 2026 exceeded the ₹1 crore threshold under Section 4(1). On limitation, the Bench relied on acknowledgments in the audited accounts, including those for FY 2024–25, and applied Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963 read with Section 238A of the IBC. It found no material showing ineligibility under Section 11. Section 10A did not apply because the default predated the suspension period beginning 25 March 2020. The Bench also held that the company’s MCA status of “Active – Non-Compliant,” the date of its last annual general meeting and its non-going-concern status did not defeat maintainability under Section 10. The Bench declined as premature the company’s request to distribute future recoveries under the Revised Distribution Framework. It reasoned that such a direction would pre-empt the statutory role of the Committee of Creditors and the waterfall under Section 53. The resolution professional must report any recovery to the NCLT and act under the IBC and applicable tribunal directions. The Bench granted liberty to apply. The NCLT admitted the petition under Section 10(4)(a), declared a moratorium under Section 14 and appointed Parshva Manoj Shah as Interim Resolution Professional (IRP), recording that no disciplinary proceedings were pending against him. It directed immediate public announcement, transfer of management to the IRP or resolution professional, cooperation by the company’s officers and periodic reporting. The operative order also directed the Financial Creditor to deposit ₹2 lakh for initial CIRP costs, subject to approval of expenses by the Committee of Creditors. Cases Discussed C.A. FULL TEXT OF THE NCLT JUDGMENT/ORDER 1. BACKGROUND 1.1 The present C.P. (IB)/218(MB)2026 was filed on 25.02.2026 by West Gujarat Expressway Limited (hereinafter referred to as the “Corporate Applicant” or the “Corporate Debtor”) under Section 10 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (hereinafter referred to as the “Code”), read with Rule 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016 (hereinafter referred to as the “AA Rules”), in Form 6, seeking initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (“CIRP”) against itself. 1.2 The Corporate Debtor was incorporated on 14.03.2005 as a special purpose vehicle (“SPV”) by IL&FS Transportation Networks Limited (“ITNL”) and Infrastructure Leasing & Financial Services Limited (“IL&FS”). Its Corporate Identity Number (“CIN”) is U60200MH2005PLC151958, and it is registered with the Registrar of Companies, Mumbai. The Corporate Debtor is an unlisted public company having its registered office at the IL&FS Financial Centre, Bandra-Kurla Complex, Mumbai. Accordingly, this Bench has territorial jurisdiction to entertain the present Petition in terms of Section 60(1) of the Code. 1.3 The authorised and paid-up share capital of the Corporate Debtor is stated to be ₹40,00,00,000/-, comprising 2,00,00,000 equity shares and 2,00,00,000 preference shares of ₹10/- each. As on 08.08.2025, IL&FS held 52,00,015 equity shares, representing 26.00% of the equity share capital, while ITNL, together with its nominees, held 1,47,99,925 equity shares, representing 73.99%. ITNL also held the entire preference share capital of the Corporate Debtor. The relevant particulars are set out in Annexures A, B and C. The master data available on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (“MCA”) portal reflects the status of the Corporate Debtor as “Active – Non-Compliant”, with its last Annual General Meeting having been held on 28.09.2022. 2. CONTENTION OF CORPORATE DEBTOR/APPLICANT 2.1 On 22.03.2005, the Corporate Debtor entered into a Concession Agreement with the National Highways Authority of India (“NHAI”) for the development, operation and maintenance of the Jetpur–Gondal–Rajkot section of National Highway No. 8B under the Build-Operate-Transfer (“BOT”) model. The Appointed Date under the Concession Agreement was 17.09.2005. The completion certificate was issued on 14.08.2008, and toll operations commenced on 16.05.2008. The concession was granted for a period of 20 years. 2.2 By an order dated 01.10.2018 passed in C.P. No. 3638/2018, instituted under Sections 241 and 242 of the Companies Act, 2013, this Tribunal superseded the erstwhile Board of Directors of IL&FS and appointed a New Board. Subsequently, by an order dated 15.10.2018, the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (“NCLAT”) stayed proceedings against the IL&FS Group. On 12.03.2020, the NCLAT approved the Resolution Framework, including the Revised Distribution Framework, under the supervision of Justice (Retd.) D.K. Jain. 2.3 Thereafter, by an order dated 06.12.2022, the NCLAT classified the Corporate Debtor as a “Red Entity”. As recorded in Note 28 to the audited financial statements for the financial year 2024–25, the Corporate Debtor has not provided for interest on its debts from the date of such classification. 2.4 NHAI issued a notice of termination of the Concession Agreement on 21.12.2021. The interim order dated 23.12.2021 passed by the NCLAT in I.A. No. 2908/2021 in Company Appeal (AT) No. 346/2018 was subsequently vacated by an order dated 16.03.2022. By an order dated 30.03.2022, passed by the High Court of Delhi in proceedings under Section 9 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, the parties were referred to conciliation, followed by arbitration. 2.5 The Project was handed over to NHAI on 01.04.2022, following which the Corporate Debtor ceased to undertake any significant business operations. The debt owed to the senior lenders, namely L&T Finance and L&T Infra Credit, was subsequently assigned to CFM ARC. 2.6 The Committee for Consideration of Infrastructure Entities (“CCIE-2”), on 24.07.2024, recorded an in-principle settlement involving payment of ₹71,00,00,000/- by NHAI to the Corporate Debtor. Pursuant thereto, a Settlement Agreement was executed on 01.07.2025 under Section 73 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, as set out in Annexure P. After applicable deductions, the Corporate Debtor received a sum of ₹69,53,00,000/- on 04.07.2025. The lien of ₹75,00,00,000/- marked by NHAI was released on 05.08.2025. 2.7 The resolution consultant, Alvarez & Marsal, by its recommendation dated 10.10.2024, recommended initiation of CIRP against the Corporate Debtor under Section 10 of the Code, following distribution of the available amounts among the creditors in accordance with the applicable framework. 2.8 The proposal for initiation of CIRP was thereafter considered and approved at various levels, as follows: a. The Board of the Corporate Debtor, by circulation, on 13.12.2024; b. The Board of ITNL on 13.12.2024; c. The New Board on 22.12.2024; d. The shareholders on 23.12.2024; e. By way of a memorandum submitted to Justice (Retd.) D.K. Jain on 09.01.2025; and f. By approval granted by Justice (Retd.) D.K. Jain on 03.02.2025. 2.9 By an order dated 27.05.2025, passed in C.A. No. 66/2025 in C.P. No. 3638(MB)/2018, this Tribunal permitted the Corporate Debtor to file an appropriate petition for initiation of CIRP under Section 10 of the Code, subject to undertaking distribution of the available distributable amounts in accordance with the Revised Distribution Framework. The relevant order forms part of Annexure O. The aforesaid order also permitted: a. Reimbursement of ₹1,43,22,334/- to ITNL; and b. Withdrawal of ₹93,85,900/- towards payments required for maintaining the Corporate Debtor as a going concern until 31.03.2026. 2.10 The aforesaid permission was granted without prejudice to the investigation being conducted by the Serious Fraud Investigation Office (“SFIO”). The Corporate Debtor states that the final distribution of ₹79,05,00,000/- was completed on 10.12.2025. 2.11 As per the report of Grant Thornton dated 30.09.2022, prepared on the basis of claims received up to 18.08.2022 and the creditors as on 15.10.2018, claims aggregating to ₹3,85,61,06,573/- were received, out of which claims amounting to ₹3,77,14,77,804/- were admitted. The report forms part of Annexure T. 2.12 The Corporate Debtor has further disclosed that a claim of ₹2,03,48,870/- towards government dues remained pending before various forums, while claims aggregating to ₹6,42,79,899/- were not admitted. The details of the amounts outstanding as on 31.03.2025, as disclosed in paragraph 7.2 of Form 6, are set out in the Petition. 2.13 The audited financial statements of the Corporate Debtor for the financial year 2024–25, audited by N.M. Raiji & Co., reflect total assets of ₹85,34,10,000/-, total liabilities of ₹3,19,81,00,000/-, and a negative net worth of ₹2,34,46,90,000/-. The said financial statements have been prepared on a non-going-concern basis. Further, interest aggregating to ₹54,40,40,000/- has not been provided for up to 31.03.2025, as disclosed in the financial statements. 2.14 As disclosed in Part III of Form 6, the total debt in default as on 14.01.2026 is stated to be ₹2,27,20,58,957.71/-, which exceeds the minimum default threshold of ₹1,00,00,000/- prescribed under Section 4(1) of the Code. The creditor-wise particulars and amounts claimed to be in default are set out in Annexure S to the Petition. 2.15 The Corporate Debtor has also disclosed a charge in favour of IDBI Trusteeship Services Limited for ₹1,41,26,00,000/-. The charge was created on 09.12.2014, bearing Charge ID 10543561, with the Certificate of Registration of Charge dated 22.01.2015. The relevant filings include Form PAS-3 dated 05.01.2015 and Form CHG-9 dated 08.01.2015. 2.16 The Corporate Debtor has proposed the appointment of Mr. Parshva Manoj Shah as the Interim Resolution Professional (“IRP”), bearing Registration No. IBBI/IPA-001/IPP-02538/2025-2026/14518. His written consent in Form 2 is dated 09.07.2025. The relevant documents form part of Annexure R. 2.17 The Corporate Debtor has stated that no winding-up or insolvency proceedings are pending against it. It has further contended that its outstanding dues have remained due since October 2018 and that the liabilities have been acknowledged in its audited financial statements. On this basis, the Corporate Debtor asserts that the present Petition is not barred by limitation. 2.18 The Corporate Debtor has also sought a direction that any amounts recovered in the future be distributed by the Resolution Professional or Liquidator, as the case may be, in accordance with the Revised Distribution Framework. 3. ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS 3.1 Heard the learned counsel for the Corporate Applicant. The Bench has perused the Petition, the annexures and the material on record. 3.2 The Petition was filed on 25.02.2026 under Section 10 of the Code, read with Rule 7 of the AAA Rules, in Form-6, and the requisite particulars have been furnished. The Corporate Applicant is a public company incorporated on 14.03.2005 (CIN: U60200MH2005PLC151958), with its registered office at IL&FS Financial Centre, Bandra-Kurla Complex, Mumbai. This Bench therefore has territorial jurisdiction under Section 60(1) of the Code. 3.3 The Corporate Applicant was incorporated as a special purpose vehicle by IL&FS Transportation Networks Limited (“ITNL”) and Infrastructure Leasing & Financial Services Limited (“IL&FS”). Under a Concession Agreement dated 22.03.2005 with the National Highways Authority of India (“NHAI”), it developed the Jetpur–Gondal–Rajkot section of NH-8B on a Build-Operate-Transfer basis for 20 years. NHAI issued a termination notice on 21.12.2021, and the Project was handed over on 01.04.2022. The Corporate Applicant has had no significant business operations since. 3.4 The Corporate Applicant is part of the IL&FS Group, which is governed by the Resolution Framework approved by the Hon’ble NCLAT on 12.03.2020 under the supervision of Justice (Retd.) D.K. Jain. By order dated 06.12.2022, the Hon’ble NCLAT classified it as a “Red Entity”. By order dated 27.05.2025 in C.A. No. 66/2025 in C.P. No. 3638(MB)/2018, this Tribunal permitted it to file a petition under Section 10, subject to distribution of the available amounts, including those received under the Settlement Agreement dated 01.07.2025 with NHAI (Annexure P), in accordance with the Revised Distribution Framework. The Corporate Applicant states that the final distribution of ₹79,05,00,000/- was completed on 10.12.2025. The permission was granted without prejudice to the investigation by the Serious Fraud Investigation Office (“SFIO”). That investigation is no bar to admission, and the Resolution Professional shall take note of it in discharging his duties, including under Sections 43 to 51 and 66 of the Code. 3.5 In compliance with Section 10(3) of the Code, the filing of the Petition stands authorised by the Board of the Corporate Applicant by the board resolution dated 13.12.2024, the Board of ITNL (13.12.2024), the New Board (22.12.2024) and the shareholders (23.12.2024), and was approved by Justice (Retd.) D.K. Jain on 03.02.2025 on a memorandum submitted on 09.01.2025. The Petition follows the recommendation of Alvarez & Marsal dated 10.10.2024. 3.6 The audited financial statements for FY 2024–25, audited by M/s N.M. Raiji & Co. and prepared on a non-going-concern basis, show total assets of ₹85,34,10,000/- against total liabilities of ₹3,19,81,00,000/-, resulting in a negative net worth of ₹2,34,46,90,000/-. Interest of ₹54,40,40,000/- has not been provided for (Note 28), consequent upon the “Red Entity” classification. [Insert FY 2025–26 figures, if filed.] Part III of Form 6 and Annexure S disclose a total debt in default of ₹2,27,20,58,957.71/- as on 14.01.2026. This is consistent with the Grant Thornton report dated 30.09.2022, which records claims of ₹3,85,61,06,573/- received and ₹3,77,14,77,804/- admitted. The senior lenders’ debt (L&T Finance and L&T Infra Credit) has been assigned to CFM ARC, and a charge for ₹1,41,26,00,000/- (Charge ID 10543561) subsists in favour of IDBI Trusteeship Services Limited. The debt is due and payable, and it far exceeds the threshold of ₹1,00,00,000/- under Section 4(1) of the Code. 3.7 As to limitation, the dues have remained outstanding since October 2018 and have been acknowledged in the audited financial statements, including those for FY 2024–25. Under Section 18 of the Limitation Act, 1963, read with Section 238A of the Code, a written acknowledgment made before expiry of the prescribed period gives rise to a fresh period of limitation. The Petition is therefore not barred by limitation. [Insert authority. Consider noting the NCLAT stay of 15.10.2018.] 3.8 The Corporate Applicant states that no winding-up or insolvency proceeding is pending against it, and nothing on record shows that it is ineligible under Section 11 of the Code. As the default predates the period 25.03.2020 to 24.03.2021, Section 10A is not attracted. Its MCA status of “Active – Non-Compliant” and the last AGM held on 28.09.2022 have no bearing on maintainability, as Section 10 neither requires compliance with the Companies Act, 2013 nor that the applicant be a going concern. 3.9 The Corporate Applicant proposes Mr. Parshva Manoj Shah (Reg. No. IBBI/IPA-001/IPP-02538/2025-2026/14518) as Interim Resolution Professional (“IRP”). His Form 2 consent dated 09.07.2025 records that no disciplinary proceedings are pending against him. 3.10 The prayer that future recoveries be distributed under the Revised Distribution Framework would, at this stage, pre-empt the statutory scheme, including the role of the Committee of Creditors and the waterfall under Section 53. It is premature and is not entertained. The Resolution Professional shall report any recovery to this Tribunal and act in accordance with the Code and any applicable directions of this Tribunal or the Hon’ble NCLAT. Liberty to apply is granted. 3.11 In view of the foregoing, this Bench is satisfied that the Petition is complete, duly authorised and within limitation, that a default above the threshold under Section 4(1) is established, and that no disciplinary proceedings are pending against the proposed IRP. There is no reason to deny admission. Accordingly, this Adjudicating Authority, under Section 10(4)(a) of the Code, admits the Petition and orders initiation of CIRP against the Corporate Applicant. ORDER In the result, C.P. (IB)/218(MB)/2026, filed by West Gujarat Expressway Limited, the Corporate Applicant/Corporate Debtor, under Section 10 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, read with Rule 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016, seeking initiation of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process against itself, is admitted. We further declare moratorium under Section 14 of the Code, with consequential directions as follows: I. We prohibit- a) the institution of suits or continuation of pending suits or proceedings against the Corporate Debtor including execution of any judgment, decree or order in any court of law, tribunal, arbitration panel or other authority; b) transferring, encumbering, alienating or disposing of by the Corporate Debtor any of its assets or any legal right or beneficial interest therein; any action to foreclose, recover or enforce any security interest created by the Corporate Debtor in respect of its property including any action under the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002 (54 of 2002); c) the recovery of any property by an owner or lessor where such property is occupied by or in possession of the Corporate Debtor. II. That the order of moratorium shall have effect from the date of this order till the completion of the CIRP or until this Bench approves the resolution plan under section 31(1) of the Code or passes an order for the liquidation of the Corporate Debtor under section 33 thereof, as the case may be. III. Notwithstanding the above, during the period of moratorium: – (a) The supply of essential goods or services to the corporate debtor, if continuing, shall not be terminated or suspended or interrupted during the moratorium period; (b) That the provisions of sub-section (1) of Section 14 of the Code shall not apply to- (i) Such transactions as may be notified by the Central Government in consultation with any financial sector regulator or any other authority; (ii) A surety in a contract of guarantee to a corporate debtor. IV. That the public announcement of the CIRP shall be made in immediately as specified under Section 13 of the Code read with Regulation 6 of the IBBI (Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons) Regulations, 2016 and other Rules and Regulations made thereunder. V. That this Bench hereby appoints Mr. Parshva Manoj Shah, a registered Insolvency Professional having Registration No. IBBI/IPA-001/IP-P-02538/2025-2026/14518 having email address: [email protected] and valid Authorisation for Assignment up to 30.06.2027 as the IRP to carry out the functions under the Code. VI. The fee payable to IRP/Resolution Professional (hereinafter referred to as “RP”) shall be in accordance with the Regulations/Circulars issued by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (hereinafter referred to as “IBBI”). VII. That during the CIRP Period, the management of the Corporate Debtor shall vest in the IRP or, as the case may be, the RP in terms of Section 17 or Section 25, as the case may be, of the Code. The officers and managers of the Corporate Debtor are directed to provide effective assistance to the IRP as and when he takes charge of the assets and management of the Corporate Debtor. The officers and managers of the Corporate Debtor shall provide all documents in their possession and furnish every information in their knowledge to the IRP/RP within a period of one week from the date of receipt of this Order and shall not commit any offence punishable under Chapter VII of Part II of the Code. Coercive steps will follow against them under the provisions of the Code read with Rule 11 of the National Company Law Tribunal Rules, 2016 (hereinafter referred to as “NCLT Rules”) for any violation of law. VIII. That the IRP/IP shall submit to this Tribunal periodical reports with regard to the progress of the CIRP in respect of the Corporate Debtor. IX. In exercise of the powers under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016, the Financial Creditor is directed to deposit a sum of Rs. 2,00,000/- (Two Lakh Rupees) with the IRP to meet the initial CIRP cost arising out of issuing public notice and inviting claims, etc. The amount so deposited shall be interim finance and paid back to the Financial Creditor on priority upon the funds becoming available with IRP/RP from the Committee of Creditors (hereinafter referred to as “CoC”). The expenses incurred by IRP out of this fund are subject to approval by the CoC. X. A copy of this Order be sent to the Registrar of Companies, Mumbai, Maharashtra, for updating the Master Data of the Corporate Debtor. XI. A copy of the Order shall also be forwarded to the IBBI for record and dissemination on their website. XII. Registry is directed to immediately communicate this Order to the Financial Creditor, the Corporate Debtor and the IRP by way of e-mail and WhatsApp, not later than two days from the date of this Order. XIII. Compliance report of the order by Designated Registrar is to be submitted today.
