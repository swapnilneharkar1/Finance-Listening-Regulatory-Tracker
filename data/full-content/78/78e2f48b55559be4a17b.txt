SBI Research Cautions RBI Against 50 Bps MoveEdited by: Priya RaghuvanshiUpdated: Nov 26, 2026, 18:38 ISTSBI Research urges RBI to limit its December rate hike to 25 bps, warning that aggressive tightening could hurt the rupee, capital flows and growth.Reserve Bank of India (File Photo)follow usfollow usThe Reserve Bank of India (RBI) should exercise caution when deciding its interest rate strategy in December, with SBI Research advising against a steep 50-basis-point (bps) increase. The research division of State Bank of India believes that a smaller hike of 25 bps would be more appropriate given the prevailing inflation outlook and growing uncertainty in international financial markets. In its latest Ecowrap report, SBI Research cautioned that an aggressive monetary tightening could put additional pressure on the Indian economy. A sharp increase in borrowing costs, the report suggested, could encourage foreign investors to withdraw capital, intensify volatility in the rupee and create fresh concerns about economic growth."A rate hike of 25 bps should be enough given the inflation trajectory rather than recourse to a jumbo one that might unleash a reverse causation of capital outflows, rupee instability and of course growth concerns," the report said.SBI Research's cautious outlook comes against the backdrop of increasing financial vulnerabilities in the United States. Weakness in the housing sector, mounting household liabilities and elevated stock market valuations have raised questions about the possibility of a prolonged correction in financial markets.The report pointed out that the recent decline in American equities has been particularly steep when compared with the early phase of the dot-com market crash.During the first 33 trading sessions following its peak, the S&P 500 dropped 11.6 per cent. By comparison, the benchmark recorded a 7.8 per cent decline over the equivalent period during the dot-com bust.The scale of the recent market losses is also reflected in the combined valuation of 499 companies included in the analysis. Their market capitalisation contracted by $6.03 trillion between October 30 and November 20, 2026.SBI Research also drew attention to difficulties in the American property market. Mortgage debt has climbed to $13.3 trillion, while existing home sales have slipped to their lowest point since August 2010. These developments suggest that financial stress could extend beyond equity markets, with further corrections remaining a possibility.Excessive Rupee Depreciation Could Deepen Economic PressuresThe report also examined India's currency management strategy, questioning whether a weaker rupee should be relied upon to cushion the domestic economy against external shocks.According to SBI Research, allowing the currency to lose too much value can create additional vulnerabilities rather than provide protection against international economic disruptions. It specifically argued that depreciation beyond the 100-per-dollar level was unnecessary and contributed to financial uncertainty."We believe exchange rate depreciation in Indian context clearly acts as shock amplifier beyond a point," the report said.At the same time, the research report acknowledged the recent improvement in the rupee's performance. The Indian currency gained 3.2 per cent from September 2026, compared with a 2.2 per cent rise in the US Dollar Index during the period examined.Federal Reserve's December Decision Could Shape Global SentimentSBI Research also outlined its expectations for the US Federal Reserve's upcoming monetary policy announcement.
