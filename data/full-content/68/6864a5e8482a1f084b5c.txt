The distinction is significant as public-sector banks increasingly focus on the strength of their underlying operations, rather than relying on treasury income and recoveries from technical write-offs (TWO), to drive profitability. Core pre-provision operating profit (PPOP) and core RoA exclude these sources of income and provide a clearer measure of recurring operating performance.Only a few public-sector banks are likely to report core RoA of more than 1% in FY28, according to estimates by ICICI Securities, which expects Bank of Maharashtra and Indian Bank to cross the threshold, while Union Bank could report core RoA of around 1%. The projections underline the relatively small number of state-owned lenders that may be able to sustain this level of underlying profitability. Advt Some banks have already made progress. Union Bank's core RoA touched 1% in Q2FY27, compared with 0.6% a year earlier. Punjab National Bank, meanwhile, has sustained year-on-year growth of more than 30% in core PPOP for the third consecutive quarter. The performance suggests that improvements in operating earnings are beginning to translate into stronger underlying returns at some lenders.The broader operating environment has also become more supportive for public-sector banks. In Q2FY27, PSBs recorded net interest income growth of 5% quarter-on-quarter and 15% year-on-year, compared with 4% and 14%, respectively, for private banks. Public-sector lenders also reported an improvement of more than 5 basis points in net interest margins, alongside loan growth of around 5% quarter-on-quarter. Their stronger NII growth reflects the improvement in margins and continued expansion in advances.FCNR(B) deposit boostThe full impact of FCNR(B) deposits and interest-rate transmission could provide further support to margins in the coming quarters. The brokerage expects the benefit to be more pronounced for PSBs in Q3FY27, even as private banks could deliver a higher quantum of NIM expansion over the full cycle. FCNR(B) deposits accounted for between 5% and 28% of Q2FY27 term deposits across banks, providing a potentially meaningful source of funding support.However, the improvement in operating income will have to be weighed against the provisioning requirements under the expected credit loss (ECL) framework. The report estimates that the transition could require additional standard asset provisioning of 12-15 basis points. It also factors in 10-20 basis points of higher year-on-year credit costs for most banks in FY28, reflecting the possibility of marginally higher net slippages and additional standard asset provisions. Advt The pressure is unlikely to be uniform across lenders. Mid-sized private banks and select public-sector banks could see more pronounced Stage 2 loan formation and provisioning requirements. The brokerage's estimates also point to varying levels of transition impact across banks, with most PSBs facing an estimated ECL impact of 70-120 basis points, while Bank of India is an outlier at 250 basis points.For public-sector banks seeking to sustain core RoA above 1%, the challenge will therefore be to preserve operating momentum while absorbing higher provisioning requirements. Stronger net interest income and improving margins provide a favourable starting point, but the ability to generate recurring core earnings and contain credit costs will ultimately determine which lenders can sustain that level of profitability.
