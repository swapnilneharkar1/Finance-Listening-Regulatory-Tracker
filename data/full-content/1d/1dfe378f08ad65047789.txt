Summary: An Initial Public Offering (IPO) should be evaluated using the issuer’s offer documents, audited financial information, risk disclosures and valuation rather than subscription figures or grey-market premiums alone. The draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) and red herring prospectus (RHP) explain the company’s business, promoters, financial performance, litigation, borrowings, related-party transactions and proposed use of funds. Investors should distinguish a fresh issue, where proceeds generally accrue to the issuer before issue expenses, from an offer for sale (OFS), where selling shareholders receive the sale proceeds. Review operating cash flow alongside reported profit, understand outstanding liabilities and contingent exposures, examine promoter dilution and compare the issue valuation with appropriate listed peers. The price-to-earnings ratio may be useful for profitable businesses, while other sector-specific measures may be necessary where earnings are volatile or negative. Risk factors should be read in conjunction with management discussion and financial statements rather than in isolation. A strong subscription response does not guarantee listing gains, and an unofficial grey-market premium is neither a regulated quotation nor a reliable forecast. This article sets out an IPO-focused document review, practical valuation illustration, due-diligence checklist and investor FAQs. It is an educational framework, not a recommendation to subscribe to any particular issue. Read the latest available document and any addenda or corrections. A DRHP is a draft disclosure document; the RHP ordinarily contains updated offer information, while final pricing and certain particulars are determined through the offer process. Check the Issue Structure Separate a fresh issue from an offer for sale. Fresh-issue proceeds are raised by the company, subject to expenses and disclosed objects. OFS proceeds belong to the selling shareholders, not the issuer. A combined issue may include both components. Examine whether the objects include capital expenditure, debt repayment, acquisitions, working capital or general corporate purposes. Evaluate the Business and Promoter Record Review revenue concentration, major customers and suppliers, sector regulation, geographic dependence, competitive position and sustainability of margins. Study pre- and post-issue promoter shareholding, pledged shares where disclosed, past transactions and governance practices. Examine related-party transactions for their commercial rationale and scale relative to the company’s operations. Read Financial Statements Beyond Net Profit Compare revenue growth, operating margins, finance costs, profitability and cash flows over the periods presented. Investigate why operating cash flow may lag reported earnings. Examine receivable days, inventory, working-capital borrowings, capital expenditure and debt maturities. Read the notes, contingent liabilities and any audit qualifications or emphasis-of-matter observations, and assess their possible financial significance. Assess IPO Valuation Against Appropriate Peers Use the offer price and post-issue share count when considering earnings-based multiples, and verify whether the issuer and peer figures use comparable periods and accounting bases. A low multiple is not automatically attractive if growth, governance or balance-sheet quality is weak; a high multiple requires correspondingly strong and sustainable expectations. Illustrative Valuation Calculation Assume an IPO is priced at ₹200 per share and the post-issue earnings per share, calculated on a consistent basis, is ₹10. The implied price-to-earnings ratio is 20 times (₹200 ÷ ₹10). If comparable listed companies trade at 16–24 times earnings, the issue falls within that illustrative range. This does not establish fair value: growth, cash conversion, debt, concentration risks and differences in accounting or business models must still be assessed. These figures are hypothetical and not a market quotation. Examine Risk Factors, Litigation and Use of Proceeds Focus on risks that could materially affect the issuer, including regulatory approvals, pending litigation, contingent liabilities, customer dependence, related-party exposure and execution of proposed projects. For debt repayment objects, identify the relevant borrowings and likely interest savings. For expansion projects, review estimated cost, timelines, funding gaps and uncertainty of projected returns. Confirm whether material statements are supported by the disclosures in the offer document. Why Grey-Market Premium and Subscription Figures Are Insufficient Grey-market premiums are unofficial and may be volatile or unreliable. Oversubscription shows demand during the offer period, not intrinsic value or a guarantee of allotment, liquidity or listing gains. A decision should rest primarily on disclosures, valuation, financial strength and the investor’s risk capacity. Practical IPO Due-Diligence Checklist Read the latest DRHP/RHP, amendments and the disclosed risk factors. Identify fresh-issue and OFS amounts and who receives the proceeds. Review promoters, governance, related parties and material litigation. Compare earnings with operating cash flows and working-capital trends. Examine borrowings, contingent liabilities and audit observations. Calculate valuation multiples using consistent post-issue figures and appropriate peers. Check the stated use of funds and execution risks. Retain the offer document, calculations and decision rationale. Frequently Asked Questions Does an Offer for Sale Bring Funds Into the Company? No. In an OFS, existing shareholders sell their shares and receive the sale proceeds, subject to applicable expenses and arrangements. A fresh issue raises capital for the issuer. Is a High IPO Subscription Level Proof of a Good Investment? No. Subscription demand may reflect several factors and does not establish fair valuation or future performance. Which Financial Indicators Deserve Special Attention? Profitability, operating cash flow, debt, working-capital movements, return ratios and the quality of earnings should be assessed together, with attention to sector-specific characteristics. Can Grey-Market Premium Predict Listing Gains? No. Grey-market activity is unofficial and is not a dependable indicator of the listing price or subsequent market performance. Where Can Investors Find the Main Offer Disclosures? The latest issuer offer documents and related updates are ordinarily made available through the issuer, lead managers, stock exchanges and relevant regulatory disclosure channels. Check the applicable version before investing. Key Takeaway An IPO assessment should connect offer structure, business quality, governance, cash flows, valuation and material risks.
