Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); SKB Enterprises Vs Commissioner of Customs (CESTAT Ahmedabad) Summary: The Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal (CESTAT), Ahmedabad, allowed an appeal filed by SKB Enterprises challenging the onerous bank guarantee conditions imposed for provisional release of imported walnut kernels classifiable under Customs Tariff Heading 0802 3200. The adjudicating authority had initially imposed conditions covering differential customs duty of Rs. 1,16,06,018, redemption fine and penalties, including an additional amount equivalent to 10% of the differential duty towards penalty. Following an earlier remand by the Commissioner (Appeals), the bank guarantee requirement was reduced to Rs. 1,39,27,000. However, the Commissioner (Appeals), vide order dated 26 October 2026, upheld the revised provisional release conditions, prompting the importer to approach the Tribunal. The appellant relied upon Circular No. 35/2017-Customs dated 16 August 2017 and contended that the discretionary power available under Section 110A of the Customs Act, 1962 for provisional release of seized goods must be exercised properly and judiciously rather than by imposing indiscriminate conditions. The seizure was based on concerns regarding the transit certificate issued by Afghanistan Customs, which did not bear the endorsement of Iran Customs, and the considerable time gap between issuance of the commercial invoice on 15 April 2025 and filing of the Bill of Entry on 10 February 2026. The appellant relied upon the Bombay High Court decision in Santa Monica Farm Produce Pvt. Ltd., wherein similar goods had been provisionally released without requiring a bank guarantee. The Revenue, however, justified the conditions imposed by the customs authorities. The Tribunal referred to its earlier decision in M/s Sadhguru Traders, Final Order No. 10813/2026 dated 4 November 2026, wherein it had emphasised the necessity of balancing the interests of Revenue with those of importers whose perishable goods remained under seizure. Prolonged detention of walnuts could result in deterioration and economic loss without serving the interests of either party. In that decision, the Tribunal had considered the Bombay High Court ruling in Santa Monica Farm Produce Pvt. Ltd. and the CESTAT Mumbai ruling in A.S. Spices Vs Commissioner of Customs, Nhava Sheva, and considered a bank guarantee equivalent to 20% of the duty amount, together with a bond covering the entire duty amount, sufficient to safeguard Revenue interests pending adjudication. Following its earlier decision, CESTAT Ahmedabad allowed the appeal of SKB Enterprises and reduced the bank guarantee requirement to 20% of the duty amount, subject to execution of a bond covering 100% of the duty amount. The Tribunal confined its decision to the conditions governing provisional release and did not adjudicate the underlying customs duty dispute on merits. Cases Discussed Santa Monica Farm Produce Pvt. Ltd. Vs Union of India & Ors. (Bombay High Court); 2026 (2) TMI 407: Relied upon by the appellant in support of provisional release of similar imported walnut consignments without furnishing a bank guarantee. The Tribunal considered this authority while determining appropriate security conditions. M/s Sadhguru Traders (CESTAT Ahmedabad); Customs Appeal No. 10822 of 2026; Final Order No. 10813/2026; 04/11/2026: Directly followed by the Tribunal for balancing Revenue interests against the risk of deterioration of perishable walnuts and reducing the bank guarantee to 20% of the duty amount, together with a bond covering the entire duty amount. A.S. Spices Vs Commissioner of Customs, Nhava Sheva (India) (CESTAT Mumbai); 2018 (4) TMI 1757: Referred to in the decision in Sadhguru Traders as providing a more appropriate approach for determining provisional release conditions where verification of the certificate of origin remained pending. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF CESTAT AHMEDABAD In the instant case, the appellant is aggrieved by the onerous condition imposed while releasing the goods i.e. Walnut Kernels classifiable under Chapter Tariff Heading 0802 3200, the Additional Commissioner being the adjudicating authority vide order dated 01.04.2026 imposed a bank guarantee without renewal clause to adjudication of the goods amounting to Rs.1,16,06,018/- equal to differential duty and amount equal to 30% as redemption fine and penalties worth Rs.31,65,278/- as also another amount of 10% differential duty towards penalty under Section 112A(ii) of the Customs Act, 1962. 1.1 Being aggrieved, the appellant knocked at the door of the Commissioner (Appeals) who remanded back the matter to the adjudicating authority for reconsideration of their provisional release order dated 14.07.2026. The provisional realest order was issued as per order of Learned Commissioner (Appeals) reducing bank guarantee to Rs.1,39,27,000/-. Being aggrieved with the said provisional release order, the appellant again approached Commissioner (Appeals) who rejected the appeal vide order dated 26.10.2026 and order of provisional release was upheld on the conditions indicated. Aggrieved by the impugned order, appellant has preferred the appeal. 1.2 The party has referred to Circular No. 35/2017 in File No. 394/13/2016 – Cus (Anti-Smuggling Unit) dated 16.08.2017 indicated that same vested discretion to be exercised properly by the authorities involved and therefore the right to seek release provisionally on proper terms is very much provided in Section 110A of the Customs Act and the same is required to be used properly and judiciously and not Indiscriminately imposing conditions that in the instant case, they had claimed the transit certificate issued of Afghanistan Customs, which do not bear the endorsement of Iran Customs and there was alleged between the commercial invoice issued on to be a long gap 15.04.2025 and filing of bill of entry on 10.02.2026 and this was the basis of placing the goods under seizure. 1.3 The Learned Advocate seeks to rely on the decision of Hon’ble High Court Bombay in the case of Santa Monica Farm Product Private Limited as reported in 2026 (2) TMI 407 BOM (H.C.) wherein similar goods were provisionally release without imposing any Bank Guarantee on the ground that there was no incriminating material to justify seizure reduction. The Learned Consultant states that same is the position in this case also and therefore, similar condition as were imposed by the Hon’ble Bombay High Court should be imposed in their case also. 2. The Learned A.R. Justifies the conditions imposed and reiterates the order. DISCUSSION & FINDINGS This court has noted the order of Santa Monica (Bombay High Court) Cited Supra in Customs Appeal No. 10822 of 2026 in the case of M/s Sadhguru Traders vide Final Order No. 10813/2026 dated 04.11.2026, the following findings were given in the order:- “9. This court has considered the rival submissions and finds that interest of Revenue as well as party whose perishable goods have been seized are required to be balanced pending finalization of the adjudication. In the instant case, goods have been in custody for quite some time and looking at the perishable nature of the walnuts there is always a possibility of decay leading to national loss and serving no purpose of either party i.e. the department or the appellant in the instant case. While bond of Rs. 1,53,34,696/-under Section 110A of the Customs Act is appropriate and ought to be furnished, the bank guarantee of Rs.2,02,41,800/- in addition to above is clearly onerous as it amounts to 132% of the value. Party has produced certificate of origin and other documents as were required to claim the lower duty benefit. On merits, pronouncing on anything at the moment shall be inappropriate. 9.1 The department is free to establish its case through investigation and in adjudication. However, the court finds that the condition of bank guarantee is clearly onerous more so as in various case law indicated by the party same has been mostly in range of 0% to 20%. Learned Advocate has relied on the case of Santa Monica Farm Produce Pvt. Ltd. Vs UOI & Ors. 2026 (2) TMI 407 (Bom.) H.C. to state that in the circumstances of the matter when the verification is still to fructify, the certificate of origin has been produced, the nil bank guarantee as in case cited (supra) in relation to walnuts should be an approach which should be followed by the court. This court finds that in the instant case when the certificate of origin has been produced by the authority which is not yet proved to be in genuine, the approach adopted in A.S. Spices vs. Commissioner of Customs Nhava Sheva (India) by CESTAT Mumbai as reported in 2018 (4) TMI 1757-CESTAT Mumbai appears to be more appropriate. 9.2 This court, therefore, reduces the bank guarantee to 20% of the duty amount involved along with bond of the whole duty amount to safeguard interest of Revenue, at this stage. 10. It is ordered accordingly; the appeal is allowed in above terms with consequential relief.” 4. Therefore, the appeal in this case is also allowed, subject to reduce the bank guarantee of 20% of the duty amount as above and 100% bond of whole duty amount. The appeal is allowed subject to the foregoing.
