Vs National Faceless Assessment Centre, quashed the final assessment order dated 30 June 2021 and the consequential demand notice of ₹56,76,09,018 for Assessment Year 2017–18, holding that the assessment could not be finalised without considering the objections filed before the Dispute Resolution Panel (DRP) within the extended statutory deadline. The petitioner challenged the assessment made under Section 143(3) read with Sections 144C(3) and 144B of the Income-tax Act, 1961, contending that the Assessing Officer had disregarded the extension granted by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT). The petitioner submitted that a draft assessment order dated 5 May 2021 had proposed total income of ₹450,02,37,902. Under Section 144C(2), objections were ordinarily required to be filed within thirty days, by 4 June 2021. However, due to the lockdown in Delhi, the petitioner’s office remained closed, and objections were filed before the DRP on 21 June 2021. The petitioner relied on CBDT Circular No. 12/2021 and Notification No. 74/2021, both dated 25 June 2021, which extended the deadline for filing DRP objections falling due on or after 1 June 2021 to 31 August 2021 and extended the assessment completion deadline to 30 September 2021. Despite the objections being filed within the extended period, the National Faceless Assessment Centre passed the final assessment order on 30 June 2021, treating the petitioner as having opted not to file objections. The petitioner argued that this action violated Section 144C(13), which requires the Assessing Officer to complete the assessment in conformity with the directions issued by the DRP under Section 144C(5). Since the objections remained pending, the Assessing Officer could not validly finalise the assessment. During the hearing, counsel for the Revenue stated that she had no objection to remitting the matter to the DRP. Taking note of the circumstances, the Delhi High Court quashed the final assessment order and the demand notice of ₹56.76 crore. It remitted the matter to the DRP for consideration under Section 144C and directed that the subsequent assessment be completed in accordance with Sections 144B(1)(xxix) to (xxxi) and 144C. The writ petition and pending application were accordingly disposed of. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF DELHI HIGH COURT 1. Allowed, subject to all just exceptions. 2.
