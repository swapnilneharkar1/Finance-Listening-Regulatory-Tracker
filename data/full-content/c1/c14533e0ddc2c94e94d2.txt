Here's What RBI Rules On Liability, Refunds And Compensation StatesEdited by: Priya RaghuvanshiUpdated: Oct 22, 2026, 17:08 ISTSBI's data breach raises questions about bank fraud liability. Know RBI rules on zero liability, refunds, compensation and steps to take after fraud.The Reserve Bank of India’s (Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct) Third Amendment Directions, 2026, will come into effect from January 1, 2027. (Representational image)follow usfollow usThe recent State Bank of India (SBI) data breach has raised concerns about the safety of customers’ banking information and the risk of unauthorised transactions. The bank said the incident affected a "very very small number of customers." "Customer balances are completely safe," the SBI said on Wednesday, adding that it was taking all possible steps to protect customers’ interests.But what happens if money is debited from your bank account without you sharing an OTP or authorising a transaction? The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has laid down rules governing customer liability in electronic banking frauds, including circumstances in which banks may have to reimburse the money lost.Experts suggest that a bank could be held negligent if inadequate fraud monitoring, weak authentication systems, ignored alerts or a data breach contributes to an unauthorised debit.The circumstances surrounding a fraudulent transaction matter when determining whether the bank or customer bears responsibility for the loss.RBI's Existing Rules Vs New 2026 Directions: What's Changing?Under the existing framework introduced in 2017, customers can have zero liability if the loss results from the bank's negligence. They can also qualify for zero liability when a third-party breach is reported within three working days.The RBI's Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct Third Amendment Directions, 2026, are scheduled to take effect from January 1, 2027.Under the revised framework, the reporting window for zero liability will increase to five calendar days. The coverage will also extend beyond unauthorised transactions to fraudulent transactions more broadly, including cases involving stolen credentials or coercion.Another important change concerns the burden of proof. Banks will have to establish that the customer was liable for the disputed transaction.Online Bank Fraud: What Should You Do Immediately?Customers who notice an unfamiliar debit should act quickly to prevent further losses and establish a record of the incident. Makhija recommends taking the following steps:Block compromised banking access: Disable or block the affected card, UPI facility or net banking access to prevent additional unauthorised transactions.Inform your bank: Report the incident through the bank's official application, helpline or branch. Obtain written acknowledgement and retain the date and time of the complaint.Contact the cybercrime helpline: Call 1930 and file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in. Prompt reporting can help authorities trace the movement of funds and take steps to freeze them where possible.Preserve evidence: Keep transaction records, bank messages, suspicious links, phishing communications and complaint reference numbers. File a police complaint where appropriate.Customers should not delay reporting while trying to determine how the fraud occurred.Does An SBI Data Breach Guarantee Compensation?A data breach does not automatically mean every affected customer is entitled to compensation for a subsequent fraudulent transaction. The connection between the exposed information and the financial loss, along with the circumstances of the incident, remains important.Customers should change compromised passwords, enable transaction alerts and remain cautious of callers who use personal or banking information to establish credibility.
