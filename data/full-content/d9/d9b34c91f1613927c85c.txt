Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Safari Fine Clothings Private Limited Vs ITO (ITAT Rajkot) Unawareness of an Online Appellate Order and Consultant’s Failure Can Justify Condonation of a 1,079-Day Delay: ITAT Rajkot Rajkot ITAT admits the appeal and directs fresh assessment Can a company’s appeal be heard after a delay of nearly three years when it was unaware that the first appellate order had been passed online and its former tax consultant had failed to discharge the work entrusted to him? In Safari Fine Clothings Private Limited v. ITO, Ward-2, Gandhidham, ITA No. 1062/RJT/2026, decided on 20 November 2026 for AY 2012–13, the Rajkot ITAT accepted the company’s affidavit-backed explanation as sufficient cause and condoned the 1,079-day delay. It then restored the assessment to the Assessing Officer for fresh adjudication. What was at stake in the assessment The company filed its return on 29 March 2013, declaring total income of ₹50,18,450 after claiming set-off of ₹73,00,185 brought forward from earlier years. The return was selected for scrutiny, and the Assessing Officer completed the assessment under Section 143(3) on 26 March 2015. In the absence of adequate explanations and supporting documents, the officer denied set-off of ₹69,37,221 in business losses and ₹3,62,964 in unabsorbed depreciation. The officer also made a ₹6 lakh addition under Section 68, disallowed interest expenditure of ₹13,34,857 and disallowed commission expenditure of ₹62,77,539. The company challenged the additions and disallowances before the CIT(A)/NFAC. That appeal was dismissed on 16 June 2023 due to non-compliance. When the company eventually approached the Tribunal, it first had to explain why it had missed the deadline for filing the second appeal. Why the Tribunal accepted the explanation for the delay The company filed a condonation petition supported by an affidavit. It stated that it was unaware that the CIT(A) had passed the order online and attributed the delay to the failure of its previous tax consultant to discharge the professional duties entrusted to him. The company maintained that the delay was neither deliberate nor intentional. The Department opposed condonation, pointing to the substantial length of the delay. The Tribunal considered the affidavit, the documents on record and the Department’s objections. It found the explanation plausible and concluded that the circumstances had affected the company’s ability to pursue its appellate remedy within time. The Bench was satisfied that the delay was neither intentional nor deliberate. It therefore condoned the delay and admitted the appeal for adjudication on merits. Supreme Court principle on sufficient cause The Tribunal relied on Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji [1987] 167 ITR 471 (SC). The Supreme Court explained that a litigant ordinarily gains nothing by filing an appeal late and that refusing condonation may shut out a meritorious dispute at the threshold. It also explained that the requirement to account for the delay must be applied pragmatically and with common sense. A long delay does not by itself establish deliberate default, culpable negligence or mala fides. The Rajkot Bench applied that reasoning to the company’s explanation. The 1,079 days made the delay substantial, but the decisive question was whether the facts explained the company’s failure to act within time. The Tribunal found sufficient cause on the record before it and allowed the company to pursue the appeal. Fresh adjudication before the Assessing Officer After the appeal was admitted, the company’s counsel acknowledged that the company had failed to comply with the first appellate notices. He stated that the company was now willing to furnish the requisite details and documentary evidence and requested a fresh opportunity before the Assessing Officer. The Revenue raised no serious objection to that request and sought compliance with notices during the remand proceedings. The Tribunal restored the matter directly to the Assessing Officer, who must give the company a reasonable opportunity of being heard and decide the disputed issues on merits. The company must respond to notices, furnish the necessary evidence, cooperate fully and avoid unnecessary adjournments. The appeal was allowed for statistical purposes. The availability of the loss and depreciation set-offs and the sustainability of the other additions will be decided in the fresh proceedings. Author’s comments The value of this ruling lies in the connection between the consultant’s lapse and the missed filing deadline. In a comparable case, an affidavit explaining who was entrusted with the appeal, when the taxpayer learned of the online order and what steps followed can help the Tribunal assess whether the default was bona fide. Correspondence or other contemporaneous records can support that chronology where available. The order illustrates how the sufficient-cause test applies even where the delay is substantial. The remand also brings the company back to the evidentiary questions that led to the original disallowances. Earlier-year returns and assessment records can establish the amounts available for set-off. The Section 68 credit, interest and commission will each require supporting records relevant to that issue. Condonation secured the hearing. The outcome of the fresh assessment depends on what the company can substantiate. Cases Discussed Collector, Land Acquisition Vs Mst. Katiji [1987] 167 ITR 471 (SC); 1987 taxmann.com 1072; [1987] 28 ELT 185; 66 STC 228 (SC): The Supreme Court laid down six principles governing condonation of delay, emphasizing a pragmatic interpretation of sufficient cause and preference for substantial justice over technical considerations. The Tribunal relied upon these principles while condoning the 1,079-day delay. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT RAJKOT Captioned appeal filed by the assessee, pertaining to Assessment Year (AY) 2012-13, is directed against the order under section 250 of the Income-tax Act, 1961 [hereinafter referred to as ‘the Act’] passed by the National Faceless Appeal Centre [hereinafter referred to as ‘NFAC’], dated 16.06.2023, which in turn arises out of an order passed by assessing officer u/s. 143(3) of the Act, dated 26.03.2015. 02. At the outset the Ld. AR stated that there is delay of 1079 days in filing the present appeal before this tribunal and in this connection a petition for condonation of delay along with an affidavit has been filed by the assessee stating the reason for such delay. We have considered the application filed by the assessee seeking condonation of delay of 1079 days in filing the present appeal before the tribunal, along with the affidavit of the assessee, explaining the reasons for such delay. As per the affidavit, the order of the Ld. CIT(A) was passed on 16.06.2023, against which the appeal before the tribunal was required to be filed within the prescribed period. It has been explained that the assessee was unaware regarding the passing of the online order by the Ld. CIT(A) and the complete failure of the previous tax consultant to discharge the professional duty entrusted to him. The assessee has, therefore, pleaded that the delay was neither deliberate nor intentional and arose due to the aforesaid circumstances. 03. We have also considered the objections raised by the Department against the condonation of such a substantial delay. However, having regard to the facts and circumstances of the case, assessee was unaware regarding the passing of the online order by the Ld. CIT(A) and the complete failure of the previous tax consultant to discharge the professional duty entrusted to him, we find that the assessee has furnished a plausible explanation for the delay in filing the appeal. 04. In our considered view, the explanation furnished by the assessee constitutes a reasonable cause for the delay. After considering the explanation furnished by the assessee and the documents placed on record, we are of the view that the delay in filing the appeal was not intentional or deliberate. The assessee has placed sufficient cause which affected the ability to pursue the appellate remedy within the prescribed time. It is a settled principle of law that a litigant should not be denied an opportunity of hearing on technical grounds when sufficient cause has been shown for the delay While considering a similar issue, the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji reported in 1987 taxmann.com 1072/[1987] 28 ELT 185/66 STC 228 (SC)/(167 ITR 471 ) laid down six principles. For the purpose of convenience, the principles laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court are reproduced hereunder: (1) Ordinarily, a litigant does not stand to benefit by lodging an appeal late. (2) Refusing to condone delay can result in a meritorious matter being thrown at the very threshold and cause of justice being defeated. As against this, when delay is condoned, the highest that can happen is that a cause would be decided on merits after hearing the parties. (3) ‘Every day’s delay must be explained’ does not mean that a pedantic approach should be made. Why not every hour’s delay, every second’s delay? The doctrine must be applied in a rational, common-sense and pragmatic manner. (4) When substantial justice and technical consideration are pitted against each other, the cause of substantial justice deserves to be preferred, for the other side cannot claim to have vested right in injustice being done because of a non-deliberate delay. (5) There is no presumption that delay is occasioned deliberately, or on account of culpable negligence, or on account of mala fides. A litigant does not stand to benefit by resorting to delay. In fact, he runs a serious risk. (6) It must be grasped that the judiciary is respected not on account of its power to legalise injustice on technical grounds but because it is capable of removing injustice and is expected to do so. The Hon’ble Supreme Court has repeatedly observed that refusal to condone delay in appropriate cases may result in perpetuating injustice on purely technical grounds, even when the appellate authority is otherwise competent to correct the error. Hence, if the application for condonation of delay is rejected despite the existence of reasonable cause, it would amount to allowing an injustice to continue merely on account of technicalities. Considering the facts of the present case explained by the assessee, we are satisfied that the assessee was prevented by reasonable cause from filing the appeal within the prescribed time. Accordingly, in the interest of justice, the delay of 1079 days in filing the present appeal is condoned and the appeal is admitted for adjudication on merits. 05. Brief facts of the case are that the assessee, filed its return of income for A.Y. 2012-13 on 29.03.2013, declaring total income of Rs.50,18,450/-after claiming set-off of brought-forward business losses of Rs.73,00,185/-. The case was selected for scrutiny through CASS. During the course of assessment proceedings, the Assessing Officer, in the absence of proper explanations and supporting documentary evidences, made the following additions/disallowances: 1. Disallowance of brought-forward business loss of Rs.69,37,221/- 2. Disallowance of brought-forward unabsorbed depreciation of Rs.3,62,964/-. 3. Addition under section 68 of the Act amounting to Rs.6,00,000/- 4. Disallowance of interest expenditure of Rs.13,34,857/-. 5. Disallowance of commission expenditure of Rs.62,77,539/-. 06. Aggrieved by the assessment order, the assessee preferred an appeal before the Ld. CIT(A)/NFAC, challenging the aforesaid additions and disallowances, including the denial of set-off of brought-forward business losses and unabsorbed depreciation and the addition made under section 68 of the Act. However, appeal of the assessee was dismissed due to non-compliance. 07. Still aggrieved by the order of the Ld. CIT(A) assessee is in appeal before this tribunal. 08. On merit, the Ld. AR fairly submitted that, owing to certain reasons, the assessee could not comply with the notices issued by the Ld. CIT(A). However, the assessee is now willing to furnish all the requisite details and documentary evidences in support of its grounds of appeal. The Ld. AR, therefore, requested that one more opportunity may be granted to the assessee by restoring the matter to the file of the Ld. AO for fresh adjudication. 09. On the other hand, the Ld. DR for the revenue has not raised any serious objection, but he stated that assessee should comply to the notices issued during remand proceedings. 10. We after considering the rival submissions of the parties and in the interest of justice and fair play, we deem it appropriate to restore the matter to the file of the Ld. AO for fresh adjudication. The assessee is directed to duly comply with the notices issued by the AO and furnish all necessary details and supporting documentary evidences. The Ld. AO is also directed to afford a reasonable opportunity of being heard to the assessee and thereafter adjudicate the grounds raised by the assessee on merits, in accordance with law. Needless to say, the assessee shall extend full cooperation and shall not seek unnecessary adjournments. Accordingly, the appeal of the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. 11. In the result, the appeal of the assessee is allowed for statistical purposes. Order pronounced in the open court on this 20th day of November, 2026.
