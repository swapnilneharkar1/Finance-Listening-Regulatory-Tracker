(Karnataka High Court) An opening balance of share application money cannot be added under Section 68 in a subsequent assessment year when the alleged unexplained credit relates to an earlier financial year. Any addition under Section 68 must be made in the assessment year in which the credit was originally recorded. Case Summary The Revenue filed an appeal under Section 260A against the ITAT order dated 30.01.2024 concerning AY 2015-16. The Assessing Officer had made an addition of Rs. 11,59,30,375 under Section 68, treating the opening balance of share application money pending allotment from director Sri B. Ravi Kumar Reddy as unexplained cash credit. The amount was admittedly carried forward from the closing balance of FY 2012-13 relevant to AY 2013-14. The AO examined the bank accounts and books of the earlier financial year and concluded that the credit was not adequately explained. However, the addition was made in AY 2014-15, on the basis that the opening balance as on 01.04.2013 remained unexplained. The assessee contended that the alleged credit related to the preceding financial year and could not be added in the subsequent assessment year. The NFAC deleted the addition, and the ITAT upheld the deletion relying upon the Karnataka High Court decision in CIT v. Sridev Enterprises. Issue Whether an opening balance of share application money carried forward from an earlier financial year could be treated as unexplained cash credit under Section 68 and added in the subsequent assessment year. AO’s Finding The AO treated Rs. 11,59,30,375 reflected as opening share application money pending allotment as unexplained cash credit. On examining records of FY 2012-13, the AO concluded that the amount was not supported by corresponding bank credits and remained unexplained. The opening balance in the subsequent year was consequently treated as unexplained and added under Section 68. High Court’s Findings The High Court held that the AO’s findings were based on examination of records relating to FY 2012-13. If the credit was considered unexplained, the addition, if legally permissible, had to be made in the assessment year relevant to the financial year in which such credit was originally recorded. An opening balance represents the closing balance of the preceding year. Its nature and character cannot change merely because it is carried forward into the next financial year. The Revenue cannot examine a credit pertaining to an earlier year and make an addition under Section 68 in a subsequent assessment year. The Court relied on CIT v. Sridev Enterprises, wherein it was held that the nature and status of an opening balance cannot differ from that of the closing balance of the immediately preceding year and consistency in the Revenue’s approach is necessary. No substantial question of law arose for consideration. Outcome The Karnataka High Court dismissed the Revenue’s appeal on the ground that no substantial question of law arose and also on account of an inordinate delay of 636 days in filing the appeal. The deletion of the addition of Rs. 11,59,30,375 under Section 68 for AY 2014-15 was upheld. Key Principle Section 68 operates in the year in which the credit is recorded in the books. An unexplained credit belonging to an earlier financial year cannot be shifted to a subsequent assessment year merely because it continues as an opening balance. Cases Discussed 1. Commissioner of Income Tax Vs Sridev Enterprises (Karnataka High Court); (1991) 59 Taxman 439 (Kar); 192 ITR 165. Relevance: The Karnataka High Court relied on this precedent concerning the consistency of treatment of amounts carried forward from earlier accounting years. It reproduced paragraph 4 of the decision and applied the principle that the nature and status of an opening balance cannot be different from its corresponding closing balance in the preceding year merely because the accounting year has changed. FULL TEXT OF THE JUDGMENT/ORDER OF KARNATAKA HIGH COURT 1. The Revenue has filed the present appeal under Section 260A of the Income Tax Act, 1961 [the Act] impugning an order dated 30.01.2024 passed by the learned Income Tax Appellate Tribunal [ITAT] in ITA No.713/Bang/2023 for the Assessment Year [AY] 2015-16. 2. The Revenue had preferred the said appeal against an order dated 28.06.2023 passed by the National Faceless Appeal Centre [NFAC] under Section 250 of the Act allowing the appeal preferred by the respondent [Assessee]. The Assessee had also filed cross objection (CO No.12/Bang/2023) in the Revenue’s appeal before the learned ITAT contending that the assessment order dated 29.12.2017 passed under Section 143(3) of the Act was barred by limitation. The learned ITAT disposed of the said cross-objection without examining it, as it dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. 3. The Assessing Officer [AO] had passed the assessment order dated 29.12.2017 under Section 143(3) of the Act, inter alia, adding an amount of ₹11,59,30,375/- under Section 68 of the Act. The AO reasoned that the said amount which was reflected as an opening balance against share application money pending for allotment by one of the directors of the assessee (Sri B. Ravi Kumar Reddy) was an unexplained cash credit and, therefore, liable to be added under Section 68 of the Act. 4. Undisputedly, the said opening balance corresponded to the closing balance of the previous year (FY 2012-13) relevant to AY 2013-14. The said amount was reflected as an outstanding receipt from Sri B. Ravikumar Reddy in the final accounts as on 31.03.2013. The AO purportedly examined the records of the prior financial year, FY 2012-13, and concluded that the Assessee had not received the said amount during FY 2012-13 as it did not correspond with the amounts credited in the bank accounts during the said financial year. The AO reasoned that the closing balance reflected on 31.03.2013 was not explained. Correspondingly, the opening balance as on 01.04.2013 also remained unexplained. 5. The Assessee disputed the correctness of the AO’s finding that the amount reflected as the closing balance as on 31.03.2013 was not supported by amounts credited in the bank account and the amount credited for value. However, the NFAC did not examine the said question. 6. The Assessee also objected to the addition on the ground that it was made on the basis of the accounts of the prior financial year, which were not the subject matter of assessment; thus, the AO could not make any addition in regard to the transactions relating to FY 2012-13 in AY 2014-15. 7. The learned ITAT found no infirmity with the decision of the learned NFAC and accordingly, dismissed the Revenue’s appeal. The learned ITAT also found that the issue was covered by the earlier decision of this Court in Commissioner of Income Tax vs. Sridev Enterprises1 and concurred with the order passed by NFAC. 8. The Revenue has projected the following questions for consideration of this Court: “1. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the Tribunal’s order is perverse in nature in deleting the addition of Rs.11,59,30,375 made by Assessing Officer by holding that the opening balance in the current assessment year cannot be considered by relying decision in case of CIT V/s Sridev Enterprises (reported in 59 taxman 439/192 ITR) ignoring that Assessing authority has not considered transaction of previous assessment year but the Assessing Authority tried to establish the correct opening balance in relevant previous years so that it would not impact on the current financial profit of the assesse. 2. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the Tribunal’s order is perverse in nature in deleting the addition of Rs.11,59,30,375 made by Assessing Officer ignoring that the said transaction in question does not pertain to any of the assessment year as same has not taken place at all between the company and Sri.B.Ravi Kumar Reddy and as such it is a bogus entry? 3. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the Tribunal’s order is perverse in nature in deleting the addition of Rs.11,59,30,375 made by Assessing Officer when the assesse failed to establish that said transaction is genuine and not bogus transaction which were actually carried out to create a cooked up story? 4. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the Tribunal’s order is perverse in nature in holding that the sale of software license did not include a right or interest in copyright, which thus did not give rise to payment of royalty and would be an income deeded to accrue in India under section 9(1)(vi) of the Act, requiring the deduction of tax at source? 4. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the Tribunal’s order is perverse in nature in not considering the fact that the mismatch in books of account and bank statement of the Company was clearly established by the Assessing Officer and it was concluded that the closing balance and the amount lent to the assesse -Company by Sri.B.Ravi Kumar Reddy does not tally? 5. Whether on the facts and in the circumstances of the case, the Tribunal’s order is perverse in nature in not appreciating the factual matrix of the case and decided the matter in vague manner without appreciating the materials on record brought on record? 9. The questions raised by the Revenue are covered by the decision of the Coordinate Bench of this Court in Commissioner of Income Tax vs. Sridev Enterprises (supra). The relevant extract of the said decision is set out below: “4. We are in agreement with the view expressed by the Tribunal. The status of the amount standing as outstanding due from Nalanda on the first day of the accounting year is the amount that stood outstanding on the last day of the previous accounting year; therefore, its nature and status cannot be different on the 1st day of the current accounting year, from its nature and status as on the last day of the previous account year. Regarding the past years, the assessee claims for deductions were allowed in respect of the sums advanced during those years; this could be, only on the assumption that those advances were not out of borrowed funds of the assessee. This finding during the previous years is the very basis of the deductions permitted during the past years, whether a specific finding was recorded or not. A departure from that finding in respect of the said amounts advanced during the previous year, would result in a contradictory finding; it will not be equitable to permit the revenue to take a different stand now, in respect of the amounts which were the subject-matter of previous years’ assessments; consistency and definiteness of approach by the revenue is necessary, in the matter of recognising the nature of an account maintained by the assessee so that the basis of a concluded assessment would not be ignored without actually reopening the assessment. The principle is similar to the cases where it has been held that a debt which had been treated by the revenue as a good debt in a particular year cannot subsequently be held by it to have become bad prior to that year.” 10. It is clear that the AO’s findings were based on examination of the records of the previous year ending on 31.03.2013. Thus, according to the AO, the amount credited in the accounts for the previous year ending 31.03.2013 remained unexplained. Plainly, any addition under Section 68 of the Act was required to be made in the Assessment Year relevant to that previous year. 11. In this view, we find no infirmity in the learned NFAC and learned ITAT in holding that the addition under Section 68 of the Act could not have been made in AY 2014-15 on the basis of examination of the books and a finding of unexplained credit during FY 2012-13. No substantial question of law arises for consideration of this court. 12. We also note an inordinate delay of 636 days in filing the present appeal. 13. The appeal is, accordingly, dismissed both on the ground that no substantial question of law arises as well as on the ground of delay.
