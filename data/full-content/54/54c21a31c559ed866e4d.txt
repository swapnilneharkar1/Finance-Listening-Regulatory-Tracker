Summary : The scheme reserved in the ledger as Nippon India Growth Fund is currently presented by the asset management company as Nippon India Growth Mid Cap Fund. The name resolution is important because Nippon India Mutual Fund also operates a separate multi-cap scheme; a direct or regular plan is a plan of the same underlying mid-cap scheme, not a different portfolio mandate. The current product page describes an open-ended equity scheme predominantly investing in mid-cap stocks, benchmarked to the Nifty Midcap 150 TRI, with inception on 8 October 1995 and Rupesh Patel identified as fund manager. The published load is nil on entry and one per cent for redemption or switch-out within one month of allotment, with nil exit load thereafter, subject to the current scheme documents. The minimum application amount shown by the AMC is ₹100. This article explains the mandate, plan and option choices, costs, risks, process and current equity-oriented-fund tax rules without ranking the scheme or predicting returns. Mid-Cap Mandate and Portfolio Construction A mid-cap fund is required to maintain the category’s prescribed exposure to mid-cap companies under the SEBI classification framework. The AMC’s objective is to seek long-term capital appreciation through a portfolio focused on mid-cap stocks. The objective is an aim, not an assurance. Mid-cap shares can offer business growth exposure, but they can also have thinner liquidity, sharper price movements, greater sensitivity to financing conditions and less diversified revenue than large companies. During stressed markets, the fund may need more time or incur greater market impact to change positions. What investors should read in the monthly portfolio The monthly factsheet and portfolio disclosure show actual sector and issuer weights, cash, derivatives if any, market-cap distribution, turnover and performance data. A category label cannot reveal concentration by itself. Readers should inspect the largest holdings, the combined weight of the top ten, sector clusters, exposure to lower-liquidity names and changes over several months. A one-month portfolio is a snapshot; comparing successive official disclosures is more informative than treating one position as permanent. Benchmark and tracking context Nifty Midcap 150 TRI includes reinvested dividends and is the appropriate broad reference identified by the AMC. An actively managed fund can differ materially from that index because of stock selection, cash, expenses and transaction timing. Performance should therefore be read over consistent periods, after expenses, and against the same plan. Regular-plan and direct-plan NAVs cannot be compared as though their cost bases were identical. Plans, Options, NAV and Minimum Application The scheme offers direct and regular plans and the options specified in its current documents. A direct plan is purchased without distributor commission embedded in the expense structure; a regular plan is routed through a distributor and generally has a higher total expense ratio. Both plans invest in the same scheme portfolio, but their NAVs differ because expenses differ. Growth and income-distribution-cum-capital-withdrawal options also have different cash-flow treatment. An IDCW distribution is not an extra return: the NAV falls to the extent of the payout and applicable statutory adjustments. The AMC product page shows a minimum application amount of ₹100. Minimums, SIP facilities, cut-off timing and transaction rules can be amended, so an investor should check the live transaction screen and current KIM. Applicable NAV depends on the valid application, funds availability and applicable cut-off rules, not merely on the time an instruction was typed. A switch is a redemption from one scheme or plan and a purchase into another and can create exit-load and tax consequences. For redemption or switch-out, one per cent applies when units are redeemed or switched within one month from allotment; after one month the exit load is nil. Exit load is applied to the applicable redemption value and is separate from tax. Each SIP instalment has its own allotment date, so the one-month test is performed lot by lot. A switch from regular to direct, or from one scheme to another, should not be assumed cost-free merely because both products belong to the same AMC. Total expense ratio TER is charged to the scheme and reflected in NAV rather than collected as a separate invoice from the investor. The AMC publishes a current TER disclosure and notices of changes; its page records a change effective 3 November 2026. Because the rate can change and direct and regular plans differ, this article deliberately does not freeze a percentage that could become stale. The correct review is the live AMC TER page for the exact plan on the transaction date, read with the SID limits and applicable regulation. Other investor-level costs may include exit load, securities transaction tax on redemption, bank or platform charges where separately levied, and income tax. These items serve different purposes and should not be combined into a single “expense ratio.” A capital-gains statement should preserve units, allotment dates, redemption consideration, cost, load and tax withholding or STT information. Riskometer and Scheme-Specific Risks The AMC’s product material places the scheme in a very-high-risk setting, consistent with a diversified mid-cap equity portfolio. Riskometer disclosure is a regulatory communication, not a prediction of a particular loss. Investors should check the latest riskometer for both scheme and benchmark because the displayed level can be updated. The principal risks include equity-price volatility, mid-cap liquidity, issuer concentration, sector cycles, valuation compression, portfolio-manager decisions and the possibility that redemptions during stressed markets realise unfavourable prices. Mid-cap liquidity can deteriorate faster than large-cap liquidity during stress. A portfolio can be diversified by count yet concentrated in a few sectors or economic drivers. Past performance tables are sensitive to the chosen start date and do not assure recurrence. Direct and regular plans produce different net outcomes because TER differs. A short holding period can add exit load and short-term tax to market loss or gain. Operational errors in folio, bank mandate, nominee or transmission records can delay service requests. Indian Tax Treatment for Resident Individuals The scheme is intended to maintain equity exposure consistent with an equity-oriented fund, but the tax classification should be confirmed from the actual scheme status and transaction. For units of an equity-oriented fund transferred on or after the applicable current dates, a holding period of more than twelve months generally produces long-term capital gain; twelve months or less generally produces short-term capital gain. Current law taxes short-term gain meeting the statutory STT conditions at 20 per cent. Long-term gain meeting the conditions is taxed at 12.5 per cent to the extent aggregate eligible long-term gains exceed ₹1.25 lakh in the financial year. Surcharge and health and education cess can apply, and special rules may cap surcharge in specified cases. The ₹1.25 lakh threshold is aggregate across covered long-term gains, not a separate exemption for each folio. Each SIP instalment has its own holding period and cost. Switches, including plan or option switches where units are redeemed and reissued, can be taxable transfers. IDCW is generally taxed as income in the unit holder’s hands at the applicable rate, with withholding rules depending on the law and threshold. Non-residents, businesses, minors, trusts and investors with carried-forward losses can have different outcomes. Tax statements from the AMC or registrar are useful but should be reconciled with demat, bank and return records.
