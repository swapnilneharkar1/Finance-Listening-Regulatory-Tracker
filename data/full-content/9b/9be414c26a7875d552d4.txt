The NSE Nifty 50 declined 277.30 points, or 1.07%, to close at 25,607.20.The selloff gathered momentum through the session, with the Sensex falling 339 points in early trade before extending losses.By 1:57 pm, it was down 749.09 points at 83,579.50, while the Nifty had declined 208.55 points to 25,675.90. Advt Markets retreated after a sharp recovery over the previous sessions, prompting investors to book profits at higher levels. The decline came after the Sensex and Nifty gained on Thursday, extending their rally for a second consecutive session.Most broader market indices ended in the red, while the majority of sectoral indices also closed lower. Nifty Chemicals, Metal, Consumer Durables, MidSmall IT & Telecom, and Media were among the sectors that bucked the trend.On the BSE, BEL, Titan, Trent, Tata Steel and IndiGo were among the top gainers. HCL Tech, TCS, Infosys, Maruti, Tech Mahindra, Axis Bank, Kotak Bank, ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, Bharti Airtel and ITC were among the major laggards.Why did the market fall?A renewed rise in crude oil prices weighed on the sentiment, with escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East raising concerns over possible supply disruptions.Brent crude climbed above $96.50 a barrel in Asian trade, while US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude moved towards $85 a barrel.“On the commodities front, WTI crude has extended its advance toward USD 85 a barrel, with renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East adding to supply concerns,” Ponmudi R, CEO of Enrich Money, an online trading and wealth-tech firm, told PTI.“While there has been no meaningful disruption to physical supply so far, the market is increasingly pricing in the risk of future disruptions, particularly in a region that accounts for a significant share of global oil flows. With the OPEC+ meeting approaching and tensions across the Gulf remaining elevated, Brent is likely to remain well supported, and any further escalation could keep risk premiums elevated in the near term,” Kaynat Chainwala, AVP Commodity Research, Kotak Neo, told ANI. Advt Rupee also gave up some of its recent gains on Friday as weakness in domestic equities and renewed interest in AI-linked markets threatened to affect foreign investment flows.Rupee opened marginally lower at 92.52 against the US dollar and weakened to 92.9925 before closing at 92.99, down 0.6% on the day. It recorded weekly losses of 0.3%, after gaining 1.7% in the previous week.Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) bought equities worth Rs 5,843.33 crore on Thursday, according to exchange data. US equity markets remained closed on Thursday for a holiday.Earlier on Thursday, the Sensex climbed 390.31 points, or 0.46%, to settle at 84,328.59. The Nifty rose 65.25 points, or 0.25%, to end at 25,884.50. Advt (Disclaimer: Recommendations and views on the stock market and other asset classes given by experts are their own.
