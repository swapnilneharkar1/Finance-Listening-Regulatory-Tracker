Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Tin Manufacturing Co. of India Vs Commissioner Customs (CESTAT Allahabad) Summary : The CESTAT Allahabad, in Tin Manufacturing Co. of India Vs Commissioner, Customs, Noida, held that amounts deposited pursuant to directions of the Allahabad High Court for securing the release of imported goods could not be treated as payment of customs duty in the absence of assessment by the proper officer. Consequently, the limitation prescribed under Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962, was inapplicable. The Tribunal set aside the rejection of the appellant’s refund claim of ₹18,91,396 and held that the appellant was entitled to refund with applicable interest from the date of deposit until the date of refund. The dispute originated in 1987, when the appellant imported tin sheets at Mumbai and Customs authorities alleged misdeclaration. The goods were transferred to a public bonded warehouse at Mohan Nagar, Ghaziabad. Although the Collector of Customs decided the original dispute in favour of the importer in October 1989, subsequent difficulties arose concerning clearance of the warehoused goods. The appellant challenged the requirement to file an ex-bond bill of entry before the Allahabad High Court. During those proceedings, the appellant deposited ₹30 lakh in cash and furnished a bank guarantee of ₹13.15 lakh pursuant to judicial directions, securing release of the goods. The writ petition was dismissed for want of prosecution in October 2009, and subsequent recall applications were unsuccessful. However, by order dated 2 February 2018, the High Court clarified that the appellant could pursue an appropriate legal remedy for any customs refund to which it was entitled. The appellant thereafter submitted a refund claim, which was rejected by the departmental authorities. The Commissioner (Appeals) upheld the rejection, reasoning that the amounts represented customs duty and that the claim, submitted approximately two decades after payment, was barred by the limitation provisions of Section 27. The appellate authority also questioned the documentary evidence supporting the claimed refund and treated the asserted customs duty liability of approximately ₹43.15 lakh as final. Before the Tribunal, the appellant argued that the deposits were made solely to comply with the High Court’s directions and were not payments against any completed customs assessment. It submitted that the judicial records themselves established the cash deposit and bank guarantee, and that the absence of historical bills of entry could not convert a court-directed deposit into assessed customs duty. Relying on Commissioner of Central Excise, Bangalore Vs KVR Construction, the appellant contended that statutory limitation governing refunds of tax or duty could not automatically apply to money deposited without a corresponding crystallised tax liability. The Tribunal accepted this distinction. It observed that the Commissioner (Appeals) had inferred a customs duty liability of ₹43.15 lakh without identifying a final assessment determining that amount. The deposits had been made under judicial directions to secure the release of goods, and a bank guarantee was not itself a mode of paying customs duty. Accordingly, the cash deposit and bank guarantee were security towards a prospective customs liability, if any, rather than payment of assessed duty. The Tribunal held that Section 27(1A) could not be invoked to reject the refund as time-barred or for non-compliance with requirements applicable to duty refunds. The Tribunal further relied upon the Karnataka High Court’s decision in KVR Construction and the Telangana High Court’s judgment concerning Credible Engineering, which recognised that limitation provisions governing statutory duty refunds do not necessarily apply to amounts paid without a legal obligation to pay tax. Applying those principles, the Tribunal set aside the impugned appellate order and held that the appellant was entitled to refund with applicable interest calculated from the date of deposit under the High Court’s directions until actual refund. The ruling turns on the legal character of the payment and the absence of a completed customs assessment; it does not establish a general exemption from limitation for every customs refund claim. FULL TEXT OF THE CESTAT ALLAHABAD ORDER M/s Tin Manufacturing Company of India, 95, Rajinder Nagar Industrial Area, Ghaziabad (hereinafter referred to as ‘appellant’) has preferred the instant appeal challenging the Order in Appeal – NOI-CUSTM-000-APP-207-2022-23, dated -19/09/2022 passed by Commissioner (Appeals) CGST & Central Excise, Noida, wherein the Ld. Commissioner (Appeals) has upheld the rejection of refund of Rs.18,91,396/- claimed by the Appellant. 2. Briefly stated facts as per the impugned are as under: i. In the year 1987, the appellant imported tin sheets at Mumbai. The Customs alleged the goods as mis-declared. The goods were transferred to CWC Mohan Nagar, Ghaziabad, a public bonded warehouse. The case was decided by the then Collector of Customs, Bombay in favour of the appellant vide order dated 25.10.1989 issued on 10.01.1990. The party sought clearance of goods stored in the bonded warehouse. Superintendent of Customs vide letter dated 11.04.1990 asked the appellant to file ex-bond bill of entry. The appellant challenged the said order through writ petition No.580 of 1990 dated 30.04.1990 before the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court. ii. During the proceedings, the Hon’ble High Court directed the appellant to deposit Rs. 30.00 lakhs in cash and a Bank Guarantee for Rs. 13.15 lakhs for release of warehoused goods. The party complied with the directions and secured the release of their goods. The writ petition filed by them was dismissed by the Hon’ble High Court Allahabad for want of prosecution vide order dated 07.10.2009. The appellants moved several applications for recall of the said order but all were rejected by the Hon’ble High Court. However, in his last order dated 02.02.2018 while resting the writ to its finality, the Hon’ble High Court observed that, ” If petitioner is entitled to any refund of Customs, he may seek appropriate remedy for the same as may be permitted by law”. iii. The appellant filed the refund claim with the department which was rejected by the Deputy Commissioner of Customs, Noida Customs Commissionerate vide Memorandum Order No. 94/Refund/Noida Customs/2019 dated 07.11.2019. The appellant went into appeal and the Commissioner (Appeals) Noida remanded the case back to adjudicating authority for re­consideration. The refund was again rejected by the Deputy Commissioner of Customs, Noida Customs Commissionerate vide Memorandum Order No. 49/Refund/Noida Customs/2021 dated 28.01.2022 on the ground that the appellant failed to produce any documents having evidentiary value as required under Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962. 3. The Appellant submitted that along with the refund claim, they have filed written submission supported with documentary evidences available with them. It is submitted that vide letter dated 11.04.1990, the Supdt. Directed the appellant to file ex-bond bill of entry. Therefore, the bills of entry were not supposed to be at Mumbai port. Once the order in appeal has given a finding that the department could not brush aside its responsibility of having such vital documents, it was for the department to trace out the same at their end. They furnished corroborative evidence along with copies of judgements of Hon’ble High Court wherein reference about payment of amount and submission of bank guarantee was given. The Appellant submits that the refund has been rejected on the ground that the appellant had failed to produce any documents having evidentiary value as required under Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962. The submission of the Appellant is that they have deposited an amount of Rs.30,00,000/- and also furnished bank guarantee in a sum of Rs. 13,15,000/-. This is a deposit made as per the direction of the Hon’ble High Court and hence this amount cannot be considered as payment of Customs Duty. Accordingly, the Appellant submits that the time limit prescribed under Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962 are not applicable to the facts and circumstances of this case. 3.1. Regarding evidence of payment, the Appellant submits that the Hon’ble High Court categorically mentioned that the Appellant has paid the amount of Rs.30,00,000/- in cash and also furnished bank guarantee in a sum of Rs. 13,15,000/-. Thus, there is no dispute about the payment of the said amount. The Appellant submits that the Ld. Commissioner (Appeals) also categorically held in the impugned order that the Appellant has paid an amount of Rs.30,00,000/- and also furnished bank guarantee in a sum of Rs. 13,15,000/-. However, he has considered the said payment as duty of customs and accordingly applied the time limit prescribed under Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962, for rejecting the refund as time barred. 3.2. In this regard, the Appellant relied on the decision of the Hon’ble Karnataka High Court in the case of Commissioner of C.Ex., Bangalore v. KVR Construction [2012 (26) S.T.R. 195 (Kar.)]., wherein it has been held that the time limit prescribed under the provisions of Finance Act, 1994 for refund of service tax is not applicable for refund of the amounts deposited during the course of investigation. The Appellant submits that the ratio of the said decision is squarely applicable to the facts and circumstances of this case. 4. Heard both sides and perused the appeal documents. 5. We find that the issue involved in the present appeal is whether the appellant is entitled for the Refund of Rs.18,91,396/- as claimed by them or the refund is liable to be rejected on the ground of ‘time bar’ as prescribed under Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962. 5.1. In this regard, we have perused the findings of the Ld. Commissioner (Appeals) in the impugned order. For ready reference, the relevant portion of the said findings is reproduced below: “6. The matrix of the case unfolds during 1987 when the appellant imported tin sheets at Bombay and alleged as mis-declared. The appellant transferred their goods in the public bonded warehouse at CWC, Mohan Nagar pending decision of their case. The then jurisdictional Collector (Customs) decided the case in favour of the party vide order in original dated 25.10.1989 issued on 10.01.1990. It is therefore clear that there was no case pending against the impugned goods after 10.01.1990 or on the date of request made by the party for clearance of their warehoused goods. The Superintendent (Customs) in-Charge CWC Mohan Nagar vide letter dated 11.04.1990 requested the appellant to file ex-bond bill of entry for clearance of the impugned goods. However, the appellant chose to file a writ before the Hon’ble High Court Allahabad against the said letter. I find that the party has submitted a copy of the order sheet dated 23.05.1990. A careful perusal of the same reveals that the appellant counsel pleaded before the Hon’ble Court that they had already furnished a bond for a sum of Rs. Twenty four lacs which is the duty payable by them on the date the goods were bonded. However, the counsel further proposed to deposit the amount in full. Accordingly, the appellant was allowed to deposit further Rs. six lacs and Bank Guarantee for Rs. 13,15,000/- for release of the goods. I further find that the Hon’ble High Court vide proceedings dated 27.08.1990 has mentioned that “Be that as it may, according to the respondents the petitioner has to pay a sum of Rs. 43,15,926/- towards Customs duty….” The Hon’ble Court in a further paragraph added that” The petitioner says that he has paid a sum of Rs. 30,00000/- in cash and also furnished bank guarantee in a sum of Rs. 13,15,000/-.” From the foregoing, it is amply clear that the customs duty leviable on the imported tin sheets of the appellant was to the tune of Rs. 43,15,000/- determined by the department. It is therefore amount of Rs.30,00,000/-and also furnished bank guarantee in a sum of Rs. 13,15,000/-. 7. I further find that the writ petition of the appellant was dismissed by the Hon’ble High Court for want of prosecution vide order dated 07.10.2009. The appellant moved several applications for recall of the order dated 07.10.2009 but the Hon’ble High Court rejected all of them. The natural corollary of the dismissal is that the stand of the Superintendent (Customs) attains finality and the customs duty amounting to Rs. 43,15,000/- stated to have been leviable on the imported tin plates imported by the appellant stands final. 7. I also find that during the proceedings of recall application No. 350502 of 2017 which was eventually rejected by the Hon’ble High Court vide order dated 02.02.2018, on the plea of the appellant’s counsel that in view of the subsequent decisions, they are entitled to refund of Customs duty, the Court observed as follows- “Be as it may be if petitioner is entitled to any refund of customs, he may seek appropriate remedy for the same as may be permitted in law but no cause for recall of the order dated 11.09.2017 is made out and no purpose would also be served by recalling the same.” The tone and tenor of the above observation shows that the Hon’ble Court has refrained itself to pass any order regarding refund of customs duty and left it on the appellant if the same is available as well as on the department to decide it as per the provisions of the law applicable during the relevant period. 9. Now I come to the question whether any refund has arisen in the instant case. I observe that the onus to prove that any refund is due to the appellant lies on the claimant i.e. the appellant in this case. The appellant has failed miserably to adduce any evidence that their duty liability was fixed as Rs. 24,23,603/- by the department. The appellant in their appeal memo has also drawn attention towards page No. 177 to 192 of the submission filed on 10.05.2018 wherein they had submitted copies of annexure D showing the breakup of duty payable in respect of respective bills of entry. I have perused that copies of 177 to 192 of the submission attached with the appeal memo. I find that they are in the form of bonds and nowhere said to be the assessment of customs duty by the department. I also observe that the amount deposited by the appellant was duty of Customs. Even for the sake of argument, if it is presumed that duty was paid in excess, the claim of refund was to be filed within one year from the date of payment of such duty under Section 27/27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962. Since the claim has been filed almost 20 years after the deposit of customs duty, even if the duty was paid in excess, the claim is barred by limitation also. I observe that the Adjudicating authority has rightly rejected the claim of the appellant on the non-submission of any supporting document to buttress his claim. 10. In view of the foregoing discussion, I am of the considered opinion that the appellant has failed to establish that any refund claim of customs duty is due to them as per the law. The appellant has failed miserably in his attempt as no supporting documents have been produced to buttress their claim. The appeal filed by the party fails and is hereby rejected.” 5.2. From the findings reproduced above, we find that the following facts emerge: i. The Appellant has paid a sum of Rs. 30,00000/- in cash and also furnished bank guarantee in a sum of Rs. 13,15,000/-. ii. The Hon’ble High Court vide order dated 02.02.2018, allowed the appellant to seek appropriate remedy and claim the refund from Customs, if they are entitled. iii. The Ld. Commissioner (Appeals) has rejected the refund claim, on the ground that the said refund was to be filed within one year from the date of payment of such duty under Section 27/27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962. Since the claim has been filed almost 20 years after the deposit of customs duty, the claim was rejected as time barred. iv. The Ld. Commissioner (Appeals) has made the following observation regarding the ultimate duty liability payable by the Appellant ‘The natural corollary of the dismissal is that the stand of the Superintendent (Customs) attains finality and the customs duty amounting to Rs. 43,15,000/- stated to have been leviable on the imported tin plates imported by the appellant stands final.’ From the above, it is evident that the customs duty liability has not been assessed and finally arrived at Rs.43,15,000/-. It is only an inference of the Ld. Commissioner (Appeals) the duty liability has been stated to have been determined as Rs.43,15,000/- (v) Finally, the Ld. Commissioner (Appeals) has rejected the refund claim on the ground of non-fulfilling the conditions mentioned in Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962 and not on account of ineligibility of the refund on merits. 5.3. In this regard, we observe that the Appellant has paid a sum of Rs. 30,00000/- in cash and also furnished bank guarantee in a sum of Rs. 13,15,000/-. These deposits have been made as directed by the Hon’ble High Court to secure release of the goods. These payments cannot be considered as customs duty as there was no assessment done by the proper officer when these payments were made as per the direction of the Hon’ble High Court. Further, we observe that ‘Customs duty’ is not paid in the form of Bank Guarantee. The payment of a sum of Rs. 13,15,000/-through bank guarantee, establishes that the said amount paid was only a deposit made as per the direction of the Hon’ble High Court. Accordingly, we hold that the amount deposited by the Appellant cannot be considered as payment of customs duty and the same has to be considered only as a deposit made towards their prospective customs duty liability, if any. Hence, we hold that the time limit prescribed under Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962 are not applicable to the facts and circumstances of the present case. 5.4. In this regard, we observe that the Hon’ble Karnataka High Court in the case of Commissioner of C.Ex., Bangalore v. KVR Construction [2012 (26) S.T.R. 195 (Kar.)]., held that the time limit prescribed under the provisions of Finance Act, 1994 for refund of service tax is not applicable for refund of the amounts deposited during the course of investigation. We observe that the ratio of the said decision is squarely applicable to the facts and circumstances of this case. For ready reference, the findings of the Hon’ble High Court on this issue is reproduced below: “17. If this Court ultimately concludes that Section 11B of the Act is applicable to the facts of the present case, then, the argument of the learned Counsel for the appellant that Writ Petition was not maintainable would merit consideration. Therefore, at this stage, we will not consider the matter regarding maintainability of the Writ Petition, as first we have to look to the provisions of 11B of the Act and then decide whether Section 11B is applicable to the facts of the case as finding thereon would have bearing for considering the issue of maintainability of Writ Petition. Section 11B of the Central Excise Act reads as under : “11B. Claims for refund of duty : (1) Any person claiming refund of any duty of excise may make an application for refund of such duty to the Assistant Commissioner of Central Page 9 of 22 Appeal No.: ST/76006/2024-SM Excise or Deputy Commissioner of Central Excise before the expiry of one year from the relevant date in such form and manner as may be prescribed and the application shall be accompanied by such documentary or other evidence (including the document referred to in Section 12A) as the applicant may furnish to establish that the amount of duty of excise in relation to which such refund is claimed was collected from, or paid by, him and the incidence of such duty had not been passed on by him to any other person.” 18. From the reading of the above Section, it refers to claim for refund of duty of excise only, it does not refer to any other amounts collected without authority of law. In the case on hand, admittedly, the amount sought for as refund was the amount paid under mistaken notion which even according to the department was not liable to be paid. 19. According to the appellant, the very fact that said amounts are paid as service tax under Finance Act, 1994 and also filing of an application in Form-R of the Central Excise Act would indicate that the applicant was intending to claim refund of the duty with reference to Section 11B, therefore, now it is not open to him to go back and say that it was not refund of duty. No doubt in the present case, FormR was used by the applicant to claim refund. It is the very case of the petitioner that they were exempted from payment of such service tax by virtue of circular dated 17­9-2004 and this is not denied by the Department and it is not even denying the nature of construction/services rendered by the petitioner was exempted from to payment of Service Tax. What one has to see is whether the amount paid by petitioner under mistaken notion was payable by the petitioner. Though under Finance Act, 1994 such service tax was payable by virtue of notification, they were not liable to pay, as there was exemption to pay such tax because of the nature of the institution for which they have made construction and rendered services. In other words, if the respondent had not paid those amounts, the authority could not have demanded the petitioner to make such payment. In other words, authority lacked authority to levy and collect such service tax. Incase, the department were to demand such payments, petitioner could have challenged it as Page 10 of 22 Appeal No.: ST/76006/2024-SM unconstitutional and without authority of law. If we look at the converse, we find mere payment of amount, would not authorize the department to regularise such payment. When once the department had no authority to demand service tax from the respondent because of its circular dated 17-9-2004, the payment made by the respondent company would not partake the character of “service tax” liable to be paid by them. Therefore, mere payment made by the respondent will neither validate the nature of payment nor the nature of transaction. In other words, mere payment of amount would not make it a “service tax” payable by them. When once there is lack of authority to demand “service tax” from the respondent company, the department lacks authority to levy and collect such amount. Therefore, it would go beyond their purview to collect such amount. When once there is lack of authority to collect such service tax by the appellant, it would not give them the authority to retain the amount paid by the petitioner, which was initially not payable by them. Therefore, mere nomenclature will not be an embargo on the right of the petitioner to demand refund of payment made by them under mistaken notion. 20. In the case of Hind Agro Industries Ltd. v. Commissioner of Customs reported in 2008 (221) E.L.T. 336 (Del.), it was the case where cess amount was paid under protest by the appellants. In that case after referring to Mafatlal Industries case (supra), the lordships of Delhi High Court have held that in Mafatlal Industries case, Hon’ble Supreme Court was dealing with the case of refund of duty payable within the meaning of either the Central Excises and Salt Act, 1944 or the Customs Act, 1962 as the case may be, wherein they have held that all claims for refund ought to be filed only in accordance with the Customs Act. Therefore, it did not include the payment made under some other enactment, which for some reason had erroneously been made to the Customs authorities. Even otherwise by referring to paragraph 137 of Mafatlal Industries case, one has to see whether the amount claimed is unconstitutional and outside the provisions of Section 11B of the Act. 21. In the case of Nataraj and Venkat Associates (supra), this was pertaining to service tax wherein Page 11 of 22 Appeal No.: ST/76006/2024-SM petitioner company was dealing in architectural services and paid service tax for the construction of the building carried on at Sri Lanka and contended it would not have attracted levy of service tax. In other words, there was an application for refund of said tax and the question that arose therein was what is the relevant date for the commencement of the period of limitation for the purpose of Section 11B and was held that it would be the date of payment of duty. It was held in the paid case that amounts paid cannot be taken to be duty of excise, therefore bar of limitation under Section 11B cannot be applied because such limitation would come in the way of any person claiming refund of any duty of excise and interest. 22. In the case of Commissioner of Central Excise, Bangalore v. Motorola India Pvt. Ltd. (supra) the Division Bench of this Court considered similar issue. It was a case where excess amount was paid over duty under Central Excise Act on the direction of the Department. There was an application for refund of amount and the same came to be rejected by the Assistant Commissioner on the ground of lapse of time. It was confirmed by both the Appellate Authority and also the Tribunal. Aggrieved by the order of the Tribunal, revenue came up before the High Court. Their lordships of the Division Bench held that order of the Tribunal to allow the claim on the basis that amount paid by mistake cannot be termed as duty in the said case was justified and therefore applying the law laid down in the decision of Apex Court in the case of India Cements Ltd. v. Collector of Central Excise – 1989 (41) E.L.T. 358, dismissed the appeal. 23. Now we are faced with a similar situation where the claim of the respondent/assessee is on the ground that they have paid the amount by mistake and therefore they are entitled for the refund of the said amount. If we consider this payment as service tax and duty payable, automatically, Section 11B would be applicable. When once there was no compulsion or duty cast to pay this service tax, the amount of Rs. 1,23,96,948/- paid by petitioner under mistaken notion, would not be a duty or “service tax” payable in law. Therefore, once it is not payable in law there was no authority for the department to retain such amount. By any stretch of imagination, it will not amount to duty of excise to Page 12 of 22 Appeal No.: ST/76006/2024-SM attract Section 11B. Therefore, it is outside the purview of Section 11B of the Act. 24. The learned Counsel for the appellant has also contended that when the order of refund was rejected, the respondent could have approached this Court instead of filing the appeal choosing a wrong forum, could not be a deficit coming in the way to claim refund. 25. However, the petitioner has not approached the Tribunal after the order passed by 1st appellate authority and they have approached this Court. Therefore, even the provisions of Section 35B(1)(b) would not be applicable. From the facts of the case as discussed herein above, it emerges that petitioner has approached this Court contending that Section 11B is not applicable as there was no duty cast on them to pay “service tax” and they have paid such amount under mistaken notion. Viewed from nay angle, we are of the opinion that the learned Single Judge was justified in setting aside that portion of the order which rejected the claim of refund and accordingly same is confirmed. 26. Accordingly, the appeals are dismissed. The concerned appellant authority is directed to refund the amounts as directed by the learned single judge within an outer limit of six weeks from the date of receipt of copy of this order. 5.5. The same view has been held by the Tribunal, Larger Bench in the case of Credible Engineering v. Commissioner of Customs and Central Excise, which has been upheld by the Hon’ble Telangana High Court [Central Excise Appeal No. 03 of 2023 vide judgement dated 15.04.2024 – Telangana High Court]. The relevant part of the said decision rendered by the Hon’ble Telangana High Court is reproduced below: “3. This appeal filed under Section 35G of the Central Excise Act, 1944 (for short “the Act, 1944”) takes exception to the order dated 05.09.2022 passed in Service Tax Appeal No.30781 of 2018 by the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Page 13 of 22 Appeal No.: ST/76006/2024-SM Tribunal, Regional Bench at Hyderabad (Tribunal), whereby the appeal filed by the respondent was allowed by the said Tribunal. 4. It was pointed out that the respondent worked pursuant to ‘Works Contract’ between the period from 30.09.2012 to 30.06.2014 and paid service tax arising thereto. On 10.02.2017, it filed an application for refund of Service Tax of Rs.1,60,81,347/- by contending that under the Act, 1944 it was not liable to pay the tax and tax was paid as a mistake of law. The said application was dismissed on 05.06.2017 by the Assistant Commissioner of Customs, Central Excise & Service Tax, Hyderabad. 5. Aggrieved by the said order, the respondentcompany preferred an appeal, which came to be dismissed on 28.02.2018 by the Commissioner of Customs and Central Tax (appeals-I), Hyderabad. The respondent then approached the Customs, Excise & Service Tax Authority by filing Service Tax Appeal No.30781 of 2018. 6. During the course of hearing, it is pointed out that there was a cleavage of opinion amongst the Members of the Tribunal regarding the aspect of limitation as per Section 11B of the Act, 1944. Resultantly, the matter was sent for obtaining another opinion of Third Member. The learned Third Member opined that when tax was paid as a mistake of law, the limitation under Section 11B of the Act, 1944 is not a hurdle for refund of tax. In view of majority opinion, the impugned decision was taken by holding that Section 11B of the Act, 1944, cannot be pressed into service, in a case of this nature where tax itself is admittedly paid as a mistake of law. 7. Dominic Fernandes, learned counsel for the appellant fairly submits that he is proposing only one substantial question of law i.e., “whether, while processing/considering a claim for refund, the limitation contemplated under Section 11B of the Excise Act, made applicable to service tax under Section 83 of the Finance Act, 1994 would be applicable or inapplicable, in a case where any amount even though it is not payable as service tax, is voluntarily paid by the assessee?” Page 14 of 22 Appeal No.: ST/76006/2024-SM 8. To elaborate, he placed reliance on the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Mafatlal Industries Ltd. v Union of India 1. He submits that in the light of this judgment, the Tribunal has committed an error of law 1997 (89) E.L.T.247 (S.C.) which has given rise to the aforesaid substantial question of law. The other side raised objection. 9. We have heard the matter at length. It is seen that the single question raised by Mr. Dominic Fernandes, learned counsel is no more res integra. In the manner proposed question is framed, it is not in dispute that service tax was not payable by the assessee. This question came up for consideration before the Delhi High Court in Hind Agro Industries Limited v. Commissioner of Customs 2. After considering the judgment of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in case of Mafatlal Industries Ltd. (supra 1), the Delhi High Court held that the judgment of Mafatlal Industries Ltd. (supra) nowhere talks of a situation where the refund of a tax paid under the relevant Act albeit erroneously was required to be made under the Excise Act or the Customs Act and under no other enactment. It was clearly held that judgment of Mafatlal Industries Ltd. (supra 1) is of no 2008 (221) ELT 336 (Del.) assistance in a case where tax is erroneously paid as a mistake of law. 10. The Tribunal has also taken note of the judgment of Karnataka High Court in Commr. of C.Ex. (Appeals), Bangalore v. KVR Construction 3. The Karnataka High Courtn also considered the judgment of Delhi High Court in case of Hind Agro Industries Limited (supra 2) and the judgment of Mafatlal Industries Ltd. (supra 1) and in no certain terms made it clear that where the tax is admittedly paid as a mistake of law, the limitation will not come in the way for refund. 11. It is pointed out by both sides that the judgment of Karnataka High Court in case of KVR Construction’s (supra 3) was unsuccessfully challenged before the Hon’ble Supreme Court and SLP was dismissed on 11.07.2011 which is taken note of by the Tribunal. 12. The judgment of this Court in Vasudha Bommireddy v. Assistant Commissioner of S.T., 2012 (26) S.T.R. 195 (Kar.) Hyderabad 4 was relied upon by other side by contending that the judgment Page 15 of 22 Appeal No.: ST/76006/2024-SM of Karnataka High Court in KVR Construction (supra 3) was considered and this Court also held that when a tax is paid as a mistake of law, the embargo of limitation will not come in the way of claim of refund. 13. We have gone through the aforesaid judgments of the Delhi, Karnataka and the judgment of this Court in case of Vasudha Bommireddy (supra 4). The common string in all the above judgments is that if the contractor was not liable to pay tax, the department cannot retain the amount paid and in that view of the matter, bar of limitation under Section 11B of the Act, 1944 cannot be pressed into service. 14. In this view of the matter, in our opinion, no substantial question of law subsists and needs to be answered, because curtains are already drawn on this issue by various High Courts. Thus, admission is declined.” 5.6. We find that the ratio of the decisions cited supra squarely applicable to the facts and circumstances of the present case. Thus, we hold that the impugned order rejecting the refund filed by the appellant on the ground of limitation or not fulfilling the conditions provided under Section 27(1A) of the Customs Act, 1962, is not sustainable and hence we set aside the same. 6. Thus, we hold that the appellant are entitled for granting of refund along with applicable interest from the date of deposit of the said amount as per the direction of the Hon’ble High Court, till the date of refund.
