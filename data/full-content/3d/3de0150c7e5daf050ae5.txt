Articles 4 min read Micro-business credit grows 21% for individual entrepreneurs, 12% for enterprises over three years: Report According to a report by TransUnion CIBIL and the National Small Industries Corporation Limited (NSIC), India's micro-business entrepreneur credit market reached ₹29.6 lakh crore as of June 2026, with individual business borrowing growing at a 21% CAGR compared with 12% for micro businesses over three years. The latest findings also highlight a sizeable enterprise lending opportunity, with 3.9 crore individual borrowers yet to establish an enterprise credit history. ETBFSI Staff ETBFSI Updated On Nov 27, 2026 at 05:04 PM IST var news_msid = 136216329; console.log(news_msid); Set ETBFSI as your preferred source on Google Copy Link Share on WhatsApp Share on Linkedin Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook -Hashmeet Kaur Credit to individual business borrowers in India grew at a 21% compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) over the last three years, compared with 12% for micro businesses, according to recent findings by TransUnion CIBIL and the National Small Industries Corporation Limited (NSIC). The report highlighted that India's micro-business entrepreneur (MBE) credit market reached ₹29.6 lakh crore in outstanding balances as of June 2026, accounting for nearly half of the country's total commercial credit portfolio.Of the total outstanding MBE credit, ₹19.1 lakh crore was attributed to individuals borrowing for business purposes through business loans, commercial vehicle and commercial equipment loans, and loans against personal property. The remaining ₹10.5 lakh crore was held by micro businesses, defined in the report as entities with credit exposure of up to ₹2 crore. Advt The report noted that the faster growth in individual business borrowing reflects the increasing role of entrepreneurs accessing formal credit in their personal capacity to finance business operations, asset creation and expansion.Enterprise Credit GapThe report identified a significant opportunity to expand enterprise-level lending, with approximately 3.9 crore individual business borrowers having never accessed credit as enterprises.Of the 4.1 crore individuals with active business-purpose loans as of March 2026, nearly 94%, or 3.9 crore borrowers, had no enterprise borrowing history. Within this group, approximately 39 lakh had enquired about enterprise credit but had not subsequently borrowed in that capacity.Their existing credit profiles indicate potential for greater formal credit participation. According to the report, 63% of these borrowers had a CIBIL Score above 730, while 54% had more than two years of business-credit experience. Further, 71% had not missed any loan payments during the preceding six months.The report highlighted that these established credit histories and repayment records could help lenders assess opportunities for extending enterprise-level financing. Cash credit, overdraft facilities and other working-capital products could address the evolving financing requirements of businesses as they mature. Advt Secured Lending TrendsCollateral-backed products continued to account for a significant share of MBE credit. Loans against property (LAP) represented the largest category, with outstanding balances of ₹10.9 lakh crore as of June 2026.Cash credit and overdraft facilities accounted for ₹5.6 lakh crore, followed by commercial vehicle loans at ₹5 lakh crore. Unsecured business loans and term loans stood at ₹2.9 lakh crore and ₹1.7 lakh crore, respectively. Together, these five categories represented 88% of outstanding MBE credit.Meanwhile, unsecured business loan balances grew at a 17% CAGR over three years. The report noted that NBFCs increased their share of outstanding unsecured business loan balances from 38% in June 2023 to 46% in June 2026, indicating their growing participation in this segment. Advt Rural Credit ExpansionCredit growth is also extending beyond established urban centres. Among the top ten states by outstanding MBE balances, semi-urban and rural markets recorded a 22% CAGR over three years, compared with 16% in metro and urban markets.The report noted that semi-urban and rural geographies accounted for 41% of outstanding MBE balances across these states, marking a four-percentage-point increase since 2023.Among individual states, Maharashtra accounted for the largest share of outstanding MBE credit at 14% as of June 2026, followed by Tamil Nadu at 10%. Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat and Karnataka each accounted for 8%, while Rajasthan represented 7%. Telangana accounted for 6%, Andhra Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh 5% each, and Haryana 4%. Advt Portfolio QualityAlongside credit expansion, overall portfolio performance improved over the three-year period. The report highlighted that balance-level delinquencies of 90 or more days past due across the MBE portfolio declined from 3% in June 2023 to 1.9% in June 2026.Individual business borrower delinquencies fell from 3.1% to 1.7% during the same period, while micro-business delinquencies declined from 2.8% to 2.3%.However, certain segments continued to warrant monitoring. Delinquencies in business loans to micro businesses increased by 90 basis points to 2.2% in June 2026 compared with June 2023.The report further highlighted that the next phase of micro-business credit growth could depend on greater adoption of cash-flow-based underwriting, credit guarantee mechanisms and digital financial information to assess businesses beyond traditional collateral-based lending. Strengthening working-capital access and facilitating the transition from individual borrowing to enterprise-level financing were also identified as important factors in expanding formal credit participation.Micro-business owners secure loans, bridging rural markets and urban expansion with optimism.
