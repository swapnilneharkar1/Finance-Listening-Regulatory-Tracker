A nearly 20 per cent decline in prices of crude oil in November improved India’s economic outlook, the report said. The Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI’s) measures to curb importers’ demand for hedging against currency fluctuations, and supply dollars directly to oil-marketing companies have further eased pressure in the interbank foreign-exchange market. However, the recovery has not yet led to a corresponding increase in exporters’ dollar sales. ANZ said exporters had been slow to step up hedging despite the rupee’s appreciation, while hedging by importers remained relatively strong. The central bank may allow further appreciation to encourage exporters to sell dollars before rebuilding its foreign-exchange reserves. “The RBI is likely to allow the rupee to strengthen before rebuilding FX reserves,” the report said. The outlook will also depend on whether inflows of foreign equity continue. ANZ flagged risks from a widening trade deficit and weaker flows outside portfolio investment, with the deficit in goods trade expected to average around $30 billion a month or higher. It sees 90-91 as a key support range for the rupee. Amit Pabari, managing director, CR Forex, said the rupee’s appreciation would depend on the RBI’s ability to rebuild its foreign-exchange reserves. India’s reserves are around $584 billion while the central bank has a net outstanding forward position of about $239 billion on the short side. After adjusting for this position, the indicative residual reserves stand at around $345 billion, compared with nearly $619 billion in 2021. Fresh foreign inflows could prompt the RBI to buy dollars to rebuild its reserves, with the timing and scale of such purchases likely to influence the rupee’s movement, Pabari said. “Considering these factors, 92 could act as a strong support zone for the rupee. However, there are higher chances of the rupee moving back towards 93.50-93.80 in the coming days, because the RBI’s dollar accumulation and global uncertainties could limit further appreciation,” he said. Over the past week, the 10- and five-year yields have declined 12 bps and 5 bps, respectively.
