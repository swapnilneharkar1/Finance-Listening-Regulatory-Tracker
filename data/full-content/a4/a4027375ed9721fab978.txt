(Supreme Court of India) Summary: The Supreme Court of India, in Ram Kotumal Issrani Vs Directorate of Enforcement & Anr., declined to interfere with the Bombay High Court’s judgment dismissing a challenge to the petitioner’s arrest and remand under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA). However, the Supreme Court expressly clarified that the impugned judgment would not prevent the petitioner from raising all available contentions while pursuing an appropriate remedy in accordance with law. Upon being informed that the petitioner’s regular bail application was pending before the High Court, the Supreme Court permitted him to proceed with that application notwithstanding the impugned order and requested the High Court to expedite its hearing. The special leave petition was dismissed with this liberty, and pending applications were disposed of. The Supreme Court’s short order did not independently examine or endorse the detailed reasoning of the High Court on arrest, remand, interrogation or the right to sleep. The underlying proceedings before the Bombay High Court arose from a petition under Article 226 of the Constitution challenging the petitioner’s arrest by the Directorate of Enforcement and the remand order dated 8 August 2023 passed by the Special PMLA Court, Mumbai, in PMLA RA No. 995/2023. The petitioner contended that his arrest was illegal because he had effectively been detained at the Enforcement Directorate’s office in Delhi from 7 August 2023, when he appeared pursuant to summons issued under Section 50 of the PMLA. According to him, his mobile phone was taken away, his movements were restricted and he was questioned throughout the night before being formally arrested at 5:30 a.m. on 8 August 2023. He argued that his production before the Special Court in Mumbai later that evening violated the constitutional requirement of production within 24 hours under Article 22(2), besides raising objections concerning production before the nearest Magistrate. The Enforcement Directorate disputed these allegations and maintained that the petitioner had voluntarily attended its Delhi office in compliance with summons under Section 50 of the PMLA. It submitted that his attendance for questioning did not amount to detention or arrest and that the statutory power of arrest under Section 19 was exercised only at 5:30 a.m. on 8 August 2023. According to the ED, the petitioner was thereafter transported from Delhi to Mumbai and produced before the competent Special Court on the same day, well within the prescribed period. The agency further argued that a person summoned under Section 50 does not automatically acquire the status of an accused and that the requirement of production before the nearest Magistrate must be understood in the context of whether production before the jurisdictional court within the permissible period is practicable. The Bombay High Court examined the chronology and accepted the Enforcement Directorate’s position that the petitioner had not been detained merely because he attended its office pursuant to summons. It observed that Section 50(2) empowers designated ED officers to summon any person whose attendance is necessary for giving evidence or producing records during an investigation or proceeding. The High Court distinguished such attendance from an arrest under Section 19, which requires the authorised officer to form the requisite belief on the basis of material in possession. On the facts before it, the High Court held that the petitioner was formally arrested at 5:30 a.m. on 8 August 2023 and produced before the Special Court in Mumbai within 24 hours. It further reasoned that even if the earlier time of alleged detention were assumed, exclusion of the relevant travel period would bring production within the statutory limit. On the question of transit remand, the Bombay High Court interpreted the reference to the nearest Magistrate under Section 167 of the Code of Criminal Procedure in the context of securing judicial authorisation when an arrested person cannot be produced before the jurisdictional Magistrate within the prescribed period. It held that where the investigating agency can safely produce the arrested person before the competent jurisdictional court within 24 hours, prior production before another Magistrate solely for transit remand is not necessary. Applying this reasoning, the High Court rejected the contention that the petitioner was required to be produced before a Magistrate in Delhi before being transported to Mumbai. It consequently found no illegality in the arrest on the grounds urged and dismissed the writ petition. Nevertheless, the Bombay High Court strongly disapproved the manner in which the petitioner, stated to be approximately 64 years old and suffering from medical issues, was kept waiting and questioned late into the night. His statement was recorded from approximately 10:30 p.m. until 3:30 a.m. The High Court observed that a person summoned under Section 50 may be a witness or another person possessing information relevant to the investigation and should not be deprived of the basic human requirement of sleep. Referring to Ramlila Maidan Incident v. Home Secretary, Union of India, (2012) 5 SCC 1, it emphasised the relationship between sleep, health, human dignity and the protection of fundamental rights under Article 21 of the Constitution. The High Court further observed that statements recorded under Section 50 of the PMLA occupy a distinct statutory position from statements recorded under Section 161 CrPC. It referred to the statutory provisions treating proceedings under Section 50(2) and (3) as judicial proceedings and to the Supreme Court’s decision in Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India. The High Court held that persons summoned for questioning should ordinarily have their statements recorded during reasonable daytime hours rather than late at night when sleep deprivation could affect their health and cognitive faculties. It considered the petitioner’s alleged consent to late-night questioning immaterial in the circumstances and directed the Enforcement Directorate to issue a circular or directions regulating the timing of statements recorded pursuant to Section 50 summons. The High Court nevertheless dismissed the petition challenging the arrest and directed the matter to be listed on 9 September 2024 for recording compliance with its directions. The Supreme Court subsequently disposed of the challenge to the High Court judgment through a brief order. While finding no ground to interfere, it preserved the petitioner’s liberty to raise all contentions in appropriate proceedings and expressly allowed the pending regular bail application to continue independently of the impugned judgment. It also requested expeditious consideration of that application by the High Court. The combined effect of the supplied orders is therefore that the Bombay High Court rejected the challenge to arrest and remand while separately condemning late-night interrogation practices, whereas the Supreme Court declined interference without supplying additional substantive reasoning and preserved the petitioner’s available remedies. The High Court’s detailed observations must accordingly be distinguished from the limited operative directions actually issued by the Supreme Court. Read also HC Judgment: Ram Kotumal Issrani Vs Directorate of Enforcement (Bombay High Court); Criminal Writ Petition (Stamp) No. 15417 of 2023; 15/04/2024 FULL TEXT OF THE SUPREME COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER We do not find any ground to interfere with the impugned order passed by the High Court. However, we make it clear that the impugned judgment will not stand in the way of the petitioner raising all the contentions while seeking an appropriate remedy in the manner known to law. We have been informed that the regular bail application of the petitioner is pending before the High Court. The petitioner is at liberty to proceed with the bail application, notwithstanding the impugned order passed. We request the High Court to expedite the hearing in the pending bail application. The Special Leave Petition is, accordingly, dismissed with the aforesaid liberty. Pending application(s), if any, shall stand disposed of.
