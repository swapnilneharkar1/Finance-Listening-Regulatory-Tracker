Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Unaprime Investment Advisors Private Limited Vs Tork Motors Private Limited (NCLT Mumbai) Summary : The National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai Bench-II, approved the ₹3.45 crore resolution plan submitted by the consortium of Venkateshwara Buildtech Private Limited and Mr. Sanjeev Kumar for Tork Motors Private Limited under Section 31(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016. The plan received 82.59% approval from the Committee of Creditors (CoC). The Tribunal found that the plan satisfied the statutory requirements governing insolvency resolution, including the treatment of operational creditors, payment of insolvency resolution costs, management restructuring and implementation arrangements. The corporate insolvency resolution process commenced on 31 October 2025 following an application by Unaprime Investment Advisors Private Limited under Section 9 of the IBC. The Resolution Professional invited expressions of interest, examined competing resolution plans and conducted successive CoC meetings. Of the ten prospective applicants who expressed interest, two ultimately submitted resolution plans. The consortium of Venkateshwara Buildtech and Mr. Sanjeev Kumar secured the requisite CoC approval. The admitted claims of creditors totalled approximately ₹19.88 crore, comprising employee claims, government dues and other operational creditor claims. The approved financial outlay of ₹3.45 crore included ₹75 lakh for employee salary arrears, ₹2 crore for other operational creditors, provision for EPFO and ESIC dues, state tax dues and CIRP costs. Payments were structured in instalments extending up to 365 days from the plan’s effective date. A significant issue concerned the Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation’s admitted claim of ₹53,03,594. The resolution applicant initially proposed ₹38,63,620, excluding ₹14,39,974 towards interest and damages under Sections 7Q and 14B of the Employees’ Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952. Following the Tribunal’s queries, the successful resolution applicant undertook to pay the entire admitted EPFO claim, including interest and damages. The additional amount would be proportionately adjusted against payments to other operational creditors without increasing the total resolution consideration. The Tribunal referred to the Supreme Court’s decision in K. Sashidhar Vs Indian Overseas Bank and Others, reiterating that the adjudicating authority’s scrutiny of a CoC-approved resolution plan is confined to the requirements prescribed under Section 30(2) of the IBC. It cannot substitute its commercial assessment for that of the CoC. Having examined statutory compliance, the Tribunal approved the plan and directed its implementation. The order also addressed pending applications involving alleged preferential and fraudulent transactions aggregating ₹4,61,69,369 under Sections 43 and 66 of the IBC. Recoveries from these proceedings were directed to be distributed to operational creditors. The Tribunal further clarified that approval of the plan would not automatically waive future statutory obligations or grant exemptions from stamp duty, fees or registration charges. The insolvency moratorium ceased upon approval, and the implementation committee was entrusted with supervising the revival process. Cases Discussed Sashidhar Vs Indian Overseas Bank and Others (Supreme Court); Civil Appeal No. 10673 of 2018; 05/02/2019; (2019) 12 SCC 150. The Supreme Court decision was relied upon for the limited jurisdiction of the adjudicating authority under Section 31 of the IBC and the primacy of the CoC’s commercial wisdom, subject to statutory compliance. ANALYSIS AND FINDINGS 11.1 We have heard the Ld. Counsel for the RP and perused the Plan and related documents submitted along with the aforesaid interlocutory application for the approval of the Resolution Plan. 11.2 The CoC has considered the feasibility and viability of the Plan and approved the same by 100% of the voting shares of the CoC. We notice that the Plan is in compliance of Section 30 of the Code in that it provides for- a) priority of payment of CIRP cost to the payment of other debts of the Corporate Debtor; b. payment of debts of the Operational Creditors; c. the management of the affairs of the Corporate Debtor after approval of the Plan; and d. the implementation and supervision of the Plan. 11.3 In K Sashidhar Vs. Indian Overseas Bank and Ors. (Civil Appeal No. 10673/2018), the Hon’ble Supreme Court held that if the committee of creditors approves a resolution plan by the requisite percentage of voting share under section 30(6), it is imperative for the resolution professional to submit the plan to the Adjudicating Authority. The Adjudicating Authority is then required to satisfy itself that the resolution plan, as approved by the CoC, meets the requirements specified in Section 30(2) of the Code. The law is now settled that the role of the Adjudicating Authority is no more and no less than the above. The role of the Adjudicating Authority with respect to a resolution plan is limited to matters specified in Section 30(2) of the Code. Further, the Adjudicating Authority is not required to interfere with the commercial wisdom of the CoC. 11.4 As far as the issue of pending dues from EPFO is concerned, upon perusal of available records, it was found that the Applicant/RP had admitted the EPFO’s claims to the amount of Rs. 53,03,594/-. However, the SRA had made provisions for the payment of Rs. 38,63,620/- i.e., principal amount only while declined to pay the remaining amount of Rs. 14,39,974/- which was the interest and damages under Section 7Q and 14B on ground of such interest and damages being assessed during the CIRP period and not crystalized prior to the insolvency commencement date i.e., 31.10.2025. In view of such difference between the EPFO’s admitted claims and actual amount to be paid under the proposed Resolution Plan, the present Interlocutory Application was listed for clarification vide our order dated 30.10.2026 pursuant to which the Applicant/RP filed its Additional Affidavit dated 03.11.2026 wherein the copy of the SRA’s affidavit dated 02.11.2026 regarding payment of EPFO dues was provided on record. As per the SRA’s Affidavit dated 02.11.2026, it was clearly stated that the SRA undertakes to pay the entire admitted claim of EPFO amounting to Rs. 53,03,594/-including the principal amount, interest and damages under Section 7Q and 14B of the EPFO & MP Act, 1952 instead of initial allocated amount of Rs. 38,63,620/- under the Plan. The SRA further states that the differential amount of Rs. 14,39,974/- shall be deducted proportionately from payments to other operational creditors in terms of Clause 3.4 and 3.6 (note C) of the Resolution Plan to ensure the total Plan value of Rs. 3,45,00,000/- remained unaltered. Since, the claims of the EPFO would be fully paid as per the Plan, we conclude that the proposed Plan of the SRA does not violate Section 30(2)(b) of the Code along with Regulation 38(1) of the CIRP Regulations. 11.5 We find that the Plan meets the requirements under Section 30(2) of the Code and that it is not in violation of provisions of any law for the time being in force. Further, in Kalpraj Dharamshi & Anr. Vs. Kotak Investment Advisors Ltd & Anr., [Civil Appeal Nos. 2943-2944 of 2019], the Hon’ble Supreme Court also held that the commercial wisdom of CoC must be adhered to unless the adjudicating authority is satisfied that the requirement of Section 30(2) of the Code has not been complied with. 11.6 In the case of Committee of Creditors of Essar Steel India Limited through Authorised Signatory Vs. Satish Kumar Gupta and Ors, [Civil Appeal No. 8766-67 of 2019], the Hon’ble Apex Court clearly held that the Adjudicating Authority would not have the power to modify the Resolution Plan which the CoC in their commercial wisdom has approved. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in the matter of Ghanshyam Mishra and Sons Private Limited Vs. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company Limited, [Civil Appeal No. 8129 of 2019] held that on the date of the approval of the resolution plan by the Adjudicating Authority, all such claims which are not a part of the resolution plan, shall stand extinguished and no person will be entitled to initiate or continue any proceedings in respect to a claim which is not a part of the resolution plan. 11.7 In view of the discussions and the law thus settled, we are of the considered view that the Plan meets the requirements of Section 30(2) of the Code and Regulations 37, 38, 38(1A), and 39(4) of the CIRP Regulations. The Plan is not in contravention of any of the provisions of Section 29A of the Code, as undertaken by the SRA, and is in accordance with the law. We are satisfied that the Plan has provisions for its effective implementation. As discussed above, we find that the present IA deserves to be allowed. ORDER The IA (I.B.C.)
