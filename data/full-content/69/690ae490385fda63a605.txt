Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); DCIT Circle 1(3)(1) Vs Gufic Biosciences Limited (ITAT Mumbai) Summary : Gufic Biosciences Limited, which researches, manufactures and sells pharmaceutical and nutraceutical products, declared income of ₹1,02,81,02,100 for AY 2023-24. The Assessing Officer (AO) assessed income at ₹1,40,80,34,949 after disallowing ₹25,81,80,443 in travel, conveyance and vehicle expenses under Section 37(1), ₹11,53,70,286 relating to statutory payments and Ind AS adjustments, ₹2,52,465 in customer-imposed penalties, ₹32,12,592 in depreciation and ₹29,17,060 under Section 40(a)(ia). The CIT(A), by order dated 2 September 2025, deleted the first three disallowances, restricted the depreciation disallowance to ₹17,56,333 and sustained the TDS disallowance. The Revenue appealed against the relief and the company filed a cross-objection on the TDS issue. The Revenue submitted a comparative chart showing that the CIT(A) had relied on material the AO said was missing at assessment. For example, the AO had received only sample invoices for ₹2,88,30,093 in conference and seminar expenses, while the CIT(A) referred to ledgers, invoices and TDS details without explaining when the records were produced or whether the AO had examined them. For ₹4,36,17,101 in foreign travel expenses and ₹32,76,336 in foreign currency purchases, the CIT(A) accepted the business-purpose explanation without addressing the missing traveller records or verifying how the currency was used. The Tribunal also identified unresolved questions about employee transport and petty cash reimbursements, the applicability of Section 40A(3), credit card charges, expenses relating to Korea, documentation supplied in response to an auditor’s query, monthly travel provisions and whether entries described as CSR were for customer service representatives. The Tribunal found that the CIT(A) had failed to deal with specific assessment objections and that the appellate order did not show that the AO had been given an opportunity to examine and rebut additional evidence under Rule 46A. It also cited Section 250(6), which requires the CIT(A) to state the points for determination, the decisions and the reasons. On the ₹11,53,70,286 grouped under Section 43B in the Revenue’s grounds, the Tribunal required verification of payment dates and reconciliation of gratuity, bonus and the disputed Ind AS entries with the books, income computation and tax audit report. It distinguished contractual charges from penalties for violating the law and required examination of the agreements supporting the delayed-delivery charges. The assessee admitted claiming both depreciation and a deduction under Section 35(1)(iv) on the same R&D assets. The Tribunal directed verification of invoices, the fixed asset register and the dates the assets were put to use to determine the excess depreciation. The cross-objection concerned a disallowance of ₹29,17,060, computed at 30% of commission and other expenses of ₹97,23,532 for alleged failure to deduct TDS under Section 194H. The assessee contended that tax on the Map Pharma payment had been deducted in an earlier year and that tax on the payment to Pharmula Innovations LLP had been deducted on 6 March 2023. It also disputed TDS liability on certain provisions and export commission. The Tribunal directed the AO to reconcile the party-wise figures with the books and avoid double counting, verify earlier deductions, examine when liabilities accrued and whether payees were identified, and consider Sections 9 and 195 and any applicable Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement for export commission. The assessee did not press its claim concerning domestic commission of ₹25,95,914, which the AO must account for when recomputing the disallowance. ITAT Mumbai set aside the CIT(A)’s findings on the issues raised by the Revenue and restored those issues to the AO for de novo consideration. It also restored the TDS issue for fresh verification after giving the assessee a reasonable opportunity to be heard. Both the Revenue’s appeal and the assessee’s cross-objection were allowed for statistical purposes. FULL TEXT OF THE ORDER OF ITAT MUMBAI The appeal filed by the Revenue and the Cross Objection filed by the vide order dated 02-Sep-2025 for the Assessment Year 2023-24. Since the appeal filed by the Revenue and the Cross Objection filed by the assessee arise out of the same order, hence they are clubbed, heard together and disposed of by this consolidated order. ITA No. 7885/Mum/2025, A.Y 2023-24. The Department has raised the following grounds of appeal: 1) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, Ld. CIT(A) erred in deleting the disallowance made u/s 37(1) of the Act on account of traveling, conveyance and vehicle expenses amounting to Rs.25,81,80,443/- without appreciating the fact that the assessee has failed to establish that such expenses were expended wholly and exclusively for the purpose of business. 2) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, Ld. CIT(A) erred in deleting the disallowance made u/s 37(1) of the Act on account of traveling, conveyance and vehicle expenses amounting to Rs. 25,81,80,443/- relying upon the additional evidence filed during the appellate proceedings without giving opportunity of being heard to AO violating the provisions of Rule 46 (A)(2) and 46A (3) of the IT Rules, 1962. 3) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, Ld. CIT(A) erred in deleting the disallowance of expenditure claimed u/s 43B of the Act amounting to Rs.11,53,70,286/- relying upon the additional evidence filed during the appellate proceedings without giving opportunity of being heard to AO violating the provisions of Rule 46 (A)(2) and 46A (3) of the IT Rules, 1962. 4) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, Ld. CIT(A) erred in deleting the disallowance of Penalty expenses amounting to Rs.2,52,465/- relying upon the additional evidence filed during the appellate proceedings without giving opportunity of being heard to AO violating the provisions of Rule 46 (A)(2) and 46A (3) of the IT Rules, 1962. 5) Whether on the facts and circumstances of the case and in law, Ld. CIT(A) erred in deleting the disallowance of depreciation amounting to Rs.32,12,592/- relying upon the additional evidence filed during the appellate proceedings without giving opportunity of being heard to AO violating the provisions of Rule 46 (A)(2) and 46A (3) of the IT Rules, 1962. 6) The Appellant craves leave to amend OR alter any ground OR to submit additional new ground, which may be necessary. 2. The brief facts of the case are that the assessee, M/s Gufic Biosciences Limited, is engaged in the business of research and development, manufacturing and sale of pharmaceutical and nutraceutical products. For A.Y. 2023-24, the assessee filed its return of income on 27.10.2023 declaring total income of ₹1,02,81,02,100/-. 3. During the assessment proceedings, the Assessing Officer called for various details and supporting evidences with respect to certain expenses claimed by the assessee. The assessee furnished submissions from time to time and also attended the hearing through video conference. However, the Assessing Officer found that the assessee had not furnished supporting documents in respect of several expenses. 4. The Assessing Officer noticed that the assessee had claimed ₹25,81,80,443/- towards travelling, conveyance and vehicle expenses, which included conference and seminar expenses of ₹2,88,30,093/-, foreign travel expenses of ₹4,36,17,101/-, purchase of foreign currency of ₹32,76,336/-, drivers’ salaries, taxi expenses, petty cash payments, credit card expenses and other expenditure. According to the Assessing Officer, the assessee had furnished only sample invoices and statements but had not produced complete ledger accounts, details of persons travelling abroad, passport particulars, air tickets, supporting vouchers and other documents to verify the genuineness and business purpose of the expenditure. The Assessing Officer also noticed certain cash payments exceeding ₹10,000/-, expenses debited under incorrect accounting heads and monthly provisions without corresponding reversal entries. The Assessing Officer therefore disallowed the entire expenditure of ₹25,81,80,443/-. 5. The Assessing Officer further disallowed a sum of ₹11,53,70,286/- comprising gratuity, bonus, Ind AS adjustments relating to notional interest, provision for debtors, rent and profit on sale of fixed assets. According to the AO, the assessee had initially claimed these amounts as allowable under section 43B of the Act but subsequently changed its explanation without properly reconciling the amounts with its books of account and computation of income. He also disallowed ₹2,52,465/- towards penalties imposed by customers for delayed delivery of goods, as the assessee had not furnished complete supporting evidence. Further, the Assessing Officer noticed that the assessee had claimed deduction under section 35(1)(iv) of the Act on capital expenditure of ₹2,14,17,282/- incurred on research and development assets and had also claimed depreciation on the same assets. Although the assessee accepted the mistake and submitted that the excess depreciation was only ₹17,56,333/-, the Assessing Officer disallowed ₹32,12,592/- for want of supporting evidence regarding the dates of purchase and use of the assets. The Assessing Officer also made a disallowance of ₹29,17,060/- under section 40(a)(ia) of the Act, being 30% of commission and other expenses of ₹97,23,532/-, for failure on the part of the assessee to deduct tax at source. Accordingly, the AO assessed the total income at ₹1,40,80,34,949/-. 6. Aggrieved by the assessment order, the assessee preferred an appeal before the learned CIT(Appeals). The assessee submitted that the travelling and other expenses were incurred for business purposes and certain expenses had merely been booked under incorrect accounting heads. It was further submitted that the amount of ₹11,53,70,286/- were statutory payments and Ind AS adjustments which were allowable while computing taxable income. The assessee also submitted that the penalties of ₹2,52,465/- were contractual payments for delayed supply of goods and not penalties for violation of law. Regarding depreciation, the assessee submitted that the excess claim was only ₹17,56,333/-. 7. The learned CIT(Appeals) accepted the assessee’s explanations and deleted the disallowances of ₹25,81,80,443/- towards travelling and conveyance expenses, ₹11,53,70,286/- relating to statutory payments and Ind AS adjustments and ₹2,52,465/- towards contractual penalties. The learned CIT(Appeals) observed that the assessee had furnished supporting documents and the Assessing Officer had not established that the expenses were not genuine. Regarding depreciation, the learned CIT(Appeals) restricted the disallowance to ₹17,56,333/- as against ₹32,12,592/- made by the Assessing Officer. However, the learned CIT(Appeals) confirmed the disallowance of ₹29,17,060/- under section 40(a)(ia) of the Act, holding that the assessee had not furnished sufficient evidence regarding deduction of tax at source. 8. The Department is in appeal before us against the relief granted by the learned CIT(Appeals), whereas the assessee has filed its Cross Objection against the confirmation of disallowance of ₹29,17,060/- under section 40(a)(ia) of the Act. 9. We have heard the rival contentions and perused the material on record. We find that the main grievance of the Department is that the learned CIT(Appeals) deleted substantial additions without properly examining the deficiencies pointed out by the Assessing Officer. The learned Departmental Representative submitted that the assessee had failed to furnish several important documents during the assessment proceedings, but the learned CIT(Appeals) accepted the explanations and additional evidence without calling for a remand report from the Assessing Officer. Ld. DR submitted that this was in violation of Rule 46A of the Income-tax Rules, 1962. In support of his contention, the learned Departmental Representative furnished a comparative chart showing the documents which, according to the Assessing Officer, were not furnished during assessment proceedings but were subsequently referred to and relied upon by the learned CIT(Appeals). 10. We have examined the assessment order, the findings of the learned CIT(Appeals) and the comparative chart furnished by the learned Departmental Representative. We find considerable merit in the objections raised by the Department. The Assessing Officer had pointed out specific deficiencies in the documents furnished by the assessee. However, the learned CIT(Appeals) accepted the assessee’s explanations without dealing with those deficiencies pointed out by the Ld. AO or giving the Assessing Officer an opportunity to examine the additional material. 11. For instance, in respect of conference and seminar expenses of ₹2,88,30,093/-, the Assessing Officer specifically observed in paragraph 3.8.1.1 of the assessment order that the assessee had furnished only sample invoices and had not produced the basic ledger accounts or complete TDS details. However, in paragraph 5.6 of the appellate order, the learned CIT(Appeals) held that the assessee had furnished ledger accounts, invoices and TDS details. The learned CIT(Appeals) did not explain how the documents which, according to the Assessing Officer, had not been furnished during assessment proceedings were available during the appellate proceedings. There is also nothing on record to show that these documents were sent to the Assessing Officer for verification. 12. Similarly, in respect of foreign travel expenses of ₹4,36,17,101/-, the Assessing Officer had specifically asked for passport details, air tickets, PAN details and the identity of the persons who travelled abroad. The Assessing Officer observed that these details had not been furnished. However, the learned CIT(Appeals) accepted the explanation that the expenses were incurred by the company’s executives for business purposes, without examining whether the specific information asked by the Assessing Officer had been provided. 13. The same deficiency was noticed in respect of the purchase of US Dollars and Euros amounting to ₹32,76,336/-. The Assessing Officer had questioned the actual utilisation of the foreign currency and had observed that mere purchase of foreign currency could not establish that the amount had been spent for business purposes. The learned CIT(Appeals), however, accepted the general explanation regarding foreign travel without examining the actual utilisation of the currency. 14. We further notice that the Assessing Officer had questioned the expenses relating to drivers’ salaries and bonus, taxi services and petty cash payments. In respect of drivers’ salaries and taxi expenses, the Assessing Officer observed that the assessee had not furnished supporting documents. The assessee submitted that these expenses related to transport facilities provided to employees and certain narrations were incorrectly recorded. The learned CIT(Appeals) accepted the explanation without verifying the supporting records. 15. Similarly, in respect of petty cash payments exceeding ₹10,000/-, the Assessing Officer had specifically raised the issue of applicability of section 40A(3) of the Act and recorded that only a few vouchers had been furnished. The assessee claimed that these were reimbursements of smaller expenses incurred by employees during the month. However, the learned CIT(Appeals) accepted this explanation without examining the individual payments and supporting vouchers. 16. The Assessing Officer had also observed that the assessee had failed to furnish documents regarding credit card expenses, the persons holding the cards and the business purpose of such expenditure. He further noticed that no explanation had been furnished regarding expenses relating to Korea and that only a sample report and brochure had been submitted in respect of expenses booked pursuant to the auditor’s query. These specific objections were not properly dealt with by the learned CIT(Appeals). 17. The Assessing Officer had questioned the monthly provisions for travelling expenses, which ranged from ₹90.90 lakh to ₹98 lakh, as corresponding reversal entries were not reflected in the statements furnished by the assessee. The learned CIT(Appeals) accepted the assessee’s explanation without reconciling these entries with the books of account. 18. The Assessing Officer had also questioned certain expenses described as CSR expenses, whereas the assessee explained that CSR referred to Customer Service Representative and not Corporate Social Responsibility. This explanation was also accepted by Ld. CIT(A) without proper verification. 19. Thus, we find that the Assessing Officer had raised specific objections regarding the nature, genuineness and business purpose of several items of expenditure. The learned CIT(Appeals), however, deleted the entire disallowance of ₹25,81,80,443/- mainly on the ground that the assessee had furnished bills and vouchers and the Assessing Officer had not established that the expenses were not genuine. In our view, the learned CIT(Appeals) ought to have examined the deficiencies pointed out by the Assessing Officer and given a clear findings on each material issue. If the assessee furnished additional evidence during the appellate proceedings, the learned CIT(Appeals) was required to follow the procedure prescribed under Rule 46A and grant the Assessing Officer an opportunity to examine and rebut such evidence. The appellate order does not show that this procedure was followed. 20. We also find that the learned CIT(Appeals) deleted the addition of ₹11,53,70,286/- relating to statutory payments and Ind AS adjustments without reconciling the individual amounts with the books of account, computation of income and tax audit report. The Assessing Officer had questioned the treatment of gratuity, bonus, notional interest, provision for debtors, rent and profit on sale of fixed assets. The assessee explained that some amounts were statutory payments made before the due date of filing the return and others were accounting adjustments required for computing taxable income. These explanations required verification of the actual payments and the relevant accounting entries. The learned CIT(Appeals) accepted the explanation without recording any specific findings on the allowability of each item. 21. With regard to penalties of ₹2,52,465/-, the Assessing Officer had observed that supporting documents had not been furnished. The learned CIT(Appeals) accepted the explanation that these were penalties imposed by customers for delay in supply of goods. While a contractual payment arising in the ordinary course of business is different from a penalty imposed for violation of law, the nature of the payment and the supporting contractual documents were required to be examined before granting relief. 22. Regarding depreciation, we find that the assessee had admittedly claimed deduction under section 35(1)(iv) on capital expenditure of ₹2,14,17,282/- and had also claimed depreciation on the same assets. The assessee accepted the mistake but submitted that the excess depreciation was ₹17,56,333/- because certain assets were put to use for less than 180 days. The Assessing Officer rejected this computation for want of adequate supporting evidence. The learned CIT(Appeals), however, accepted the assessee’s computation without obtaining verification from the Assessing Officer. In our view, the correct amount of excess depreciation requires verification of the invoices, fixed asset register, dates of purchase and dates of putting the assets to use. 23. We are of the considered view that that the learned CIT(Appeals) has not dealt with the specific findings recorded by the Assessing Officer. Section 250(6) of the Act requires the Ld. CIT(A) to state the points for determination, the decision thereon and the reasons for the decision. The learned CIT(Appeals) was therefore required to pass a reasoned order dealing with the factual objections raised in the assessment order. In the present case, substantial relief has been granted without properly dealing with those objections. Further, the comparative chart furnished by the learned Departmental Representative raises a serious issue regarding admission and reliance upon additional evidence without following Rule 46A. The Ld. CIT(A) order does not disclose that any remand report was called for or that the Assessing Officer was given an opportunity to examine such evidence. 24. Considering the above facts, we are of the view that the matter requires fresh examination by the Assessing Officer. The Assessing Officer has raised several specific objections regarding the supporting documents and allowability of expenditure, whereas the learned CIT(Appeals) has granted substantial relief without properly examining those objections. We therefore set aside the findings of the learned CIT(Appeals) on the issues raised in the Department’s appeal and restore the matter to the file of the Assessing Officer for de novo consideration. 25. In the result, the appeal of the Department is allowed for statistical purposes. CO No. 30/Mum/2026, A.Y: 2023-24. The assessee has raised the following grounds of appeal: CROSS OBJECTION 1: DISALLOWANCE U/S 40(a)(ia) OF THE ACT AMOUNTING TO RS. 29,17,060/- ON ACCOUNT OF NON DEDUCTION OF TAX AT SOURCE 1.1 On the facts and in the circumstances of the case and in law, the ld. CIT(A) has erred in disallowing an amount of Rs. 29,17,060/- allegedly on account of non- deduction of tax at source on certain expenditures. 1.2 The Id. CIT(A) failed to appreciate and ought to have held that in respect of a part of the said expenditure, tax was duly deducted at source and relevant evidences were furnished. In respect of the remaining amount, tax was either not required to be deducted under the provisions of the Act or the same was paid before the due date of filing the return of income. 1.3 The Cross objector prays that the disallowance made by the ld. CIT(A) be deleted. CROSS OBJECTION 2: GENERAL The Cross-objector craves leave to add to, alter, and/or amend all or any of the above Cross-objections. 26. We now take up the Cross Objection filed by the assessee against the disallowance of ₹29,17,060/- under section 40(a)(ia) of the Act. The Assessing Officer had made this disallowance being 30% of commission and other expenses of ₹97,23,532/- for failure to deduct tax at source under section 194H of the Act. The learned CIT(Appeals) confirmed the disallowance on the ground that the assessee had not furnished complete supporting documents. 27. Before us, the learned Authorised Representative furnished a party-wise chart and submitted that the disallowance was not justified in respect of several payments. It was submitted that TDS relating to payments made to Map Pharma amounting to ₹10,43,750/- had already been deducted in earlier financial year and TDS on ₹26,736/- paid to Pharmula Innovations LLP was deducted on 06.03.2023. The learned Authorised Representative further submitted that certain amounts were provisions made in the books of account and that no TDS was required on the export commission payments. He also fairly stated that the assessee was not pressing its claim in respect of domestic commission amounting to ₹25,95,914/-. 28. We have considered the submissions of both sides. We find that the disallowance under section 40(a)(ia) of the Act involves different categories of payments which require separate verification. The assessee has claimed that tax was already deducted on certain payments, certain provisions did not attract TDS and export commission payments were not liable for deduction of tax at source. These claims need to be examined. 29. We also notice that the assessee has furnished a breakup of sundry expenses payable of ₹86,53,046/- and referred to domestic commission of ₹25,95,914/- and export commission amounts of ₹19,85,037/-, ₹54,02,412/- and ₹12,65,597/-. The figures furnished in the chart need to be reconciled with the books of account and the amount of ₹97,23,532/- considered by the Assessing Officer. The Assessing Officer shall verify the correct amounts and ensure that no item is considered twice. He shall also verify the assessee’s claim that TDS was deducted in an earlier financial year and examine the nature of the export commission payments, the applicability of sections 9 and 195 of the Act and the relevant Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement, wherever applicable. In respect of provisions for expenses, the Assessing Officer shall examine whether the liability had accrued, whether the payees were identified and whether the relevant TDS provisions were attracted. 30. As regards domestic commission of ₹25,95,914/-, since the learned Authorised Representative has expressly stated that the assessee is not pressing its claim in respect of this amount, the Assessing Officer shall take this into account while recomputing the disallowance. We accordingly restore the issue relating to section 40(a)(ia) to the file of the Assessing Officer for fresh consideration. The Assessing Officer shall verify the relevant evidence and grant appropriate relief in accordance with law after providing a reasonable opportunity of being heard. 31. In the result, the appeal filed by the Department and the Cross Objection filed by the assessee are allowed for statistical purposes.
