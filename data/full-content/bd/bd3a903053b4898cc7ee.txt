Vs Commissioner of Customs & Others (Karnataka High Court) Summary: The Karnataka High Court disposed of a writ petition filed by Volvo CE India Private Limited challenging an Investigation Report dated 20.05.2026 and the consequential Show Cause Notice dated 18.08.2026 concerning the determination of the transaction value of goods imported from a related foreign entity. The controversy concerned whether royalty or licence fees payable at 1.75% of the net sale value of licensed products should be added to the declared value of imported components, parts and aggregates used in manufacturing licensed products in India. Proceedings concerning the disputed valuation had been deferred for over three years at the petitioner’s request because an identical issue involving another group company was pending before CESTAT, Bengaluru, in Appeal No. C/27663/2013. The Tribunal, by its order dated 06.05.2026, decided the issue in favour of the group company. The petitioner furnished a copy of the Tribunal’s order on 13.08.2026, along with a detailed response explaining its applicability to the pending investigation. Despite the submission of the favourable Tribunal ruling, the customs authorities issued the impugned Show Cause Notice on 18.08.2026. The petitioner contended that its specific submissions concerning the applicability of the Tribunal’s decision had not been considered. It further submitted that customs authorities at various ports were insisting upon payment of duty in accordance with the Investigation Report based on provisional assessments, adversely affecting its business operations. The Revenue opposed interference at the show cause notice stage, arguing that the petitioner could reiterate its submissions before the adjudicating authority. The High Court observed that the Investigation Report did not constitute a final determination. However, when proceedings had previously been deferred because an identical issue was pending before the Tribunal, the authorities ought to have considered the reasons underlying the Tribunal’s favourable decision before issuing the Show Cause Notice. Nevertheless, the Court held that this omission did not, by itself, justify interference with or quashing of the Investigation Report or the Show Cause Notice, since the adjudicating authority could still examine whether the proposed proceedings survived in light of the Tribunal’s findings. The Court specifically referred to paragraph 41 of the Tribunal’s decision, wherein the Tribunal had observed that the adjudicating authority had not examined the pricing arrangement between the importer and the overseas supplier to determine whether payment of royalty and technical know-how fees constituted a condition of sale of imported parts. The Tribunal had also rejected the notional addition of management fees at 4% for the period from 01.08.2007 to 31.03.2012 as being based on assumptions and presumptions. Consequently, the Tribunal had held that royalty and technical assistance or know-how fees paid to AB Volvo could not be added to the transaction value in the circumstances of that case. The High Court directed that all proceedings under the impugned Investigation Report remain deferred pending consideration of the petitioner’s response. The petitioner was granted four weeks from receipt of the certified copy of the order to file its detailed response reiterating its earlier submissions. The third respondent was directed to communicate the deferment to customs authorities at all ports, provide an opportunity of personal hearing and communicate the outcome within three months from receipt of the response. All substantive questions concerning customs valuation and royalty inclusion were expressly left open for consideration in light of the Tribunal’s observations. It also rejected the hypothetical addition of management fees. FULL TEXT OF THE KARNATAKA HIGH COURT JUDGMENT/ORDER The petitioner, an erstwhile Group Company of M/s. Volvo CE India Private Limited, has impugned the Investigation Report dated 20.05.2026. This Report is for determination of the transactional value of imports by the petitioner from a related Foreign Entity. The petitioner is also aggrieved by the Show Cause Notice, which is consequent to the Investigation Report. 2. Mr. Ravi Raghavan, the learned counsel for the petitioner, submits that the Investigation Report relates to whether the Royalty/License Fee at 1.75% of net sale value of licensed products should be added to the declared value of components, parts and aggregates imported which are used for manufacture of licensed products. The proceedings based on the impugned Investigation Report are deferred on the petitioner’s request for over three [3] years, and the petitioner has made this request for deferment because an identical question was pending with the Customs, Excise and Service Tax Appellate Tribunal [for short, ‘the Tribunal’], Bengaluru in Appeal No. C/27663/2013 at the instance of another Group Company. 3. The Tribunal has answered this question in favour of the Group Company holding that the importer would not be liable to pay customs duty on the royalty if the royalty is part of the sale price paid and if the imported parts are used in on-site manufacturing though with the assistance of the technical know-how made known. The petitioner has furnished a copy of the Tribunal’s order dated 06.05.2026 and the petitioner has furnished a copy of this order only on 13.08.2026 because the certified copy was received much later. 4. The impugned Show Cause Notice is issued on 18.08.2026 notwithstanding the petitioner producing a copy of the Tribunal’s order with a detailed response specifically stating that the issue investigated and report filed is answered by the Tribunal in favour of a Group Company which is similarly situated as the petitioner but the impugned Show Cause Notice is issued without considering this specific reply. 5. Sri Ravi Raghavan submits that the petitioner’s difficulty, and therefore the cause for the present petition, is because the Authorities at the ports are insisting upon the petitioner to discharge the duty in terms of the Investigation Report based on the provisional assessment and that this insistence is affecting the petitioner’s business. Mr. Jeevan J. Neeralgi, a learned Senior Standing Counsel who accepts notice for the respondents, is heard, and the learned Senior Standing counsel submits that this Court may not interfere at this stage because the petitioner is only served with a Show Cause Notice and it is open to the petitioner to file a further reply reiterating its response dated 13.08.2026. 6. This Court must opine that the impugned Investigation Report is not the final word and this stands out with the Authorities issuing the impugned Show Cause Notice to the petitioner. This Court must also observe that if the Authorities had deferred proceedings under the impugned Inspection Report for over three [3] years because the issue covered under the Inspection Report was being considered by the Tribunal and such issue is answered by the Tribunal in favour of a party who is similarly placed as the petitioner, the reasons that have prevailed with the Tribunal should have been considered same before issuance of the impugned Show Cause. 7. However, this Court is of considered view that this in itself cannot be a reason to interfere with the impugned Investigation Report or the Show Cause Notice because, as emphasized by Mr. Jeevan J. Neeralgi, it would be open to the third respondent to consider whether the issue which is examined in the Investigation Report and for which the Show Cause Notice is issued would survive in the light of the Tribunal’s finding and the petitioner’s specific assertion that it cannot be called upon to pay duty on the Royalty when the parts of the imported goods are used for manufacturing in India though with the assistance of the technical know-how shared by the related Foreign Entity. 8. In fact, this Court opines that the third respondent must consider the petitioner’s response in the light of the following observation by the Tribunal. “41. Applying the said principles to the present case, we find that no scrutiny has been made by the adjudicating authority to examine about the pricing arrangement between the appellant and the overseas supplier so as to find out whether the royalty and technical know-how fees have been charged as a condition of sale and such payment is made by the appellant to the overseas buyer in relation to the parts imported and used in the manufacture of products. The same yardstick is applicable to technical fees paid during the period 2007-08 & 2008-2009 and not the subsequent years. Also, we find that the appellant has discontinued payment of management fees to AB Volvo during the relevant period. However, the learned Commissioner proposed to add management fees notionally @ 4% for the period 01.08.2007 to 31.03.2012 which is also on a hypothetical basis and rests on assumptions and presumptions, hence cannot be sustained. Therefore, we are of the opinion that the royalty, technical assistance/know-how fees paid by the appellant to the AB Volvo, the overseas supplier of the parts for which the appellant was granted licence to manufacture products cannot be added to the transaction value.” The next aspect that must be considered is whether there must be any observation against precipitation based on the Investigation Report when the third respondent is required to consider the petitioner’s response in the light of this Court’s observation. 9. If the Authorities have deferred action pursuant to the Investigation Report because the issue under investigation was being examined by the Tribunal, the proceedings must stand deferred when the petitioner’s response is yet to be considered resulting in a final decision. The third respondent must ensure that there is due communication about the pending proceedings, the deferment of the Investigation Report and the outcome of the consideration until the response is considered. In the light of the afore, the following. ORDER [A] The petition stands disposed of deferring all proceedings under the impugned Investigation Report dated 20.05.2026 with liberty to the petitioner to file a detailed response to the impugned Show Cause Notice dated 18.08.2026 in reiteration of the reply dated 13.08.2026. [B] The petitioner shall file a detailed response as aforesaid along with the certified copy of this order within four [4] weeks from the date of receipt thereof. [C] The third respondent, upon being informed about the outcome in this petition, shall ensure that there is due communication to the Authorities at all the ports about the pending proceedings and the deferment of the proceedings under the Investigation Report during the consideration of the petitioner’s response to the Show Cause Notice. This communication shall be addressed at the earliest. [D] The third respondent shall also extend an opportunity of personal hearing to the petitioner. [E] It is needless to observe that all questions are left open for consideration but in the light of the Tribunal’s observation as extracted above. [F] The third respondent shall also communicate the outcome of the consideration of the petitioner’s response within three [3] months from the date of receipt of the petitioner’s response.
