Advertisement (adsbygoogle = window.adsbygoogle || []).push({}); Medicare Environmental Management Private Limited Vs Sunil Kumar (GSTAT Patna) Summary: The Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), State Bench Patna, Division Bench Court No. II, held that a mere arithmetical difference between turnover disclosed in GSTR-9C and a VAT return cannot independently establish liability under GST. The Tribunal set aside the demand of Rs.36,59,726, comprising tax of Rs.17,59,576, interest of Rs.17,24,192 and penalty of Rs.1,75,958, raised against Medicare Environmental Management Private Limited, and remanded the matter to the original adjudicating authority for fresh adjudication under Section 113(1) of the CGST Act, 2017. The appellant was engaged in providing bio-medical waste management services and selling goods, including plastic waste. Before the introduction of GST on 1 July 2017, it held a centralised service tax registration at Gurgaon and VAT registrations in Bihar and six other States. For FY 2017-18, its audited financial statements reflected PAN-India turnover of Rs.59,93,11,155, including turnover attributable to the pre-GST period from April to June 2017. The appellant disclosed Rs.1,09,42,270 in Table 5G of GSTR-9C towards pre-GST turnover in Bihar. The Department compared this figure with turnover of Rs.6,52,347 disclosed in the Bihar VAT return in Form RT-1 and treated the difference of Rs.1,02,89,923 as suppressed turnover liable to GST. A show cause notice under Section 73(1) was issued on 30 September 2023. Despite seeking adjournments, the appellant did not furnish its reply, resulting in an ex parte adjudication order dated 29 December 2023. The First Appellate Authority confirmed the demand on 29 August 2025, holding, inter alia, that even exempt service turnover should have been disclosed in the VAT return. The appellant contended that GSTR-9C included turnover from both goods and services, whereas the Bihar VAT return covered transactions in goods alone. It submitted that the alleged suppressed turnover substantially represented bio-medical waste management services of Rs.1,02,98,422 rendered during April to June 2017, which were claimed to be exempt under Entry 2B of Notification No.25/2012-ST dated 20 June 2012. It also contended that Table 5G of GSTR-9C specifically required deduction of pre-GST turnover from the audited annual turnover and could not itself constitute evidence of taxable supplies under GST. The appellant produced audited financial statements, service tax returns in Form ST-3, GSTR-9, GSTR-9C, VAT returns, Chartered Accountant certificates and transaction-wise statements to reconcile the disputed turnover. It further submitted that the pre-GST transactions were governed by the respective VAT and service tax enactments and that Section 142(11) prevented taxation under GST to the extent tax was leviable under the earlier laws. Reliance was placed on the GSTAT Thane decision in Tata Unistore Limited Vs Commissioner, CGST & Ex., Navi Mumbai concerning the examination of matters arising under the erstwhile tax regime. The Department disputed the reconciliation and contended that production of the ST-3 return alone was insufficient to establish that the entire disputed turnover related to supplies made before 1 July 2017. It sought verification of invoices, ledger entries, dates of supplies, allocation of turnover to Bihar and the possibility of duplication in post-GST returns. The Department also raised an objection regarding limitation and alternatively requested remand for verification instead of unconditional deletion of the demand. On limitation, the Tribunal referred to Notification S.O. 4220(E) dated 17 September 2025, which permitted appeals against orders communicated before 1 April 2026 to be filed up to 30 June 2026. As the impugned appellate order was dated 29 August 2025 and the appeal was filed on 17 February 2026, the Tribunal held that the appeal was within limitation. Examining the nature of Table 5G of GSTR-9C, the Tribunal observed that the audited accounts for FY 2017-18 covered twelve months, whereas GST applied only during the last nine months. Table 5G was specifically designed to exclude turnover attributable to April to June 2017. Although a discrepancy in the reconciliation statement could justify an inquiry under Section 73, it could not independently sustain a tax demand. The proper officer was required to establish through material evidence that a taxable supply had occurred without payment of GST and record reasons supporting that conclusion. The Tribunal further held that Section 24(1) of the Bihar VAT Act required returns concerning transactions in goods. Service transactions were governed by Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994, and reported in Form ST-3. In the absence of any statutory requirement to disclose service receipts in Form RT-1, their non-disclosure could not by itself establish suppression. The First Appellate Authority had also erroneously proceeded on the assumption that the final ST-3 return covered April to September 2017, whereas Notification No.18/2017-ST dated 22 June 2017 restricted the final return to the quarter ending 30 June 2017. Regarding transitional taxation, the Tribunal held that once the disputed turnover was factually established as relating to the pre-GST period, GST could not be demanded on those transactions merely because they had remained undeclared or under-declared under the VAT or service tax regimes. Any resulting liability or inquiry would fall within the jurisdiction of the competent authorities under the respective earlier enactments. The burden of establishing the taxable event remained on the Revenue and did not shift merely because the taxpayer failed to respond to a show cause notice. However, the Tribunal clarified that the appellant was required to produce records concerning invoice dates, dates of provision of services and allocation of turnover to Bihar, since these particulars were within its knowledge. If exemption under Entry 75 of Notification No.12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) was claimed for supplies made on or after 1 July 2017, the appellant would have to establish compliance with the applicable exemption conditions. Although the original ex parte proceedings were held to be procedurally valid, the Tribunal found that the adjudicating authority had failed to establish a factual connection between the mismatch and any taxable supply under GST. The First Appellate Authority had also failed to adequately examine the service turnover reconciliation. Further, the impugned order did not explain the computation of tax of Rs.17,59,576 on alleged suppressed turnover of Rs.1,02,89,923, which resulted in an effective rate of approximately 17.1%. The Tribunal declined to allow the appeal outright because crucial transaction-wise statements and the Chartered Accountant certificate dated 26 October 2026 had been produced for the first time before it. Accordingly, the original adjudication order and the appellate order were set aside, including the associated tax, interest and penalty. The matter was remanded under Section 113(1) for verification of relevant records and fresh adjudication after providing an opportunity of hearing. The Tribunal expressly restricted verification to its legal findings so that the remand would not become a fresh roving inquiry. Interest and penalty would abide by the outcome of fresh adjudication. No order as to costs was made. Cases Discussed: Tata Unistore Limited Vs Commissioner, CGST & Ex., Navi Mumbai (GSTAT Thane); 2026 (8) TMI 139. Ratan Housing Development Ltd. LEGAL SUBMISSIONS OF THE APPELLANT 9. The appellant submitted as follows: (a) The difference relates to a pre-GST period and is not taxable under GST. GST came into force on 01.07.2017. For April to June 2017, goods were taxed under the Bihar VAT Act and services under Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994. Section 142(11) keeps such transactions outside GST. Reliance is placed on Tata Unistore Limited v. Commissioner, CGST & Ex., Navi Mumbai, 2026 (8) TMI 139 (GSTAT, Thane), where it was held that issues belonging to the repealed regime cannot be reopened under GST. VAT assessment for April to June 2017 stands completed under Section 26(1) of the Bihar VAT Act, and nothing is pending. Any doubt about these receipts had to be pursued under those Acts, not under the GST Act. (b) Table 5G of GSTR-9C is a deduction, not an addition. Under the GSTR-9C format (Notification No. 49/2018-CT), Row 5G deducts the April–June 2017 turnover from the audited full-year turnover. The appellant did exactly what the form required. The Department has treated a figure, which the form excludes from GST turnover, as taxable GST turnover and the impugned order does not deal with this. (c) Service turnover was never reportable in RT-1. Section 24(1) of the BVAT Act and Rule 19 of the BVAT Rules require a return only of transactions in goods. RT-1 has no column for service receipts. The First Appellate Authority’s question why exempt turnover was not shown in the VAT return therefore starts from a wrong premise. Accordingly, Services were returned in ST-3 under Rule 7 of the Service Tax Rules, 1994, and under Notification No. 18/2017-ST the last return covered April to June 2017 and was due by 15.08.2017. The impugned order wrongly treats it as covering April to September 2017. (d) The figures match: that the bio-medical waste disposal service turnover is Rs. 1,02,98,422/-, whereas the difference of turnover (alleged suppressed figure) considered in the proceedings was Rs. 1,02,89,923/-. The difference is Rs. 8,499/- less than the exempt service turnover of Rs. 1,02,98,422/-, because RT-1 showed Rs. 8,774/- more goods turnover than the books. (e) Documents on record. The appellant has filed the audited financial statements (Annexure-8), Form ST-3 (Annexure-4), GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C (Annexure-5), VAT Return RT-1 (Annexure-6) and a CA certificate (Annexure-9). The appellant submits that it is the reconciliation in compliance of the submissions made by Respondent, and as such he has discharged his initial burden. (f) The services were exempt under both regimes: under Entry 2B of Notification No. 25/2012-ST, and under Entry 75 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), subject to its conditions. So even under GST no tax would be payable. (g) Burden on the Revenue. The burden of proving taxability lies on the Revenue (Ratan Housing Development Ltd., 2025 (5) TMI 773 (CESTAT, Allahabad)), and a demand cannot rest on surmise (Triveni Engineering, 2016 (334) E.L.T. 595 (All.); SVA Steel Re-Rolling Mills, 2018 (362) E.L.T. 411 (Mad.); Anant Jignesh Shah, 2021 (44) G.S.T.L. 342 (Guj.)). ISSUES FOR DETERMINATION We have heard both sides and perused the record, including the written submissions, the counter reply, the rejoinder and the documents filed. On the basis of pleadings of both the parties, the following issues arose for determination: i. Whether the appeal is within limitation period. ii. Whether the figure in Table 5G of GSTR-9C (April–June 2017) can, by itself or by comparison with the VAT Return RT-1, be treated as turnover liable to GST. iii. Whether receipts from services rendered during April–June 2017 were required to be disclosed in RT-1 under the Bihar VAT Act, and their absence amounts to “suppression”. iv. Whether GST can be levied on supplies made before 01.07.2017, and upon whom the burden lies to establish a taxable supply under the Act. v. Whether the ex parte Order-in-Original and the impugned order are sustainable, in the eye of law both procedurally and on substance. I. DISCUSSION AND FINDINGS Issue I – Limitation 12. The Respondent has objected to limitation in general terms, without specifying any delay. The impugned order is dated 29.08.2025 and was therefore communicated before 01.04.2026. It is judicious to note here that Notification of Government of India in the Ministry of Finance, Department of Revenue, S.O. 4220(E) dated 17.09.2025, issued under Section 112(1) of GST Act, allows an appeal against such an order to be filed up to 30.06.2026. This appeal has been filed on 17.02.2026, and as such the same is within time. The issue is decided accordingly. Issue II – Nature of the Table 5G figure in GSTR-9C 13. Form GSTR-9C and its instructions were notified by Notification No. 49/2018-Central Tax. Part II reconciles the turnover in the audited financial statements with the turnover declared in the annual return. Serial No. 5G, “Turnover from April 2017 to June 2017”, is a deduction from the audited turnover, shown with a minus sign. For FY 2017-18 the audited accounts covered twelve months, while GST applied only for nine. The form therefore required every taxpayer to carve out pre-GST turnover to arrive at the net GST-period turnover. 13.1. It follows that a figure in Table 5G is, by the design of the form, turnover the taxpayer declares as not belonging to the GST period. The show cause notice itself records that the Rs. 1,09,42,270/- “was reduced from the total turnover”, and that tax on the July 2017–March 2018 turnover was accepted. Having so recorded, the proper officer treated the same pre-GST figure as GST turnover, without any material showing that any part of it was a supply made on or after 01.07.2017. 13.2. The officer has assumed that a mismatch of turnover between GSTR – 9C and RT-1 equals a taxable supply under GST. This is a legal leap without factual foundation. A mere arithmetical difference between two returns under two different laws does not establish liability under GST. 13.3. It is obvious that GSTR – 9C is not a meaningless document, a discrepancy in it may prompt an enquiry u/s 73 of the Act but it cannot sustain a tax demand on its own. Under section 73 (1), the proper officer is legally required to establish, on the basis of material evidence, that a taxable supply took place without payment of tax, and must record reasons for such conclusion. A bare perusal of impugned order reveals that any such finding or reasoned justification is completely absent, rendering the demand unsustainable in law. Issue – III Whether service receipts had to be shown in RT-1 14. It is fair to note here that u/s 24 (1) of VAT Act, a registered dealer is required to file returns strictly in respect of transactions involving the sales, purchases, receipts and dispatch of goods. Since, the VAT Act levies tax exclusively on sale of goods and does not extend to services. It is equally fair to note here that services fall under union taxation and are governed by chapter–V of Finance Act 1994, and are reported/declared in form ST-3. There is nothing on record or nothing has been shown to us that there is legal requirement to declare service receipts in form RT-1, whether as exempt turnover or otherwise. Furthermore, the appellant has long standing practice of reporting only goods in its VAT Returns over several years without prior departmental objection. Accordingly, omitting service turnover from form RT-1 does not, by itself, establish suppression. 14.1 It would not be out of place to mention here that the First Appellate Authority also observed to determine whether the amount was taxable or exempt lay within the domain of the Adjudicating Authority. However, for the period from April to June 2017, jurisdiction to make such a determination is vested solely with the competent authority under VAT Act, in respect of goods or under the Finance Act, 1994, in respect of services, and not with proper officer under GST Act. 14.2 Additionally, the First Appellate Authority also proceeded erroneously that the ST-3 return covered April to September 2017. However, Notification no. 18/2017-ST dt. 22.06.2017 limited the final return under Finance Act, 1994, to the quarter ending 30.06.2017 requiring it to be filed by 15.08.2017. The appellant’s filing on 15.08.2017 is wholly consistent with this mandate. The Adjudicating Authority can easily verify from the record whether July to September 2017 columns were shown as NIL. Therefore, the period printed on the pre-amended format of standard form cannot, by itself, serve as a valid ground to discard the explanation placed by the appellant. The issue is answered accordingly. Issue IV – Pre-GST supplies and the burden of proof 15. The GST Act came into force on 01.07.2017. GST is levied under Section 9 on “supply” as defined in Section 7 of the Act. For a transaction to be liable to GST, the supply must have occurred on or after that date. Transactions before it remains within the exclusive domain of the earlier laws. Perusal of Section 142(11)(a) and (b)of the Act reflects that: no tax is payable under the Act on goods or services to the extent tax was leviable on them under the State VAT law or Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994. 15.1 Therefore, once it is factually established that mismatch or discrepancy relates to the pre-GST period, no GST can be demanded on those transactions even if such transactions remained undeclared or under declared under VAT or Service Tax regimes. Any resulting tax liability or inquiry lies solely with the authorities empowered under those respective acts, not under the GST regime. 15.2. In tax proceedings, the burden to establish the taxable event lies on the Revenue. A taxpayer’s failure to reply does not shift it. An ex-parte order must still rest on cogent reasons and material on record, not on an inference drawn from silence. 15.3 At the same time, details such as invoice dates, the dates on which services were provided and how the turnover was allocated to Bihar are best known to the appellant. The appellant must therefore produce these records before the adjudicating authority. Likewise, if the appellant claims exemption under Entry 75 of Notification No. 12/2017-CT (Rate) for any supply made on or after 01.07.2017, it must show that the conditions of that entry are met. To this extent, the verification sought by the Respondent is reasonable. The issue is decided accordingly. Issue V – Sustainability of the orders below 16. Although, the ex-parte order was procedurally valid, due to appellant’s repeated adjournments without filing a reply, the Adjudicating Authority was still bound to act judiciously, record cogent reasons and link the mismatch or discrepancy to a taxable supply under GST. In the case at hand, the First Appellate Authority failed to examine the figure of Rs. 1, 02, 98, 422/- which accounts for the entire mismatch and also erred in holding that service receipts are required to be disclosed in form RT-1. 16.1 Furthermore, neither the impugned order explains how a tax of Rs. 17, 59, 576/- was calculated on turnover of Rs. 1, 02, 89, 923/- as the resulting rate of 17.1%, which is entirely unknown to the GST rate schedule. Therefore, the impugned order lacks legal sustainability and is liable to be set aside. 17. We do not allow the appeal outright for the reasons that the appellant failed to file his reply before the adjudicating authority and the key evidence viz. the transaction-wise listings and the CA certificate dated 26.10.2026 has been produced for the first time before this Tribunal. No authority has examined it on earlier occasion. Besides, the Respondent also seeks its verification during course of hearing. 18. Section 113(1) of the Act empowers to refer the case back to the original Adjudicating Authority, with directions, for fresh adjudication after taking additional evidence, if necessary. Taking note of above facts, it is apparent that this is a fit case to refer the case back to the original Adjudicating Authority for fresh adjudication. The appellant shall produce all relevant documents, accounts, invoices, records, statements, returns and CA certificates before the Adjudicating Authority. The verification must, however, be confined within the legal findings above only, so that the remand does not become a fresh roving enquiry. Interest and penalty 19. Interest and penalty depend on the tax. They are set aside along with the demand, and will abide by the result of the fresh adjudication. Now taking into consideration, the above discussions we order as follows: 1. The appeal is allowed by way of remand. 2. The Order-in-Appeal bearing Reference No. ZD100825036048M dated 29.08.2025, and the Order-in-Original bearing Reference No. ZD1012230405336 dated 29.12.2023 are set aside, including the tax of Rs. 17,59,576/-, interest of Rs. 17,24,192/- and penalty of Rs. 1,75,958/-. 3. The matter is remanded under Section 113(1) to the original adjudicating authority for detailed verification. The authority shall pass a fresh, reasoned order in accordance with law and the findings above, after hearing the appellant. 4. The appeal is disposed of in the above terms. No order as to costs.
