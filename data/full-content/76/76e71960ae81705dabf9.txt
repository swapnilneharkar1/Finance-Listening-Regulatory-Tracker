Articles 1 min read Institutional investors step up bets on private credit as insurers, pension funds and DFIs back new funds Institutional investors are now allocating more funds to private credit, significantly from insurance companies and pension funds. Neo Alternative Asset Managers has seen its second fund receive 22-25% contributions from these investors. Ascertis Credit's fourth fund also attracted commitments exceeding $700 million from multiple institutional investors. With increased interest in private credit, annual investment opportunities are now estimated around ₹1 lakh crore. Ascertis Credit's fourth fund, which crossed $700 million in commitments in October, attracted both existing and new institutional investors At Neo, domestic insurance companies form a large part of the institutional pool, said chief executive Hemant Daga. It also received commitments from overseas pension funds. Advt Ascertis Credit, whose fourth fund crossed $700 million (around ₹6,500 crore) in commitments in October, saw investments from existing and new institutional investors, including insurance companies and development finance institutions."The strong reception comes amid increasing institutional interest in performing private credit strategies as investors seek resilient income-generating investments away from market volatility with attractive risk-adjusted returns," Ascertis said in a statement.Institutional investors are increasingly evaluating private credit separately from private equity and venture capital, with greater focus on the risk, returns and track record of individual credit funds.Neo is targeting 35-40 investments, with an average size of ₹250-300 crore, and expects gross returns of 21-23%. It will focus on collateral-backed lending to businesses with established operating cash flows, with about 95% of transactions involving operating firms. Annual investment opportunities are estimated to be nearly ₹1 lakh crore, double the level of around ₹50,000 crore three years ago. Reported transactions were nearly $7 billion in the first nine months of the year, said Daga.
