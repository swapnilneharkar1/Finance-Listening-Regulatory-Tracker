'Helping to instil civic sense': Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw thanks Serbian man for cleaning India's streets

Keeping public spaces clean is everyone’s responsibility, yet littering remains a concern across several Indian cities. A Serbian man living in Bengaluru has been leading by example, spending years cleaning India’s streets. His efforts have now earned praise from Biocon founder Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw, who said he is helping promote civic sense among Indians.

Lazar Jankovic, who has been living in India for eight years, started picking up litter from roads and public spaces five years ago. What began as a personal effort has since grown into an initiative, through which he has organised clean-up drives across the country.

Who is Lazar Jankovic and why is he cleaning India’s streets?

Jankovic recently spoke to the Hands On Foundation during a clean-up drive organised on the occasion of Kannada Rajyotsava. During the conversation, he shared what inspired him to take up the initiative and how his efforts have expanded over the years.

When asked why he decided to clean public spaces, Jankovic had a simple answer. He said, “Love for India, love for Karnataka.”

He further shared, “I started five years ago, but I created the 4cleanIndia initiative two years ago. So, I’ve been cleaning around 20 states in India and a couple of Union Territories as well, promoting civic sense and individual responsibility.”

Explaining how the initiative began, Jankovic said he started by simply picking up litter. He believes people often make the idea of cleaning public spaces seem more complicated than it is. For him, taking responsibility begins with small actions rather than waiting for someone else to step in.

Through 4CleanIndia, he wants to encourage people to understand that keeping the country clean is not just the responsibility of government authorities. Individual participation, patriotism and respect for the country’s culture should also reflect in how people treat their surroundings.

Jankovic highlights the ‘char diwari’ mentality

While organising clean-up drives, Jankovic has come across different kinds of waste discarded in public spaces. He said broken glass was particularly concerning because it could injure pedestrians and animals. Plastic waste was another issue that troubled him, as careless disposal of garbage was affecting the surroundings.

However, he believes the problem goes beyond littering. Sharing his observations, he said, “I see in India you have Atithi Devo Bhava, right? So, when people see their guests cleaning, they feel bad and start doing it themselves. But here, the problem is the ‘char diwari’ mentality. People think whatever is inside my house is my problem. Everything outside is not. So, we have to change this because all of this is our land. We cannot expect the government to clean when we’re going to make it dirty again.”

Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw praises the Serbian man’s efforts

Jankovic’s work received appreciation from Biocon founder Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw, who reacted to his initiative on social media platform X.

Praising his contribution, she wrote, “A foreigner is helping to instil civic sense to Indians to keep our environment clean. Thank you.”
